barebones blogHow Crunch Mode works

Crunch Mode cuts a subject down to the notes your target grade actually needs. Here is how it decides what to keep, and how we check it against real exam papers.

What it does

You tell Crunch Mode the grade you're aiming for and the mark you expect in your coursework. It then hides the note points you're least likely to need for that grade, so you spend your time on the ones that earn marks.

Nothing is deleted. Every hidden point is one tap away with Show crunched points, in your notes and in your flashcards. If you'd rather not see low-value points at all, switch on Completely hide low-value points. You can change any of this at any time.

Your AAC counts first

Your coursework is marked separately from the written exam, so Crunch Mode takes it off first and works out what you need on the written paper.

In Biology the Biology in Practice Investigation is worth 40% of your grade. An H3 needs 75% overall, so with 80% in the investigation you need (75 − 0.4 × 80) ÷ 0.6 = 72% on the written paper. The notes only have to cover that 72%.

If your coursework alone gets you the grade, Crunch says so. If the written paper would need over 100%, it tells you that too and keeps everything.

How each point is scored

Every Higher Level exam question from 2015 to 2026 is matched to the note points it tests. A point that keeps earning marks scores highly. One that has rarely or never come up scores low.

Foundations matter too. A point that later topics build on gets credit for the marks those topics earn, so the basics you need to understand the rest aren't cut just because they're rarely asked about directly. Where a paper lets you choose which questions to answer, Crunch Mode counts on you picking the ones you've studied, so you don't need every option.

Crunch Mode then hides points from the lowest score up until what's left just covers the mark you need.

Checked against real papers

To check it works, we replay the past. For each of the last seven papers (2020 to 2026), Crunch Mode is rebuilt using only the papers that came before it. Then we score that year's real paper as if you had learned only the points it kept.

Exams vary from year to year, so Crunch keeps a safety margin. It keeps enough that you would have reached your written target in about four years out of five, not just on an average year.

Most marks first

If you're aiming for an H1 or H2, almost everything counts, so there's little to cut. Crunch Mode still helps by ordering your work. You start with the points worth the most marks (enough for an H3), and Show crunched points adds the rest when you're ready.

What "less study" means

The percentage is the share of study time Crunch Mode saves you. Study time is measured at about 40 words a minute of active learning (reading, understanding and testing yourself), with long or complex points counting one and a half times. All of Biology's notes come to about 135 hours this way.

What it can't do

Crunch Mode is built from past papers, and past papers are a guide, not a promise. The exam can ask about anything on the course. If you have the time, learn the crunched points too. Crunch Mode is for when time is short and you want every hour to count.
