# Leaving Cert Geography (Higher Level) — course outline for the content overhaul

## Read this first (instructions for the content model)

- **The scope comes from the NCCA *Curriculum Specification for Leaving Certificate
  Geography*** (for introduction September 2026; first exam 2028). It is in the repo at
  `relevant-resources/syllabi/geography.pdf`. Each chapter below is one topic heading from that
  spec. Each subsection is one learning outcome (LO), with the spec code shown in brackets.
- **"Cover"** lists what the spec says students learn about. Every item must be taught.
- **"SimpleStudy"** names the matching SimpleStudy topic or topics, taken from a screen
  recording of their Higher Level Geography course on 2026-10-15. Use them as a guide to
  structure and depth only, and write all prose in your own words. SimpleStudy still teaches
  the *outgoing* 2006 syllabus, so many matches are only partial. **"none"** means the topic is
  new in 2026 and must be written from scratch.
- Do **not** create content for the SimpleStudy topics listed in Appendix A. They are not in
  the 2026 spec.
- Higher Level depth: apply key concepts, use named Irish and international examples, and
  include case studies wherever the spec says "one Global North / one Global South setting"
  or similar.
- Assessment: written exam 60%, Applied Geography Project 40%.

## Strand 1 — The physical environment

### Chapter 1: Tectonics

**1.1 Plate tectonics and plate boundaries (LO 1.1)**
- Cover: the global geography of lithospheric plates; constructive, transform and destructive
  boundaries; convection currents, subduction, sea-floor spreading, plate formation, slab pull.
- SimpleStudy: The Earth's Structure; Continental Drift; Sea-Floor Spreading; The Pacific Ring
  of Fire; Plate Boundaries; The Theory of Plate Tectonics.

**1.2 Fold mountains (LO 1.2)**
- Cover: how folding forms mountain ranges, with two examples: one in Ireland (e.g. the
  Munster ridge-and-valley mountains, Caledonian folding in Donegal/Mayo) and one international
  (e.g. the Alps, Himalayas or Andes).
- SimpleStudy: Folding and Faulting Activity (use only the folding part).

**1.3 Volcanic activity and landforms (LO 1.3)**
- Cover: volcanic activity at constructive margins, destructive margins and hotspots, and the
  resulting landforms, with Irish (e.g. Giant's Causeway, Antrim plateau) and international
  examples.
- SimpleStudy: Volcanic Activity.

**1.4 Impacts of volcanic activity (LO 1.4)**
- Cover: positive and negative economic, environmental and social impacts in one Global North
  setting (e.g. Iceland) and one Global South setting (e.g. Montserrat, Indonesia).
- SimpleStudy: Volcanic Activity (partial).

**1.5 Earthquakes: causes and effects (LO 1.5)**
- Cover: the causes of earthquakes, including the different types of plate movement, and
  their effects in one Global North and one Global South setting.
- SimpleStudy: Earthquake Activity.

**1.6 Earthquake preparation and response (LO 1.6)**
- Cover: short-term and long-term preparation and responses, and an evaluation of each, in one
  Global North (e.g. Japan) and one Global South (e.g. Haiti, Nepal) setting.
- SimpleStudy: Earthquake Activity (partial).

### Chapter 2: The Rock Cycle

**2.1 The rock cycle and rock formation (LO 1.7)**
- Cover: endogenic processes (metamorphism, magma cooling) and exogenic processes (volcanic
  activity, mechanical and chemical weathering, erosion, lithification); igneous, sedimentary
  and metamorphic rocks, with an Irish location for each type (e.g. basalt in Antrim, granite in
  Wicklow, limestone in the Burren, sandstone in Munster, marble in Connemara, quartzite at the
  Sugarloaf).
- SimpleStudy: Rocks; Weathering Processes and Landforms (use only the weathering processes).

### Chapter 3: Surface Processes

**3.1 Fluvial environments (LO 1.8)**
- Cover: river erosion, transport and deposition, and the landforms they create, in Irish
  settings.
- SimpleStudy: River Action.

**3.2 Coastal environments (LO 1.8)**
- Cover: coastal erosion, transport and deposition, and the landforms they create, in Irish
  settings.
- SimpleStudy: Coasts and the Sea.

**3.3 Glacial environments (LO 1.8)**
- Cover: glacial erosion, transport and deposition, and the landforms they create, in Irish
  settings.
- SimpleStudy: Glaciation and Glacial Processes.

**3.4 Flood management in Ireland (LO 1.9)**
- Cover: one flood management strategy (e.g. Shannon/OPW flood relief schemes,
  CFRAM), with discussion of how well it works.
- SimpleStudy: none.

**3.5 Coastal management in Ireland (LO 1.9)**
- Cover: one coastal management strategy (e.g. rock armour, groynes or managed retreat at a
  named Irish coast), with discussion of how well it works.
- SimpleStudy: none.

### Chapter 4: Atmosphere and Weather

**4.1 Structure of the atmosphere (LO 1.10)**
- Cover: the five layers: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere.
- SimpleStudy: The Atmosphere and Ocean Environments (partial).

**4.2 High- and low-pressure systems affecting Ireland (LO 1.11)**
- Cover: the uneven distribution of solar radiation; global atmospheric circulation and the
  Coriolis effect; how high- and low-pressure systems (including storms) form; their
  temperature, precipitation and wind characteristics.
- SimpleStudy: Solar Energy and Global Temperatures; Moisture in the Atmosphere; Oceans,
  Atmosphere, Weather and Climate; Weather Maps.

### Chapter 5: Climate and Climate Change

**5.1 Climate zones (LO 1.12)**
- Cover: tropical, temperate, continental, polar and arid climate zones.
- SimpleStudy: Climatic Environments.

**5.2 Ireland's temperate rainforest biome (LO 1.13)**
- Cover: how climate shapes its soils, flora and fauna.
- SimpleStudy: The Development of Soil (partial). SimpleStudy's biomes are hot desert and
  tropical rainforest, not this one.

**5.3 Impacts of climate change (LO 1.14)**
- Cover: impacts on physical and human environments: melting glaciers, ice caps and permafrost;
  ocean circulation; sea level rise; extreme weather events; farming practices; climate
  migration. Give Irish and international examples for each impact.
- SimpleStudy: none.

**5.4 Responses to climate change (LO 1.15)**
- Cover: international agreements (e.g. Kyoto, Paris); mitigation, including carbon capture and
  reducing greenhouse gas emissions; adaptation, including early warning systems and
  climate-resilient infrastructure. Give Irish, international and global examples.
- SimpleStudy: none (Environmental Impact is only loosely related).

## Strand 2 — The human environment

### Chapter 6: Human Settlement

**6.1 Site, situation and function (LO 2.1)**
- Cover: rural and urban settlements in Ireland, including local examples; evidence of site,
  situation and function in Irish-language placenames (e.g. baile, cill, áth, dún, droichead).
- SimpleStudy: The Dynamics of Settlement; Other Landscapes and Settlements (OS maps).

**6.2 Distribution of rural settlement (LO 2.2)**
- Cover: linear, dispersed and clustered patterns; physical and human factors: soil, drainage,
  relief, transport and other services, proximity to other settlements.
- SimpleStudy: The Dynamics of Settlement (partial).

**6.3 Sustainable responses to rural challenges (LO 2.3)**
- Cover: planning strategies at local and national level (e.g. Project Ireland 2040/National
  Planning Framework, Our Rural Future); community-based initiatives.
- SimpleStudy: none.

**6.4 Factors affecting urban development (LO 2.4)**
- Cover: development plans, employment, globalisation, service provision.
- SimpleStudy: Urban Land Use and Planning; Urban Growth and the Expansion of City Regions.

**6.5 Impacts of urban development and responses (LO 2.5)**
- Cover: the impacts of urban development on gentrification, land use, natural and built
  environments, service provision and urban sprawl. Discuss responses in one Irish city:
  conservation, renewal, redevelopment, ecocities and smart cities.
- SimpleStudy: Urban Problems; Urban Land Use and Planning.

### Chapter 7: Population and Migration

**7.1 Population size and distribution (LO 2.6)**
- Cover: the impact of conflict, culture, food, gender equality, health, physical landscape and
  natural resources, in Ireland and in one Global South country.
- SimpleStudy: The Dynamics of Population; Overpopulation; India — Human Processes.

**7.2 Population structure (LO 2.7)**
- Cover: population pyramids and characteristics for Ireland (including the student's local
  setting) and for one Global South country.
- SimpleStudy: The Dynamics of Population.

**7.3 Population change (LO 2.8)**
- Cover: factors affecting birth rates and ageing, gender equality, migration and rural
  depopulation, in Ireland (including the local setting) and in one Global South country.
- SimpleStudy: The Dynamics of Population; Overpopulation.

**7.4 Factors affecting international migration (LO 2.9)**
- Cover: colonial history, conflict, climate change, culture, demography, economy and
  government policy.
- SimpleStudy: Migration.

**7.5 Impacts of migration in Ireland (LO 2.10)**
- Cover: Ireland as both a receiving and a donor country; impacts on community relations,
  diversity, employment, service provision and remittances, including the student's local
  setting.
- SimpleStudy: Migration.

## Strand 3 — The global environment

### Chapter 8: Agriculture and Fisheries

**8.1 Agriculture in Ireland (LO 3.1)**
- Cover: the economic, environmental and social factors affecting agriculture; changing
  practices: intensification, organic approaches, urban food production and the use of
  technology.
- SimpleStudy: Primary Economic Activities (Irish and Paris Basin regions, partial).

**8.2 The fishing industry in Ireland (LO 3.2)**
- Cover: the development of aquaculture, inland fisheries and conservation.
- SimpleStudy: Primary Economic Activities (partial).

**8.3 EU policies on agriculture and fisheries (LO 3.3)**
- Cover: the impact of the CAP and the Common Fisheries Policy on Ireland.
- SimpleStudy: Ireland and the European Union.

### Chapter 9: Tourism

**9.1 Tourism patterns and trends in Ireland (LO 3.4)**
- Cover: tourist attractions; tourism infrastructure; patterns and trends, including visitor
  numbers and revenue.
- SimpleStudy: Tertiary Economic Activities (Irish regions); Factors Influencing the
  Development of Tourism in an Area.

**9.2 Mass tourism in a Mediterranean coastal setting (LO 3.5)**
- Cover: how mass tourism developed there, and its positive and negative economic,
  environmental and social impacts (e.g. Costa del Sol, Benidorm, Mallorca).
- SimpleStudy: Tertiary Economic Activities (Mezzogiorno, partial).

### Chapter 10: Globalisation

**10.1 Factors influencing globalisation (LO 3.6)**
- Cover: digital technology, finance, research and development, tax policies, transport.
- SimpleStudy: The Global Economy.

**10.2 The four freedoms of the EU (LO 3.7)**
- Cover: the opportunities and challenges of the free movement of goods, services, capital and
  labour.
- SimpleStudy: Ireland and the European Union; Development and Expansion of the European Union
  (partial).

**10.3 Location of an MNC in Ireland (LO 3.8)**
- Cover: access to infrastructure and services, markets, product life cycle, raw materials,
  skilled workforce, trade and tax policy, applied to one named MNC (e.g. Intel in Leixlip,
  Apple in Cork).
- SimpleStudy: Factors Influencing the Development of an Industry or Activity; The Global
  Economy.

### Chapter 11: Development Assistance and Cooperation

**11.1 Approaches to development and how it is measured (LO 3.9)**
- Cover: protectionism, modernisation and sustainable development as approaches; GDP, GNI,
  HDI and GDI as measures, with an evaluation of each; the three dimensions of the HDI (a long
  and healthy life, knowledge, a decent standard of living).
- SimpleStudy: Gross Domestic Product (GDP); Gross National Product (GNP, outdated; the spec
  uses GNI); The Human Development Index (HDI); Models of Development; Sustainable
  Development.

**11.2 Factors affecting the HDI (LO 3.10)**
- Cover: climate, colonial history, conflict and peace, debt and trade, government policies and
  physical landscape, in Ireland and in one Global South country.
- SimpleStudy: Economic Development in Ireland: Case Study; Economic Development in India: Case
  Study; Colonialism: A Development Issue; An Interdependent Global Economy.

**11.3 Ireland's humanitarian and development aid (LO 3.11)**
- Cover: the Government of Ireland's programme (Irish Aid); humanitarian assistance in one
  humanitarian crisis; overseas development cooperation in one Global South country.
- SimpleStudy: Empowering Economic and Human Development (partial).

### Chapter 12: Geopolitics

**12.1 The concept of geopolitics (LO 3.12)**
- Cover: geopolitics as the interrelationship between geography, demographics, economics,
  environment and politics.
- SimpleStudy: The Interaction of Economic, Political, and Cultural Activities (partial).

**12.2 Superpowers: hard and soft power (LO 3.13)**
- SimpleStudy: none.

**12.3 A geopolitical territorial dispute (LO 3.14)**
- Cover: one land, maritime or airspace dispute (e.g. the South China Sea); the actors
  involved; causes and impacts; proposed or realised responses.
- SimpleStudy: Changes in Political Boundaries and Their Impact on Cultural Groups; The
  Interaction of Different Cultural Groups and Political Regions; Nationality and the Nation
  State (all partial).

## Unifying Strand — Applying geographical thinking and skills

Students apply this strand across all chapters, and the exam tests it directly.

### Chapter 13: Geographical Thinking and Key Concepts

**13.1 The value of geography (LO U1–U2)**
- Cover: the scope of the discipline; the geographical perspective; how transferable
  geographical skills are; geography and geospatial technologies in planning and
  decision-making.
- SimpleStudy: none.

**13.2 Key concepts (LO U3)**
- Cover: change over time, power and influence, interactions, process, interconnections, scale,
  implications, spatial distribution, location, sustainability, patterns, systems, place,
  region.
- SimpleStudy: The Concept of a Region (only for the "region" concept).

### Chapter 14: Geographical Skills

**14.1 Ordnance Survey maps (LO U7, U9)**
- Cover: scale, grid references and direction; distance and area; height and slope; sketch maps
  and cross-sections; drainage patterns and the physical landscape; other landscapes and
  settlements.
- SimpleStudy: Ordnance Survey Maps (all six topics).

**14.2 Aerial photographs and satellite imagery (LO U7)**
- SimpleStudy: Aerial Photographs; Satellite Imagery.

**14.3 Weather maps (LO U7)**
- SimpleStudy: Weather Maps.

**14.4 Graphs, charts and statistics (LO U7, U9)**
- SimpleStudy: Graphs and Charts.

**14.5 Geospatial data and GIS (LO U7, U9)**
- SimpleStudy: Geographic Information Systems.

### Chapter 15: Geographical Inquiry and the Applied Geography Project

**15.1 Geographical questions and inquiry (LO U4–U5)**
- Cover: Who, What, Where, When, Why and How questions; the inquiry stages: research question,
  planning, gathering data, analysis, conclusions, communicating, evaluating and reflecting.

**15.2 Research methods and data (LO U6–U7)**
- Cover: desk-based research and fieldwork, including ethical, safety and sustainability
  considerations; primary vs secondary data; qualitative vs quantitative data.

**15.3 Evaluating and communicating inquiries (LO U8–U9)**
- Cover: how effective the question, methods and sources were; communicating ethically,
  including cultural awareness.

**15.4 The Applied Geography Project (40%)**
- Cover: the annual SEC common brief; around 20 hours of work; an individual report; the
  descriptors of quality (planning and conducting; analysis and communication; evaluation and
  reflection).
- SimpleStudy: Geographical Investigation (outgoing 20% component, partial); Written Exam.
