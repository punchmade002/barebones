# Package: ch0-s3 — Analysing, evaluating and presenting findings

- **loId:** `ch0-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch0`
- **Syllabus scope:** Unifying Strand, U4.1–U4.5 and U5.1–U5.4 of the NCCA *Curriculum Specification for Leaving Certificate Business* (first examined June 2027). Students learn about the need to analyse and evaluate findings to draw informed conclusions (U4) and the importance of communicating in a format and style appropriate to the target audience (U5). Students should be able to: analyse and interpret information and data (U4.1); evaluate the range of perspectives within the findings (U4.2); synthesise findings to create new or enhanced understanding (U4.3); appraise the value of this information (U4.4); evaluate findings to make informed decisions, reach informed conclusions or make recommendations (U4.5); use appropriate forms of communication (oral, visual, written, multimodal) for different purposes and audiences (U5.1); use graphs, charts, diagrams and graphics to support the presentation of findings (U5.2); identify how and why their perspectives may have changed based on their research (U5.3); present conclusions, perspectives and/or recommendations logically, using correct business terminology (U5.4; the outline's wording). No outcome is Higher Level only.
- **Neighbouring subsections (by subject):** developing research questions and managing and acknowledging sources; project planning; the Investigative Study itself (report sections, marks, formatting).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Mean, median and mode (junior cycle Mathematics), percentages and how to read a table.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Reading axes, units and titles on a graph.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Perspectives and stakeholders: owners, employees, customers, regulators, the community.

Interpreting data [U4.1]
- [ ] D1 Describe before explaining: quote start and end values and the direction.
- [ ] D2 Trends, comparisons (which is larger and by how much), percentages (state the base), averages (mean, median, mode; how an extreme value distorts the mean).
- [ ] D3 Patterns, trends and contradictions in the data; the most important aspects relative to the question (NCCA prompts).
- [ ] D4 Breaking information into smaller parts (NCCA wording).

Choosing and building graphics [U5.2]
- [ ] G1 Line graph for change over time; bar chart for comparing categories; pie chart for parts of a whole (few categories); scatter graph for a relationship between two variables.
- [ ] G2 A scatter graph shows association, not cause.
- [ ] G3 Label axes with units; a title; label each figure and refer to it in the text; a vertical axis that does not start at zero can exaggerate change.
- [ ] G4 Tables, diagrams and infographics: one main message per visual.

Analysis, evaluation, synthesis, appraisal [U4.1–U4.5]
- [ ] V1 The Appendix 1 definitions of analyse, evaluate (data), synthesise, appraise and interpret, quoted (section 2).
- [ ] V2 The three tasks differ: analysis breaks down and explains; evaluation judges; synthesis combines into a new answer; appraisal judges the value of information.
- [ ] V3 Evaluate the range of perspectives: weigh whose view is represented and whose is missing.
- [ ] V4 Describing data when asked to analyse earns little; the reasoning ("because", "so", "which means for the business") is what is asked for.

Conclusions, recommendations, limitations [U4.5]
- [ ] C1 A justified conclusion: restates and answers the research question, supported by evidence from more than one source, with a statement of what the evidence cannot show.
- [ ] C2 A recommendation: a specific action for a named audience that follows from the conclusion; no new evidence in it.
- [ ] C3 Limitations: name the weakness, explain its effect on the conclusion, rank by seriousness, suggest a fix; limitations of the question design, the research gathered and the analysis (Descriptors of Quality).
- [ ] C4 Do not overclaim: a small sample supports a tentative conclusion.

Reflecting on changed perspective [U5.3]
- [ ] R1 Record the starting view early; name the evidence that changed it; explain why; state any effect on the conclusion. (NCCA: how findings "influenced my perspective(s)".)

Presenting for an audience [U5.1, U5.4]
- [ ] P1 The audience decides format and detail: oral, visual, written, multimodal; a few characteristics of each.
- [ ] P2 Conclusions presented logically, with correct business terminology, defined where the audience may not know a term.
- [ ] P3 Coherent and consistent language throughout (Descriptors of Quality).

Cross-cutting angle
- [ ] X1 Ethics: report findings honestly, including results that go against your hypothesis; do not cherry-pick; do not mislead with a truncated axis.
- [ ] X2 Digital: spreadsheet charts and online tools build graphics; AI-generated analysis must be checked and acknowledged.
- [ ] X3 Sustainability: findings should be related to the cross-cutting themes where relevant (Descriptors of Quality, applying).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "analysis" and "evaluation" as interchangeable. Analysis breaks down and explains; evaluation judges worth against evidence (Appendix 1).
- Do not use: gross margin and market share as examples of terminology to define, because they belong to the finance and marketing topics and no checked definition is given here; use any business term the student already knows, such as "stakeholder".
- Do not use: "a pie chart can show any proportion data" without the limit, because a pie works only with a few categories that make up a total.
- Do not use: "a scatter graph shows that one variable causes the other", because association is not cause.
- Do not use: "a recommendation may introduce new evidence", because it must follow from the conclusion.
- Do not use: a fixed word or sentence count for conclusions or limitations as a syllabus rule, because the specification gives none; the report's suggested word counts belong to the Investigative Study.
- Confusable: a trend (direction over time) versus a pattern (any regularity) versus an outlier; percentage versus percentage point change (do not teach the latter unless needed); mean versus median; a finding (what the data shows) versus a conclusion (the answer to the question) versus a recommendation (what to do); a limitation of the study versus a risk in the plan versus a gap in information.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: SMART, sampling and judging a source's credibility (questions and sources); the parts of the plan and monitoring (project planning); the report's sections, marks, word limit, 10-image limit and formatting rules (the Investigative Study). In this subsection, mention graphics only for how to choose and label them.

## 4. What exams ask

The outline records no 2025 sample-paper question for this subsection. The Unifying Strand is assessed mainly through the Investigative Study, but the specification says the written exam also assesses "the learning described in ... the unifying strand", and the outline records that Higher Level questions use short stimulus (a graph, headlines, a company profile) and applied tools, so interpretation skills appear there. Verbs below are from Appendix 1; forms are expected uses, not recorded questions; no marks or frequencies are claimed.
- **Interpret / describe** a table or graph: start value, end value and direction, with figures, and a comparison or percentage with its base. A full answer quotes numbers; "it went up" is not enough.
- **Analyse** a trend: break it into parts, give a likely cause for each, link cause to effect on the business or a stakeholder.
- **Evaluate** a source or a finding: weigh strengths and limits against criteria (relevance, reliability, recency, bias, fact or opinion), then judge how far it supports the conclusion.
- **Distinguish** analysis from evaluation (or from synthesis): a definition of each, then the difference applied to one sample study.
- **Suggest / recommend**: a specific action, for a named audience, justified by a named finding.
- **State and explain limitations**: name two, state the effect of each on the conclusion, suggest a remedy.
- **Present**: pick a form for a given audience and justify it (written for a board, visual for the public, oral with visuals for a class).
- Common mistakes: describing when asked to analyse; no figures quoted; wrong chart for the data; a truncated axis ignored; a conclusion that does not answer the question; a recommendation with no link to the evidence; limitations written as "the sample was small" with no effect stated.
