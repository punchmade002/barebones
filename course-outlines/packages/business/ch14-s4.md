# Package: ch14-s4 — Teamwork and sustainable workplaces

- **loId:** `ch14-s4` · **chapterId:** `ch14`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 3, section 3.2, outcomes [3.2.9, 3.2.10]: teamwork and how collaboration affects the employee and the organisation; the opportunities and challenges of teams for employees and employers; how employers and employees can contribute to more sustainable workplaces. Outline scope: what makes an effective team; stages of team development as assumed knowledge; opportunities (skills sharing, creativity, motivation, productivity) and challenges (conflict, free-riding, groupthink, slower decisions) for each side; a sustainable workplace (environmental and social): energy and waste reduction, sustainable travel, green procurement, hybrid work's footprint, green teams, fair work; what each side contributes. No Appendix 2 glossary term (ESG and circular economy appear in the glossary; use only as noted below). Not Higher Level only.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A team is a group of people working together towards a shared goal; groups and teams in a business are formed by management.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Stages of team development: forming, storming, norming, performing; conflict often appears (storming) before the team settles.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Sustainability: meeting present needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs; covers environment, society and economy.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Stakeholders: employees, employers, customers, communities and the environment are affected by workplace decisions.

What makes an effective team [3.2.9]
- [ ] T1 Shared goals give common purpose.
- [ ] T2 Clear roles and responsibilities reduce duplication and confusion.
- [ ] T3 Open communication: information, concerns and feedback shared early.
- [ ] T4 Trust and psychological safety: members can disagree and admit mistakes without fear of punishment.
- [ ] T5 Balanced mix of skills and personalities.
- [ ] T6 Team development stages: forming, storming, norming, performing.

Opportunities of teamwork
- [ ] O1 Skills sharing: builds human capital across the team.
- [ ] O2 Creativity: a range of perspectives, more ideas, innovation.
- [ ] O3 Motivation: engagement and shared achievement.
- [ ] O4 Productivity: tasks divided by strengths, faster work, better quality.
- [ ] O5 Better decisions: several viewpoints reduce errors.
- [ ] O6 Support: colleagues to turn to, less stress.
- [ ] O7 Effects on the employee (learning, belonging, support) and on the organisation (output, innovation, retention) kept distinct.

Challenges of teamwork
- [ ] C1 Conflict: differences in personality, working style or priorities slow work.
- [ ] C2 Free-riding: some contribute less, causing resentment and an unfair workload.
- [ ] C3 Groupthink: seeking agreement so strongly that alternatives and dissent are ignored, giving poor decisions.
- [ ] C4 Slower decisions: consulting many people takes time.
- [ ] C5 Hidden individual performance makes fair appraisal harder.
- [ ] C6 Employers can limit these challenges with clear team goals and roles and by monitoring each member's contribution.
- [ ] C7 Effects on employees (stress, unfairness) vs employers (delay, poor decisions) kept distinct.

Sustainable workplaces [3.2.10]
- [ ] S1 Definition: a workplace that reduces its environmental impact and supports the social wellbeing of its people.
- [ ] S2 Environmental sustainability: fewer resources, less waste, lower emissions.
- [ ] S3 Social sustainability: fair work, decent pay, safe conditions, inclusion, respect.
- [ ] S4 Economic sustainability: measures affordable enough to maintain over time.
- [ ] S5 Energy reduction: switching off equipment, efficient lighting, heating controls, energy-efficient equipment.
- [ ] S6 Waste reduction: recycling, less paper, no single-use items in the canteen.
- [ ] S7 Sustainable travel: walking, cycling, public transport, car-sharing, electric vehicles for business travel where practical.
- [ ] S8 Green procurement: suppliers and products with lower environmental impact and a sound record.
- [ ] S9 Hybrid working footprint: fewer commutes and smaller offices reduce footprint, but home energy use must be counted.
- [ ] S10 Green teams: staff-led groups that propose and monitor improvements; spreads ownership.
- [ ] S11 Fair work: fair pay, safe conditions, inclusion.

Contributions
- [ ] E1 Employers: set sustainability policies, provide resources (recycling facilities), fund energy upgrades.
- [ ] E2 Employers: measure and report environmental performance.
- [ ] E3 Employers: train staff in sustainable practices.
- [ ] E4 Employees: apply sustainable habits (print less, switch off equipment).
- [ ] E5 Employees: join green teams or propose ideas.
- [ ] E6 Employees: sustainable commuting and choosing eco-friendly suppliers for team supplies.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] X1 Ethics: fair sharing of workload and credit in teams; inclusion of all members; greenwashing (claiming more than the firm does) is unethical.
- [ ] X2 Sustainability: all of S and E above; Corporate reporting may be framed as ESG (environmental, social, governance), see Appendix 2 below.
- [ ] X3 Digital: collaboration platforms support teams across locations (including remote and blended teams); digital tools reduce paper and travel; devices create electronic waste, so repair and reuse matter.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any energy-saving, emissions or cost-saving percentage or carbon figure, because none is checked and the amounts depend on the firm.
- Do not use: "remote or hybrid working is always better for the environment", because home energy use, device waste and extra travel can offset savings; the notes state this balance.
- Do not use: "teamwork always raises productivity", because free-riding, groupthink and conflict can lower it.
- Confusable: groupthink (the team ignores dissent) vs conflict (open disagreement) vs free-riding (unequal effort); a team (shared goal, interdependent roles) vs a group (people who happen to be together).
- Confusable: environmental vs social vs economic sustainability; green procurement (what the firm buys) vs sustainable travel (how people move); sustainable workplace vs corporate wellness (wellbeing initiatives).
- Boundary: motivation and appraisal (including appraising team members) belong to the motivation subsection; human capital and training belong to the HCM subsection; the full costs and benefits of remote and blended working, the ethics of surveillance, and the right to request remote work belong to the subsection on digital technology in the workplace; corporate wellness initiatives belong to the wellness subsection; leadership styles belong to a different subsection on leadership.
- Boundary: mention hybrid work here only as it affects the footprint.

## 4. What exams ask

- The 2025 sample papers, as summarised in the outline, record no item for this subsection. Do not claim one. Teamwork and sustainability still appear in the specification's cross-cutting theme of ethics and sustainability, and may be asked in any applied question, so give notes tips based on the command verbs only.
- "Identify" and "outline" tasks (Appendix 1: identify = recognise and state briefly; outline = give the main points) are likely for opportunities and challenges of teamwork: name the point and add a few words of effect. Common mistake: naming a challenge without saying who is affected (employee or employer).
- "Discuss" or "evaluate" tasks on whether teamwork helps a business want balanced points: two or three opportunities and two or three challenges, a way an employer limits the challenges, and a judgement tied to the case. Common mistake: only benefits.
- Sustainability questions ("explain how a business can contribute", "suggest measures") want measures that fit the case's business (energy reduction for a factory, paper and travel for an office), each with its effect on cost or the environment, and a contribution from both employers and employees. Common mistakes: general statements ("be green"), forgetting the social side (fair work), and forgetting home energy use when praising hybrid work.
- Distinct points count more than repeated ones: the three sustainability dimensions (environmental, social, economic) give a structure for a developed answer.
