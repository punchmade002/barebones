# Package: ch19-s3 — The annual Budget and workers; informed decisions as an employee

- **loId:** `ch19-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch19`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 4, section 4.3, [4.3.5] and [4.3.6]. Students learn about how government decisions affect people in employment and investigate how the annual Budget affects workers; they appreciate informed decisions as an employee and discuss workplace news. Outline scope: what the Budget is and when it happens; measures affecting workers (income tax bands and credits, USC, PRSI, minimum wage decisions, social welfare, childcare and housing supports, auto-enrolment pensions, cost-of-living measures); reading a simple payslip (assumed); one or two current workplace news stories. Footnote 3: use current employment legislation. This is the last subsection of the chapter, so it carries the "Applying my learning" news rule [4.3.6].

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The government raises money mainly through taxes and spends it on services and social welfare; a surplus or deficit follows from the difference.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Gross pay, deductions and net (take-home) pay.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) The Oireachtas (Dáil and Seanad) passes laws; the Minister for Finance presents the Budget.

The Budget
- [ ] B1 The annual Budget is the government's plan for raising tax and spending for the coming year; usually announced in the autumn for the following year.
- [ ] B2 Tax changes are given legal effect through the Finance Act after debate in the Oireachtas.
- [ ] B3 It sets tax bands, tax credits, social welfare rates and spending on health, education and housing.
- [ ] B4 Workers are affected directly (take-home pay) and indirectly (public services).

Measures that affect workers
- [ ] M1 Income tax: charged at different rates across bands; widening a band lowers tax for those earning above the old threshold.
- [ ] M2 Tax credits reduce the tax owed; a larger credit raises take-home pay for everyone claiming it.
- [ ] M3 Universal Social Charge (USC): separate charge on gross income with its own bands, paid in addition to income tax.
- [ ] M4 PRSI: contribution from pay funding certain social welfare benefits (illness benefit, State pension) and affecting entitlement.
- [ ] M5 A Budget change to any of these can alter net pay while gross pay is unchanged.
- [ ] M6 Minimum wage decisions: raise low-paid incomes, raise employers' costs and may affect hiring.
- [ ] M7 Social welfare: affects households with children, jobseekers, carers, pensioners; some payments are means tested.
- [ ] M8 Childcare supports: lower the cost of care and make it easier for parents to take up or keep work.
- [ ] M9 Housing supports (social housing, help with rent, rent tax credit): change income left after housing costs.
- [ ] M10 Cost-of-living measures: one-off payments or credits boosting income for a period without changing the tax system.
- [ ] M11 Indirect taxes (VAT, excise duties) affect spending power even when income taxes are unchanged.

Auto-enrolment pensions
- [ ] P1 Eligible workers are enrolled in a workplace pension by default, with the option to opt out.
- [ ] P2 Contributions come from the worker, the employer and the State.
- [ ] P3 Many workers have no pension; saving early benefits from long-term growth.
- [ ] P4 Before opting out, check the contribution rate, the employer's contribution and the State top-up.

Trade-offs and informed decisions
- [ ] T1 A Budget is a trade-off: tax cuts can reduce funds for services; more spending may need higher taxes or borrowing.
- [ ] T2 Who gains: low-paid workers from minimum wage rises and targeted credits; higher earners from band changes.
- [ ] T3 Payslip: gross pay, deductions (income tax, USC, employee PRSI, agreed pension or union contributions), net pay; check against hours and agreed rate to spot errors.
- [ ] T4 Informed employee: check the contract, pension options and statutory rights; compare jobs on total benefits (pension matching, sick pay, flexible working) not headline pay; understand how Budget changes affect take-home pay.
- [ ] T5 Reading workplace news critically: identify the decision, who is affected and the evidence; weigh gainers and losers, the government's reasons and the trade-offs.

Applying my learning (news)
- [ ] N1 One or two current Irish news examples linked to the topic, with month, year and source (see §2): Budget 2027, October 2026; the start of MyFutureFund auto-enrolment, January 2026. Show how to analyse each: the decision, effect on workers, reasons and trade-offs.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical: fairness of who gains or loses from a Budget; whether a worker should opt out of a pension for short-term pay; honesty in reading claims about "average" gains.
- [ ] E2 Sustainability: Budget decisions on fuel excise and carbon-related taxes balance cost-of-living relief against environmental goals; long-term pension saving as financial sustainability.
- [ ] E3 Digital: pension enrolment and contributions are managed through an online portal; payslips and tax records are digital, so employees should check them online.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any income tax rate, USC rate, PRSI rate, tax credit amount or tax band other than the dated Budget 2027 figures above, because they change each year and are not checked.
- Do not use: "Budget 2026" measures as current, because Budget 2027 was announced in October 2026 and its changes take effect from January 2027.
- Do not use: any age limit or earnings threshold for auto-enrolment, because they are not checked.
- Do not use: "the Budget is always on a fixed day", because only the autumn, usually October, pattern is checked.
- Do not use: the old syllabus's household budgeting, household management and ratio material, because it is old syllabus only.
- Do not use: personalised financial advice (such as "you should opt out"); explain what to check instead.
- Confusable: tax credit (reduces tax owed) versus tax band (income taxed at one rate); gross versus net pay; USC versus PRSI versus income tax; the Budget (announcement) versus the Finance Act (law).
- Confusable: PRSI (insurance record for benefits) versus auto-enrolment contributions (pension savings).
- Neighbouring subsection: the minimum wage as a legal right under the National Minimum Wage Act, and the Acts on working time, sick leave and dismissal, belong in the notes on employee rights. Here the minimum wage appears only as a Budget decision.
- Neighbouring subsection: trade unions, collective bargaining and the gig economy belong in the notes on trade unions and the gig economy; the notes on the Budget mention gig workers only as workers whose tax and pension position differs.
- Neighbouring chapter: personal financial products such as savings, loans and insurance are taught in the personal finance chapter; here cover only pensions through auto-enrolment.

## 4. What exams ask

- The outline records no 2025 sample paper question for this subsection. Do not claim one. Apply the Appendix 1 verbs: "identify" a measure, "outline" what a Budget change means for a worker, "explain" how a change alters net pay, "analyse" or "discuss" a news story (who gains, who loses, trade-offs), "evaluate" whether a measure is fair. The specification's news outcomes mean a headline may be given as stimulus, in the style the SEC uses for real current Irish stories.
- A full answer: names the measure (for example a tax credit increase), says how it changes gross pay, deductions or net pay, names the groups affected (low-paid, parents, renters) and one trade-off (cost to the Exchequer, effect on employers). For a news story: identify the decision, effect on workers, the government's reason, and a balanced judgement with evidence from the story.
- Common mistakes: confusing credits with bands; saying a tax cut raises gross pay; treating USC or PRSI as the same as income tax; giving only gains and no trade-offs; stating rates or amounts from memory; copying a headline without analysis.
- Tip for notes: a short worked payslip (gross, income tax, USC, PRSI, pension, net) with sample numbers labelled "sample business" is an effective model for explaining net pay.
