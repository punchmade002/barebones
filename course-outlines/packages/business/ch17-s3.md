# Package: ch17-s3 — Protecting personal data; making informed consumer decisions

- **loId:** `ch17-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch17`
- **Syllabus:** Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 4, section 4.1, outcomes [4.1.4] and [4.1.5]. Students learn how European regulation protects personal data and investigate it; they appreciate the importance of informed consumer decisions and discuss consumer stories in the news ("Applying my learning"). The GDPR taught here cannot be the student's chosen EU regulation for the section on the European Union.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) An EU regulation is binding law in every member state without needing to be turned into national law (unlike a directive).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Businesses collect personal data (orders, accounts, loyalty cards, website tracking) and consumers share it in exchange for service.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) A regulator is a public body that enforces a law.

The GDPR
- [ ] G1 The General Data Protection Regulation: an EU regulation, applying directly in every member state including Ireland; applied from 25 May 2018, replacing earlier EU rules.
- [ ] G2 Scope: organisations processing the personal data of people in the EU, wherever the organisation is based.
- [ ] G3 Personal data: any information relating to an identified or identifiable living person (name, email, location).
- [ ] G4 Special category data (for example health) has stronger protection and needs an additional condition.
- [ ] G5 Data controller (decides purposes and means; main responsibility) and data processor (acts on the controller's instructions); online shop as controller, a firm sending emails from its list as processor; written contract required between them.
- [ ] G6 Six lawful bases; give consent, contract and legal obligation in detail, and name vital interests, public task and legitimate interests with a short gloss.
- [ ] G7 Valid consent: freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, by clear positive action (pre-ticked box invalid); can be withdrawn at any time, as easily as given.
- [ ] G8 Core principles (Article 5): lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; accountability.
- [ ] G9 Data subject rights: access, rectification, erasure ("right to be forgotten"), portability, objection (including to direct marketing, which must then stop).
- [ ] G10 Breaches: notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours where feasible; tell affected individuals without undue delay where high risk (example: lost laptop with customer records).
- [ ] G11 Enforcement: the Data Protection Commission as Ireland's supervisory authority; the Data Protection Act 2018 gives further effect to the GDPR in Irish law and sets the DPC's powers; fines up to the higher tier (see section 2); other sanctions (order to comply or stop processing).
- [ ] G12 Consumers: rights to see, correct and delete, and to complain to the DPC.
- [ ] G13 Businesses: transparent privacy notices (what, why, how long); data protection by design; compliance costs (training, audits) against the benefit of customer trust.
- [ ] G14 Digital and ethical angle: online tracking, cookies, targeted advertising, apps and AI use of data; ethical handling of data builds trust; data minimisation as good practice.

Protecting your own data
- [ ] P1 Strong, unique passwords and two-factor authentication.
- [ ] P2 Review privacy settings; limit data shared.
- [ ] P3 Decline non-essential cookies.
- [ ] P4 Treat unsolicited messages asking for personal or payment details as possible phishing.
- [ ] P5 Read privacy notices; ask for accounts and data to be deleted when no longer used.

Informed consumer decisions and the news
- [ ] I1 Value for money: compare price, quality and terms, avoid paying for extras.
- [ ] I2 Avoiding fraud and scams: check seller identity, reviews, payment security.
- [ ] I3 Sustainability and ethics: align spending with values.
- [ ] I4 "Applying my learning": use a current Irish news story to show how the rules work; check facts and give an informed opinion (see the news row in section 2).
- [ ] I5 Link back: where relevant, name the right from the notes on consumer rights that the story shows being used or breached.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the claim that "consent is the only lawful basis"; there are six.
- Do not use: "Vital interests, public task and legitimate interests are ... covering emergencies, public functions and business interests" as a precise legal test; use it only as a short gloss.
- Do not use: any fine figure other than "up to EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide turnover" and the PTSB amounts; the DPC's larger fines (against named multinationals) are unchecked.
- Do not use: "within 72 hours" without "where feasible" and "unless unlikely to risk individuals".
- Confusable: controller versus processor; personal data versus special category data; consent (one basis) versus any lawful basis; DPC (regulator) versus CCPC (consumer and competition regulator); GDPR (EU regulation) versus the Data Protection Act 2018 (Irish Act); right to erasure is not absolute ("in certain circumstances").
- Neighbouring subsections: consumer Acts, remedies, the CCPC and buying outside the EU belong to the notes on consumer rights and legislation; the effects of digital technology on buying behaviour, ethics and sustainability and the circular economy belong to the notes on ethical and sustainable consumption. Mention them here only to link an informed decision to them.
- GDPR must not be offered as the EU regulation for the section on the European Union.

## 4. What exams ask

The outline lists no 2025 sample paper question on this subsection, so nothing is drawn from the papers. What the verbs imply:
- "Describe" or "explain" a GDPR right or principle: name it, say what it lets the person do or obliges the business to do, with a short example (for example a customer asking a shop to delete their account data). Mistake: listing right names with no meaning.
- "Distinguish" controller and processor: make the difference clear with an example of each.
- "Outline" consumer actions to protect data: essential points only (passwords, privacy settings, cookies, phishing).
- "Discuss" or "evaluate" the impact of the GDPR on businesses and consumers: both sides (rights and trust for consumers; compliance costs and fines versus trust for businesses) and a judgement. Mistake: one-sided answers.
- "Discuss" a news story (outcome 4.1.5): state the facts briefly, link it to the rule it shows, say what the business and the consumers should learn, give an informed opinion. A full answer uses a real, accurate example from the package.
- Applied questions with a data-breach stimulus: identify the breach, the duty to notify (72 hours where feasible), who must be told and the possible penalty.
- Higher Level answers need 3-4 developed points with effects.
