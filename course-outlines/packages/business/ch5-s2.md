## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Exports (goods and services sold abroad) and imports (bought from abroad); visible trade (goods) and invisible trade (services).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Tariffs, quotas and the common external tariff of a customs union (developed in the notes on trading blocs).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Exchange rate: the price of one currency in terms of another; a stronger euro buys more foreign currency.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Economies of scale: average cost per unit falls as output rises.
- [ ] A5 (assumed) Methods of entering markets: exporting, agents and distributors, licensing, franchising, joint ventures, direct investment. Each with its control, cost and risk.
- [ ] A6 (assumed) Surplus and deficit: a surplus is a positive balance; a deficit is a negative balance.

Ireland as a small open economy [1.5.5]
- [ ] S1 Small open economy: small home market, depends heavily on exports and imports.
- [ ] S2 Because the home market is small, firms need overseas customers to grow.
- [ ] S3 Dependence on trade means a fall in demand in a major partner can reach Irish jobs quickly.
- [ ] S4 What Ireland exports (pharmaceuticals, ICT services, food), much of it by multinational firms.
- [ ] S5 Part of the profits of foreign-owned exporters leaves Ireland, so export earnings do not all stay in the economy.

Why Irish businesses trade internationally [1.5.4, 1.5.5]
- [ ] R1 Growth: more customers than the home market.
- [ ] R2 Economies of scale: higher output lowers cost per unit.
- [ ] R3 Spreading risk: sales in several markets reduce reliance on one economy or customer.
- [ ] R4 Access to inputs: raw materials, components and skilled staff that are cheaper or better abroad.

Factors to consider before entering a market [1.5.4]
- [ ] F1 Taxes and tariffs. F2 Costs (labour, transport, distribution, rent). F3 Exchange rates. F4 Regulation (standards, labelling, licensing). F5 Competition. F6 Language and culture. Each explained with how it affects cost, price or marketing, and a reason; a full answer links the factor to the business's decision.

Methods of entering a foreign market (assumed knowledge)
- [ ] M1 Exporting: least cost and commitment, limited control.
- [ ] M2 Agents and distributors: local knowledge, less control over price.
- [ ] M3 Licensing: royalties for use of patents, brand or technology.
- [ ] M4 Franchising: fees for use of the brand and business model.
- [ ] M5 Joint venture: shared ownership and risk with a local partner.
- [ ] M6 Direct investment (subsidiary or factory): most control, highest risk.

Benefits and challenges, compared [1.5.5]
- [ ] C1 Benefits: larger customer base and profits; competition pushes quality and innovation; diversified markets.
- [ ] C2 Challenges: language, legal and business-custom differences; currency movements; trade barriers, sanctions or political change; distance and transport, especially perishable goods.
- [ ] C3 Compare means similarities and differences with reference to both throughout; end with a judgement.

Balance of trade and balance of payments [1.5.6]
- [ ] P1 Balance of trade = value of visible exports minus value of visible imports; surplus or deficit.
- [ ] P2 Balance of payments: the record of all transactions between Ireland and the rest of the world over a period.
- [ ] P3 Current account: goods, services, primary income (profits, interest) and secondary income (transfers).
- [ ] P4 Capital account (capital transfers) and financial account (investment flows such as FDI); errors and omissions item.
- [ ] P5 Distinguish the two: balance of trade counts only goods; balance of payments counts goods, services, income and capital and financial flows.
- [ ] P6 Calculation 1: balance of trade from given figures. Calculation 2: current account (goods + services + primary income + secondary income) from given figures. Show every stage of the working (Appendix 1: calculate).
- [ ] P7 Irish figures are heavily influenced by multinational companies, because the current account includes profit flows; the CSO publishes a modified measure for this reason.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] X1 Sustainability: longer supply chains and freight add emissions; sourcing nearer home cuts transport emissions.
- [ ] X2 Ethical: sourcing inputs where labour costs are lowest raises concerns about wages and working conditions in supplier factories; businesses may be expected to check suppliers.
- [ ] X3 Digital: online marketplaces and websites let small Irish firms sell abroad without a local presence; digital customs documentation and payment systems ease exporting; digital services are part of services trade in the balance of payments.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Balance of payments is the combined total of the current, capital and financial accounts" as a stand-alone definition, because the three accounts are recorded together, they balance in accounting terms (with errors and omissions) and a "balance of payments deficit or surplus" in Irish exam questions means the current account. Say: the balance of payments records the current account (goods, services, income) together with the capital and financial accounts; the current account balance is the figure normally calculated.
- Do not use: any real Irish export or import figure, goods or services share, or exchange rate, because they change and are unchecked; the 2025 CSO figures in section 2 are the only ones allowed, dated.
- Do not use: "Ireland exports mostly its own food", because much of the export value comes from foreign-owned firms, as the notes say; keep the reasoning rather than shares.
- Confusable: balance of trade (goods only, visible) versus balance of payments (all transactions). A trade surplus does not mean a current account surplus, because services and income flows can reverse it (profit outflows are large for Ireland). Here the balance of trade means visible goods only, as the outline states.
- Confusable: tariffs (taxes on imports) versus quotas (limits on quantity); exporting versus foreign direct investment; licensing (royalties) versus franchising (fees and the whole business model).
- Confusable: a stronger euro hurts exporters (their goods cost more abroad) but helps importers; do not state it the other way round.
- Belongs elsewhere: what trading blocs are and EU membership (trading blocs); social and environmental consequences, political and cultural risk, interdependence and technology (globalisation); FDI as an inflow and IDA Ireland (FDI). Mention FDI here only as an item in the financial account.

## 4. What exams ask

- Students name or explain three factors a firm should consider before entering an international market. A full answer gives three distinct factors from taxes and tariffs, costs, exchange rates, regulation, competition and language and culture, and explains each with its effect on the firm (for example, a tariff raises the price against local rivals). Common mistake: naming factors with no effect, or giving two factors that are really the same.
- Fill-in-the-blank items test balance of trade as visible exports minus visible imports (goods only). Common mistake: including services.
- Calculate: given export and import figures, work out the balance of trade and state whether it is a surplus or a deficit; or work out the current account from four lines. A full answer shows each stage of the working, gives the answer with the unit, and labels surplus or deficit. Common mistake: wrong sign on net income lines; leaving out the label.
- Distinguish balance of trade from balance of payments (Appendix 1: make the differences between the two clear). A full answer says what each records and gives at least one difference (goods only versus goods, services, income and financial flows).
- Explain why Irish businesses trade globally with reference to the open economy: link a small home market to the need for exports, with a reason for each point.
- Compare the benefits and challenges of trading internationally: a full answer covers both, referring to each throughout, and ends with a judgement. Common mistake: two separate lists with no comparison.
