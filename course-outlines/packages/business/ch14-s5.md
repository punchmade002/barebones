# Package: ch14-s5 — Digital technology in the workplace: remote and blended working

- **loId:** `ch14-s5` · **chapterId:** `ch14`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 3, section 3.2, outcomes [3.2.11–3.2.13]: the disruptive impact of digital technology, focusing on how diverse ways of working, including remote and blended work, affect employers, employees and the world of work; investigate digital technology's impact on the workplace; identify the opportunities and challenges of remote and blended work for both sides; analyse the associated ethical and sustainability issues. Outline scope: digital technologies changing work; remote vs blended (hybrid) defined; opportunities and challenges for employers and for employees; the right to request remote work under the Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023 and the WRC code of practice; ethical issues (surveillance, fairness, right to disconnect); sustainability issues. Glossary term: disruptive impact. This is the topic of the 2027 Business Alive Investigative Study brief (an individual report on how a work practice using digital technology affects employers or employees); notes explain the content, never write a student's report. Not Higher Level only.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Digital technology: electronic tools and systems (internet, software, data, devices) used to create, store, share and process information.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Primary and secondary sources of information (needed for the investigative study).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Employees have a duty of care owed to them by employers (health and safety).
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Personal data is regulated by the GDPR.

How digital technology changes work [3.2.11]
- [ ] D1 Disruptive impact defined with the Appendix 2 wording (section 2); it can be positive or negative.
- [ ] D2 Collaboration platforms (Microsoft Teams, Slack, Zoom): communicate, share files, meet across locations.
- [ ] D3 Cloud computing: data and software on remote servers, accessible from any device with internet.
- [ ] D4 Automation and artificial intelligence: handle repetitive tasks (data entry, routine customer queries), changing jobs and skills needed.
- [ ] D5 Monitoring software: records log-in times, keystrokes or screen use; productivity data, privacy concerns.
- [ ] D6 Disruptive technology can make established ways of working obsolete quickly; firms and workers must adapt processes and skills.

Remote and blended working defined
- [ ] R1 Remote working: paid work from a location other than the employer's premises, usually at home, with digital tools.
- [ ] R2 Blended (hybrid) working: a mix of employer premises and remote (for example two office days, three home days).
- [ ] R3 A fully remote firm has no central office for most staff; a blended firm keeps an office used some days.
- [ ] R4 Other diverse ways of working: flexible hours, compressed hours, four-day week; can combine with remote or blended.

Employers: opportunities
- [ ] EO1 Wider talent pool (rural areas, abroad), fills skills gaps.
- [ ] EO2 Lower office costs (smaller offices, hot-desking; rent, utilities, maintenance).
- [ ] EO3 Productivity gains for some (fewer interruptions, no commute) depending on work type and management.
- [ ] EO4 Retention and reduced absence.
- [ ] EO5 Business continuity during disruption (severe weather, premises closure).

Employers: challenges
- [ ] EC1 Supervision: manage by results; clear objectives and regular communication.
- [ ] EC2 Culture: informal contact weakens.
- [ ] EC3 Security: home networks, personal devices raise breach, phishing and data-loss risk.
- [ ] EC4 Health and safety: duty of care continues; assess the home workspace much as an office.
- [ ] EC5 Equipment and support: equipment, connectivity, IT support.
- [ ] EC6 Two-tier workforce: on-site staff may gain visibility and opportunities.

Employees: opportunities
- [ ] PO1 Flexibility (childcare, caring, study).
- [ ] PO2 Time and cost savings from no commute.
- [ ] PO3 Work-life balance.
- [ ] PO4 Autonomy and higher job satisfaction.
- [ ] PO5 Access to work for people for whom travel is difficult, including some people with disabilities.
- [ ] PO6 Quieter conditions help some people concentrate (depends on home).

Employees: challenges
- [ ] PC1 Isolation and weaker sense of belonging.
- [ ] PC2 Blurred boundaries: hard to switch off, always on call.
- [ ] PC3 Career visibility: missed networking, overlooked for promotion (proximity bias).
- [ ] PC4 Less informal learning.
- [ ] PC5 Poor home workspace (posture, comfort, no quiet place).
- [ ] PC6 Extra household costs (heating, electricity, internet).

Legal rights [3.2.12]
- [ ] L1 The Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023 gives employees a statutory right to request remote working.
- [ ] L2 The employer must consider the request properly (objectively, fairly and reasonably, as in the WRC code) and should explain a refusal.
- [ ] L3 The WRC code of practice explains how requests should be handled (name in section 2).
- [ ] L4 Handle requests consistently to avoid unfair-treatment claims.

Ethical issues [3.2.13]
- [ ] H1 Surveillance: intrusive, damages trust without clear purpose.
- [ ] H2 Data protection: monitoring must be lawful, transparent, proportionate (GDPR); tell staff what is collected and why.
- [ ] H3 Fairness: judging visible activity rather than results penalises efficient workers and those with caring duties.
- [ ] H4 Equal access: staff without suitable space, broadband or privacy are disadvantaged.
- [ ] H5 Right to disconnect: switching off outside hours; Ireland has a code of practice (status in section 2).
- [ ] H6 Mental health: always-on expectations cause stress and burnout; duty of care extends to remote workers.
- [ ] H7 Equal treatment: promotion and development must not depend on being in the office.

Sustainability issues [3.2.13]
- [ ] U1 Fewer commutes: lower transport emissions and congestion.
- [ ] U2 Home energy use offsets part of the saving, especially with fossil-fuel heating.
- [ ] U3 Office footprint: smaller offices use less, but a partly used building still uses energy.
- [ ] U4 Equipment: more laptops, monitors and devices means electronic waste and manufacturing emissions; repair and reuse policies matter.
- [ ] U5 Regional development: people can live in rural areas, supporting local services and easing city pressure, but city-centre footfall may fall.
- [ ] U6 Outcomes depend on policies: renewable energy, reusing equipment, avoiding unnecessary meeting travel.

Applying my learning and the study
- [ ] N1 One or two current Irish news examples on hybrid and remote working (section 2).
- [ ] N2 Guidance for the Business Alive study: identify a work practice that uses digital technology, its effects on employers or employees, and sources (primary and secondary) to use; never write a report.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "may refuse it only on reasonable grounds" as a statement of the legal test in the Act, because the checked sources say only that the employer must consider the request objectively, fairly and reasonably under the WRC code; the grounds, time limits and any service requirement were not checked, so give none.
- Do not use: a time limit (for example "within four weeks") or a service requirement for requests, because it is unchecked.
- Do not use: "Ireland has a legal right to disconnect", because the right to disconnect is a WRC code of practice, not a statutory right.
- Do not use: "a new right-to-disconnect code was introduced in 2026", because the checked sources show the 2021 code is the current one and no 2026 revision was found.
- Do not use: percentages of staff working from home, productivity gains or commuting emissions, because they change and are unchecked (the checked Irish Times survey is the only figure source, dated September 2026).
- Confusable: remote (away from the employer's premises) vs blended or hybrid (mix); flexible working (any change to hours or place) vs remote working; monitoring (collecting data) vs surveillance (the ethical concern when intrusive).
- Confusable: disruptive impact (a change that can be positive or negative) vs disruption (a problem); the employer's duty of care vs the employee's right to request.
- Boundary: teamwork and the general footprint of workplaces belong to the subsection on teamwork and sustainable workplaces; wellbeing and the leader's role belong to the wellness subsection; the nine grounds and statutory employee rights belong to the chapter on employee rights; recruitment technology (applicant tracking, AI screening) belongs to the recruitment subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Sample paper 1, Question 1(b) (2025) asks students to identify three challenges a firm faces with a four-day week and blended working. "Identify" (Appendix 1) means recognise and state briefly. A full answer gives three distinct challenges (supervision, culture, security, equal treatment of staff, coordination of days, customer coverage) each with a short phrase tying it to the firm in the stimulus. Common mistakes: giving benefits instead of challenges, three versions of the same point, and ignoring the four-day week in the stimulus.
- Sample paper 2, Question 2(e) (2025) asks students to evaluate how digital technologies have affected the modern workplace. "Evaluate" (Appendix 1) means making judgements about ideas or methods and weighing evidence. A full answer covers several technologies (collaboration platforms, cloud, automation and AI, monitoring) with effects on employers and employees, positive and negative, followed by a judgement (for example benefits depend on management and policy). Common mistakes: listing technologies without effects, only positives, and no conclusion.
- "Analyse the ethical and sustainability issues" tasks want named issues (surveillance, fairness, right to disconnect; commutes, home energy, device waste) with who is affected and a consequence, not a mere list.
- "Discuss" items on remote work want both sides for both employer and employee, then a view.
