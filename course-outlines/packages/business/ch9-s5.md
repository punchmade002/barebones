# Package: ch9-s5 — Analytical tools: the power-interest grid for customers and STEEPLE

- **loId:** `ch9-s5` · **chapterId:** `ch9`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 2, section 2.4, learning outcomes [2.4.7] and [2.4.8]; also the "Applying my learning" outcome [2.8], which asks students to analyse business news and give an informed opinion. Students learn the potential of a range of analytical tools to analyse customer interest and the external environment; use a power-interest grid to analyse customer interest and adjust the marketing mix as necessary; conduct a STEEPLE analysis to understand the external environment and identify issues of concern for a business. Outline scope: the power-interest grid (defined in the earlier notes on stakeholder mapping) applied to customer groups, with how the mix is adjusted for each quadrant; the seven STEEPLE factors with examples for an Irish business; STEEPLE assesses external factors only; how findings feed decisions; limits of both tools. Glossary terms: power-interest grid; STEEPLE analysis. No Higher Level only content.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Stakeholders and stakeholder mapping, and the four quadrants with their actions (manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor), from the earlier notes on stakeholder mapping. Recall in two lines, then apply to customers.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Customer groups and segmentation, and the seven Ps (the mix to be adjusted).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) A business's internal and external environment: internal factors can be controlled, external ones cannot.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) SWOT as a tool for internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats (one-line recall for contrast only).

Power-interest grid applied to customers
- [ ] G1 Quote the Appendix 2 definition of the power-interest grid.
- [ ] G2 Vertical axis power: ability to influence or block the business (for customers: purchasing weight or influence through reviews). Horizontal axis interest: how strongly the group cares about or is affected by the business's choices.
- [ ] G3 Four quadrants: high power and high interest, high power and low interest, low power and high interest, low power and low interest.
- [ ] G4 Purpose when applied to customers: show which customer groups can most affect sales or reputation and how closely each should be managed.
- [ ] G5 Steps: list the customer groups; judge power and interest with a reason; place each; choose the mix adjustment.

Adjusting the mix by quadrant
- [ ] Q1 High power, high interest (manage closely): key accounts and loyal high-spending customers; tailored product, personal service, regular engagement (a dedicated account manager).
- [ ] Q2 High power, low interest (keep satisfied): influential customers not focused on the brand; reliable service, a fair price, occasional updates to stop them switching.
- [ ] Q3 Low power, high interest (keep informed): enthusiastic customers such as a brand community; regular content, early access to launches, a route for feedback.
- [ ] Q4 Low power, low interest (monitor): occasional buyers; low-cost promotion such as automated offers.
- [ ] Q5 Adjust price, promotion, product emphasis as a group's power or interest changes; do not treat every customer the same.
- [ ] Q6 A worked placement of at least four customer groups of a sample Irish business, with a reason for each and the mix adjustment.

Limitations of the grid
- [ ] L1 Static snapshot: power and interest change over time.
- [ ] L2 Subjective: two managers may place a group differently.
- [ ] L3 Does not show reasons for interest or links between groups (one influential customer shaping others).
- [ ] L4 Needs research data to support placement.

STEEPLE
- [ ] S1 Quote the Appendix 2 definition of STEEPLE analysis.
- [ ] S2 Social: population, lifestyle and attitudes (more people living alone, smaller packs).
- [ ] S3 Technological: new technology that changes products or processes (online ordering, contactless payment).
- [ ] S4 Economic: inflation, interest rates, employment levels, exchange rates.
- [ ] S5 Environmental: climate change, waste rules, scarcity of resources.
- [ ] S6 Political: government policy, elections, trade relations (taxes, subsidies, market access).
- [ ] S7 Legal: laws on consumer rights, employment, data protection, health and safety.
- [ ] S8 Ethical: society's expectations of fair treatment, honesty and responsible behaviour; shape reputation.
- [ ] S9 STEEPLE assesses external factors only; it does not assess internal strengths and weaknesses (SWOT covers both).
- [ ] S10 Example for an Irish business for every one of the seven factors (section 2).
- [ ] S11 Steps: list factors, rate each by likelihood of change and effect on the business, identify the issues of concern, decide the response.
- [ ] S12 Findings feed the business plan, the marketing mix and strategy; opportunities matter as much as threats.
- [ ] S13 Limits: long lists without ranking; uncertain forecasts; shows the environment but not how well the business can respond; depends on current, credible sources.
- [ ] S14 Compare the two tools (Appendix 1 "Compare"): the grid looks at customers and their influence; STEEPLE looks at external factors; use both with research data.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical and environmental factors in STEEPLE are the topic's ethics and sustainability angle; link to customers who value ethical and green products.
- [ ] E2 Digital: technological factor, and customer power through online reviews and social media; analytics provide the data for placing customers.

Applying my learning: current Irish news (required last item of the chapter)
- [ ] N1 Apply STEEPLE to the Combilift tariff story (news example 1): identify the political and economic factors, the effect on pricing, and the response; give an informed opinion with a reason.
- [ ] N2 Apply STEEPLE or the packaging link to the packaging and green-claims rules that began in 2026 (news example 2): identify the legal, environmental and ethical factors and the effect on the product and packaging decisions.
- [ ] N3 An informed opinion (a short evaluative sentence weighing the business's options, with a reason and what evidence would change it).

## 3. Traps

Do not use:
- Do not use: "STEEPLE assesses internal factors" as true; it is false. STEEPLE assesses external factors only (the sample-paper true/false item tests this).
- Do not use: "a government levy on sugary drinks" as a general example without naming it as the Irish drinks levy of 2018, and do not give its rate or yield.
- Do not use: "a moderate level of power" for a customer group. The grid uses high or low only; place the group in one quadrant and give the reason.
- Do not use: the existing keyTerm wording that the grid "plots stakeholders" without saying that, for this subsection, the stakeholders are customer groups; and keep the Appendix 2 definition as the wording.
- Do not use: SWOT as part of STEEPLE. Mention it once only as the tool that covers internal strengths and weaknesses.
- Do not use: any tariff percentage, loss, or sales change for Combilift other than the 15% tariff and the pricing response in section 2, because nothing else was verified.
- Do not use: a placement of customers as fixed facts about real firms; present all placements as judgements for the sample.

Confusable terms
- Power vs interest: high interest does not mean high power.
- Manage closely (high power, high interest) vs keep informed (low power, high interest) vs keep satisfied (high power, low interest) vs monitor (low power, low interest).
- STEEPLE (external only) vs SWOT (internal and external) vs PESTLE (a related older name; the course says STEEPLE).
- Political vs legal factors: policy and government action versus laws and regulations that must be obeyed.
- Environmental vs ethical factors: climate, waste and resources versus society's expectations of fair, honest conduct.
- Economic factors (inflation, interest, exchange rates) vs the business's own finances (internal).

## 4. What exams ask

(Source: SEC 2025 Higher Level Sample Paper 1, question 4(a)(ii), a true/false item stating that STEEPLE assesses internal factors, answer false; Appendix 1 verbs; the "applying my learning" outcome. No marks or frequencies are invented.)
- True or false on STEEPLE: the answer is that STEEPLE assesses external factors only. Common mistake: believing it includes strengths and weaknesses.
- Conduct a STEEPLE analysis (conduct = perform an activity): a full answer applies the factors to the business in the stimulus, with one concrete factor for each of the seven or the relevant ones, rates their effect and states the issues of concern and the response. Common mistake: defining each letter with no link to the business.
- Use the power-interest grid to analyse customers and adjust the marketing mix: a full answer places each customer group in a quadrant with a reason for its power and its interest, then gives a specific mix change for each. Common mistakes: placing without a reason, mixing up the quadrant actions, adjusting no P.
- Explain or discuss the limits of the tools (discuss = balanced review): a full answer gives two or three limits each with its effect and states the benefits, with a view on how to reduce the limits (research data, regular review).
- Compare the tools: customers' influence against the external environment, and say how they work together.
- Applying learning to news: analyse a current Irish business story with these tools and give an informed opinion. A full answer names the factors from the story only, links each to a business decision, and gives a reasoned view. Common mistake: summarising the story without analysis.
