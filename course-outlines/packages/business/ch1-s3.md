# Package: ch1-s3 — Stakeholder mapping and prioritising stakeholder interests

- **loId:** `ch1-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch1`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 1, section 1.1, learning outcome [1.1.4]; also the "Applying my learning" outcome [1.6], which asks students to analyse business news and give an informed opinion. Students learn to conduct stakeholder mapping and explain the importance of prioritising different stakeholder interests. Glossary terms: stakeholder mapping; power-interest grid (applied tool). No Higher Level only content.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The stakeholders and their needs; internal and external; interdependence. One line recall; they are the people to place on the map.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Conflict between stakeholders arises from competing interests; mapping helps predict and avoid it.

Stakeholder mapping
- [ ] M1 What stakeholder mapping is: identifies each stakeholder affected by a decision and records how much power and interest each has; can be written or visual. Quote the Appendix 2 definition from section 2.
- [ ] M2 A map records stakeholders, their interdependencies, inter-relationships, and their power and influence over the decision.
- [ ] M3 Interdependencies: reliance one stakeholder has on another (a supplier that depends on a retailer's orders).
- [ ] M4 Inter-relationships: working relationships between stakeholders, co-operative or competitive.
- [ ] M5 Power: the ability to affect a decision directly (a vote, a contract, legal authority); influence works indirectly through persuasion or public pressure. State this as the notes' distinction.

The power-interest grid (applied tool: use the Skills block type)
- [ ] G1 Purpose: shows what action each stakeholder requires. Quote the Appendix 2 definition (it maps stakeholders by their relative power and interest).
- [ ] G2 High power, high interest: manage closely, by involving them in the decision and keeping them fully informed.
- [ ] G3 High power, low interest: keep satisfied, by meeting key concerns without overloading them with detail.
- [ ] G4 Low power, high interest: keep informed, by sharing updates so they feel heard and can respond.
- [ ] G5 Low power, low interest: monitor, by checking periodically whether their position has changed.
- [ ] G6 The four action labels as keyTerms (manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor).

Method: mapping a decision step by step (numbered steps)
- [ ] P1 Step 1: state the decision clearly (for example whether a restaurant should add plant-based dishes to its menu).
- [ ] P2 Step 2: list every stakeholder affected, internal and external, including those affected only indirectly.
- [ ] P3 Step 3: assess each stakeholder's interest and power to influence, giving a reason for each judgement.
- [ ] P4 Step 4: place each on the grid and state the action for each quadrant.
- [ ] P5 Step 5: note interdependencies and likely conflicts, since they affect how the actions are carried out.

Prioritising
- [ ] Q1 Why prioritise: no business can meet all demands in full.
- [ ] Q2 Limited resources: time, money and management attention go to stakeholders with the most power and interest.
- [ ] Q3 Decision speed: a clear order lets the business decide and act without waiting for every group.
- [ ] Q4 Avoiding conflict: addressing the most powerful concerns early reduces later disputes.
- [ ] Q5 Legitimacy: showing concerns were weighed fairly makes the decision more acceptable.
- [ ] Q6 Ethics and sustainability: a community or environmental concern may need priority even where the group has little formal power, because the harm is serious.
- [ ] Q7 Prioritising is not ignoring: low-priority stakeholders are still monitored or kept informed.

Limitations
- [ ] L1 Positions change (interest can rise after a news story), so the map needs regular updating.
- [ ] L2 Subjective judgements: two people may place the same stakeholder differently.
- [ ] L3 Oversimplification: a two-dimensional grid can hide values and complexity.
- [ ] L4 Hidden stakeholders (such as future customers) may be missed.

Worked mapping
- [ ] W1 One fully worked mapping of a sample Irish business decision (in section 2), with a reason for every placement and the action for each.
- [ ] W2 The restaurant menu decision as a second, shorter sample mapping of four stakeholders (identify and prioritise four), using the exam format.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical and sustainability: the weaker voices (neighbours, the environment, future generations) must not be dropped because they hold little formal power; this ties to Q6.
- [ ] E2 Digital: online reviews and social media can raise a low-power group's influence quickly; mapping tools (spreadsheets, online whiteboards) can keep a map up to date.

Applying my learning: current Irish news (required last item of the chapter)
- [ ] N1 Map the stakeholders in the Google Grange Castle data centre decision (news example 1 in section 2): decision, four stakeholders placed on the grid with reasons.
- [ ] N2 Use the Killala decision (news example 2) to show a low-power group (an individual objector) gaining influence through the planning process and the public, linking to limitation L1.
- [ ] N3 Give an informed opinion (as the "applying my learning" outcome asks): a short evaluative sentence weighing business, community and sustainability interests, with a reason, and say what evidence would change it.

## 3. Traps

Do not use:
- Do not use: the current worked example's placement of local residents as "moderate but real power, so manage closely", because the grid has only high or low power. Place residents in one quadrant and justify it: if their planning objections could block or delay the shop, high power and high interest, so manage closely; if not, low power and high interest, so keep informed. Say which and why.
- Do not use: the supplier placement as a rule that suppliers are always low power and low interest. A sole supplier of an essential input has high power. Present the bakery placements as illustrative judgements for that sample only.
- Do not use: the bank as having "limited interest", as a fixed fact. A lender is interested in repayment, so state the placement as a judgement (interest in daily operations is low, interest in repayment is high) or place it as high power, high interest, and give the reason.
- Do not use: "power" and "influence" as the glossary's own distinction. The glossary says power and influence together; the direct-and-indirect split is the notes' working distinction and must not be quoted as the glossary.
- Do not use: any named stakeholder, firm or person not in section 2, and do not claim any judicial review outcome for the news examples.
- Do not use: the quote "premature in the absence of this detail" for the Killala decision, because it could not be verified in any source; also do not say Sally Rooney argued about fossil-fuel electricity, because her reported wording is "waste of energy". The Killala decision date is reported inconsistently (17 or 18 September 2026), so give only "September 2026".
- Do not use: terms from other tools (SWOT, STEEPLE or Porter's five forces) in this subsection; the glossary tool is the power-interest grid.

Confusable terms
- Stakeholder mapping (the general activity) vs the power-interest grid (one form of it).
- Power (ability to affect the decision) vs interest (how much the decision matters to them). High interest does not mean high power.
- Manage closely vs keep informed: the first is for high power, the second for low power.
- Prioritising (ranking by importance) vs ignoring (low priority still needs action: monitor).
- Interdependencies (reliance on each other) vs inter-relationships (the working relationship, co-operative or competitive).

Belongs to neighbouring subsections (mention only as stated)
- Who the stakeholders are, their needs and interdependence: the notes on stakeholders and interdependence. Here: recall only.
- Conflict causes and resolution methods (negotiation, mediation): the notes on stakeholder interaction and conflict. Here: only note likely conflicts at step five, with no resolution detail.
- Applying the power-interest grid to customer groups: the later marketing chapter. Here: stakeholder decisions only.
- Other applied tools (cost-benefit analysis, force-field analysis, Porter's five forces): their own later chapters.

## 4. What exams ask

(Source: SEC 2025 sample papers, Higher Level; no marks or frequencies invented.)
- Conduct stakeholder mapping (conduct = perform an activity): one sample paper asks students to map four stakeholders affected by a restaurant's menu decision, identifying and prioritising them. A full answer names four relevant stakeholders, gives each a level of power and interest with a reason, places each in a quadrant and states the action (manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor). Common mistakes: placing without a reason, naming stakeholders unaffected by the decision, mixing up the quadrant actions, giving only one or two stakeholders.
- Explain the importance of prioritising: a full answer gives reasons (explain = reasons or causes): limited resources, decision speed, avoiding conflict, legitimacy, with an effect on the business for each. Common mistake: saying "some are more important" without the reason.
- Prioritise: order the stakeholders and justify the order, including a fair treatment of weaker voices.
- Limits of the tool (evaluate or discuss = balanced judgement): positions change, subjective, simplified, hidden stakeholders. A strong answer balances limits with benefits.
- Applying learning to news: analyse a current Irish business story, identify the stakeholders and give an informed opinion (appraise, assess or evaluate judge the evidence). A full answer names the stakeholders from the stimulus only, links power and interest to the decision, and gives a reasoned view. Common mistake: summarising the story without analysis.
- Ethical, sustainability and digital themes: link the placements to environmental harm, fair treatment or online pressure.
