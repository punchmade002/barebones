# Package: ch8-s4 — The digital economy and technology-driven business models

- **loId:** `ch8-s4` · **chapterId:** `ch8`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 2, section 2.3 (Business planning), [2.3.6] and [2.3.7]. Students learn about the digital economy, including marketplace, subscription, crowdfunding and advertising-supported models; their similarities to and differences from non-digital models; and how businesses have adapted to or developed from the digital economy and other internal or external drivers. They should be able to outline how digital technology drives change in business, and identify and compare technology-driven models and their key characteristics.

This is the last subsection of the chapter, so it ends with the "Applying my learning" news examples (Section 2).

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) What a business model is and the subscription model in general (the notes on common business models). Digital subscription builds on it.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Revenue, cost structure, scalability, fixed versus extra (marginal) cost of serving one more customer.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Stakeholders, shares and interest (equity and lending are meant at a basic level; sources of finance are taught elsewhere).
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Internal and external drivers of change: internal (the business seeking lower costs); external (customers' behaviour, competitors, technology).

Digital technology as a driver of change
- [ ] D1 E-commerce: selling online to customers anywhere, reducing the need for physical premises in every location.
- [ ] D2 Platforms: connect buyers and sellers, or users and content; the owner earns income from the connections.
- [ ] D3 Data: collected from customers allows targeting of products and understanding of buying patterns previously hidden.
- [ ] D4 AI and automation: cut labour costs and speed up customer service and stock control, but can displace some jobs.
- [ ] D5 Internal drivers (such as lower costs) and external drivers (customers who now shop online).
- [ ] D6 Digital change creates both threats and opportunities.

Marketplace model
- [ ] M1 Brings buyers and sellers together on a digital platform and earns a fee on transactions.
- [ ] M2 Income: commission or listing fee on each sale or booking completed on the platform.
- [ ] M3 Characteristics: owns little or no stock; grows as more buyers and sellers join; benefits from network effects.
- [ ] M4 Advantages: low inventory risk; rapid scalability; wide reach for small sellers.
- [ ] M5 Disadvantages: must attract enough users on both sides; disputes over quality, delivery and fees.
- [ ] M6 Example: Etsy (Section 2).

Subscription model in the digital economy
- [ ] S1 Recurring fee for continuous access to online content, software or services.
- [ ] S2 Revenue from monthly or annual payments, often with a free or cheaper tier that brings users in before they pay.
- [ ] S3 Characteristics: predictable revenue; the service is updated continuously; keeping customers is central.
- [ ] S4 Advantages: predictable income; close link to customers through usage data; lower marketing cost for existing users.
- [ ] S5 Disadvantages: subscribers can cancel easily so the business must keep adding value; cancellations (churn) are costly.
- [ ] S6 Example: Netflix (Section 2).

Crowdfunding (a model and a source of finance)
- [ ] C1 Raises money from many people, usually through an online platform; both a business model and a way of obtaining finance.
- [ ] C2 The platform takes a fee on the funds raised; the business gains finance from many backers at once.
- [ ] C3 Reward crowdfunding: backers receive a product or perk; suits consumer products.
- [ ] C4 Equity crowdfunding: backers buy shares, so share in success and risk.
- [ ] C5 Lending crowdfunding: backers lend money, repaid with interest over an agreed period.
- [ ] C6 Advantages: finance without a bank loan; a market test before launch; a ready group of supporters.
- [ ] C7 Disadvantages: campaigns can miss targets; platform fees; reward backers may wait a long time.
- [ ] C8 Example: an unnamed small food or design business pre-selling a first product run to backers (sample example).
- [ ] C9 Regulation (ethical and digital angle): crowdfunding platforms in Ireland are regulated under an EU regulation and the Central Bank of Ireland is the authority (Section 2); this protects backers from loss and misleading claims.

Advertising-supported model
- [ ] V1 Users get the service free; income comes from selling access to their attention to advertisers.
- [ ] V2 Advertisers pay for placements, often by views, clicks or targeted audience reached.
- [ ] V3 Characteristics: a large user base is essential; data is used to target adverts.
- [ ] V4 Advantages: users pay nothing, widening reach; income grows with audience.
- [ ] V5 Disadvantages: revenue depends on the advertising market; some users dislike adverts; privacy concerns can bring tighter regulation.
- [ ] V6 Example: an unnamed social media platform or free search engine (sample example).
- [ ] V7 Ethical and digital angle: using personal data to target adverts raises privacy and consent questions; honesty in adverts.

Digital models compared with non-digital models
- [ ] N1 Similarity: both must create value for customers and capture enough income to cover costs.
- [ ] N2 Cost structure: digital models often have high fixed costs (software, data) but a low extra cost per additional user.
- [ ] N3 Reach: a digital business can serve customers abroad with little extra physical presence.
- [ ] N4 Growth: network and scale effects let digital models grow faster than models needing premises or staff for every new customer.
- [ ] N5 Data: digital models collect customer behaviour continuously, which non-digital models rarely do at the same scale.
- [ ] N6 Comparison headings for a "compare" answer: revenue generation, access and cost, scalability.

How Irish businesses have adapted
- [ ] I1 A traditional retailer sells online and offers click-and-collect.
- [ ] I2 A food producer launches a subscription box, creating recurring income.
- [ ] I3 Tesco Ireland: online grocery ordering with home delivery and Click+Collect, extending a store-based business into digital channels (Section 2).

Sustainability and ethics angle
- [ ] E1 Digital models can cut travel and premises (online selling, subscriptions replacing ownership) but data centres and deliveries use energy; a sustainable model weighs both.
- [ ] E2 Ethical issues: fair treatment of gig and platform workers and sellers, clear cancellation and pricing for subscribers, honest data use. State as general principles.

- [ ] N7 One or two current Irish news examples linked to digital business models, each with the month, year and source, and the model it shows: Netflix's ad tier announcement for Ireland (September 2026) and Vodafone Business's smartphone subscription launch (October 2026).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Etsy earns a commission", as a bare statement, because Etsy charges several kinds of seller fees; say "fees, such as a charge on each sale".
- Do not use: "the platform owns no stock" as an absolute, because the marketplace model owns little or no stock; keep the qualifier "little or no".
- Do not use: Netflix as a purely subscription service for Irish viewers after March 2027, because Netflix confirmed an advertising tier for Ireland from 1 March 2027. Describe it as a subscription model that is adding an advertising-supported tier.
- Do not use: any Irish crowdfunding platform name or campaign, because none is checked.
- Confusable: marketplace (earns a fee on transactions between others) versus e-commerce retailer (sells its own stock); a marketplace owns little or no stock.
- Confusable: subscription (users pay recurring fees) versus advertising-supported (users pay nothing; advertisers pay). A "freemium" tier is a free or cheaper tier that brings users in before they pay.
- Confusable: crowdfunding types (reward: product or perk; equity: shares; lending: repaid with interest) and crowdfunding versus a bank loan or venture capital (sources of finance belong to the notes on finance).
- Confusable: churn (subscribers cancelling) versus network effects (value rising as more users join).
- Boundary: the five general models (retail, manufacturing, subscription in general, franchise, affiliate) belong to the notes on common business models; the canvas and the plan belong to the notes on business models and the business plan. Sources of finance and growth strategies belong to later notes on finance and growth; use crowdfunding here only as a model.
- Boundary: digital marketing (social media, search advertising techniques) belongs to the notes on promotion; adapting the marketing mix and business model to external drivers belongs to the notes on responding to the changing environment. GDPR and data protection law belong to the notes on consumer protection; mention privacy here only as a general concern.
- Boundary: the Business Alive investigative study on digital work practices belongs to the unifying strand notes.

## 4. What exams ask

From the outline's Exam lines for the 2025 sample papers (Higher Level Sample 1 and Sample 2): one asks students to explain crowdfunding and to explain two characteristics of the subscription and advertising models; the other asks them to compare a subscription service with another technology-driven model under headings such as revenue generation, access and cost, and scalability. No marks or frequencies are recorded here.
- Explain crowdfunding ("a detailed account including reasons or causes"). A full answer says money is raised from many people through an online platform, names the types (reward, equity, lending) with what backers get, and gives a reason or effect (finance without a bank loan, a market test). Common mistake: only saying "people give money" with no types.
- Explain characteristics of the subscription and advertising-supported models. A full answer gives each characteristic and a reason: subscription (predictable revenue so easier forecasting; keeping customers central because they can cancel); advertising (large user base needed because income grows with audience; data used to target adverts). Common mistake: listing advantages when characteristics were asked.
- Compare a subscription service with another technology-driven model, "referring to both throughout". Use the headings the question gives, typically revenue generation (recurring fee versus commission, advertising or crowd-raised funds), access and cost (paid access versus free to users or a fee on transactions), and scalability (low extra cost per user; network effects). Common mistake: describing each model in turn.
- Outline how digital technology drives change (e-commerce, platforms, data, AI and automation), with an effect for each. Common mistake: naming technologies without an effect on the business.
- Identify a model from a stimulus: name it and give the feature that shows it (a commission on bookings means marketplace; free to users, paid by advertisers means advertising-supported).
- Applying news: link a current Irish story (for example a subscription firm adding an advertising tier) to a model and its characteristics, then give a reasoned opinion. A full answer states the model, its effect on revenue, customers and costs, and a justified viewpoint. Common mistake: retelling the story.
- Ethical, sustainability and digital angle: data and privacy in advertising-supported models, clear cancellation in subscriptions, fair treatment of platform sellers, and the circular economy in device subscriptions.
- Tips to build: for any model, state how it earns money, then a characteristic, then an effect; for a comparison, use the headings and name both models in every point.
