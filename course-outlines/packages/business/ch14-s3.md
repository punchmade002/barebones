# Package: ch14-s3 — Human capital management, induction, training and development

- **loId:** `ch14-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch14`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 3, section 3.2, outcomes [3.2.7, 3.2.8]: the significance of human capital management, induction, training and professional development for organisations and employees, and how employees learn from experience; explain HCM and how the organisational environment affects it; investigate the types of training and development and why ongoing development matters. Outline scope: human capital (glossary) and HCM; organisational environment (culture, leadership, resources, learning climate); induction ("onboarding") and its purpose; on-the-job vs off-the-job training, mentoring, coaching, job rotation, e-learning, further education, CPD; benefits for employer and employee; learning from experience (reflection, feedback). Glossary term: human capital. Not Higher Level only.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The recruitment process ends with an offer and a contract; induction follows (see the subsection on recruitment).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Skills gap: the difference between the skills a business needs and the skills its staff have.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Employee turnover and retention (staff leaving or staying) and their cost.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Organisational culture: shared values and ways of behaving in a business.

Human capital and HCM [3.2.7]
- [ ] H1 Glossary: human capital, defined with the Appendix 2 wording (section 2). Link it to people as an asset that can grow.
- [ ] H2 Human capital is hard for competitors to copy, because a team's know-how and working relationships take years to build.
- [ ] H3 HCM: the strategic approach to attracting, developing, retaining and deploying people so their value to the business grows.
- [ ] H4 HCM links people decisions to business goals (recruitment, training, reward support strategy, not handled in isolation).
- [ ] H5 HCM treats development as an investment; returns appear in productivity, quality, retention and innovation.
- [ ] H6 Learning from experience adds to human capital: a skill stays with the person even if their role changes.

The organisational environment
- [ ] O1 Culture decides whether people share ideas, admit mistakes and ask for help.
- [ ] O2 Leadership sets priorities for development and models learning.
- [ ] O3 Resources (budget, time, tools) limit how much training is possible.
- [ ] O4 Learning climate: curiosity and feedback; learning seen as part of the job.
- [ ] O5 No development opportunities means skilled staff may leave for competitors.

Induction (onboarding) [3.2.7]
- [ ] I1 Definition: introducing a new employee to the organisation, its policies and their role; also called onboarding.
- [ ] I2 Content: purpose, structure, policies, culture; the role, duties, performance expectations, who to ask for help; health and safety, equipment, systems, practical first-week details.
- [ ] I3 Benefits for the employee: less anxiety, faster understanding of the role, quicker working relationships.
- [ ] I4 Benefits for the employer: faster productivity, lower early resignation, protects the recruitment investment.

Types of training and development [3.2.8]
- [ ] T1 On-the-job: in the normal work setting; skills used immediately; low cost of time away.
- [ ] T2 Coaching: one-to-one guidance from a more experienced person on a specific skill or performance.
- [ ] T3 Mentoring: longer-term relationship, advice and support on career and development.
- [ ] T4 Job rotation: moving between roles builds breadth and understanding.
- [ ] T5 Off-the-job: external courses, workshops, seminars; brings in outside knowledge.
- [ ] T6 E-learning: online, own pace, flexible, scalable across locations.
- [ ] T7 Further education (part-time degrees, diplomas): formal qualifications benefiting both sides.
- [ ] T8 Continuing professional development (CPD): ongoing learning to keep professionals up to date; many professional bodies require it.
- [ ] T9 Compare on-the-job vs off-the-job (cost, relevance, disruption, outside knowledge).

Benefits and why ongoing development matters
- [ ] B1 Employer: productivity and quality up, errors down.
- [ ] B2 Employer: closes skills gaps, less external recruitment for specialist roles.
- [ ] B3 Employer: staff who see a future stay, lowering turnover costs.
- [ ] B4 Employer: better placed to adopt new technology and innovate.
- [ ] B5 Employee: skills and qualifications raise employability and career options.
- [ ] B6 Employee: confidence, job satisfaction, sense of progress.
- [ ] B7 Organisations adapt to change (technology and markets shift), so skills need updating.

Learning from experience
- [ ] L1 Kolb's experiential learning cycle: concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation, active experimentation.
- [ ] L2 Reflection turns experience into lessons.
- [ ] L3 Feedback from colleagues, managers or customers.
- [ ] L4 Supportive culture treats mistakes as learning.
- [ ] L5 Informal learning (watching a colleague handle a difficult customer) adds to formal training.

Evaluating training
- [ ] G1 Check the learner gained knowledge or skill (test, demonstration).
- [ ] G2 Check it is applied at work (behaviour and output).
- [ ] G3 Learner feedback on relevance and delivery.
- [ ] G4 Compare cost with benefits (fewer errors, lower turnover); some benefits hard to measure.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] X1 Ethics: equal access to training for all staff (part-time, remote, older workers); fair selection for development; honest use of training as investment, not only compliance.
- [ ] X2 Sustainability: e-learning reduces travel; training in sustainable practice builds green skills; human capital as a long-term sustainable asset.
- [ ] X3 Digital: e-learning platforms, online induction, digital skills training for technology change (automation, AI); learner data protected under GDPR.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the current key term "Human capital: The knowledge, skills, experience and abilities that employees bring to a business, which create value for it", because it does not match the Appendix 2 definition; use the quoted Appendix 2 wording.
- Do not use: learning-style labels (visual, auditory, kinaesthetic) or Honey and Mumford, because learning-styles theory is doubtful and is not in the specification or the current notes. Kolb's cycle itself is fine.
- Do not use: training cost, grant or tax-relief figures, because they change and are unchecked.
- Do not use: "human capital is on the balance sheet" or any accounting treatment, because employees are not recorded as assets and the specification does not require it.
- Confusable: coaching (short-term, a specific skill) vs mentoring (long-term, career-wide); induction (new starters) vs training (any staff) vs development (long-term growth).
- Confusable: on-the-job vs off-the-job; further education (formal qualifications) vs CPD (keeping professionals current); human capital (the asset) vs human capital management (the strategy).
- Confusable: job rotation here is a training method; in the motivation notes it is a motivator.
- Boundary: motivation, rewards and appraisal belong to the subsection on motivation and appraisal; recruitment stages and interviews to the subsection on recruitment; teams and group development to the teamwork subsection; employee wellbeing to the corporate wellness subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Sample paper 1, Question 4(a)(ii) (2025) is a true/false item: induction training is also known as onboarding (the answer is true). A full answer is one word, so know the equivalence. Common mistake: confusing induction with training in general.
- Explain/describe items (Appendix 1: "explain" gives reasons or causes) ask for the significance of HCM, induction or training. A full answer states what it is, gives benefits for both the employer (productivity, retention, adaptation to change) and the employee (employability, confidence), and links them to the firm in the case. Common mistakes: giving employer benefits only; confusing HCM with HR administration.
- "Investigate" or "identify the types of training" items want named types with a one-line distinction (on-the-job vs off-the-job, coaching vs mentoring). Common mistake: listing types without saying when each suits.
- "Explain how the organisational environment affects HCM" wants culture, leadership, resources and learning climate with an effect each, not just the list.
- Use the case's context (sector, size, technology change) when asked to discuss why ongoing development matters; a strong answer ties it to technology and market change.
- No question wording is copied here.
