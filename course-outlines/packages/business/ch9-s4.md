# Package: ch9-s4 — Digital technology, ethics and sustainability in marketing

- **loId:** `ch9-s4` · **chapterId:** `ch9`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 2, section 2.4, learning outcomes [2.4.5] and [2.4.6]. Students learn how technology changes market research and marketing, including the disruptive impact of digital technology on both; and the importance of ethics and sustainability in marketing, including its influence on marketing companies and strategies; they evaluate that influence. Outline scope: the glossary's "disruptive impact" (positive and negative); digital research (online surveys, web analytics, social listening, big data, AI) and digital marketing (social media, influencers, search and targeted ads, e-commerce, personalisation), with benefits and drawbacks for firms and consumers; ethical marketing (honest claims, no exploitation of children or vulnerable groups, data privacy, influencer disclosure); sustainability in marketing (green products and packaging, greenwashing and its risks, EU rules on green claims); how ethics and sustainability shape strategy and reputation. No Higher Level only content.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Market research methods (surveys, observation, analytics) and primary and secondary data, from the notes on market research.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) The seven Ps and the promotion methods, including social media and influencer marketing, from the notes on the marketing mix.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Consumers have rights; businesses have ethical duties to customers, society and the environment (one-line recall).

Disruptive impact
- [ ] D1 Quote the Appendix 2 definition of disruptive impact, then apply it to research and marketing.
- [ ] D2 Positive: lower cost of reaching customers, faster feedback, new markets opened.
- [ ] D3 Negative for firms: established businesses lose share to digital entrants offering lower prices or greater convenience.
- [ ] D4 Negative for consumers: information overload, privacy risks, manipulative targeting.
- [ ] D5 New skills needed (data analysis, content creation).

Digital market research
- [ ] R1 Online surveys: large samples, quick and cheap, may under-represent people who do not use the internet.
- [ ] R2 Web analytics: visits, clicks, time on page; how visitors behave.
- [ ] R3 Social listening: monitoring public posts and comments about a brand or market.
- [ ] R4 Big data: very large volumes of transaction and behaviour records reveal patterns a single survey would miss.
- [ ] R5 Artificial intelligence: predicts trends and groups customers; results only as good as the data.
- [ ] R6 Benefits and drawbacks of digital research (speed, cost, reach against bias, privacy, data quality).

Digital marketing methods
- [ ] M1 Social media marketing: community and engagement; loss of control over negative conversations.
- [ ] M2 Influencer marketing: reaches niche audiences through trusted voices; audiences may distrust paid endorsements.
- [ ] M3 Search and targeted advertising: reaches people already looking; costs rise where many firms bid for the same terms.
- [ ] M4 E-commerce and personalisation: easier buying and relevant recommendations; can encourage overspending and raise privacy concerns.
- [ ] M5 For firms: lower cost per contact, measurable results, quick testing; dependence on platforms that can change rules or fees.
- [ ] M6 For consumers: easier price comparison and wider choice; tracking, targeted pressure, content hard to identify as advertising.

Ethical marketing
- [ ] ET1 Honest claims: accurate, supported by evidence, not exaggerated or misleading.
- [ ] ET2 No exploitation of children or vulnerable groups (including people in financial or emotional difficulty).
- [ ] ET3 Data privacy: personal data collected lawfully and used only for stated purposes (see section 2).
- [ ] ET4 Influencer disclosure: paid or gifted endorsements clearly labelled (see section 2).
- [ ] ET5 Consent for electronic direct marketing: generally opt-in (see section 2).
- [ ] ET6 Why ethics matters (an outline of three ways): protects customers, builds trust and reputation, avoids legal penalties and boycotts, protects staff and brand.

Sustainability in marketing
- [ ] S1 Green products: designed to use less energy, last longer or contain recycled materials.
- [ ] S2 Sustainable packaging: less packaging, recyclable materials, clear disposal guidance.
- [ ] S3 Greenwashing: presenting a product or business as more environmentally friendly than it is.
- [ ] S4 Risks of greenwashing: legal penalties, reputational damage, lost customer trust.
- [ ] S5 Regulation: EU and Irish rules on environmental claims (see section 2).
- [ ] S6 Substantiated claims: specific, evidenced, with the evidence available.

Influence on strategy and reputation
- [ ] I1 Strategy: choose a target market that values sustainability and build the mix around it.
- [ ] I2 Reputation: consistent ethical conduct builds trust and loyalty.
- [ ] I3 Influence on marketing companies and strategies: agencies and brands may refuse work or change a campaign to avoid unethical or misleading practices.
- [ ] I4 Evaluation (Appendix 1 "Evaluate"): ethical marketing can cost more in the short term but lowers the risk of regulatory action and boycotts; a justified overall judgement.

Cross-cutting angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical, sustainability and digital: this whole subsection is the chapter's ethical and digital angle; do not add a separate add-on block.

## 3. Traps

Do not use:
- Do not use: "the EU Green Claims Directive" as a law in force or as the regulation students should cite, because it was a Commission proposal that was halted in June 2025 and its status is disputed; the checked rules are Directive (EU) 2024/825 and the Irish regulations of 2026.
- Do not use: "the EU has rules on environmental claims that require them to be specific and substantiated" without naming the checked instrument, because that is the vague form in the existing notes; replace it with the checked facts in section 2.
- Do not use: the date S.I. No. 124 of 2026 was "made", because sources differ (27 March 2026 is the transposition deadline; the Iris Oifigiúil notice is 3 April 2026); give the commencement date only.
- Do not use: any fine amount (for example a percentage of turnover) for greenwashing or influencer breaches, because no figure was verified from a primary source.
- Do not use: the claim that the CCPC is the only enforcer or that influencers are always prosecuted. Say the guidance says non-compliance may lead to enforcement action.
- Do not use: "social media posts are always unregulated advertising"; paid and gifted content is advertising and must be labelled.
- Do not use: any named platform, tool or AI product, because none is checked.
- Do not use: "AI is always accurate" or "big data proves causes"; say results depend on the data and show patterns, not reasons.

Confusable terms
- Disruptive impact (Appendix 2: how digital technology changes how businesses operate and consumers interact; positive and negative) vs disruption by a single new firm.
- Social listening (monitoring what is said online) vs web analytics (what visitors do on a site) vs online survey (asking questions).
- Ethical marketing (honest, fair, respectful) vs sustainable marketing (environment and society) vs greenwashing (a false or exaggerated sustainability claim).
- Influencer marketing (the method) vs influencer disclosure (the ethical duty to label it).
- Consent for electronic marketing (opt-in) vs general data protection (lawful basis, purpose limits).

## 4. What exams ask

(Source: SEC 2025 Higher Level Sample Paper 1, question 5(b) asks students to outline three ways ethics matters when designing a marketing strategy; the 2025 sample AAC brief was on ethics in marketing for a service business; Appendix 1 verbs. No marks or frequencies are invented.)
- Outline three ways ethics matters in a marketing strategy (outline = main points only): a full answer gives three distinct points (honest claims, protecting vulnerable groups, data privacy, trust and reputation, avoiding legal penalties), each with its effect on the business. Common mistake: three versions of the same point, or no link to a strategy.
- Explain the disruptive impact of digital technology (explain = reasons or causes): a full answer gives a positive and a negative effect, for firms and for consumers, using the Appendix 2 meaning. Common mistake: only positives.
- Discuss or evaluate the influence of ethics and sustainability on a marketing company or strategy (balanced review with evidence, then a judgement): a full answer gives benefits (reputation, loyalty, lower regulatory risk) and costs (higher short-term cost, risk of exposure if claims are weak) and reaches a justified conclusion. Common mistake: a list of rules with no judgement.
- Identify or describe digital research and marketing methods: name a method and one benefit and one drawback.
- Greenwashing: define it, give an example, explain two risks. Common mistake: confusing it with genuine sustainability.
- AAC link: the investigative study applies these ideas to a service business, so notes should support giving evidence, analysis and a conclusion on ethics in marketing; do not describe the AAC report format here.
