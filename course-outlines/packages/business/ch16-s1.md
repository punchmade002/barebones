# Package: ch16-s1 — Change, resistance to change and fostering innovation

- **loId:** `ch16-s1` · **chapterId:** `ch16`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 3, section 3.4 (The rationale for planning), learning outcomes [3.4.1] to [3.4.4]. Students learn about the internal and external changes that impact on an organisation, how resistance to change can arise, and how to foster innovation and approach resistance. They should be able to outline the internal and external changes organisations meet over their lifetime; analyse the reasons for resistance to change; identify the practices that promote innovation and entrepreneurial and intrapreneurial thinking; and outline approaches to overcoming resistance. No Higher Level only content. Glossary term for the chapter: force-field analysis (used in the next subsection, not here).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) An organisation (a business, public body or not-for-profit) has an internal environment it controls (leadership, staff, structure, technology, products) and an external environment it cannot control. Recall in a line or two.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) STEEPLE analysis, from the earlier notes on analytical tools: social, technological, economic, environmental, political, legal and ethical factors; it assesses external factors only.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Innovation, entrepreneur and intrapreneur, from the earlier notes on enterprise and innovation. Recall the difference in one or two lines, then apply it to change.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Employees are stakeholders in the organisation, and managers lead and manage them (the notes on leadership and management).

Internal and external change [3.4.1]
- [ ] C1 Organisations face change throughout their lives; it comes from inside the business or from its environment.
- [ ] C2 Internal changes: new leadership, restructuring, new technology, growth, new products. Give the effect of each (for example a new chief executive changes strategy, culture and priorities across departments; restructuring changes reporting lines, roles and who decides).
- [ ] C3 External changes: the STEEPLE factors give a checklist; economy, competition, regulation, technology, climate, pandemics.
- [ ] C4 Distinguish internal from external change: the business controls or starts the first and can only respond to the second. Give one example of each for a small business.
- [ ] C5 Ethical, sustainability and digital angle: climate change and environmental regulation as external pressures to change; ethical expectations of customers and staff; digital technology (for example a move to cloud systems or automation) as a source of both internal and external change, and its disruptive impact.

Why people resist change [3.4.2]
- [ ] R1 Resistance is a natural human response; managers need to understand its causes before addressing it. Define resistance to change (opposition, open or hidden, to a planned change).
- [ ] R2 Fear of job loss (reduced headcount, changed roles, cut hours).
- [ ] R3 Loss of status or control (influence, expertise, authority over own work).
- [ ] R4 Uncertainty about what the change means for them, even where the change is beneficial.
- [ ] R5 Habit: attachment to familiar routines, reluctance to learn new methods or systems.
- [ ] R6 Lack of trust in management or lack of information about why the change is happening.
- [ ] R7 Extra workload during the transition (for example learning new software while keeping up with existing tasks).
- [ ] R8 Each reason explained with its cause and its effect, so the notes support "analyse" depth (four developed reasons).
- [ ] R9 Why unmanaged resistance matters: slows implementation, lowers productivity or quality, staff leave (recruitment and training costs), a change can fail completely and waste money and time; fair, open handling respects employees and sustains trust for future change.

Fostering innovation and entrepreneurial thinking [3.4.3]
- [ ] I1 Innovation thrives where staff are encouraged to generate ideas and failure is treated as learning. Define innovation (see Checked facts).
- [ ] I2 Empowerment.
- [ ] I3 Idea schemes (formal route for suggestions, assessed and rewarded).
- [ ] I4 Time for projects during working hours.
- [ ] I5 Rewarding risk-taking.
- [ ] I6 Cross-functional teams.
- [ ] I7 Tolerance of failure.
- [ ] I8 Entrepreneurial thinking: spotting an opportunity and accepting the financial risk of starting or growing a business to exploit it.
- [ ] I9 Intrapreneurial thinking: the same approach by an employee inside an existing organisation (for example developing a new product line for the employer) without carrying the personal financial risk of founding a business.
- [ ] I10 Why it matters: established firms adapt faster than competitors that rely only on existing routines.
- [ ] I11 Business and financial literacy: an idea needs costing and a sound financial case before it is pursued.
- [ ] I12 Digital angle: digital tools (collaboration platforms, online suggestion systems, data to test ideas) can support idea generation; sustainability angle: innovation that cuts waste or energy use. Ethics: risk-taking must stay within legal and ethical limits.

Overcoming resistance [3.4.4]
- [ ] O1 Match the approach to the cause of the resistance.
- [ ] O2 Communication (reasons, what it involves, how each group is affected; reduces uncertainty).
- [ ] O3 Consultation and participation (involvement in planning builds ownership).
- [ ] O4 Training (skills; addresses fear of failing or being left behind).
- [ ] O5 Support (coaching or extra help in the transition; eases workload).
- [ ] O6 Negotiation and incentives (a bonus or an agreed job-security guarantee).
- [ ] O7 Leading by example (managers visibly committed).
- [ ] O8 Phased change: introduce in stages; advantages (time to adapt, problems corrected early) and disadvantages (takes longer, prolongs uncertainty, gives opposition time to build up between stages).
- [ ] O9 Link each reason in R2 to R7 to at least one matching approach, so the notes can answer "outline two ways to overcome resistance" and "suggest how management could respond".

Key terms (each defined as used; also in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Internal change; external change; resistance to change; innovation; intrapreneurship; empowerment; phased change; cross-functional team.
- [ ] K2 Entrepreneur compared with intrapreneur: an intrapreneur develops new ideas inside an existing firm without founding it; an entrepreneur starts or runs a new venture and bears its financial risk.

## 3. Traps

- Do not add: the claim that all resistance is irrational or purely negative. Resistance can flag real problems with a plan, so managers should listen to the reasons.
- Do not add: a named Irish firm as an example of resistance, intrapreneurship or a change programme, or any statistic on change failure or employee attitudes. None is checked; use "a sample business".
- Do not add: Kotter's eight steps, Lewin's unfreeze-change-refreeze or ADKAR. The specification names none of them for this section.
- Confusable: internal vs external change. A new competitor, a new law and a pandemic are external; a new manager, a restructuring and a new product launch are internal (a firm chooses them). New technology can be either: internal when the firm chooses to install it, external when it arrives in the market and forces the firm to respond. Say which it is.
- Confusable: entrepreneur vs intrapreneur. Both spot opportunities; only the entrepreneur founds the venture and bears the personal financial risk.
- Confusable: reasons for resistance vs ways to overcome it. Each reason is a cause (fear of job loss), each way is an action by management (negotiation, job guarantee). Do not list an action under a reason.
- Confusable: innovation (new ideas applied successfully) vs invention (a new idea). An idea with no application is not innovation.
- Boundary: the notes on enterprise and innovation own the meaning of enterprise, the competencies of entrepreneurs and the sources of business ideas. Here only apply innovation and intrapreneurship to how an organisation copes with change; do not rewrite those definitions at length.
- Boundary: the notes on strategic planning and force-field analysis own driving and restraining forces, scoring, the planning cycle and planning for change. Here the drivers of change are only named (internal and external change); do not teach the scoring tool.
- Boundary: the notes on contingency planning and crisis management own sudden crises and plans for them. A pandemic or cyber-attack here is only an external change; do not teach crisis stages.
- Boundary: the notes on leadership styles and motivation own the theory of leading people. Here only "leading by example" as one approach.

## 4. What exams ask

- **Analyse reasons for resistance (2025 sample paper, Higher Level Sample 1, Question 5(d): analyse four reasons for resistance to change).** Appendix 1: "analyse" means "study or examine something in detail, break down in order to bring out the essential elements or structure; identify parts and relationships, and to interpret information to reach conclusions". A full answer names four distinct reasons (for example fear of job loss, loss of status or control, uncertainty, extra workload) and for each gives the cause and its effect on the change or the business, tied to the stimulus. Common mistakes: naming reasons without explaining them; giving ways to overcome them instead of reasons; two reasons that are really the same (uncertainty and lack of information); ignoring the context given.
- **Outline ways to overcome resistance (Sample 2, Question 3(b): outline two ways, stimulus a return-to-office mandate).** Appendix 1: "outline" means "give the main points; restrict to essential points of information". A full answer names two approaches (for example communication, consultation and participation, phased change) and states briefly how each reduces the resistance in the stimulus. Common mistakes: naming only one; stating an approach with no link to the stimulus; writing a long discussion where a short point per approach is needed.
- **Likely applications (the same pattern as the two items above, no marks implied):** identify or distinguish internal and external changes with an example from the stimulus; explain how a business can foster innovation (three or four practices, each with its effect); explain the difference between an entrepreneur and an intrapreneur. Command verbs from Appendix 1: identify (state briefly), explain ("give a detailed account including reasons or causes"), distinguish ("make the differences between two or more concepts or items clear").
- **Cross-cutting angle:** answers that mention the digital, ethical or sustainability side of change (a technology change, fair treatment of staff, climate regulation) show the applied understanding the written exam rewards. Keep it relevant to the stimulus.
- **Tip sources for the writer:** four developed reasons for resistance; match each way to overcome to a named cause; always link back to the stimulus business; the phased-change advantages and disadvantages; the entrepreneur/intrapreneur distinction.
