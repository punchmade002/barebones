# Package: ch11-s4 — Adapting to external drivers through the marketing mix and business model

- **loId:** `ch11-s4` · **chapterId:** `ch11`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 2, section 6 (Growth, development and expansion), outcome [2.6.6]; also section 8 (Applying my learning), outcome [2.8.1]. Students learn about "how a business may have to adapt to external drivers (including customer demographics, competition, and economic factors) to enhance financial sustainability, and how the business can address issues through considering their product/service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process, and place and/or business model." They should be able to outline strategies that a business may employ to adapt based on their marketing mix and/or business model. [2.8.1]: analyse information from the news and media about enterprise and innovation and provide an informed opinion.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`. Specification first examined June 2027. Nothing here is Higher Level only.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The 7 Ps: product/service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place (the specification's list; never "physical evidence").
- [ ] A2 (assumed) A business model outlines how a company will operate, create, deliver and capture value (definition in section 2).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Margin, revenue and costs; a minimum wage is the lowest legal hourly pay.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Customer demographics: age, household type, income, location.

External drivers and financial sustainability
- [ ] D1 External drivers: changes outside the business that force it to adapt; STEEPLE is a checklist (social, technological, economic, environmental, political, legal, ethical).
- [ ] D2 Customer demographics (ageing population, rise in single-person households) change what and how customers buy.
- [ ] D3 Competition (new entrants, substitutes) changes the prices and features a business must match.
- [ ] D4 Economic factors (inflation, interest rates, consumer incomes) change costs and spending power.
- [ ] D5 Technological, legal and environmental drivers (new digital channels, a rise in the minimum wage, stricter packaging rules) change costs and expectations.
- [ ] D6 Financial sustainability as the aim: a business that adapts early keeps revenue and cost structure under control; one that waits may be forced into deep cuts.

Adapting each P
- [ ] P1 Product/service: change the range to meet demand (plant-based dishes); adjust for a customer group (smaller portions for older customers); drop low-selling or high-cost lines to protect margin.
- [ ] P2 Pricing: pass on cost rises only if customers still see good value; value packs and bundles when budgets tighten; loyalty discounts or subscriptions give regular income for commitment.
- [ ] P3 Promotion: move spend from print to digital (social media, email); reach older customers by local newspapers or community noticeboards; loyalty schemes and personal offers when a competitor opens nearby.
- [ ] P4 People: retrain or hire for new skills (online order handling); flexible rosters or part-time contracts to manage wage costs, with the morale risk.
- [ ] P5 Process: self-ordering screens or app ordering cut staff per sale; tighter stock control cuts waste.
- [ ] P6 Packaging: eco-friendly materials for sustainability goals; smaller or resealable packs for single-person households.
- [ ] P7 Place: online ordering, delivery or click-and-collect; opening where population is growing rather than waiting for the local market to shrink.

Adapting the business model
- [ ] B1 New revenue streams (catering, merchandise, meal kits).
- [ ] B2 New channels (marketplace listing, delivery partnership).
- [ ] B3 Subscription model: turns one-off sales into regular payments, easier to forecast.
- [ ] B4 Outsourcing non-core activities (payroll, delivery, cleaning): turns some fixed costs into variable payments and frees managers' time.

Worked examples
- [ ] W1 Restaurant facing a minimum-wage rise: direct effect higher labour cost squeezing margin; modest price rise with a value lunch option kept; tablet ordering at tables; shorter menu on shared ingredients; weekly meal subscription for local office workers; evaluation: changes take time and may not fully offset the cost, so track margins after each.
- [ ] W2 Café in a town with an ageing population: product (smaller portions, softer foods, quieter seating); place (step-free access, accessible toilets); promotion (flyers in community centres, local radio); business model (morning coffee club with a fixed monthly fee).

Launching a new product (challenges and responses)
- [ ] N1 Low awareness: launch promotion, free samples.
- [ ] N2 High development and launch costs: phased launch in one region before wider roll-out.
- [ ] N3 Cannibalisation (the new product takes sales from an existing line): position at a different customer group or price point.
- [ ] N4 Supply problems: agree supplier capacity with key partners before launch.

Cross-cutting themes
- [ ] X1 Ethical angle: passing on costs fairly to customers; the effect of rosters and contract changes on staff wellbeing; accessibility for older and disabled customers; honest sustainability claims (avoid overstating "eco" packaging).
- [ ] X2 Sustainability angle: packaging, waste reduction and local sourcing are both adaptation and cost saving; financial sustainability and environmental sustainability can pull the same way or conflict.
- [ ] X3 Digital angle: app ordering, online channels, data on customer habits, marketplaces and digital promotion.

Applying my learning [2.8.1] (the closing item)
- [ ] J1 Include one or two current Irish news examples on growth and expansion (section 2), each with month, year and source, and show how to read a news story for what it says about growth strategy, competition, costs and stakeholders; invite the student to form an informed opinion (give a view, with a reason, and a weighing of benefits against risks).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any amount for a future minimum wage (such as a 2027 figure or a national living wage date), because a Low Pay Commission recommendation is not law until legislated.
- Do not use: "physical evidence" as a P, because the specification's 7 Ps are product/service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place.
- Do not use: market data on ageing population, restaurant margins or wage shares, because none is checked.
- Do not use: the CCPC merger threshold amounts in the news items, because they changed twice in 2026.
- Do not use: the exact month the Uniphar/TouchStore deal completed, because CCPC pages give December 2025 and January 2026; also the exact notification day (26 vs 27 April 2026), keep to month level.
- Do not present the restaurant and café examples as real businesses; they are sample examples.
- Confusable: marketing mix (the 7 Ps) versus business model (how the firm earns and delivers value); STEEPLE (finding drivers) versus Porter's five forces (analysing competition); cannibalisation versus competition from rivals.
- Confusable: financial sustainability (covering costs over time) is not environmental sustainability, though they can overlap.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: competitive advantage and the five forces; the expansion strategies (organic and inorganic, technology); and the cost-benefit method. Mention them only to link, not to teach again. The product life cycle and full marketing-mix teaching belong to the marketing chapters; here only the adaptations.
- Subscription and outsourcing are business-model changes, not extra Ps.

## 4. What exams ask

- Challenges of introducing new products and how to address them (2025 Higher Level Sample 2, Question 1(b)(ii)): a full answer names a challenge (awareness, costs, cannibalisation, supply), pairs each with a response, and ties it to the stimulus firm. Common mistake: listing challenges without how to address them.
- Impact of a minimum-wage increase on a restaurant (2025 Higher Level Sample 2, Question 1(a)): "outline" or "explain" the effect: higher labour costs, squeezed margin, possible price rises, fewer hours, process changes, effect on staff and customers. A full answer gives both the impact and a response. Common mistakes: only the cost side; no link to the restaurant; no stakeholder effects.
- Strategy items: outline how a business can adapt through the P that fits the driver (for example demographics: product, place and promotion; rising costs: pricing, process, business model). Name the driver, the adaptation, the reason it helps and the risk.
- Applying my learning: expect a news headline or company profile followed by analyse or discuss. Identify the growth strategy or driver, explain the likely effects on stakeholders, and give a reasoned opinion.
- Appendix 1: outline means main points only; analyse means break down and reach conclusions; discuss means a balanced review with evidence. Give three or four developed points.
- Never invent mark allocations or question wording.
