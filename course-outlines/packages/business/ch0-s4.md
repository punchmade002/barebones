# Package: ch0-s4 — The Business Alive Investigative Study (AAC)

- **loId:** `ch0-s4` · **chapterId:** `ch0`
- **Syllabus scope:** the Additional Assessment Component section of the NCCA *Curriculum Specification for Leaving Certificate Business* (pp. 32–34) and its Descriptors of Quality (planning; investigating; applying; communicating), first examined June 2027. The Investigative Study asks students to "conduct, analyse and evaluate research on a particular issue, using a variety of sources and considering a range of perspectives, while drawing on their ongoing engagement with the world of business", and to present informed perspectives, conclusions and/or recommendations. The 2027 brief (per the outline): investigate how a work practice that uses digital technology is impacting either employers or employees, supported by primary and/or secondary data. Guidance only: the notes must never write a student's report. No outcome is Higher Level only.
- **Neighbouring subsections (by subject):** developing research questions and managing and acknowledging sources; project planning; analysing, evaluating and presenting findings. This subsection says what the study is and how it is marked, and points to those for the skills.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The Unifying Strand skills: SMART question, sources, project plan, analysis, conclusions (taught in the other notes of this chapter).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) A report has a structure with headings, and images and graphs should be labelled.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Digital technology in the workplace: video calls, cloud platforms, collaboration tools, remote working.

What the study is and how it is weighted
- [ ] W1 The AAC: a student-led report to an SEC brief; common brief; up to 20 hours; individual digital report.
- [ ] W2 40% of the overall marks (200 marks); written examination 60%; the study is assessed at the level at which the student sits the final examination (one common brief).
- [ ] W3 The brief is issued by the SEC in term two of 5th year; the report is submitted digitally in 6th year (spec); the 2027 date for work to be completed and submitted to the class teacher is 12 March 2027 (outline, from the SEC brief; see section 2).
- [ ] W4 SEC mark allocation: Introduction 20, Investigation, Findings, Analysis and Evaluation 100, Conclusion 30, Overall Coherence 50 (outline, from the SEC brief; see section 2).
- [ ] W5 Overall Coherence is a mark for the whole report, not a section.

Report requirements (2027 brief, per outline)
- [ ] F1 Length: no more than 1,500 words; references, data tables, graphs, diagrams, images and labels not counted; at most 10 images; images labelled Figure 1, 2, ...; images not used to carry extra text.
- [ ] F2 Formatting: Arial; headings black size 14 bold; body black size 12 with 1.5 line spacing; portrait; page numbers bottom centre; 20 mm margins; no coloured text; the SEC cover page first; PDF, proof-read in print.
- [ ] F3 The five headings, each on a new page, with suggested word counts: Introduction about 200; Investigation and Findings about 400; Analysis and Evaluation about 600; Conclusions about 300; References (not counted).
- [ ] F4 What belongs in each section (see section 2 rows) and that there is no separate plan section.

The six stages and tools
- [ ] T1 Getting started; developing a question; project plan; sources and data; analysis and evaluation; applying learning and conclusions, each with its purpose and NCCA indicative timing.
- [ ] T2 Ongoing monitoring and reflection across all stages.
- [ ] T3 The investigative folder (encouraged, not submitted) and the project plan (shared with the teacher, not submitted).
- [ ] T4 Applying: relate findings to the world of business, business stories in the media and the cross-cutting themes.

Descriptors of Quality (high, moderate, low) [one item each]
- [ ] Q1 Planning: engagement with planning throughout and reflection on how far it helped; a clear and probing research question underpinned by its importance to business.
- [ ] Q2 Investigating: a wide range of appropriate sources evaluated; in-depth analysis and evaluation justifying the choice of sources; conclusions justified by the analysis; limitations clearly identified across question design, research gathered and analysis.
- [ ] Q3 Applying: reflect on how findings relate to the world of business and media stories, and on how perspectives were influenced; relate findings clearly to the cross-cutting themes.
- [ ] Q4 Communicating: clear data, information and analysis using a range of relevant graphics; coherent, consistent language; clearly acknowledged supporting references; reflections on the study.

The 2027 topic
- [ ] Y1 A digital work practice (a way of working that relies on digital technology) and its impact on either employers or employees.
- [ ] Y2 Possible impacts on employers (costs, productivity, recruitment, security) and on employees (flexibility, workload, skills, wellbeing, the right to disconnect); the brief wording is unverified, so check whether the student must pick one side.
- [ ] Y3 Narrowing to one practice and one group so the question stays answerable in 1,500 words; supported by primary and/or secondary data.
- [ ] Y4 Link to the notes on digital transformation and on how technology affects employees (by subject, never by id).

Authentication, referencing and AI
- [ ] Z1 The work must be the student's own; authentication is a process across the study (teacher sees plan and work in progress).
- [ ] Z2 Teachers should not correct the report for redrafting.
- [ ] Z3 Referencing and AI acknowledgement as in the notes on sources: in-text citation, reference list, AI tool name, date, how used, URL or prompt.
- [ ] Z4 Plagiarism and penalties up to withholding of results.

Applying my learning: Irish news and real-world examples
- [ ] N1 Two checked real-world examples linked to the topic (section 2): the right to request remote working (March 2024) and the right to disconnect code (April 2021), plus the CSO's working-from-home counts (Q4 2025).
- [ ] N2 How to use a news example in an answer: state the fact, link it to employers or employees, give one benefit and one drawback, add a judgement (ethical and digital angle).

Cross-cutting angle
- [ ] X1 Ethics: honest data, consent from interview participants, own work, AI acknowledged.
- [ ] X2 Digital: the brief itself is a digital-transformation topic; data security and monitoring of employees; AI tools need checking.
- [ ] X3 Sustainability: remote work and commuting; digital use of energy (only as qualitative points, no figures).
- [ ] X4 Entrepreneurial thinking: apply the planning, risk and adaptation mindset to the study.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "the SEC Authentication Form P.2", because the form number is not verified.
- Do not use: "teachers may give general, non-directive feedback" or "may not supply model text" as a rule, because the checked text says only that teachers should not correct the report for redrafting; teachers may discuss the proposed question (NCCA Stage 2).
- Do not use: "the SEC treats it as cheating", because the checked wording is a significant breach of regulations with penalties up to withholding of results.
- Do not use: "The NCCA Descriptors of Quality give a mark scheme", because the descriptors describe high, moderate and low achievement; marks come from the SEC brief's allocation.
- Do not use: "Overall Coherence is awarded for [a list of components]", because the list in the current notes is not verified; say only that it is a 50-mark award for the whole report, and that the communicating descriptor rewards coherent, consistent language, clear graphics and acknowledged references.
- Do not use: "the investigative folder as the specification recommends", because it is "encouraged" in the NCCA guidelines and is not submitted.
- Do not use: "Harvard" as required; no formal style is required.
- Do not use: "the topic must be about remote working", because the brief names any digital work practice; remote working is only one example.
- Do not use: old-syllabus content: the Applied Business Question, ratios, SEC papers 1999 to 2026 as models for the study; none relate to this study.
- Confusable: the AAC (the study, 40%) versus the written exam (60%); the report (submitted) versus the project plan and folder (not submitted); a digital work practice versus digital technology in general; employers versus employees (one side only); the cover page (not counted) versus the body; Overall Coherence (a mark) versus the Conclusion (a section).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: how to write a SMART question, judge sources and reference (questions and sources); the parts of a plan and a risk assessment (project planning); interpreting data and drawing conclusions (analysing and presenting). Give each a one-line pointer by subject, not a re-teach.
- Do not use: the full text, a model answer or a model report for a student's study; give guidance only.

## 4. What exams ask

The outline records no 2025 sample-paper question on the study itself; the AAC is marked through the report, with the SEC allocation in section 2. The written paper can ask about the Unifying Strand skills and the digital theme. The following are expected uses of Appendix 1 verbs, not recorded questions; no marks or frequencies are claimed.
- In the report itself: the Introduction states the question, rationale and engagement with the plan; Investigation and Findings justify methods and sources and show varied perspectives; Analysis and Evaluation analyse and evaluate data with originality and limitations; Conclusions are justified by the analysis, reflect on planning and changed perspective, and link to business. A full answer to each section uses its descriptor: a range of sources evaluated, limits identified, graphics labelled and referred to, references traceable.
- **Outline / describe** the requirements or stages: the stage names in order with one purpose each.
- **Explain** how a student earns credit for planning when the plan is not submitted: through reflection in the Introduction and Conclusions, citing specific changes made and their effect.
- **Identify and explain** a formatting or length rule and why it matters (any rule from the 2027 brief that the student can see).
- **Evaluate / discuss** a digital work practice (applying the skills): one benefit and one drawback for employers or for employees, each developed, with a justified judgement; link to a checked Irish example (the right to request remote working; the right to disconnect).
- Common mistakes: writing a plan section when none is required; going over the word count; describing sources without judging them; limitations stated without effect; no link to business or the media; studying employers and employees both; unacknowledged AI use; images that carry extra text; unlabelled graphs; a conclusion that does not answer the question.
