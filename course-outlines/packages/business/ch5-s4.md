# Package: ch5-s4 — Foreign direct investment

- **loId:** `ch5-s4` · **chapterId:** `ch5`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 1, outcomes [1.5.10, 1.5.11] and 1.6 Applying my learning [1.6.1]: the concept of foreign direct investment (FDI) and how Ireland attracts it (human capital development, pro-enterprise policy, access to markets, research); its opportunities and challenges for Ireland in employment, revenue, research, infrastructure and innovation. Students explain FDI, investigate how the government promotes it and outline its contribution; they analyse business news and give an informed opinion. Nothing is marked Higher Level only. Glossary term: human capital.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A multinational enterprise owns or controls operations in more than one country; most FDI comes from multinationals.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Corporation tax is a tax on company profits.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Gross domestic product (GDP) measures output within a country; a modified measure, GNI*, is used in Ireland because profits of multinationals flow abroad.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) EU membership gives access to the single market (developed in the notes on trading blocs).

The concept of FDI [1.5.10]
- [ ] F1 Definition: investment by a company in one country that creates a lasting interest and some control over a business or asset in another country. Explain it with an example of a sample firm.
- [ ] F2 Portfolio investment (buying shares or bonds without seeking control) versus FDI.
- [ ] F3 Greenfield investment: building new facilities (new capacity). Acquisition (brownfield): buying an existing business or site (ownership transfers, no new capacity at once).
- [ ] F4 FDI is usually long term, so less volatile than portfolio flows.

How Ireland attracts FDI [1.5.10, 1.6.1]
- [ ] H1 IDA Ireland: the state agency for attracting and supporting foreign investment in manufacturing and internationally traded services; it promotes Ireland abroad, offers grants and supports, helps firms find sites and property and links them with skills providers. This answers "investigate how the government promotes it".
- [ ] H2 Human capital development (Appendix 2 definition): educated, English-speaking workforce; third-level institutions and further education and training bodies.
- [ ] H3 Pro-enterprise policy: stable legal and regulatory framework for long-term confidence.
- [ ] H4 Access to markets: EU single market without tariffs, plus a network of trade agreements.
- [ ] H5 Research: universities and research centres and state research funding attract R&D investment.
- [ ] H6 Clusters: related firms in one area (ICT in Dublin; pharmaceutical and medical technology firms in Cork, Limerick and Galway) draw more investment and specialist suppliers.
- [ ] H7 Corporation tax: the 12.5 per cent rate on trading income; the OECD minimum tax (Pillar Two) at 15 per cent for very large groups; Ireland's appeal then rests more on non-tax factors such as skills and infrastructure.

Opportunities of FDI [1.5.11]
- [ ] O1 Employment: direct jobs and indirect jobs in suppliers and services.
- [ ] O2 Revenue: corporation tax; income tax and the Universal Social Charge paid by employees.
- [ ] O3 Research and development and innovation: R&D centres and high-skilled jobs.
- [ ] O4 Skills transfer: trained staff move to Irish firms.
- [ ] O5 Supplier linkages: purchases from local firms help SMEs grow.
- [ ] O6 Regional development: jobs outside Dublin.
- [ ] O7 Infrastructure: investment can support demands for better transport, utilities and digital infrastructure (link to the challenge below).

Challenges of FDI [1.5.11]
- [ ] C1 Concentration: a few large firms provide a large share of corporation tax receipts, so public finances are exposed.
- [ ] C2 Footloose investment: firms can relocate if taxes, costs or regulation change.
- [ ] C3 Pressure on housing and infrastructure: higher rents, congestion and demands on water and transport.
- [ ] C4 Profit repatriation: profits paid to overseas parents leave the economy, which is why GNI* is used alongside GDP.
- [ ] C5 Effect on Irish SMEs: competition for skilled staff and higher wages.
- [ ] C6 Limited linkages: some multinationals buy most inputs abroad.

Applying my learning: analysing business news [1.6.1]
- [ ] N1 Method: identify the central claim; check evidence (jobs, timescale, amount, announced or confirmed); identify stakeholders; weigh short and long term; form a judgement with at least two pieces of evidence and one limitation.
- [ ] N2 One or two current Irish news examples linked to the chapter's topic, with month, year and source (section 2), used to practise the method.
- [ ] N3 Analyse, examine, evaluate (Appendix 1): examine the story's assumptions and perspectives; do not accept a headline figure without asking what it counts.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] X1 Ethical: tax fairness (the global minimum tax aims to limit competition between countries on tax); reliance on a few firms; housing pressure on local people.
- [ ] X2 Sustainability: energy and water demand of data centres and of large manufacturing sites; firms may be expected to report on environmental, social and governance (ESG) factors (Appendix 2 definition below).
- [ ] X3 Digital: much FDI in Ireland is in ICT, digital services and data centres; digital technology firms bring skills and innovation but also demands on the electricity grid.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Science Foundation Ireland" as the research funder, because it merged into Research Ireland in August 2024; write "state research funding" or "Research Ireland".
- Do not use: "The OECD global minimum tax ... for very large multinational groups" without the threshold, because only groups with revenue of at least 750 million euro are in scope; give the threshold with its source.
- Do not use: any figure for corporation tax receipts, share from multinationals, jobs promised, or investment amounts other than the dated IDA and CSO figures above, because they change.
- Do not use: "FDI is always a long-term commitment", because footloose investment can leave; say "usually long term".
- Do not use: the Irish Times July 2026 piece on why US multinationals keep choosing Ireland, because it is behind a subscription and only the headline was seen.
- Do not use: IDA's results for the first half of 2026, because the figures were not read from the source.
- Confusable: FDI (lasting interest and control) versus portfolio investment (no control); greenfield (new) versus acquisition (existing); GDP versus GNI*; corporation tax (on company profits) versus income tax (on individuals).
- Confusable: IDA Ireland (attracts foreign firms) versus Enterprise Ireland (helps Irish firms to export); they must not be swapped. Do not describe Enterprise Ireland here beyond that contrast.
- Confusable: FDI into Ireland (inward) versus Irish firms investing abroad (outward); this subsection is about inward FDI.
- Belongs elsewhere: what a trading bloc is and the benefits of EU membership (trading blocs); entry methods, balance of payments and exchange rates (trading internationally); globalisation, supply-chain risk and technology (globalisation). Mention EU access here only as a reason to locate in Ireland.

## 4. What exams ask

- Students give two reasons a firm would locate in Ireland. A full answer gives two distinct reasons drawn from human capital, pro-enterprise policy, access to EU markets, research, corporation tax, English language and clusters, each with a short reason. Common mistake: "low tax" alone, with no link to the firm's decision, or two reasons that are the same.
- Students outline two opportunities and two challenges of FDI for Ireland. A full answer gives four distinct points, each with its effect on the economy, public finances or communities. Common mistake: naming jobs and tax only; giving challenges with no explanation (such as "housing" without the link to rents).
- Explain FDI (Appendix 1: give a detailed account including reasons or causes): definition, one example, and why countries compete for it.
- Investigate how the government promotes FDI: a full answer names IDA Ireland, what it does, and at least two of the four factors the specification names.
- Applying my learning: analyse a news story and give an informed opinion. A full answer states the claim, checks the evidence, names stakeholders, weighs short and long term, gives a judgement supported by two pieces of evidence, and states a limit of the view. Common mistake: restating the headline.
- Exam tip material: link each reason to a firm's decision; pair every opportunity with a limit; do not invent figures; give the ethical or sustainability angle (housing, energy demand, tax fairness) where asked to evaluate.
