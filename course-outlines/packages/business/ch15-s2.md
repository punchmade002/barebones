## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The communication process: sender, message, mode, receiver, feedback; a mode (medium) is the method used to deliver a message.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) One-way versus two-way communication; formal and informal communication.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Barriers such as information overload, wrong medium and culture and language (from the previous subsection's notes), used here to judge each mode.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Personal data is information about an identifiable person; organisations must protect it.

Verbal communication
- [ ] V1 Face-to-face: immediate feedback, full range of cues; suits sensitive or complex discussions.
- [ ] V2 Meetings: several people share information or decide; take time, can be dominated by a few voices.
- [ ] V3 Telephone calls: quick two-way talk when apart; no visual cues, no record unless notes are kept.
- [ ] V4 Video calls: speech plus facial expression and gesture; helps spread-out teams.
- [ ] V5 Verbal communication leaves no permanent record, so decisions often need written confirmation.

Non-verbal communication
- [ ] N1 Body language (posture, gestures) shows confidence, interest or unease.
- [ ] N2 Facial expressions show emotional response; a frown may be read as disagreement.
- [ ] N3 Tone of voice, pace and volume show attitude and urgency; the same words can sound supportive or hostile.
- [ ] N4 Eye contact shows attention and sincerity to many listeners; meaning varies across cultures.
- [ ] N5 Non-verbal cues are hard to control and can contradict the spoken message, undermining trust.

Written and visual communication
- [ ] W1 Email: fast, cheap, many recipients, searchable record; overuse causes information overload and slow replies to urgent matters.
- [ ] W2 Instant messaging: quick informal exchange; can be ignored or misread without tone.
- [ ] W3 Reports: detailed, structured, can be referred back to; take time to prepare and read.
- [ ] W4 Noticeboards, intranet posts, newsletters: wide audience, low cost, no direct response.
- [ ] W5 Visual: charts and graphs (trends, comparisons), diagrams and flowcharts (processes), presentations (slides, images and speech; overloaded slides reduce clarity), videos and infographics (engaging, shareable, costly to produce well).

Choosing a mode and its efficacy
- [ ] C1 Seven criteria, each explained: urgency, cost, confidentiality, audience (number, location, preferences), need for a record, complexity, feedback.
- [ ] C2 Weigh the criteria together; the best answer is often a combination (a live session backed by a written summary).
- [ ] C3 Efficacy defined: how well a mode achieves its communication goal.
- [ ] C4 Efficacy analysis: face-to-face (trust, complex issues; slow and costly if far apart); email (detailed recorded messages to many; can be buried); video calls (remote teams, two-way; poor connections, screen fatigue); noticeboards (cheap, reach everyone on site; weak for complex or sensitive messages, cannot confirm receipt); visual presentations (explain data; fail if the audience cannot see or interpret them).

Impact of technology: the eight parameters (positive and negative for each) [3.3.3]
- [ ] T1 Modes: more options (video calls, messaging, collaboration platforms) but more choices to manage.
- [ ] T2 Speed: instant messages and files, faster decisions; pressure to reply immediately.
- [ ] T3 Cost: usually cheaper than printing and travel; subscriptions, devices and support cost money.
- [ ] T4 Access: cloud tools give access from anywhere; people without reliable internet or digital skills can be excluded.
- [ ] T5 Collaboration: shared documents and platforms let many people edit and comment in real time.
- [ ] T6 Sustainable practices: less paper and travel; data centres and devices use energy and create electronic waste.
- [ ] T7 Productivity: automation of routine messages and scheduling saves time; constant notifications interrupt focused work.
- [ ] T8 Security: phishing, malware, data breaches, unauthorised access; passwords, staff training, access controls.
- [ ] T9 How technology enables communication: discuss overall, with a judgement on whether the effect is positive and on what it depends (managing overload and treating security as part of communication).

Technology and the wider organisation; ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] D1 Digital transformation: redesigning communication processes around digital tools so knowledge is shared across departments and locations.
- [ ] D2 Data protection: the GDPR applies to workplace messages containing personal data, so organisations control who can see what (ethical duty to protect privacy).
- [ ] D3 Communication policy: which tools for which purposes; reduces confusion and protects data.
- [ ] D4 Small firms can reach customers and partners cheaply, for example through social media.
- [ ] D5 Ethical: privacy, honest use of monitoring, inclusion of staff without digital access; sustainability: paperless and reduced travel against energy use and e-waste (links to T4 and T6).
- [ ] D6 Phishing defined: a fraudulent message that tries to trick the recipient into revealing data or clicking a harmful link.

Applying my learning [3.5]
- [ ] N6 One or two current Irish news examples linked to communication and technology, with month, year and source (section 2): use the AI-communication example for ongoing engagement, managing change and ethics, and the cyber example for security.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the HSE cyber attack of May 2021 or any other named attack as the current news example, because it is not current; the checked 2026 examples above are the news examples.
- Do not use: "93% of communication is non-verbal" or any percentage split between words, tone and body language, because it is unverified and a known misreading of research.
- Do not use: any named software product, platform or app as the example of a mode (for example a named video-call or messaging service), because none is checked; describe the type of tool.
- Do not use: figures for email volumes, costs, time lost to notifications or energy use, because they change and are unchecked.
- Do not use: the cyber-insurance percentage from the Hiscox report, because published sources give different figures.
- Confusable: verbal (uses spoken words, including phone and video) versus non-verbal (body, face, tone, eye contact); a video call is verbal with non-verbal cues visible.
- Confusable: written (words kept as a record) versus visual (images, charts, diagrams); a report may be both.
- Confusable: efficacy (how well a mode meets its goal) versus efficiency (speed and cost); phishing (a deceptive message) versus malware (harmful software) versus a data breach (unauthorised access to or loss of data).
- Boundary: why communication matters (the six purposes), barriers and the four ways to improve communication, including active listening, feedback and two-way channels, belong to the previous subsection on why communication matters. Here name them only to judge a mode.
- Boundary: remote and blended working, monitoring software and the right to disconnect belong to the notes on digital technology in the workplace; the business-alive study brief is covered elsewhere. Use them here only in one line.
- Boundary: consumer data rights and the full GDPR content belong to the consumer chapter; here use only "personal data in workplace messages must be protected".
- Boundary: memos, business letters, agendas and minutes, and visual presentation of data as a drafting exercise are old-syllabus only (Appendix A4); leave out.

## 4. What exams ask

- The 2025 Sample Paper 2 asks students to outline two considerations when choosing a mode of communication. Appendix 1 "outline" is the main points only. A full answer names two of the seven criteria (urgency, cost, confidentiality, audience, record, complexity, feedback) and says in a line each why it matters, applied to the message in the stimulus. Common mistakes: naming a mode instead of a consideration; listing criteria with no reason; developing four points where two are asked.
- "Identify" and "analyse" questions on modes: identify the mode, then analyse its efficacy as strengths and weaknesses for the given message and audience (for example confidential news to a dispersed workforce).
- "Discuss how technology enables communication" or "evaluate the impact": cover several of the eight parameters, each with a positive and a negative, apply to the business named, and finish with a judgement and what it depends on. Common mistakes: only positives; naming parameters with no effect; treating security as a separate IT matter rather than part of communication.
- Choice-of-mode scenarios usually reward a combined approach (live two-way session plus written record) justified by the criteria.
- Include the ethical, sustainability and digital angle when the stimulus gives one (privacy and data protection, paperless communication against energy use, exclusion of staff without digital access).
- "Applying my learning" tasks: link a current news item to a point in the notes (managing change, ongoing engagement, security) and give an opinion supported by a reason, in the Appendix 1 sense of "discuss" or "evaluate".
