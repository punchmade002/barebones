# Package: ch19-s2 — Trade unions and the gig economy

- **loId:** `ch19-s2` · **chapterId:** `ch19`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 4, section 4.3, [4.3.3] and [4.3.4]. Students learn about the function of trade unions in supporting employees (describe their role) and the impact on workers of on-demand, freelance jobs paid "per piece"; they should be able to discuss the gig economy and its opportunities and challenges for workers. Glossary term: gig economy. Footnote 3: use current employment legislation. Outline scope: what a union is and its functions (collective bargaining, representation, advice, protecting jobs, lobbying); shop steward; ICTU; types of union (assumed); the gig economy with examples; opportunities and challenges; the EU Platform Work Directive and Irish developments.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) An employee works for one employer under a contract; a self-employed person works for themselves, and different rights follow from each status. (Rights of employees are taught in the notes on employee rights.)
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Why individual workers have less bargaining power than an employer.

Trade unions
- [ ] T1 A trade union is an organisation of workers that represents its members in dealings with employers and government; collective strength lets individuals negotiate on more equal terms.
- [ ] T2 Members pay subscriptions that fund staff, advice and representation; unions are independent of employers and government, though they can work with both.
- [ ] T3 Collective bargaining: negotiation of pay, hours and conditions for a group, with an employer or employer body.
- [ ] T4 Representation at grievance, disciplinary and dismissal hearings; taking cases to the WRC or the Labour Court.
- [ ] T5 Advice and information on rights, contracts and workplace problems, often with legal support.
- [ ] T6 Protecting jobs: redundancy terms and employment during restructuring.
- [ ] T7 Lobbying for stronger employment laws and social policies (for example minimum wage increases and safety rules).
- [ ] T8 Shop steward: an elected union member in a workplace who links members and management and deals with early problems.
- [ ] T9 ICTU: the federation of most Irish trade unions, speaking for workers at national level.
- [ ] T10 (assumed) Types of union: craft, general, industrial, white-collar.
- [ ] T11 Describe the role: a short account tying T3 to T8 into "how a union supports an employee".

The gig economy
- [ ] G1 Definition (Appendix 2, quoted in §2).
- [ ] G2 Paid per task, per job or per piece rather than by salary; on-demand and freelance work, often arranged through digital platforms.
- [ ] G3 Examples: food and parcel delivery riders, ride-hailing drivers, freelancers on online marketplaces.
- [ ] G4 Platforms often label gig workers as self-employed contractors, which can deny employee rights.
- [ ] G5 Opportunities: flexibility, extra income, low barriers to entry, building skills and a portfolio.
- [ ] G6 Challenges: insecure income; no sick pay, holiday pay, pension or redundancy protection; employment-status disputes; low effective hourly pay when unpaid waiting time is not counted; algorithmic management (ratings, automated allocation, deactivation, little right of appeal); weak collective bargaining; road safety risks.
- [ ] G7 Balanced "discuss" conclusion: who gains (students, carers, people seeking supplementary income) and who loses (those depending on gig work as their only income).

Law and developments
- [ ] L1 The key legal question is employee or self-employed, decided on factors such as control, integration into the business and whether the worker is in business on their own account.
- [ ] L2 Irish Supreme Court, Domino's Pizza case (2023): delivery drivers held to be employees, not contractors; Revenue guidance followed.
- [ ] L3 EU Platform Work Directive: aims to improve conditions for platform workers; presumption of employment where facts show control; transparency and human oversight of algorithmic management.
- [ ] L4 EU directives must be transposed into national law; Irish law decides how the rules apply; the deadline is 2 December 2026.
- [ ] L5 Unions have campaigned for employee protections and access to collective bargaining for gig workers.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical: fairness of pay for unpaid waiting time, transparency of ratings and deactivation, and the pressure of insecure work on wellbeing.
- [ ] E2 Sustainability: precarious work and the social side of ESG; platforms may argue flexibility, unions argue decent work.
- [ ] E3 Digital: platforms, apps and algorithms allocate and monitor work; the Directive is the EU's response to algorithmic management.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Ireland has passed a law on platform work", because no Irish transposing law was found as of 2026-11-23; say the Directive must be transposed by 2 December 2026.
- Do not use: "the Directive makes all gig workers employees", because it creates a rebuttable presumption based on facts of control, not automatic status.
- Do not use: any named gig platform (Deliveroo, Uber, Just Eat and so on) with a claim about its Irish classification, because no 2026 Irish source was verified.
- Do not use: union membership numbers or the number of Irish unions other than the dated ICTU figures, because they change.
- Do not use: "mutual obligations" as the named Irish test, because the 2023 case set a five-step test; give the factors only.
- Do not use: strike, picketing or industrial action law, because it is not in the specification outcome and no Act is checked.
- Confusable: collective bargaining (group negotiation) versus an individual contract; trade union versus employers' association (Ibec is not taught here); gig worker versus freelancer (a freelancer is one kind of gig worker); piece rate versus hourly pay.
- Neighbouring subsection: the Acts on pay, working time, discrimination and dismissal, the WRC and codes of practice belong in the notes on employee rights and responsibilities. Here refer to them only as the rights gig workers may lack.
- Neighbouring subsection: the Budget, tax, pensions and payslips belong in the notes on the Budget and informed decisions, including auto-enrolment for workers.
- Neighbouring chapter: leadership styles and motivation of staff are taught in the management chapter; do not use them here.

## 4. What exams ask

- Sample paper 1, Question 5 (a), asks students to explain the gig economy and identify three challenges for gig workers. A full answer defines the gig economy (temporary or freelance work, often via platforms, paid per task) with an example, then names three distinct challenges (insecure income, no sick pay or pension, status disputes or algorithmic control) and says why each matters. Mistakes: giving only benefits, naming three challenges that are the same point reworded, defining it as "any self-employment".
- Verbs from Appendix 1: "describe" needs a detailed account of the main points (a union's role); "discuss" a balanced review with a range of arguments (opportunities against challenges), supported by an example; "identify" brief named points; "explain" reasons or causes. Likely forms (from the verbs, not any paper): describe two functions of a trade union, identify ways a union supports a worker, discuss whether the gig economy benefits workers.
- A "discuss" answer gives three or four developed points on each side, uses an example, and finishes with a judgement. Link to the digital angle where the question gives an app or platform.
- Common mistakes: confusing a trade union with a professional body; saying unions only organise strikes; forgetting representation and advice; treating the Directive as Irish law.
