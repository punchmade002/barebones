# Package: ch0-s2 — Project planning

- **loId:** `ch0-s2` · **chapterId:** `ch0`
- **Syllabus scope:** Unifying Strand, U3.1–U3.3 of the NCCA *Curriculum Specification for Leaving Certificate Business* (first examined June 2027). Students learn about the significance of a detailed project plan and of monitoring progress as part of project management, and the need for a project plan that has a clear purpose, goals and criteria for success and includes roles and responsibilities, resources, objectives, a detailed time schedule, risk assessment, monitoring and evaluation. Students should be able to: demonstrate understanding of the importance of clearly defined purpose, goals and criteria for success (U3.1); outline the main elements of a project plan and develop a project plan which includes them (U3.2); demonstrate understanding of the importance of monitoring and evaluating project progress (U3.3). No outcome is Higher Level only.
- **Neighbouring subsections (by subject):** developing research questions and managing sources; analysing, evaluating and presenting findings; the Investigative Study requirements and marking.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A Gantt chart is a bar chart of tasks against time (junior cycle Business Studies / project work).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Reading a calendar or timetable; counting backward from a deadline.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Likelihood and impact can be rated low, medium or high.

Why plan [U3.1, U3.3]
- [ ] P1 Significance of a detailed plan: a clear route from question to conclusion; manageable goals; early warning of delay; evidence of process.
- [ ] P2 Clearly defined purpose (what the study will find out and why it matters to business).
- [ ] P3 Goals (what must be achieved) and criteria for success (how success will be judged, set in advance).
- [ ] P4 Consequences of not planning: late evidence collection, rushed analysis, unfinished work.

Main elements of a plan [U3.2]
- [ ] E1 Purpose, goals and criteria for success.
- [ ] E2 Roles and responsibilities: who does each task, who must provide information or access (teacher, interviewees, the student).
- [ ] E3 Resources: time, money, equipment, software, access to people, data and libraries.
- [ ] E4 Objectives linked to the research question: measurable steps.
- [ ] E5 A detailed time schedule: tasks, start and end dates, dependencies, a Gantt-style chart; the up-to-20-hour limit.
- [ ] E6 Risk assessment: identify, rate likelihood and impact, plan a response, review.
- [ ] E7 Monitoring and evaluation (named as plan elements).
- [ ] E8 A short worked sample plan covering all elements (labelled sample).

Monitoring and evaluating [U3.3]
- [ ] M1 Monitoring: checking actual progress against the plan; milestones; a dated progress log noting issues and how they were overcome.
- [ ] M2 The investigative folder (encouraged by NCCA) as a store for notes, sources, dates and thoughts.
- [ ] M3 Revisiting the plan regularly and noting any change and why.
- [ ] M4 Evaluating: comparing planned with actual, naming causes, judging how far the planning helped complete the study.
- [ ] M5 Sharing the plan with the teacher is part of ongoing authentication; the plan is not submitted to the SEC; the report refers to it by reflecting on and evaluating it.
- [ ] M6 Glossary: no Appendix 2 term is defined for this subsection; use the outline's plain definitions.

Cross-cutting angle
- [ ] X1 Entrepreneurial thinking: planning for risk, diversifying (a second interviewee) and adapting to change is the entrepreneurial mindset (spec's Unifying Strand introduction).
- [ ] X2 Ethics and sustainability: the plan includes ethical steps (permission before interviewing, data privacy) and uses digital sources before printing or travel where suitable.
- [ ] X3 Digital: digital tools for planning (shared calendar, spreadsheet Gantt, cloud folder with backups) and a risk of lost files.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the current sample plan's hour allocation (hours 1–2 choose a topic, 3–4 plan, 5–9 collect data, 10–13 organise, 14–16 evaluate, 17–20 write up), because it does not match the NCCA's indicative timings. Use the NCCA stage times in section 2, which add up to roughly 13 to 20 hours, and say they are indicative and depend on the brief.
- Do not use: "the investigative folder as recommended by the specification" or any claim that the folder is compulsory or submitted, because the NCCA guidelines say students are encouraged to keep one, and neither it nor the plan is submitted.
- Do not use: a separate "project plan" section in the report, because the plan is not submitted and planning is shown by reflection in the report (see the notes on the Investigative Study).
- Do not use: "likelihood and impact rated low/medium/high" as a syllabus requirement, because it is a common project-management method, not in the specification; present it as one way to do a risk assessment.
- Confusable: goals (what the study will achieve) versus objectives (measurable steps toward them) versus criteria for success (how achievement is judged); a risk (could happen) versus an issue (has happened); monitoring (during) versus evaluating (judging how far the plan helped); a milestone (a point) versus a task (an activity); a dependency (cannot start until another finishes).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: refining the research question with SMART and judging sources (questions and sources); interpreting data, limitations of findings and conclusions (analysing and presenting); the report's sections, marks, authentication form and AI rules (the Investigative Study). Limitations of the study's findings are not "risks to the plan".
- Do not use: Gantt charts or critical path as taught project-management topics beyond a one-line explanation; the outline treats a Gantt-style schedule as assumed knowledge.

## 4. What exams ask

The outline records no 2025 sample-paper question on this subsection. The strand is assessed mainly through the Investigative Study: the Descriptors of Quality reward "effective engagement with planning throughout" and a clear reflection on and evaluation of "the extent to which the planning undertaken contributed to the effective completion of the study" (high), "some" engagement and reflection (moderate), and "limited engagement" (low). Written-exam forms below are expected uses of the Appendix 1 verbs, not recorded questions; no marks or frequencies are claimed.
- **Outline** the main elements of a project plan: name all of purpose, goals and criteria for success, roles and responsibilities, resources, objectives, time schedule, risk assessment, monitoring and evaluation, with a few words on each. "Outline" means the main points only (Appendix 1).
- **Demonstrate understanding / explain** why a plan matters or why monitoring matters: a reason, an effect on the study, and a short example from a sample study.
- **Develop** a plan (U3.2): a plan that actually includes each element for a given scenario, with named tasks, dates within the available hours, a risk and its response.
- **Identify** a risk and give a response: a specific risk to the study (not a general "something goes wrong") and a response that is planned in advance.
- **Evaluate** how far planning helped (Investigative Study): compare planned and actual, give a cause for any change, and judge the effect.
- Common mistakes: writing only that "a plan was made" (low achievement); giving a plan with no dates; risks that are unrelated to the study; confusing goals with objectives; saying monitoring is only a final check; forgetting that the plan is not submitted but must be reflected on in the report.
