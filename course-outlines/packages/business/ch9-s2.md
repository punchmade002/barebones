# Package: ch9-s2 — The marketing mix and the target market

- **loId:** `ch9-s2` · **chapterId:** `ch9`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 2, section 2.4, learning outcome [2.4.2]. Students outline the elements of the marketing mix (product/service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place) and explain their significance for a business and how they are influenced by the target market. Outline scope: each of the seven elements, with its definition, the decisions it involves and how the target market shapes it. Product: features, quality, branding, product life cycle (assumed). Pricing strategies: cost-plus, penetration, skimming, competitive, psychological, premium, loss leader. Promotion: advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations, sponsorship, social-media and influencer marketing. Place: channels of distribution, direct and online. No Higher Level only content.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The target market and its segments, from the notes on market research (one-line recall).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Product life cycle: introduction, growth, maturity, decline; each stage needs different marketing decisions.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) The difference between a good (tangible) and a service (intangible, delivered by people).
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Costs, selling price and profit: price must cover costs for the business to earn a profit.

The marketing mix as a whole
- [ ] M1 The marketing mix is the set of decisions a business controls to satisfy its target market.
- [ ] M2 The seven Ps in this course: product or service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place. "Physical evidence" is not one of them.
- [ ] M3 Significance: the mix determines whether customers buy, how much they pay and whether the business earns a profit.
- [ ] M4 The Ps are interdependent (a premium price affects packaging, promotion and outlets); inconsistency weakens the brand.
- [ ] M5 The target market shapes every element: what customers want, can afford and respond to determines the mix.

Product or service
- [ ] PR1 Features and quality: set what the product does and how well, judged against what the target market values.
- [ ] PR2 Branding: a name, identity and reputation that help customers recognise and trust the product.
- [ ] PR3 Range and life-cycle stage (assumed) affect the decisions.
- [ ] PR4 Services are intangible, variable and perishable, so quality depends on the people delivering them.

Pricing
- [ ] PC1 Pricing sets what customers pay and must reflect costs, competitors and what the target market will pay.
- [ ] PC2 Cost-plus: a fixed mark-up on cost; simple but ignores what customers will pay.
- [ ] PC3 Penetration: low initial price to win share quickly.
- [ ] PC4 Skimming: high initial price for customers who will pay more, lowered as competition grows.
- [ ] PC5 Competitive: in line with, or just below, rivals.
- [ ] PC6 Premium: higher price to signal quality or exclusivity.
- [ ] PC7 Psychological: prices such as 9.99 euro that look lower than a round figure.
- [ ] PC8 Loss leader: one product sold below cost to attract customers who buy more profitable products.
- [ ] PC9 How the target market influences price (income, price sensitivity, what it associates with quality).

Promotion
- [ ] PM1 Promotion communicates the offer and persuades the target market to buy; methods can be combined (the promotional mix).
- [ ] PM2 Advertising: paid, non-personal communication (television, radio, outdoor, online display).
- [ ] PM3 Sales promotion: short-term incentives (discounts, coupons, competitions).
- [ ] PM4 Personal selling: one-to-one by a sales representative; suits complex or high-value products.
- [ ] PM5 Public relations: goodwill through media coverage, press releases and events; not paid for in the same way as advertising.
- [ ] PM6 Sponsorship: links a brand with an event, team or cause.
- [ ] PM7 Social media and influencer marketing: two-way engagement; influencers paid to promote to their audiences. (Ethics and detail belong to the notes on digital technology, ethics and sustainability in marketing.)
- [ ] PM8 How the target market chooses the media (where its customers spend their time).

People, packaging, process
- [ ] PE1 People: the staff and others who deliver the offer; recruitment, training, motivation and reward shape the customer experience; in services the employee is often the part of the offer customers experience most.
- [ ] PE2 Customer-facing staff should suit the target market (for example knowledgeable advisers for a premium brand).
- [ ] PK1 Packaging: protects the product, makes it easier to handle, and communicates through design, colour and labelling.
- [ ] PK2 Labelling must be accurate (ingredients, weight, use-by date) as required by law for many products.
- [ ] PK3 Sustainable packaging (recyclable materials, less material) appeals to environmentally conscious customers.
- [ ] PS1 Process: the system by which the offer is made and delivered (steps, speed, ease for the customer).
- [ ] PS2 Examples of process choices: online ordering, self-service checkouts, tracked delivery.
- [ ] PS3 A process must be consistent so customers get the same quality each time.

Place
- [ ] PL1 Place: how the product reaches customers through a channel of distribution.
- [ ] PL2 Direct channel: producer to customer (farm shop, own website). Indirect channel: through intermediaries (wholesalers, retailers).
- [ ] PL3 Online channels widen reach but add delivery costs.
- [ ] PL4 Location of a physical outlet depends on where the target market shops, travels and works.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical: honest labelling and claims, fair pricing (no exploitation of vulnerable customers), responsible promotion to children.
- [ ] E2 Sustainability: packaging and delivery choices have environmental costs; customers increasingly expect recyclable packaging.
- [ ] E3 Digital: online ordering, social media promotion, tracked delivery and online channels change process, promotion and place.

## 3. Traps

Do not use:
- Do not use: "physical evidence" as one of the Ps, or as a fourth element of a service mix, because the specification lists seven Ps with packaging and without physical evidence.
- Do not use: any older "4 Ps" or "marketing mix of four elements" statement, and note that the existing key term for "marketing mix" mentions only offer, price, promotion and distribution. State the mix as seven Ps.
- Do not use: "services are intangible, variable and perishable" as the only possible list of service characteristics; the three are the usual ones in this course's notes but some sources add "inseparable". Say "usually described as" and keep the three.
- Do not use: loss leader pricing as illegal or always unethical. It is a legitimate strategy; note only that selling below cost is a short-term loss the business accepts.
- Do not use: any claim that labelling rules or packaging rules have one fixed date or penalty, because only the 12 August 2026 start of the EU packaging regulation is checked.

Confusable terms
- Penetration (low start, win share) vs skimming (high start, then lower) vs premium (permanently high to signal quality) vs competitive (match rivals).
- Advertising (paid, non-personal) vs sales promotion (short-term incentive) vs public relations (earned goodwill) vs sponsorship (brand linked to an event).
- Packaging (the 7 Ps item: protection, design, labelling) vs branding (a product decision) vs the product itself.
- Process (how the offer is delivered) vs place (where and through whom it is sold).
- Direct channel (producer to customer, including online) vs indirect channel (through intermediaries).
- People (staff delivering the offer) vs the target market (customers).

## 4. What exams ask

(Source: the outline records no 2025 sample-paper item for this subsection; the points below follow the Appendix 1 verbs and the marketing-mix items the outline lists for a later subsection. No marks or frequencies are invented.)
- Outline (give the main points): outline the elements of the marketing mix for a business in a stimulus. A full answer gives the seven Ps from the specification's list, each with one decision applied to the business. Common mistake: listing four or five Ps, or including physical evidence.
- Explain (detailed account including reasons or causes): explain how the target market influences one or more Ps. A full answer names the target market's characteristics, then links them to a decision (an older, price-sensitive market suits a lower price and printed promotion). Common mistake: describing the P with no link to the target market.
- Identify: identify a pricing strategy, promotion method or distribution channel from a description. A full answer names it in one line. Common mistake: confusing penetration with competitive pricing, or sales promotion with advertising.
- Describe (detailed account of the main points): describe a pricing strategy or channel with an example.
- Discuss or evaluate: the pros and cons of two pricing strategies or of a direct against an indirect channel for a stated business; a full answer weighs both sides against the target market and ends with a justified choice.
- Common mistakes: ignoring the business's context, giving definitions only, and treating the Ps as separate rather than consistent with each other.
