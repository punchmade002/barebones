# Package: ch10-s2 — Costs and revenue streams

- **loId:** `ch10-s2` · **chapterId:** `ch10`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA *Curriculum Specification for Leaving Certificate Business* (first examined June 2027), Strand 2, section 2.5 "Operations and finance", learning outcome [2.5.2] (costs part): students learn about the key costs incurred (variable costs, fixed costs) and the potential revenue streams (single transactions or recurring sales), and that these may evolve over time; they should be able to identify key costs and explore why they change over the lifecycle of the product or service.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A cost is money a business spends to operate; revenue is money earned from sales and other sources. The canvas blocks "cost structure" and "revenue streams" show both; the operational choices in the notes on the operational model drive the costs.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Profit equals total revenue minus total costs; a loss is the reverse.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Break-even is the output at which total revenue equals total cost, so profit is zero. Break-even quantity = fixed costs divided by contribution per unit; contribution per unit = selling price minus variable cost per unit.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) The product life cycle has four stages: introduction, growth, maturity, decline. The business life cycle is the business's own path from start-up through growth to maturity (and possible decline or renewal).

Costs
- [ ] C1 Fixed costs: stay the same in total whatever the level of output in the short run (rent, insurance, salaries of permanent staff).
- [ ] C2 Variable costs: change directly with output (raw materials, packaging, wages paid per item made).
- [ ] C3 Classification test: ask "does the total change if output doubles?" Classification questions are examined, so notes must give a clear test and a list of typical items, not only definitions.
- [ ] C4 Semi-variable costs: have a fixed element and a usage element (electricity with a standing charge plus a usage charge). State how an exam that offers only "fixed" or "variable" would treat such an item: by its main behaviour (see section 3).
- [ ] C5 Total cost = fixed costs + variable costs at a given level of output.
- [ ] C6 Start-up costs: incurred before trading begins (legal fees, fit-out, equipment, initial stock); usually one-off; weigh heavily on early cash.
- [ ] C7 Running costs: ongoing costs of operating once trading starts (wages, rent, utilities, marketing). Underestimating them can cause a cash shortage even when sales grow.
- [ ] C8 Start-up and running costs are a different grouping from fixed and variable: the same cost (rent) can be both a running cost and a fixed cost.

Revenue streams
- [ ] R1 A revenue stream is a source of income. Main difference between streams: how often the customer pays.
- [ ] R2 One-off transaction (single sale, customer pays once: a laptop, a restaurant meal).
- [ ] R3 Recurring revenue (repeated income, easier to predict cash flow): subscription (regular fee for continued access, such as a monthly gym membership), licensing (fees for letting others use intellectual property such as a brand, patent or software), usage fees (charge by how much is used, such as per-unit mobile data), advertising (selling space or airtime to other businesses, how many free apps and websites earn income).
- [ ] R4 A business can combine streams.

Profit and break-even (assumed)
- [ ] B1 Profit = total revenue minus total costs.
- [ ] B2 Contribution per unit and break-even quantity formulas, with the worked sample (invented sample figures): fixed costs €6,000, selling price €15, variable cost €9, so contribution €6 and break-even 1,000 units. Above break-even each extra unit adds to profit; below it the business makes a loss.

Lifecycle
- [ ] L1 Introduction: low sales, high promotion costs, possible loss.
- [ ] L2 Growth: sales rise quickly, unit costs fall as output increases, profit grows.
- [ ] L3 Maturity: sales level off, competition increases, price pressure reduces margins.
- [ ] L4 Decline: sales fall; the business may cut costs, narrow its range or withdraw the product.
- [ ] L5 Over the business life cycle the mix of costs and revenue streams shifts; a mature firm may rely more on recurring income.
- [ ] L6 Explain why costs change over the lifecycle (promotion spend early, economies of scale in growth, discounting under competition, cost cutting in decline).

Evaluation and cross-cutting angle
- [ ] E1 One-off sales give less predictable cash flow than recurring income.
- [ ] E2 High fixed costs make profit sensitive to sales: a small fall can turn profit into loss.
- [ ] E3 Subscriptions build loyalty but customers may cancel if value is not delivered.
- [ ] X1 Ethical: fair and clear charging; subscription terms that are easy to understand and cancel; not hiding fees; paying workers fairly is a cost an ethical business accepts even when cheaper options exist.
- [ ] X2 Sustainability: sustainable materials, energy-efficient equipment and waste reduction change costs (higher start-up cost, lower running cost over time); reuse, repair or take-back schemes as new revenue streams in a circular economy (definition in section 2).
- [ ] X3 Digital: software-as-a-service subscriptions and cloud services charged by usage change variable cost; in-app advertising, platform fees and usage-based pricing as digital revenue streams.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: a verdict that "electricity is variable" or "electricity is fixed" without explanation, because it is semi-variable and exam classification depends on the item's main behaviour. Explain the test and let the student apply it.
- Do not use: a verdict on the 2025 sample true/false statement that variable costs include wages, insurance and electricity, because its mark scheme answer was not checked for this package. Teach the test instead: insurance is a fixed cost (it does not change with output), so a list of variable costs that includes insurance is not a list of variable costs. Wages are variable only when linked to output.
- Do not use: final accounts, balance sheets, profitability ratios or debt/equity ratio, because they are old-syllabus only (Appendix A4).
- Do not use: "break-even chart" drawing or margin-of-safety calculations as topics, because the outline gives break-even only as assumed knowledge (formula and meaning).
- Do not use: "overheads" and "direct costs" as extra categories unless explained as terms already in the notes; the outline requires fixed and variable only. Direct (variable) and indirect (fixed) can be a one-line gloss.
- Confusable: fixed/variable (behaviour as output changes) versus start-up/running (timing). A single cost can be described both ways.
- Confusable: a one-off transaction (customer pays once) versus recurring revenue (repeated payments). A licence fee can be one-off or recurring; classify by the example given.
- Confusable: revenue (income) versus profit (revenue minus costs); contribution (price minus variable cost per unit) versus profit.
- Boundary: the operational model's resources, activities and partners belong to the notes on the operational model. Sources of finance (overdraft, loans, equity) belong to the notes on sources of finance, not here. Cash timing, deficits and remedies belong to the notes on cash-flow analysis. Pricing strategies (cost-plus, penetration, skimming) belong to the notes on the marketing mix.

## 4. What exams ask

- Appendix 1 verbs: **identify** ("recognise patterns, facts, or details; provide an answer from a number of possibilities; recognize and state briefly a distinguishing fact or feature", as printed in Appendix 1), **explain**, **outline**, and for the lifecycle outcome the syllabus word **explore**, which is not in the Appendix 1 glossary; the author's own reading is to teach it to explain-depth, giving causes. Calculation items use **calculate** ("obtain a numerical answer showing the relevant stages in the working").
- The 2025 Sample 1 paper (S1 Q4(a)(ii)) includes a true/false item on whether a stated list of items are variable costs (the outline's paraphrase names wages, insurance and electricity). This shows classification of costs is examined in short-answer form. Full answer: decide true or false from the test, with the reason. Common mistake: classing insurance or permanent salaries as variable.
- Identify costs from a stimulus: a full answer names each cost, gives its type (fixed or variable) and ties it to the business. Common mistake: listing costs with no classification.
- Explain how costs or revenue change over the lifecycle: stage, then change (such as high promotion costs at introduction), then reason, then effect on profit. Common mistake: naming the stages with no cost or revenue effect.
- Revenue stream questions: identify the stream and say why recurring income helps (predictable cash). Common mistake: treating a one-off sale as a subscription.
- Calculation of contribution or break-even (if set): show the formula, the substitution and the unit. Common mistake: dividing by price instead of contribution.
- The SEC uses real, current, named Irish firms in stimuli; notes should show students how to use the firm in the stimulus, not rely on one remembered example.
