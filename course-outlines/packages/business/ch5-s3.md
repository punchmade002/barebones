# Package: ch5-s3 — Globalisation, interdependence and technology

- **loId:** `ch5-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch5`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 1, section 1.5, outcomes [1.5.7–1.5.9]: the social and environmental consequences of globalisation, including political risks, financial risks and cultural barriers; evaluate the impact of Irish organisations trading internationally (positive and negative, social and environmental); explain how globalisation increases interdependence and evaluate the consequences for businesses and consumers; the role of technology in globalisation (trade, work practices, how the business operates) and an evaluation of it. Nothing is marked Higher Level only. Glossary terms: globalisation, interdependence, international trade.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A multinational firm owns or controls operations in more than one country.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) A supply chain: the network of suppliers, producers, transport firms and distributors that moves a product from raw material to customer.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Trade barriers (tariffs, quotas) and trading blocs reduce them (developed in the notes on trading blocs).
- [ ] A4 (assumed) The marketing mix: product, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place (the 7 Ps). Standardise versus adapt applies to these elements.

Globalisation and interdependence [1.5.7, 1.5.8]
- [ ] G1 Definition of globalisation (Appendix 2, quoted in section 2) in the student's own words, with the cross-border trade and movements it names: goods, services, digital technology, investment, people, data and information.
- [ ] G2 Drivers: fewer trade barriers, falling transport and communication costs, growth of multinational firms, digital technology.
- [ ] G3 Definition of interdependence (Appendix 2): one group dependent on another for necessary goods and services.
- [ ] G4 How globalisation increases interdependence: a downturn in a major economy reaches partners through demand, prices and finance; Irish firms depend on imported components, energy and raw materials; consumers depend on imports.
- [ ] G5 Consequences for businesses (wider markets and inputs; exposure to disruption and cost shocks) and for consumers (choice and lower prices; exposure to shortages and price spikes). Evaluate: weigh both.

Positive and negative effects, social and environmental [1.5.7]
- [ ] E1 Positive: jobs and output from investment and exports; transfer of technology and management practice.
- [ ] E2 Negative: job relocation and job losses at home; exploitation of workers in low-cost countries; carbon emissions from long-distance freight and from carbon-intensive production; cultural homogenisation.
- [ ] E3 The Irish organisation abroad, social: jobs and skills in host countries (positive); poor labour standards in supplier factories (negative, reputational damage).
- [ ] E4 The Irish organisation abroad, environmental: emissions from long supply chains and pressure on scarce local resources (negative); improved standards through supplier audits and sustainability requirements (positive).
- [ ] E5 Evaluation test: are overseas standards the same as at home (ethics and sustainability).

Risks [1.5.7]
- [ ] K1 Political risk: change of government, trade wars, sanctions, seizure of assets; response: spread markets, monitor policy.
- [ ] K2 Financial risk: exchange-rate movements; customers failing to pay; responses: hedging, credit insurance, secure payment methods.
- [ ] K3 Cultural barriers: language, religion, customs, business etiquette; tastes that need product adaptation.

Supply chains and disruption [1.5.8]
- [ ] D1 A supply chain spreads production across countries; lowers cost, creates exposure if one link fails.
- [ ] D2 Recent disruptions: the COVID-19 pandemic closing factories and ports and causing component shortages; the blocking of the Suez Canal; the war in Ukraine raising energy and food prices.
- [ ] D3 Responses: diversify suppliers, hold more stock, move production closer to home (nearshoring); shorter chains cut transport emissions and risk but may raise unit costs.

Global marketing: standardise or adapt [1.5.8, assumed use of the 7 Ps]
- [ ] M1 Standardisation: same product, brand and promotion everywhere; lower cost, consistent brand.
- [ ] M2 Adaptation: change product, price, promotion or packaging to suit tastes, laws and incomes.
- [ ] M3 A worked example of adaptation (McDonald's in India, section 2).
- [ ] M4 Hybrid approach: standardise the core product, adapt packaging or language.

Technology's role [1.5.9]
- [ ] T1 How trade happens: e-commerce, online marketplaces, digital payments, logistics tracking and digital customs documents.
- [ ] T2 Work practices: video conferencing, cloud collaboration, remote teams across time zones.
- [ ] T3 How the business operates: cross-border data flows and cloud services; real-time data on stock, suppliers and demand; automation and robotics, which can bring production back to high-wage countries.
- [ ] T4 Evaluate: benefits (low barriers to entry for small firms; data-driven management); risks (cyber-attacks and breaches, digital divide, job displacement with demand for new skills); ethical concern (personal data collected across borders must meet data-protection rules).

Consequences for Irish consumers
- [ ] U1 Benefits: wide choice of imports at competitive prices.
- [ ] U2 Challenges: household prices depend on global prices and shipping; shortages and price spikes; safety risks from weaker inspection abroad; pressure on local shops from global online retailers (three challenges needed for an exam answer).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any figure for freight costs, shortages, price rises or emissions, because they change and are unchecked.
- Do not use: the wording "Interdependence is the reliance countries and firms have on one another" as the definition, because the specification's definition is one group becoming dependent on another for necessary goods and services; the notes' key terms must match Appendix 2.
- Confusable: globalisation (the process of growing connection) versus interdependence (the dependence it creates); international trade (movement of goods and services) versus FDI (investment); political risk (government action) versus financial risk (currency and payment) versus cultural barrier (language and custom).
- Confusable: standardise (one marketing mix everywhere) versus adapt (change it by market). A firm can do both.
- Confusable: hedging (fixing the exchange rate in advance) versus credit insurance (protection against non-payment).
- Do not use: "physical evidence" as one of the 7 Ps, because the specification's 7 Ps are product/service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place.
- Belongs elsewhere: the definition and types of trading bloc and EU membership (trading blocs); entry methods, factors before entering a market and the balance of payments (trading internationally); FDI, IDA Ireland and corporation tax (FDI). Technology here is about trade and work practices; digital finance and fintech belong to the personal finance strand.

## 4. What exams ask

- Students explain three challenges for Irish consumers arising from the country's reliance on globalisation. A full answer gives three distinct challenges (for example price exposure, shortages, product-safety risk, pressure on local shops), each explained with a reason and its effect on households, not only named. Common mistake: listing benefits, or giving the same point three ways.
- Explain how globalisation increases interdependence, then evaluate the consequences for businesses and consumers (Appendix 1: evaluate means make judgements using evidence). A full answer defines interdependence, gives a supply-chain example, gives effects for both groups, positive and negative, and ends with a judgement.
- Evaluate the impact of an Irish organisation trading internationally, positive and negative, social and environmental. A full answer covers all four combinations, names the stakeholders affected, and states whether overseas standards match home standards. Common mistake: only economic effects.
- Outline or analyse political, financial and cultural risks. A full answer gives a response to each risk, not only a description.
- Evaluate technology in globalisation: three or four developed points over trade, work practices and operations, including a risk or an ethical point (data protection, digital divide) and a judgement.
- Exam tip material: cover both social and environmental effects when asked for both; use "sample business" rather than invented real examples; define the glossary term first, then apply it.
