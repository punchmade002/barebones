# Package: ch14-s1 — Workforce planning, recruitment and selection

- **loId:** `ch14-s1` · **chapterId:** `ch14`
- **Syllabus:** Strand 3, section 3.2 (Leading and managing people), outcomes [3.2.1–3.2.3]: internal and external factors affecting workforce planning; the key stages in recruitment (identification, application and selection); the significance of ethics, including equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI); how digital developments change recruitment and selection; evaluate them. Outline scope: definition of workforce planning; internal and external factors; internal vs external recruitment; the stages from vacancy to contract; ethical and EDI-based recruitment (nine grounds, reasonable accommodation, blind shortlisting); digital recruitment evaluated (reach, speed, cost vs bias, privacy, exclusion). Glossary term "human capital" is used in the next subsection, not here. Not Higher Level only.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Employees (the workforce) are one of a firm's resources; people are recruited to carry out the firm's plans, so recruitment follows from the business plan and objectives.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Discrimination means treating a person less favourably on a protected ground; Irish equality law protects employees and job applicants (the full employment-rights law sits in the neighbouring chapter on employee rights).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) A contract of employment is a legally binding agreement between employer and employee, setting out the main terms of the job.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Personal data (names, CVs, photos) is regulated by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Workforce planning [3.2.1]
- [ ] W1 Definition: making sure the business has the right number of people, with the right skills, in the right roles at the right time.
- [ ] W2 Purpose: match the supply of labour to need so shortages or surpluses are spotted early, not in a crisis.
- [ ] W3 Internal factors: planned growth or contraction, staff turnover, retirements, the skills and qualifications the workforce already has.
- [ ] W4 External factors: the labour market, employment legislation, technological change, the wider economy.
- [ ] W5 Applying it: a firm expecting fast growth in an area with few skilled workers may train existing staff or recruit earlier.

Recruitment routes
- [ ] R1 Internal recruitment (promotion or transfer): quicker, cheaper, rewards loyal staff.
- [ ] R2 Internal limits: fewer fresh ideas; the move leaves a gap to fill (a chain of vacancies).
- [ ] R3 External recruitment: new skills, experience and perspectives; wider candidate pool.
- [ ] R4 External limits: more expensive and slower; longer induction before staff are fully productive.

Recruitment and selection stages [3.2.2]
- [ ] S1 Identifying the vacancy: confirm the role is needed, as a replacement or a new post in the workforce plan.
- [ ] S2 Job description: purpose, duties, responsibilities, reporting lines.
- [ ] S3 Person specification: qualifications, skills, experience, personal attributes.
- [ ] S4 Advertising and application: advert attracts candidates; application forms, CVs, cover letters.
- [ ] S5 Shortlisting against the person specification.
- [ ] S6 Interviews and tests (selection).
- [ ] S7 References checked before an offer.
- [ ] S8 Job offer, accepted or declined.
- [ ] S9 Written statement of main terms and contract of employment issued after acceptance.
- [ ] S10 The three phases the specification names: identification, application and selection, and which stages sit in each.

Ethics, equality, diversity and inclusion [3.2.3]
- [ ] E1 The nine grounds under the Employment Equality Acts (list in section 2).
- [ ] E2 Adverts and job descriptions must not state or imply a preference for an age, gender or background unless the law allows it.
- [ ] E3 Interview questions must relate to the job (not family plans, religion, health unless they bear on the role).
- [ ] E4 Reasonable accommodation: adjust the process (accessible room, extra time) so a candidate with a disability competes on equal terms.
- [ ] E5 Blind shortlisting: remove names, photos, ages, addresses so decisions rest on skills and experience.
- [ ] E6 Business benefit of inclusion: wider range of ideas and a larger talent pool.
- [ ] E7 Ethical angle: fair, honest, transparent treatment of candidates, including how their data is used.

Digital recruitment (benefits) [3.2.3]
- [ ] D1 Online job boards and LinkedIn: large, geographically wide reach, quickly, at relatively low cost.
- [ ] D2 Applicant tracking systems: store applications in one place, filter by keywords, send automated updates.
- [ ] D3 AI screening: ranks applications against the person specification, saves time with hundreds of applicants.
- [ ] D4 Video interviews: no travel cost; recorded answers can be reviewed later.
- [ ] D5 Gamified tests and online assessments: measure problem-solving and aptitude, scored automatically.

Digital recruitment (drawbacks) and evaluation
- [ ] V1 Algorithmic bias: AI trained on past hiring data can repeat historic preferences and disadvantage women, older applicants or people with unusual career paths.
- [ ] V2 Keyword filtering can wrongly reject strong candidates whose CVs use different wording.
- [ ] V3 Privacy: candidate data must follow GDPR (transparency, limits on use).
- [ ] V4 Exclusion: no reliable internet, digital skills or assistive technology means disadvantage.
- [ ] V5 Candidate experience: automated rejections and one-way video interviews feel impersonal.
- [ ] V6 Evaluation judgement: strongest where reach, speed and cost matter; best with human judgement at key decision points.

Applied sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] X1 Sustainability/social: inclusive recruitment widens the talent pool and supports fair work; video interviews cut candidate and employer travel (fewer journeys).
- [ ] X2 Digital: the whole of D and V above (digital developments are the digital angle).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any recruitment cost, time-to-hire, application-volume or AI-accuracy percentage, because none is checked and they change.
- Do not use: "the 1998 Act" alone as the source of the nine grounds, because the grounds come from the Employment Equality Acts 1998 to 2015 taken together; write "the Employment Equality Acts".
- Do not use: "gender and civil status" as one ground, because they are two separate grounds; "family status" is the ground, not "family".
- Do not use: "AI screening is illegal" or "AI screening is banned", because the AI Act classifies it as high-risk and regulates it, it does not ban it.
- Do not use: ratios, contract law, or the old syllabus recruitment calculations, because they are old-syllabus (Appendix A4).
- Confusable: job description (what the job is) versus person specification (who is needed); recruitment (attracting candidates) versus selection (choosing among them); shortlisting (narrowing applications) versus interviewing.
- Confusable: equality (equal treatment and opportunity) versus diversity (a varied workforce) versus inclusion (everyone able to take part); reasonable accommodation versus positive discrimination.
- Boundary: induction, training and onboarding after the offer belong to the subsection on human capital management; motivation and appraisal belong to the subsection on motivation; the detailed rights of employees under employment law belong to the chapter on employee rights.
- Boundary: only mention induction here in the external-recruitment point (longer induction needed), not its content.

## 4. What exams ask

- Sample paper 1, Question 4(c) (2025) asks students to evaluate how digital technologies influence recruitment and selection. "Evaluate" (Appendix 1) means making judgements about ideas, solutions or methods using evidence and identifying limitations. A full answer gives at least two benefits (reach, speed, cost, ATS filtering, video interviews) each linked to the business effect, at least two drawbacks (algorithmic bias, privacy under GDPR, digital exclusion, impersonal candidate experience), and a closing judgement (for example effective for high-volume hiring if human judgement stays at key steps). Common mistakes: listing tools without saying what they do for the firm, giving only benefits, and no conclusion.
- Likely command verbs for this subsection: identify and outline (name or give the main points of the stages of recruitment), explain (reasons: why internal recruitment is cheaper), discuss (balanced internal vs external). For "outline the stages" give the stages in order, each with its purpose in a few words. For "explain" give the cause, then the effect on the business.
- EDI answers should name the nine grounds accurately and give one concrete action (blind shortlisting, reasonable accommodation) with its effect on fairness and on the talent pool. Common mistake: general statements such as "treat everyone equally" with no action.
- Workforce-planning answers must separate internal factors (from inside the firm) from external ones (labour market, legislation, technology, economy); a common mistake is mixing them or listing only one side.
- Tip basis: answers that link each point to the firm in the case study score better than lists; apply the point to the scenario's size, sector or location.
