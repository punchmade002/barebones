# Package: ch6-s2 — The role of government in supporting enterprise

- **loId:** `ch6-s2` · **chapterId:** `ch6`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 2, sections 2.1 and 2.8, learning outcomes [2.1.3] and the news-analysis part of [2.8] (applying my learning). Students learn about the role of the Irish government in supporting business and enterprise in Ireland, both locally and nationally, and investigate how government fosters enterprise and supports business development and growth. They also analyse current Irish news on enterprise and give an informed opinion. No glossary term is named for this subsection. No Higher Level only content.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Why government supports enterprise
- [ ] W1 Market failure: private finance is not available for good ideas that are too risky or too small for banks (keyTerm Market failure).
- [ ] W2 Support helps firms grow into exporters, bringing foreign earnings into the economy.
- [ ] W3 Support outside Dublin and the other large cities spreads jobs across regions.
- [ ] W4 Support encourages firms to adopt sustainable and digital practices (ethical, sustainability and digital angle).
- [ ] W5 (assumed) Three barriers support addresses: finance, skills and training, access to markets (factCard).

Agencies and schemes
- [ ] A1 Local Enterprise Offices: first point of contact for micro and small firms, start-ups and growing firms; located in each local authority area (keyTerm).
- [ ] A2 LEO services: grants for start-up and expansion costs, mentoring, training, advice on business planning, financial management and marketing (factCard).
- [ ] A3 Trading Online Voucher scheme: helps small firms build an online presence and sell online (digital angle).
- [ ] A4 Enterprise Ireland: Irish-owned firms, high-potential start-ups, exporters; market research, export marketing, introductions to overseas buyers (keyTerm).
- [ ] A5 IDA Ireland: attracts foreign direct investment (keyTerm FDI: money invested by a foreign company to set up or expand operations); jobs and skills; Irish suppliers sell to the foreign firms.
- [ ] A6 Enterprise Ireland compared with IDA Ireland (factCard).
- [ ] A7 MicroFinance Ireland: small loans, usually with mentoring, to people who cannot get a bank loan for their business (keyTerm).
- [ ] A8 SBCI: funds loan schemes that partner banks offer to small and medium-sized firms, lowering the cost of borrowing or the banks' risk (keyTerm).
- [ ] A9 Údarás na Gaeltachta: enterprise support in Irish-speaking areas through grants and advice.
- [ ] A10 Skillnet Ireland: funds business networks that provide training to groups of firms.
- [ ] A11 Education and Training Boards (ETBs): courses in business, technology and craft skills.
- [ ] A12 (assumed) Local authority and national level: the LEO is the local, the agencies and departments the national, level. The syllabus asks for both.

Tax measures
- [ ] T1 Low headline corporation tax rate attracts profitable and foreign firms.
- [ ] T2 R&D tax credit: reduces tax for firms spending on research and development.
- [ ] T3 Start-up relief: eases the early years (checked form: SURE, below).
- [ ] T4 Employment and Investment Incentive Scheme (EIIS): income tax relief to investors who buy shares in qualifying small firms (keyTerm).
- [ ] T5 Tax measures cost the state revenue; weigh against the activity encouraged.

Infrastructure, regulation and policy
- [ ] F1 Broadband and transport infrastructure for rural firms to sell online and reach customers.
- [ ] F2 Regional and rural supports (grants, place-based programmes) (factCard).
- [ ] F3 Regulation: standards (food safety, employment rights) that protect consumers and workers.
- [ ] F4 Clear rules protect fair competition.
- [ ] F5 Reducing red tape lowers the cost of compliance for small firms.
- [ ] F6 National enterprise strategy sets priorities over several years (White Paper on Enterprise).

Benefits and limits (evaluation)
- [ ] B1 Benefits: finance, advice, training, export contacts a small firm could not easily find alone.
- [ ] B2 Limits: dependence on grants.
- [ ] B3 Limits: support may favour firms good at applying, not those with the best ideas.
- [ ] B4 Limits: funded by taxes, so competes with other public spending (health, education).
- [ ] B5 Evaluation: works best where it addresses a real gap, not where it subsidises firms that would succeed anyway.
- [ ] B6 Ethical angle: public money needs accountability and fairness of access.

Applying my learning [2.8]
- [ ] N1 One or two current Irish news examples linked to government support for enterprise, with month, year and source (section 2), and how to analyse them: which agency, which barrier, who benefits, a limit, an informed opinion.

## 2. Checked facts and examples (the only names, dates and figures the notes may use)

| Item | Checked facts |
|---|---|
| Local Enterprise Offices | The "first-stop-shop" for advice, guidance and financial assistance for those starting or growing a business; 31 LEOs nationwide (localenterprise.ie). Offer the Start Your Own Business programme, mentoring and training. Say each LEO serves a local authority area. |
| Trading Online Voucher scheme | Co-funds a small firm's online trading project: third-party costs only, approval before any spending, for businesses with a limited or no e-commerce presence; brochure websites not funded (localenterprise.ie). The terms differ between LEO pages and change: do not give a grant figure, trading period or employee limit. |
| MicroFinance Ireland (MFI) | State-supported lender of small loans to micro-enterprises (fewer than 10 employees, turnover up to €2m) that cannot get finance from commercial lenders; applying through a LEO adds business advice and mentoring (localenterprise.ie). The Department of Enterprise raised its lending limit in 2024 (gov.ie); do not give loan amounts or interest rates, because LEO pages differ. |
| Strategic Banking Corporation of Ireland (SBCI) | State-owned; it does not lend to firms directly but through partner banks and non-bank lenders ("on-lenders"), which must pass on the benefit of its lower-cost funding. It also runs risk-sharing guarantee schemes, and the Future Growth Loan Scheme for long-term strategic investment (sbci.gov.ie, Oireachtas). Lending began in March 2015. Do not give scheme amounts. |
| Enterprise Ireland | The Irish government's trade and innovation agency; supports Irish-owned firms to start, compete, scale and connect globally; High Potential Start-Up (HPSU) and Pre-Seed Start Fund programmes; more than 40 international offices; works with the LEOs to bring on first-time exporters (enterprise-ireland.com). 2025 results: €32.9 million invested in 198 start-ups, 90 of them HPSUs (Enterprise Ireland, early May 2026). |
| IDA Ireland | The government's inward investment promotion agency; a non-commercial body that wins and keeps foreign direct investment, partnering with client companies and giving grant assistance. Founded 1949; since 1994 focused exclusively on FDI. Strategy 2025-2029 is titled "Adapt Intelligently" and targets digitalisation and AI, semiconductors, health and sustainability, and locations outside Dublin (idaireland.com, enterprise.gov.ie). Client counts and employment figures differ between sources: give none. |
| Údarás na Gaeltachta | Overall aim: that Irish remains the main community language of the Gaeltacht, pursued by funding enterprise development and job creation as well as language, cultural and community activity (udaras.ie). Helps its client companies access Enterprise Ireland programmes under a memorandum of understanding. Give no job or grant figures. |
| Skillnet Ireland | Government-backed; funded through the National Training Fund; funds business networks, groups of firms in a sector or region, that deliver subsidised training, part-funded by the participating firms (skillnetireland.ie). Number of networks varies by source: give none. |
| Education and Training Boards (ETBs) | No checked example. Do not name one beyond the general statement that ETBs provide further education and training courses. |
| Corporation tax | Standard rate 12.5%, in place since 2003; an effective 15% rate applies only to in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenues of €750 million or more (gov.ie, revenue.ie; as of 2026). Over 99% of companies in Ireland are outside the 15% rule (gov.ie). |
| R&D tax credit | A corporation tax credit for qualifying research and development spending (revenue.ie). The credit rate has risen more than once recently (Revenue eBrief 085/26, 23 April 2026, notes a rise under Finance Act 2025): give no percentage. |
| Start-up relief | Start-Up Relief for Entrepreneurs (SURE): refunds income tax paid in earlier years to a person who leaves mainly PAYE employment, sets up a new company carrying on a new qualifying trade, works full-time in it and invests cash in new shares held at least four years (revenue.ie). Availability beyond 2026 is not stated on the Revenue page: describe as "currently on Revenue's list of reliefs". |

Do not give investment limits or relief percentages. |

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Údarás na Gaeltachta ... property for firms that locate there" as a stated fact, because the check confirmed grants, advice and enterprise development only.
- Do not use: "Local Enterprise Offices ... based within local authorities" as a precise legal claim; say each LEO serves a local authority area.
- Do not use: "Start-up tax reliefs reduce tax for new firms in their early years" without a name, because the checked relief, SURE, benefits the founder-investor through an income tax refund, not the firm. If the notes mention a relief for new companies themselves, do not name one.
- Do not use: "The research and development tax credit reduces tax ... a loss-making start-up gets little from tax reliefs" (the old model answer), because the R&D credit can be claimed by loss-making firms in instalments; the statement is true only of reliefs that depend on tax paid or on profit. Avoid the claim for R&D.
- Do not use: any grant or loan amount, tax rate other than the 12.5% above, or percentage, because they change.
- Do not use: "SBCI ... reduces the risk for banks" for every SBCI scheme; its on-lending lowers the cost of funding passed to borrowers, and its guarantee schemes share risk. Both are fine if stated that way.
- Do not use: "IDA Ireland ... Irish suppliers benefit" as a figure-backed claim; keep it as a reasoned effect.
- Confusable: Enterprise Ireland (Irish-owned firms, exporters) against IDA Ireland (foreign firms setting up in Ireland) against a Local Enterprise Office (micro and small firms, local).
- Confusable: MicroFinance Ireland (a lender) against a LEO grant (money that does not have to be repaid) against SBCI (funds on-lenders, no direct lending).
- Confusable: foreign direct investment (a foreign firm sets up here) against exports (Irish firms sell abroad).
- Confusable: a tax credit (reduces the tax bill) against a tax relief or exemption.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the definitions of enterprise, innovation, entrepreneur and intrapreneur, the reasons for starting an enterprise and the competencies of innovators. Also the sources of finance (overdraft, loans, venture capital, equity) in the finance chapter: refer, do not repeat. Consumer, employment and competition law belong to the chapters on legislation; here regulation is only a one-line support-and-protection point.
- Old syllabus only: do not teach the old Unit 1 "community development" or state-agency lists beyond those above (Appendix A4).

## 4. What exams ask

- The 2025 Higher Level Sample 2 paper asks students to discuss three ways the Irish government can support a growing Irish firm. Verb: discuss, "a considered, balanced review ... supported by appropriate evidence" (Appendix 1). A full answer gives three distinct supports (for example finance through a state-backed scheme, advice and export help from Enterprise Ireland, training through Skillnet), each developed with what it does and its effect on the firm, and ideally a limit or condition. Common mistakes: listing agency names with no effect; giving three supports that are all grants; ignoring the word "growing" (export, scaling and financing matter more than start-up basics).
- Likely question shapes (no frequencies known): identify two agencies; outline how a LEO supports a start-up; explain how a named scheme helps a rural firm; discuss supports for an exporter; evaluate whether support is worth its cost to the state.
- Evaluate: "describe how evidence supports or does not support a judgement ... make judgments" (Appendix 1). Give benefits, limits (dependence, favouring good applicants, cost to taxpayers) and a reasoned conclusion about when support works.
- Match the support to the firm's barrier: a first-time exporter needs market advice and finance; a start-up needs mentoring and a small loan; a rural firm needs infrastructure and regional help.
- Use the stimulus: the exam gives a real, current Irish firm or story. Apply the support to that firm; do not recite a list of agencies.
- Ethical, sustainability and digital angle: one line each on public money and fairness, on supports for climate and digital readiness, and on online trading.
- Applying my learning: analyse a news item by naming the agency, the barrier it addresses, who gains and one limit, then give a short justified opinion.
