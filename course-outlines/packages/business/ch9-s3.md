# Package: ch9-s3 — Developing, evaluating and improving a marketing mix; the USP

- **loId:** `ch9-s3` · **chapterId:** `ch9`
- **Syllabus:** NCCA Leaving Certificate Business specification (first examined June 2027), Strand 2, section 2.4, learning outcomes [2.4.3] and [2.4.4]. Students identify the unique selling point and develop and modify the marketing mix; develop a marketing mix for a product and for a service of choice, with a USP analysis for each; evaluate and suggest ways of improving an existing marketing mix. Outline scope: what a USP is and how to analyse one (what makes it unique, why customers value it, whether it can be copied); a fully developed 7-P mix for one product and one service, each with a USP analysis; criteria for evaluating a mix (fit with the target market, consistency between the Ps, competitiveness, profitability, ethics and sustainability); typical improvements; how a mix is modified over time. No Higher Level only content.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The writer works only from this package and `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The seven Ps and what each decision involves, and how the target market shapes them (one-line recall; detail is in the notes on the marketing mix).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Competitors and competitive advantage: a feature that lets a business win customers from rivals.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Product life cycle: introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Market research methods can test whether customers value a feature.

The unique selling point
- [ ] U1 A USP is the feature or benefit that sets a product or service apart from competitors and gives customers a reason to choose it.
- [ ] U2 A USP must be something customers value; a feature no one wants is not a USP. It is tied to the target market.
- [ ] U3 A strong USP is distinctive, relevant to the target market and difficult for competitors to imitate quickly.
- [ ] U4 A USP is not a slogan: the slogan communicates the USP, but the USP must be grounded in what the offer actually delivers.
- [ ] U5 Sources of a USP: price, quality, convenience, speed, origin, design, service, sustainability, branding.

USP analysis (method)
- [ ] UA1 Uniqueness: what exactly is different; compare with the main competitors' offers.
- [ ] UA2 Customer value: does the difference matter to the target market; test it in research.
- [ ] UA3 Defensibility: can competitors copy it quickly; barriers such as patents or brand loyalty.
- [ ] UA4 Conclusion: a USP that is unique and valued but easily copied needs supporting advantages (service, brand reputation).
- [ ] UA5 A USP claim is only as strong as its evidence; an unsupported claim becomes a liability (links to honest marketing).

Developing a mix: product (worked)
- [ ] DP1 One fully developed 7-P mix for a product of choice: product, pricing (with named strategy and reason), promotion, people, packaging, process, place; every P tied to the target market and consistent with the others.
- [ ] DP2 A USP analysis for that product using the three tests (UA1–UA3) with a conclusion.

Developing a mix: service (worked)
- [ ] DS1 One fully developed 7-P mix for a service of choice, same layout; packaging for a service means presentation (branded vehicle, printed job card, finish); process matters especially (booking, delivery, payment).
- [ ] DS2 A USP analysis for the service, including a suggested extension.
- [ ] DS3 Students choose their own product and service in the exam; the notes teach the method with the two samples and say that other choices follow the same steps.

Evaluating a mix
- [ ] EV1 Fit with the target market: each P matches the needs, budget and media habits of the target customers.
- [ ] EV2 Consistency between the Ps: a premium price with heavy discounting sends mixed signals.
- [ ] EV3 Competitiveness: gives customers a reason to choose the business over rivals, built on the USP.
- [ ] EV4 Profitability: revenue must exceed the costs of the mix, including promotion, packaging and delivery.
- [ ] EV5 Ethics and sustainability: no misleading claims, no wasteful packaging; these damage trust over time.
- [ ] EV6 How to evaluate (Appendix 1 "Evaluate"): judge each criterion with evidence, state strengths and weaknesses, reach a justified overall judgement.

Improving an existing mix
- [ ] IM1 Pricing: bundles or an introductory offer when sales are weak against cheaper rivals.
- [ ] IM2 Promotion: move spending to the channels the target market uses and measure results.
- [ ] IM3 Process: replace slow or manual steps (such as phone-only booking) with online ordering.
- [ ] IM4 Packaging: refresh a dated or wasteful design with lighter or more recyclable materials.
- [ ] IM5 People: training and clearer service standards where customers report poor experiences.
- [ ] IM6 Match the improvement to the weakest element or the mismatch the evaluation reveals.

Modifying a mix over time
- [ ] MO1 Introduction: awareness, possible penetration pricing, heavy promotion.
- [ ] MO2 Growth: widen distribution, build the brand (assumed link to the life cycle).
- [ ] MO3 Maturity: loyalty, price competition, cost control.
- [ ] MO4 Decline: cut spending and drop weak channels, or reposition for a new group.
- [ ] MO5 Competitor moves, new technology and changing costs can force changes to place and process at any stage.

Recommending a launch strategy (links to the sample paper)
- [ ] L1 Identify a firm's competitive advantage from a stimulus and recommend a launch strategy: name the advantage, then recommend a pricing, promotion and place choice that uses it, with a reason.

Ethical, sustainability and digital angle
- [ ] E1 Ethical: a USP must be true (for example origin or ingredients claims); misleading claims harm trust and may breach consumer law.
- [ ] E2 Sustainability: recyclable packaging, local sourcing and low-impact delivery can be both a USP and a cost; evaluate them honestly.
- [ ] E3 Digital: online booking, social media promotion and online sales are common improvements; digital tools also make a USP easier to copy.

## 3. Traps

Do not use:
- Do not use: the names "Driftwood Oats" and "Spokes at Your Door" from the existing notes, because they are invented names that could match real firms and have not been checked. Use "a sample porridge-pot business" and "a sample mobile bike-repair service in Galway", and label them sample businesses.
- Do not use: the existing wording "for a service, packaging means presentation" as if it were a specification statement. Packaging is one of the 7 Ps; for a service, teach it as the presentation of the service (vehicle, uniform, printed documents, finish) and label that as the notes' application.
- Do not use: "a USP is always unique forever" or "a USP can never be copied". A USP is unique relative to competitors at a point in time, and many can be copied; that is why defensibility is analysed.
- Do not use: "physical evidence" as an extra P in either sample mix.
- Do not use: the claim that "some shops now offer collection" as a market fact. It is an illustrative judgement for the sample only; say "a competitor might offer collection".
- Do not use: any named leadership or brand from another chapter.

Confusable terms
- USP (a distinctive benefit customers value) vs competitive advantage (any edge over rivals; a USP is one source) vs slogan (how the USP is communicated).
- USP analysis (testing one advantage) vs evaluating the mix (judging all seven Ps).
- Evaluating a mix (judging it) vs improving it (changing it) vs modifying it (changing it over time as life cycle and market change).
- Product mix for a physical good vs a service mix: the seven Ps are the same, but people, process and packaging weigh more heavily for a service.

## 4. What exams ask

(Source: exam-style task, paper and question number not verified; Appendix 1 verbs. No marks or frequencies are invented.)
- Identify a firm's competitive advantage (identify = state briefly a distinguishing feature): a full answer names the advantage from the stimulus and links it to the customer. Common mistake: listing features the stimulus does not show.
- Recommend a launch strategy (a justified proposal): a full answer chooses specific marketing-mix decisions (price, promotion, place) that use the advantage and gives a reason tied to the target market. Common mistake: a general list of the Ps with no link to the advantage.
- Develop a marketing mix for a product or service (develop = expand in detail): a full answer covers all seven Ps, ties each to the target market and keeps them consistent. Common mistake: missing people, packaging or process, or including physical evidence.
- Analyse the USP (study in detail, bring out the essential elements): a full answer tests uniqueness, customer value and defensibility and reaches a conclusion.
- Evaluate a marketing mix (make judgements with evidence, with the limits of the evidence): a full answer applies at least three criteria (fit, consistency, competitiveness, profitability, ethics), gives strength and weakness for each and ends with an overall judgement. Common mistake: describing the mix without judging it.
- Suggest improvements (propose solutions): a full answer names the weakest P, a specific change and its expected effect. Common mistake: a vague "improve advertising".
- Common mistakes: confusing a slogan with a USP, ignoring defensibility, and naming real firms with invented facts.
