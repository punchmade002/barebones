## 1. Must cover

Structure of the hen's egg (one block, with diagram)
- [ ] S1 Shell: hard, porous, made mainly of calcium carbonate; protects the contents and lets gases pass in and out through tiny pores; the outer coating (cuticle, "bloom") reduces entry of bacteria. Shell colour (white or brown) depends on the breed of hen and does not affect nutritive value.
- [ ] S2 Shell membranes: two thin membranes lining the shell, outer and inner; they help keep bacteria out.
- [ ] S3 Air cell (air space): a pocket between the two membranes at the blunt (broad) end; it grows as the egg ages as water evaporates through the shell, so a bigger air cell means an older egg (basis of the water test: a fresh egg sinks and lies flat; an old egg floats).
- [ ] S4 Albumen (white): the transparent liquid around the yolk, mainly water (about 90%) and protein (albumin, ovalbumin, globulin); has two layers, thick and thin; fat free. The thick white is the sign of a fresh egg.
- [ ] S5 Chalazae: two twisted cords of albumen at each end of the yolk that anchor the yolk in the centre of the egg.
- [ ] S6 Yolk: the yellow centre, held in the vitelline membrane; it holds the fat, lecithin, cholesterol, protein (vitellin and livetin), the fat-soluble vitamins and the iron; colour depends on the hen's feed (carotenoid pigments).
- [ ] S7 Germinal disc (blastodisc): a small pale spot on the surface of the yolk; it would develop into a chick if the egg were fertilised. Eggs sold in shops are normally unfertilised.
- [ ] S8 Diagram: a labelled cross-section: shell, outer and inner shell membranes, air cell, thick and thin albumen, chalazae, vitelline membrane, yolk, germinal disc. The diagram is asked often enough to be drawn from memory.

Nutritive value (one block; the usual "nutritive value of eggs" answer)
- [ ] N1 Protein: about 13%, HBV with a biological value of 100% (the reference protein; contains all the essential amino acids in the proportions the body needs). White protein: albumin (ovalbumin) and globulin. Yolk protein: vitellin and livetin. Roles: growth and repair, making hormones and enzymes.
- [ ] N2 Fat: about 12%, all in the yolk; mostly saturated, in a very fine emulsion because of lecithin, so easily digested; the yolk contains cholesterol; the white is fat free. Gives heat and energy.
- [ ] N3 Carbohydrate: none (0%); fibre: none. Serve with carbohydrate foods (toast, potatoes) and with fibre.
- [ ] N4 Vitamins: A (healthy membranes and eyes), D (bones and teeth), E (antioxidant) and K (blood clotting) in the yolk; B group (B1, B2 for energy release; niacin and B12 for metabolism) in white and yolk; amounts of some vitamins higher in free-range eggs (2015H scheme); no vitamin C.
- [ ] N5 Minerals: about 1%: calcium and phosphorus (bones and teeth), iron in the yolk (making haemoglobin), zinc and sulphur.
- [ ] N6 Water: about 75% of the egg (the white is mostly water).
- [ ] N7 Lecithin: a natural emulsifier in the yolk (properties in the next subsection).
- [ ] N8 Energy: the yolk supplies most of the energy; do not give kilocalorie figures.

Contribution to the diet (dietetic value; one block)
- [ ] D1 Cheap and nutritious, so suitable for people on a low budget; a protein alternative to meat and fish.
- [ ] D2 HBV protein for vegetarians who eat eggs and dairy (ovo-lacto vegetarians).
- [ ] D3 Easily digested (fat already emulsified, protein soft when lightly cooked), so suitable for children, older people, invalids and convalescents; growth and repair for children, adolescents and pregnant women.
- [ ] D4 Quick and easy to cook (saves fuel), versatile in sweet and savoury dishes, can be eaten alone; a meal when served with carbohydrate (egg on toast, omelette with potatoes).
- [ ] D5 Limits: saturated fat and cholesterol, so those with raised cholesterol or heart disease should eat eggs in moderation; lacks vitamin C and fibre, so serve with vegetables, fruit and wholegrain foods.
- [ ] D6 Safety links: eggs can carry Salmonella, so cook thoroughly for babies, older people, pregnant women and the ill; wash hands and utensils after handling raw egg; selection and storage are in the next subsection.
- [ ] D7 Menu use: eggs as the protein in one meal of a day's menu (for example scrambled egg on wholemeal toast, or quiche with salad), balanced with fibre and vitamin C foods.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "lacto-vegetarians", although a past scheme uses it, because lacto-vegetarians eat dairy but not eggs; eggs suit ovo-lacto vegetarians (who eat dairy products and eggs).
- Do not use: calorie, cholesterol or vitamin amounts for an egg or a daily egg limit, because they are changing guidance figures and are not in the schemes; say "moderate intake for those limiting cholesterol".
- Do not use: "eggs are low in calories" as a headline fact, because an egg is moderate in energy (the fat in the yolk); say "moderate energy" and "easily cooked without added fat". (The 2016O scheme says low in kilocalories for calorie-controlled diets; use it only for a boiled or poached egg.)
- The white is fat free and holds most of the water; cholesterol, fat, fat-soluble vitamins and iron are all in the yolk.
- Biological value of 100% is a reference figure for egg protein; do not say eggs are "100% protein".
- The air cell is at the blunt end, not the pointed end; the chalazae are not the germinal disc.
- The germinal disc is not a "blood spot" or an embryo in a shop egg.
- Shell colour is not a measure of quality or nutrition; brown versus white depends on breed.
- Eggs are not a source of vitamin C or of fibre or carbohydrate; say so in "nutrients missing".
- Quality, selection, labelling and storage, and the properties (coagulation, aeration, emulsification), belong to the next subsection on egg selection, storage and properties; the cooking of eggs belongs there.

## 4. What exams ask

- "Nutritive value and dietetic value of eggs" (2025O, 2021O, 2016O, 2011O; 20 marks): four to six points, each with a function. HBV protein (growth and repair; white albumin and globulin, yolk vitellin and livetin; BV 100%); fat in the yolk, saturated, emulsified by lecithin, with cholesterol; no carbohydrate; vitamins A, D, E, K and B group, no C; calcium, phosphorus, iron, zinc; 75% water. Dietetic: cheap, versatile, easily digested, quick to cook, a meal with toast, useful for vegetarians who eat eggs, caution about saturated fat and cholesterol, balance with fibre and vitamin C foods.
- Higher Level 20-mark "nutritional significance of eggs" (2021H): nutrient by nutrient with a function for each, including the percentage figures and the advice to combine with complex carbohydrate.
- A "study" format (2015H, 40 marks): nutritional significance, contribution to the diet, and properties with culinary uses, six points at 4 marks.
- Section A: true or false on eggs (saturated fat present, HBV protein, not a source of vitamin C; 2026O).
- Structure: draw and label the egg (shell, membranes, air cell, albumen, chalazae, yolk, vitelline membrane, germinal disc), and say what the chalazae and the air cell do.
- A day's menu with eggs in one meal (2025O), balanced with fibre and vitamin C foods.
