# Package: hom-carbohydrates-s1 — Formation of carbohydrates in plants

- **loId:** `hom-carbohydrates-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-carbohydrates` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.3
- **Syllabus scope:** formation of carbohydrates in plants: photosynthesis. No Settings. No "Also examined" items for this subsection (no past question has asked it directly). No [HL] items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23, from the outline, the current notes and the past-paper extracts. Ordinary and Higher Level. This is a short, food-focused subsection: the student needs to be able to state what photosynthesis is, give the equation, say where the raw materials come from and say what the plant does with the glucose it makes, so that the later subsections (classification, sources, functions) have a starting point. The two stages of photosynthesis (light-dependent and light-independent), electron carriers and the chloroplast's internal membranes are not on this syllabus; do not teach them. The classification of the carbohydrates the plant makes is in the composition, structure and classification subsection; where they appear in food is in the sources subsection.

## 1. Must cover

What photosynthesis is (one block)
- [ ] P1 Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make carbohydrate (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water, using light energy absorbed by chlorophyll, and release oxygen as a by-product. Plants are producers (they make their own food); animals, including people, are consumers that get their carbohydrate by eating plants or animals that ate plants.
- [ ] P2 Word equation: carbon dioxide + water, in the presence of light and chlorophyll, gives glucose + oxygen.
- [ ] P3 Balanced equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light energy and chlorophyll written above the arrow). Check the balance: 6 C, 12 H and 18 O on each side.
- [ ] P4 Energy conversion: light energy is changed into chemical energy stored in the glucose molecule. This stored energy is what a person later releases from carbohydrate in respiration (the energy value of carbohydrate is in the energy value subsection).
- [ ] P5 Four requirements: light energy, chlorophyll, carbon dioxide and water. Two products: glucose and oxygen.
- [ ] P6 Chlorophyll is the green pigment in the chloroplasts of leaf cells. It absorbs light energy (mainly red and blue light; it reflects green, which is why leaves look green). The chloroplast is the organelle where photosynthesis takes place.

Raw materials and the by-product (one block)
- [ ] R1 Carbon dioxide enters the leaf from the air by diffusion through stomata, small pores mainly on the lower surface of the leaf.
- [ ] R2 Water is absorbed from the soil by root hair cells and carried up the stem to the leaves in the xylem.
- [ ] R3 Light comes from the sun.
- [ ] R4 Oxygen, the by-product, leaves the leaf through the stomata (some is used by the plant in its own respiration).
- [ ] R5 (assumed) Respiration goes on in plant cells all the time, day and night, whereas photosynthesis needs light.

What the plant does with its glucose (one block)
- [ ] G1 Respiration: some glucose is used immediately to release energy for growth and the plant's other processes.
- [ ] G2 Starch: surplus glucose is joined into starch, an insoluble polysaccharide kept as a store in leaves, stems, roots and tubers such as potatoes. Starch is insoluble, so it can be stored in large amounts without affecting the water balance of the cell.
- [ ] G3 Sucrose: glucose is converted to sucrose, which is carried in the phloem from the leaves to the other parts of the plant (growing tips, roots, fruits, storage organs).
- [ ] G4 Cellulose: glucose is joined into cellulose, the main structural material of plant cell walls.
- [ ] G5 (assumed) Plants also make fruit sugars (glucose and fructose) and, with nitrogen from the soil, proteins and, from glucose, oils and other lipids. One phrase only; the nutrients are taught in their own chapters.
- [ ] G6 Pectin is also made in the cell walls of plants (see the sources subsection for where it is found in fruit); give it one phrase here.

Photosynthesis and human nutrition (one block)
- [ ] N1 Every sugar, starch and fibre in the diet was first made by a plant in photosynthesis; plant foods supply carbohydrate directly and animal foods supply it indirectly (animal feed is plant material). Lactose in milk is made by the animal from glucose; it is not made by plants.
- [ ] N2 Photosynthesis also releases the oxygen that people use in aerobic respiration.
- [ ] N3 Diagram (optional, as in the current notes): a leaf cross-section labelled with stomata, palisade cells containing chloroplasts, xylem and phloem, with arrows for carbon dioxide in, oxygen out, water in through the xylem and sucrose out through the phloem. Keep it simple; no leaf anatomy beyond these labels.

## 3. Traps

- Plants respire all the time and photosynthesise only in light; do not say plants "breathe in carbon dioxide at day and oxygen at night" or that photosynthesis is the opposite of breathing in animals.
- The oxygen is a by-product, not a raw material; carbon dioxide and water are the raw materials.
- Chlorophyll reflects green light; it does not absorb it.
- Glucose is not the form in which a plant stores or moves carbohydrate: it is stored as starch and transported as sucrose (a sugar of two units). Do not say glucose travels in the phloem.
- Do not describe the light-dependent and light-independent stages, photolysis, ATP, NADPH or the Calvin cycle; they are not on this syllabus. A single phrase such as "driven by light energy" is enough.
- Starch, sucrose and cellulose are defined and classified in the composition, structure and classification subsection; this subsection only says the plant makes them from glucose. Do not give formulas or structures here other than the equation.
- Where each carbohydrate is found in the diet belongs to the sources subsection; the dietary role of fibre belongs to the functions subsection. Mention each in a word only.
- How the body uses carbohydrate (digestion, absorption and glucose in the blood) belongs to the digestion and metabolism chapter.
- Testing a leaf for starch with iodine is not on the syllabus; do not add it.
- Do not add a "how plants make fat and protein" section. The plant's other products get one phrase (G5).

## 4. What exams ask

- No Leaving Certificate paper in the extract has asked about photosynthesis on its own. The topic is the starting point for carbohydrates: it supports the answer to "where do carbohydrates come from" and the sources, classification and fibre questions that follow.
- Expected knowledge if asked: the word or balanced equation; the four requirements; that sunlight energy is converted to chemical energy in glucose; that plants store the carbohydrate as starch, transport it as sucrose and build cell walls from cellulose; that fibre (cellulose) in the diet is plant cell-wall material.
- A student may be asked for the three classes of carbohydrate with an example and food source each; the opening of that answer is that plants make glucose by photosynthesis and join it into larger carbohydrates. Make sure the notes make that link clearly in a sentence.
- Exam tip material: a short answer should give the equation and name the three uses of glucose (respiration, starch for storage, cellulose for cell walls) and the transport form (sucrose); give the raw materials and their routes (stomata, root hair cells and xylem) in a line.
