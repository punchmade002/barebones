# Package: hom-consumer-rights-s4 — Consumer protection legislation

- **loId:** `hom-consumer-rights-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-consumer-rights` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2.3
- **Syllabus scope:** the rights of the consumer as defined by current legislation, to include the Consumer Rights Act 2022 (statutory rights and remedies for goods, services and digital content bought by consumers) and the Consumer Protection Act 2007 (misleading, aggressive and prohibited commercial practices, enforced by the CCPC). Also examined: Consumer Protection Act 2007 (2024H, 2017H); the schemes also ask the protection given to the buyer of faulty goods or services (2004, 2008, 2010, 2013, 2018, 2024, 2026). No [HL] items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Ordinary and Higher Level. The syllabus (2001) names the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980 and the Consumer Information Act 1978; for June 2027 these are replaced by the Consumer Rights Act 2022 and the Consumer Protection Act 2007 (Appendix A of the outline). Papers up to 2026 still name the old Acts in the question; the notes teach the current Acts only, and the rights (satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described, services with care and skill, repair, replacement or refund) carry over. Credit law is in the consumer choices subsection (and the household finance chapter).

## 1. Must cover

Consumer Rights Act 2022: scope
- [ ] A1 Consumer Rights Act 2022: gives consumers statutory rights and remedies for goods, services and digital content bought from a trader. It applies to contracts made on or after 29 November 2022 and replaced the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980 for consumer contracts.
- [ ] A2 A trader supplies in the course of business; a consumer is an individual acting outside their business.
- [ ] A3 Statutory rights apply automatically, whether or not the trader mentions them; a notice such as "no refunds" or a term in the small print cannot remove them.
- [ ] A4 Digital content (downloaded software, music, films) is held to the same kinds of standard as goods.

Rights for goods
- [ ] G1 Satisfactory quality: goods must be of a standard a reasonable person would consider satisfactory, taking account of price and description; this includes durability, safety, freedom from minor defects, appearance and finish.
- [ ] G2 Fit for purpose: fit for the purposes goods of that kind are normally used for, and for any particular purpose the buyer made known to the trader.
- [ ] G3 As described: goods match the description given by the trader, the label or the advertisement.
- [ ] G4 Match sample or model: goods match any sample or model the consumer was shown.
- [ ] G5 The retailer is responsible for faulty goods and must deal with the complaint, even if the fault is the manufacturer's; the trader is the one to approach, not the manufacturer.

Rights for services
- [ ] S1 Services must be carried out with reasonable care and skill (by a competent provider).
- [ ] S2 Materials used must be of satisfactory quality and fit for their purpose.
- [ ] S3 Services must match what the trader said or agreed.
- [ ] S4 Marking-scheme wording to cover: services done to a high standard, by a qualified person, with good materials.

Remedies
- [ ] R1 Faulty goods: first remedy is repair or replacement, at the trader's cost, within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience.
- [ ] R2 Second remedy: price reduction or refund if repair or replacement is impossible, not done in a reasonable time or causes significant inconvenience.
- [ ] R3 Short-term right to reject: faulty goods can be rejected within 30 days of delivery, with a full refund.
- [ ] R4 Services not done properly: the trader must repeat the service or the consumer can ask for a price reduction.
- [ ] R5 Guarantee: a manufacturer's guarantee is extra to statutory rights and cannot reduce them.

When rights do not apply (exam scenarios)
- [ ] E1 No statutory right to a refund merely because the buyer changed their mind or dislikes the colour; any refund, exchange or credit note is the shop's own policy.
- [ ] E2 Goods bought in a sale carry the same rights as at full price, except for a fault pointed out to the buyer before purchase.
- [ ] E3 Misuse or ordinary wear and tear is not a fault for which the trader is liable.
- [ ] E4 Proof of purchase (receipt, bank statement or order confirmation) is needed to show the goods came from this trader.

Consumer Protection Act 2007
- [ ] P1 Consumer Protection Act 2007: prohibits unfair commercial practices by traders and protects consumers from false and misleading claims. It replaced the Consumer Information Act 1978.
- [ ] P2 Misleading actions: false or deceptive information about a product, its price, its origin, or the trader.
- [ ] P3 Misleading omissions: leaving out or hiding material information a consumer needs to decide.
- [ ] P4 Aggressive practices: harassment, coercion, undue influence or pressure that limits freedom of choice.
- [ ] P5 Prohibited practices: a list of practices banned in all cases, such as claiming a product has an approval it does not have, or using a quality mark without permission.
- [ ] P6 Price claims: false or misleading prices, a false claim of a previous price or reduction, or a recommended retail price used to suggest a saving that does not exist.
- [ ] P7 Enforced by the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC), which investigates and can prosecute.
- [ ] P8 Benefit to the consumer: protection from false advertising, honest price information, and a state body that acts.

## 3. Traps

- Teach the current Acts. The 2001 syllabus text and papers up to 2026 name the 1980 and 1978 Acts; where a past question names one, the answer rests on the same rights taught here under the new Act, but the notes never name the old Acts as the law.
- The Consumer Rights Act 2022 is about what consumers can claim for a faulty purchase; the Consumer Protection Act 2007 is about how traders behave (advertising, pricing, selling). Keep them apart.
- The CCPC enforces consumer protection; it does not settle individual disputes. The route for individual claims is the trader, then the small claims procedure (complaints subsection).
- The Consumer Credit Act 1995 is in the credit and household finance material, not here.
- Do not use: "merchantable quality", because it is the 1980 Act's phrase (still in a 2026 scheme); write satisfactory quality.
- Do not use: the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980 or the Consumer Information Act 1978 by name as current law, because both are superseded.
- Do not use: "the manufacturer accepts responsibility for faults" as a statutory rule (2004 scheme), because the statutory duty is on the retailer.
- Do not use: a specific period during which a fault is presumed to have existed at purchase, because it is unchecked; write only that a fault present at supply is the trader's responsibility even if it shows later.
- Checked (CCPC guidance and the Act, ss. 78 and 83): where no time was agreed, a service must be supplied within a reasonable time; where no price was agreed, the consumer pays a reasonable price. Keep both.
- Do not use: any figure for fines, compensation or the lowest-price period for reductions, because they change or are unchecked.

## 4. What exams ask

- State or outline three ways the law protects the buyer: goods of satisfactory quality (durable, safe, free of defects), fit for purpose, as described, match sample; services with skill and materials; repair, replacement or refund; the retailer is responsible. Marks go to each named right plus a short explanation.
- Describe the protection of the Consumer Protection Act 2007 (12 marks, 2024H): false or misleading claims about goods, services and price, including previous prices and recommended retail prices; enforcement by the CCPC.
- Scenario: shop refused a refund (faulty or change of mind); the answer decides entitlement, names the right, and gives the remedy or the reason for no remedy.
- Explain the difference between a statutory right and a guarantee.
- Outline protection for a consumer who bought a faulty service (care, skill, materials, repeat or price reduction).
