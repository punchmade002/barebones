# Package: hom-unemployment-poverty-s5 — Causes and effects of poverty

## 1. Must cover

Reasons poverty continues in modern western society (one block, common to both levels)
- [ ] R1 Unemployment and long-term unemployment: no earned income; welfare payments are lower than wages (recession and high unemployment are the usual first reason in the schemes).
- [ ] R2 Low pay and low-skilled work: unskilled and minimum-wage workers may not earn enough for an acceptable standard of living (the "working poor"); part-time and insecure work.
- [ ] R3 Poor education and lack of skills: early school leavers and people with few qualifications get low-paid or no work.
- [ ] R4 Family structure: lone-parent families and large families depend on one income to support several people; childcare costs stop lone parents working.
- [ ] R5 High living costs: housing (rent, mortgage, shortage of affordable housing), energy, transport and childcare take a large share of low incomes.
- [ ] R6 Social problems: alcohol, drug and gambling addiction divert money from essentials and cause debt; ill health and disability can stop people working.
- [ ] R7 Economic change: recession, industrial decline and the closure of a major employer; globalisation and automation reduce demand for unskilled labour.
- [ ] R8 Inequality in society: wealth and power are unequally shared, and some groups face discrimination (Travellers, ethnic minorities).
- [ ] R9 Heading structure (2016H): group reasons as social (family structure, addiction, cycle of poverty, poverty trap) and economic (unemployment, low pay, recession, high costs) when the question asks for both.

The cycle of poverty [HL] (one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] C1 [HL] Cycle of poverty: poverty is passed from one generation to the next. Children of low-income parents often have poorer housing, diet, health and educational opportunities, may leave school early, find only low-paid work or unemployment, and then raise their own children in the same conditions.
- [ ] C2 [HL] Each stage reinforces the next (low income leads to poor housing and diet, which affect health and attendance at school, which limit qualifications, which limit income). The cycle is broken by action at a weak point: early-years education (for example free pre-school and DEIS supports), family support, access to training and employment.

The influence of social policy on poverty [HL] (one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Social policy means State decisions on welfare payments, housing, education, health, minimum pay and employment. These decisions affect how many people are poor and for how long.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Policy can reduce poverty: welfare payments, the national minimum wage, free or subsidised health care and housing supports, education measures for disadvantaged areas.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Policy can also keep people poor if it is inadequate or badly designed: payments below the cost of basic needs, a gap between welfare and wages, withdrawal of benefits when a person takes a job (the poverty trap), and housing policy that fails to supply affordable homes. The 2010H scheme: social policy can perpetuate poverty where losing benefits makes low-paid work financially unattractive.

The cycle of deprivation in families and geographical areas [HL] (one block)
- [ ] D1 [HL] Deprivation means lacking resources, services and opportunities that are normal in society. The cycle of deprivation is the way deprivation, across several areas of life together (income, education, housing, health, services), reinforces itself and is passed on.
- [ ] D2 [HL] In families: low income, low education and poor health limit what parents can give their children; the children grow up with few examples of working life and low expectations and repeat the pattern.
- [ ] D3 [HL] In geographical areas: unemployment, welfare dependence and poor housing are concentrated in the same place (black spots, some disadvantaged urban estates and some rural areas). Services such as shops, buses, schools and health centres close, the area gets a poor reputation, employers and skilled people leave, and local spending and opportunities fall further.
- [ ] D4 [HL] Responses must work on the place as well as the household (community development, local training and jobs); the examples belong to the statutory and community subsections.

The poverty trap [HL] (one block)
- [ ] T1 [HL] The poverty trap: a person on welfare who takes a low-paid job loses benefits or supports (and may pay tax and PRSI), so net income rises little or not at all, and may even fall. The 2021H scheme: returning to low-paid work leaves a person no better off than on welfare.
- [ ] T2 [HL] The costs of working (transport, childcare, work clothes) reduce the gain further, so there is little incentive to take the job and the person stays dependent on welfare.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Supports lost may include a medical card or housing and rent support; reducing supports gradually and in-work payments such as the Working Family Payment (a tax-free payment for low-paid working families with children) lessen the trap.

Effects of poverty (one block, both levels)
- [ ] E1 Individual: stress, depression and worry, loss of self-esteem and status, isolation and exclusion, poor diet and health, poor or cold housing, and, for some, addiction as an escape.
- [ ] E2 Family: unable to afford food, clothing, heating or adequate housing; arrears and debt; strain on relationships and possible family breakdown; homelessness in the worst cases.
- [ ] E3 Children (2025O, 2004O): poor diet and health, poor development and concentration; no quiet place to study, books or internet; unable to join school trips, sport or clubs; embarrassment and low self-esteem; early school leaving; the risk of being poor as adults (the cycle of poverty).
- [ ] E4 Community and State: more demand for welfare, health and housing services; crime and antisocial behaviour in some areas; a poorer area as shops and services close; loss of skills and productive capacity in the economy.

## 3. Traps

- The cycle of poverty is poverty passed between generations; the cycle of deprivation is multiple deprivation reinforcing itself in a family or area; the poverty trap is the benefit-withdrawal problem. Examiners ask each separately; do not blur them. Two or three sentences per item at Higher Level.
- The cycle of poverty and the cycle of deprivation are both [HL]; do not mark general reasons (unemployment, low pay) as [HL].
- Poverty concepts (absolute, relative, the poverty line, the groups) belong to the previous subsection; the schemes, welfare payments and voluntary groups that respond to poverty belong to the responses subsections. Here payments appear only as examples of social policy and of the trap.
- Effects of unemployment are covered in an earlier subsection; the effects here are those of poverty, which overlap in debt and family strain; add poor health, housing and the effects on children.
- Do not use: "some children from poorer families place less value on education" (current notes), because it is unsourced and reads as a stereotype; say that disadvantage (poor study conditions, low expectations, financial pressure) leads to early school leaving.
- Do not use: "generous welfare payments reduce the incentive to seek work so sustain dependence" as the main account of social policy, because it is a contested view; the schemes use the benefit-withdrawal explanation. If used, present it as a criticism ("critics argue").
- Do not use: Working Family Payment hours, rates, the percentage of the income gap paid, or income limits (the 2023H scheme quotes 19 hours per fortnight (38 for couples combined) and 60% of the gap), because these are changing figures; say it is a tax-free weekly payment for low-paid working parents.
- Do not use: the names Family Income Supplement, Rent Allowance, Back to Work Allowance, Jobstart, mortgage allowance scheme or RAPID as current supports (they appear in old schemes), because they are replaced or ended; Housing Assistance Payment and the Working Family Payment are the current names.
- Do not use: figures such as the 755,570 people in poverty or "one in four children" from past stems, because they are changing figures.

## 4. What exams ask

- Four reasons poverty continues (Ordinary, 20-24 marks): four reasons at 5-6 marks, each explained; unemployment and recession, low education and low pay, lone-parent and large families, high living and housing costs, addiction.
- Social and economic reasons (Higher, 20 marks): two social, two economic, one other.
- Explain the cycle of poverty and the poverty trap (Higher, 12-18 marks as parts): the generation-to-generation sequence; the loss of benefits that makes work pay little.
- Discuss named contributory factors (Higher 30 marks in 2010H): recession, social policy, the cycle of deprivation in families and areas, each as a developed point.
- Discuss why poverty continues with a point on the cycle of poverty and a point on social policy (2006H).
- Effects of poverty on families and children (Ordinary 2025O, 2004O): a list of effects, each with an explanation.
- Tips: give the cycle as a sequence of stages; give the trap with an example (loss of a medical card or supports plus costs of work); for effects, name the group (individual, family, child) before each effect.
