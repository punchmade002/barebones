# Package: hom-resource-management-s2 — Components of management and decision-making

- **loId:** `hom-resource-management-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-resource-management` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.1
- **Syllabus scope:** [2.1.1] **[HL]** inputs (human and material resources, needs, wants, goals), throughputs (planning, organising, implementing) and outputs (goals achieved, changes in values, goals and standards, satisfaction, evaluation and effective use of feedback); decision-making and communication.
  - Settings: case studies planning routines for modern patterns of living, including work sharing, responsibility for family tasks, delegation and gender equity; checklists for routines and work schedules.
  - Also examined: naming the components (2017H, 2015H, 2011H); applying the management framework to a named event (2021H, 2013H, 2006H); decision-making (2013H); resources other than money (2011O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. In the outline the [HL] mark applies to the inputs, throughputs and outputs list; Appendix C lists "inputs, throughputs and outputs" as the Higher Level item. Decision-making, communication, routines and resources other than money are common to both levels. Past questions on naming the components and on planning an event with the framework are all on Higher papers.

## 1. Must cover

Inputs (Higher Level only block; heading ends " (Higher Level only)")
- [ ] I1 Inputs are what goes into the management process: the demands made on the family and the resources available to meet them.
- [ ] I2 Demands: needs (necessities such as food, shelter, warmth and clothing); wants (things desired beyond necessities, such as a holiday or a new appliance); goals (what the family sets out to achieve); values (what the family believes is important).
- [ ] I3 Resources: human (time, energy, skills, knowledge, people), material (equipment, goods, food), economic (money: income, savings) and environmental (space, surroundings, community facilities). The syllabus names human and material; marking schemes list all four.
- [ ] I4 Needs and wants differ: a need must be met; a want can be postponed or dropped when resources are short. Clear goals are the first step; vague goals give poor results.

Throughputs (Higher Level only block)
- [ ] TH1 Throughputs are the actions that change inputs into outputs: planning, organising and implementing.
- [ ] TH2 Planning: gathering information, considering alternatives, deciding on a course of action.
- [ ] TH3 Organising: allocating tasks and responsibilities (who does what, when), arranging the resources needed, communicating with those involved.
- [ ] TH4 Implementing: putting the plan into action (booking, buying, cooking, cleaning) and making changes as needs arise.
- [ ] TH5 Decision-making and communication run through all three throughputs (see D and C below).

Outputs (Higher Level only block)
- [ ] O1 Outputs are the results, judged against the inputs. The syllabus lists: goals achieved; changes in values, goals and standards; satisfaction; evaluation; effective use of feedback.
- [ ] O2 Goals achieved: were the goals met, and were needs and wants fulfilled.
- [ ] O3 Changes in values, goals and standards: the experience may change what the family thinks is important or reasonable, or show that a goal or standard was unrealistic.
- [ ] O4 Satisfaction: whether members are happy with the result.
- [ ] O5 Evaluation: were resources used effectively, was the task organised efficiently, did the result meet the family's needs.
- [ ] O6 Effective use of feedback: what is learned goes back in as an input to the next cycle, so the process is a loop.

Applying the framework to a named event (Higher Level only block; also examined 2021H, 2013H, 2006H)
- [ ] A1 The framework is used to set out a plan for any event or task: a graduation, a family holiday, a party, a meal, a house move. Give one fully worked example with at least two points for each of inputs, throughputs and outputs. Marking schemes award marks per point in each of the three parts (a 2013H 18-mark question gave three 2-mark points for each), so the example must be balanced across the three.
- [ ] A2 The framework suits meal planning, money and home management, problem solving and decision-making (the uses a marking scheme lists).

Decision-making (common to both levels; examined 2013H)
- [ ] D1 A decision is a choice between alternatives. A simple sequence: identify the problem or need; gather information; list the alternatives; weigh each against goals and resources; choose and act; review the result.
- [ ] D2 Why decision-making matters in family resource management (points from a 12-mark scheme): it involves sharing information and ideas; it is needed to allocate tasks; it shares values, views and concerns; it helps achieve goals; it resolves conflict and issues; evaluation gives lessons for the future; it improves efficiency; discussion gives a broader view of issues.
- [ ] D3 Decisions are easier when family values and goals have been discussed in advance. Involving members builds commitment to the result.

Communication (common to both levels)
- [ ] C1 Communication is sharing information, ideas, values and concerns between members. It is needed at every stage: agreeing goals, allocating tasks, reporting progress, giving feedback.
- [ ] C2 Practical methods: a family discussion or meeting, notes, shared lists and calendars, messaging.
- [ ] C3 Poor communication leads to duplicated or missed tasks, misunderstandings and conflict.

Planning routines for modern patterns of living (settings; common to both levels)
- [ ] RT1 Modern patterns: two working adults, shift work, lone-parent households, working from home, children in childcare or after-school activities. Routines have to be fitted around these.
- [ ] RT2 Work sharing: tasks (cooking, shopping, laundry, cleaning) are divided between members.
- [ ] RT3 Responsibility for family tasks: each member has a defined task they are accountable for, matched to age and ability.
- [ ] RT4 Delegation: passing a task to another member with the instructions, time and resources needed to do it, and trusting them to complete it.
- [ ] RT5 Gender equity: tasks are shared on ability, availability and fairness, not by sex.
- [ ] RT6 Checklists and work schedules: a written list or timetable (weekly cleaning rota, meal planner, daily checklist) showing tasks, who does them and when.

Resources other than money (examined 2011O)
- [ ] X1 Five resources, other than money, that contribute to a good management system, each with its contribution: people (make and carry out decisions and tasks); time (allows planning and avoids waste); skills and knowledge (support problem solving, healthy meals, budgeting); equipment (saves time and energy; a computer, for example, aids a job search); human energy (lets tasks be completed in an acceptable, harmonious way).

## 3. Traps

- Throughputs are not outputs. Planning, organising and implementing are actions; the results are outputs. Students often put "evaluation" under throughputs; it is an output.
- Needs are not wants. Keep the definitions apart (I2, I4).
- Resources sit under inputs, alongside needs, wants, goals and values, not under outputs.
- "Feedback" returns to the next cycle: do not present the framework as a straight line that ends at outputs.
- The purpose of resource management, resource types and management systems belong to "Family resource management"; refer in one phrase only.
- The influences that change management (life-cycle stage, employment pattern, culture, values, standards, sex roles, dual role, socio-economic status, family composition) belong to "Factors that affect management". Mention values and standards only as items inside inputs and outputs.
- Budgeting, income and expenditure detail belong to household finance; use "money" only as a resource.
- Keep Higher Level labelling exact: the inputs, throughputs and outputs blocks (and the applied example that uses them) are [HL]; decision-making, communication, routines and resources other than money are not. Do not put " (Higher Level only)" on the others.
- Do not put "(Also examined)" in a block heading; it is not a student-facing label.
- Do not use: a fixed count of steps in decision-making ("the four steps") in a heading or fact card title, because the schemes give no count; give the sequence in D1 without a number.
- Do not use: time, cost or hours figures in routines or schedules, because they change and are not in the syllabus.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name the three components of the management process and give one use of it (6 marks, 2015H pattern): inputs, throughputs, outputs; used in meal planning, money/home management, problem solving or decision-making.
- Plan an event using the framework (18 marks, 2013H pattern: chair of a graduation committee): needs, goals, wants, values and resources as inputs; planning, organising and implementing as throughputs, each applied to the event; outputs and evaluation (goals reached, values changed, needs met, wants fulfilled, resources used effectively, people satisfied). Write each part with event-specific points.
- Explain the importance of decision-making in family resource management (12 marks, three points at four marks each): sharing information and ideas, allocating tasks, resolving conflict, achieving goals, improving efficiency, a broader view.
- Give three or five resources other than money with how each helps a management system (2011O): people, time, skills and knowledge, equipment, human energy.
- Describe how a family could share work in a modern household: work sharing, delegation, a rota or checklist, gender equity.
- Expected answers use applied examples, not definitions alone.
