# Package: hom-proteins-s6 — Biological functions

- **loId:** `hom-proteins-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-proteins` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.2
- **Syllabus scope:** structural proteins, physiologically active proteins and nutrient proteins. Also examined: functions as a heading in account-of-protein questions (2025O, 2023O, 2018O, 2013O, 2007O); the three-class table (2013H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels; no [HL] items. The 2013H scheme sets the three classes: structural (growth and repair of cells, muscle, skin), physiologically active (hormones, enzymes, antibodies, blood proteins, nucleoproteins), nutrient (provide essential amino acids; excess may be used for energy). Ordinary papers ask for two functions in plain words (growth and repair, energy, hormones, enzymes, antibodies, blood plasma proteins, cell membranes). Energy value and deamination are in the energy subsection; essential amino acids are in the composition subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Overview
- [ ] O1 Protein has three broad roles: building structure, regulating and defending the body, and supplying amino acids (nutrient role). The notes should begin with the three-way split.

Structural proteins
- [ ] S1 Growth and repair of body cells: protein supplies the amino acids to make new cells (growth, pregnancy, childhood) and repair damaged tissue (wounds).
- [ ] S2 Muscle: myosin and actin make up muscle fibres that contract.
- [ ] S3 Skin and connective tissue: collagen gives strength to skin, tendons and ligaments; keratin forms hair and nails.
- [ ] S4 Cell membranes contain protein.

Physiologically active proteins
- [ ] A1 Hormones: protein hormones, e.g. insulin controls blood glucose.
- [ ] A2 Enzymes: protein catalysts that speed chemical reactions, e.g. digestive enzymes such as pepsin.
- [ ] A3 Antibodies: made by white blood cells to fight infection (immune response).
- [ ] A4 Blood proteins: albumin in plasma, clotting proteins (fibrinogen/fibrin), haemoglobin carries oxygen.
- [ ] A5 Nucleoproteins (proteins combined with nucleic acid, in chromosomes): listed in the 2013H scheme as a physiologically active protein.

Nutrient proteins
- [ ] N1 Protein in food supplies the essential amino acids the body cannot make; these are used to build its own structural and active proteins.
- [ ] N2 Surplus protein is used for energy (cross-reference by title: "Energy value").
- [ ] N3 Table: three classes against functions with one example each (a good fact-card layout).

## 3. Traps

- Muscle, skin and cell membranes are structural; hormones, enzymes and antibodies are physiologically active. Do not file enzymes as structural.
- "Nutrient protein" means the protein of food as a source of amino acids, not a protein with a nutrient function inside cells.
- Do not make energy the main function: it is a secondary use when carbohydrate and fat are short.
- Composition (amino acids, peptide bond) belongs to the composition subsection; the classes simple/conjugated belong to the classification subsection; deamination to energy value.
- Do not use: "protein is stored in the body as a reserve" or similar, because the body does not store surplus amino acids as protein.
- Do not use: insulin being called an enzyme or "controls protein"; it is a hormone controlling blood glucose.

## 4. What exams ask

- State two biological functions of protein and name one source (Ordinary 2025, 6 marks): growth and repair of cells, energy, muscle, skin, hormones, enzymes, antibodies; one food source.
- Complete a table of the three groups' functions (2013H, 6 marks): structural (growth and repair of cells, muscles, skin); physiologically active (hormones, enzymes, antibodies, blood proteins, nucleoproteins); nutrient (essential amino acids; excess for energy).
- Account of protein under "functions in the body" (Ordinary 2013, 2018, 2023, 8 marks for two functions at 4 marks each): growth and repair, muscle, skin, cell membranes, essential amino acids, hormones, enzymes, antibodies, blood proteins, energy.
- Exam tips to build: "Always include growth and repair"; "Give a named example for each of hormone (insulin), enzyme and antibody"; "Use the three-class headings for Higher Level."
