# Package: hom-consumer-rights-s6 — Complaints and the small claims procedure

- **loId:** `hom-consumer-rights-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-consumer-rights` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2.3
- **Syllabus scope:** procedures to follow when a problem occurs with a product or service; the function and operation of the small claims procedure. Also examined: 2024O, 2021H, 2018O, 2013O, 2010O (also 2008O, 2007H, 2004O). No [HL] items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Ordinary and Higher Level. The rights that justify a complaint are in the consumer protection legislation subsection; the bodies a consumer can contact are in the bodies subsection; keep both to a line. Past schemes quote the small claims fee and monetary limit as euro figures that have changed between papers; do not give the amounts.

## 1. Must cover

Complaining about faulty goods (one block, the procedure)
- [ ] G1 Stop using the product (especially if unsafe) and keep it safe for inspection.
- [ ] G2 Go back to the retailer as soon as possible, with the receipt or other proof of purchase, and speak to the manager or customer service.
- [ ] G3 Describe the fault briefly and state the redress wanted (repair, replacement or refund), one that fits the fault and the value of the goods.
- [ ] G4 If this fails, complain in writing (a letter or email): what was bought, where and when, the fault, a copy of the receipt, the redress sought and a reasonable date for a reply.
- [ ] G5 Keep copies of all letters, emails and replies and note dates of calls: evidence if the matter goes further.
- [ ] G6 The retailer remains responsible even where there is a manufacturer's guarantee; the manufacturer can also be contacted.
- [ ] G7 If the trader still refuses, get advice (Citizens Information, the CCPC) and then use the small claims procedure.
- [ ] G8 Cross-border online purchases: European Consumer Centre Ireland.

Complaining about a faulty service
- [ ] V1 Complain promptly to the supplier, in writing if needed, referring to the quote, contract or what was agreed.
- [ ] V2 Ask for the service to be done again, a price reduction or a refund.
- [ ] V3 Keep invoices, quotes and correspondence.
- [ ] V4 If still unresolved, use the small claims procedure.

Complaining about advertising or misleading claims
- [ ] A1 Report a misleading or offensive advertisement to the Advertising Standards Authority; report misleading prices or practices to the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission.
- [ ] A2 Keep a copy or screenshot with date and where it appeared.
- [ ] A3 Complaints about banks or public bodies go to the provider first, then the relevant Ombudsman.

Small claims procedure: what it is and how it works
- [ ] S1 Function: a special, simple way within the District Court structure for resolving small disputes, without a solicitor, about goods or services bought for private use from a trader.
- [ ] S2 Only claims up to a monetary limit set by law can be taken; bigger claims go through the ordinary courts.
- [ ] S3 The consumer completes a claim form at the District Court office (the form can be sent or lodged) and pays a small fixed fee. The consumer should already have tried to settle the matter with the trader.
- [ ] S4 The Registrar sends a copy of the claim to the respondent (the trader).
- [ ] S5 The respondent has 15 days to admit the claim, dispute it or make a counterclaim.
- [ ] S6 If the respondent does not reply or disputes it, the Registrar tries to resolve the dispute between the parties (a settlement).
- [ ] S7 If it is not settled, the case goes to a hearing before the District Court judge, where each side presents evidence (receipts, letters).
- [ ] S8 The scheme: if the respondent accepts or fails to reply, the claimant succeeds, and the respondent has 28 days to comply.
- [ ] S9 Terms: claimant (the consumer making the claim); respondent (the trader answering it).

Benefits and limits
- [ ] B1 Inexpensive (small fixed fee), fast and simple; no solicitor needed; no need to go through a full court case; available at local District Court offices.
- [ ] B2 Limits: the claim must be within the monetary limit; not suited to complex disputes; the consumer needs evidence.
- [ ] B3 Typical examples for scenarios: a faulty washing machine where the shop will not give redress; poorly done building or repair work.

## 3. Traps

- The small claims procedure is not a separate court building; schemes call it the "Small Claims Court", but it is a procedure within the District Court. Use "small claims procedure" and mention the schemes' name once.
- The procedure suits consumer disputes with traders. It is not for personal injury claims or for large claims.
- A complaint order: trader first, in person then in writing; then advice or a body; then the small claims procedure. A student who goes to the court office first has skipped the steps.
- The CCPC and Citizens Information give advice and information; they do not decide the dispute.
- Do not use: the claim fee or the monetary limit as a figure, because both have changed between papers; write "a small fixed fee" and "a limit set in law".
- Do not use: the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980 or the Consumer Information Act 1978 as the basis for the complaint (older and 2026 schemes), because both are superseded; the basis is the Consumer Rights Act 2022.
- Do not use: "the CCPC will take up your individual complaint with the trader", because it enforces the law and gives guidance but does not resolve individual disputes.
- Do not use: the 15-day and 28-day periods as advice on how long a consumer can wait before claiming, because they are the respondent's procedural deadlines and no source fixes a general deadline for the consumer; write "complain promptly".

## Worked outline for a scenario answer

A typical scenario: a couple buy a washing machine that breaks down and the shop refuses redress. A full answer follows the order: (1) stop using it and find the receipt; (2) return to the shop and ask for a repair, replacement or refund, because the retailer is responsible for faulty goods; (3) write to the shop with the details if the manager refuses and keep a copy; (4) seek advice from Citizens Information or the CCPC; (5) complete the small claims form at the District Court office, pay the fixed fee and name the shop as respondent; (6) the Registrar sends the claim to the shop, which has 15 days to answer; (7) the Registrar tries to settle, and if that fails the claim goes to a hearing where the couple show the receipt, the letters and the fault. Marks in these questions go to each separate step, so each should be a separate sentence.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline the procedure for making a complaint (5 points at 3 marks each, or 3 points at 4 marks): stop using, return to retailer with receipt, speak to manager, state redress, written complaint, keep copies, advice, small claims.
- Give an account of the small claims procedure (20 marks): claim form to the District Court office and fixed fee; Registrar sends it to the respondent; 15 days to admit, dispute, ignore or counterclaim; Registrar tries to settle; unresolved cases go to a hearing; no solicitor.
- State two benefits of the small claims procedure (6 marks): cheap, fast, simple, no solicitor or lawyer, handles product and service disputes, local.
- Explain how a couple could use the procedure for a faulty washing machine when the shop refuses redress (12 marks).
- Explain three benefits of the small claims procedure (15 marks).
- State the function of the procedure: mechanism for resolving small claims within the District Court.
