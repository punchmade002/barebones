# Package: hom-family-law-s5 — The Child Care Act 1991

- **loId:** `hom-family-law-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-law` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.7
- **Syllabus scope:** "The Child Care Act 1991" [3.1.7] **[HL]**. Higher Level only: every block heading in the notes ends " (Higher Level only)".
- **Also examined:** protection for children under current family law (2013H, 2012H). 2012H asked for an informative note on the Act (14 marks); a 2013H answer credited protection through foster care and removal where necessary, Family Support Projects and this Act.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The Act remains in force (as amended) and keeps its syllabus name; its functions have been exercised since 2014 by Tusla, the Child and Family Agency (outline, Appendix A). Past marking schemes say "health boards", which is superseded: use Tusla. Sources checked: Citizens Information (children and the care system), Tusla guides, irishstatutebook.ie.

## 1. Must cover

What the Act does
- [ ] A1 The Child Care Act 1991 makes provision for the care and protection of children (a child is a person under 18). It gives the State, through Tusla, the Child and Family Agency, the duty to promote the welfare of children who are not receiving adequate care and protection, and powers to act where a child is at risk.
- [ ] A2 Tusla's duties include providing child care and family support services and investigating where a child may be at risk. (Family Support Projects are credited in one exam scheme; one phrase.)
- [ ] A3 Intervention: Tusla has power to intervene in a family situation where a child is deemed to be at risk, for example because the child is assaulted, neglected or sexually abused, or ill-treated (the scheme's examples; students should give these three). More widely: where the child's health, development or welfare is likely to be harmed and the child is unlikely to get the care needed without Tusla's help.
- [ ] A4 Only Tusla, not individuals, applies to the District Court for the orders below (assumed: the court is the District Court).
- [ ] A5 Voluntary care: where parents ask for or agree to it, Tusla can take a child into care without a court order (assumed; one line only, shows that care is not always compulsory).

Orders, in order of urgency
- [ ] O1 Garda power: where there is an immediate and serious risk to a child, a Garda can remove the child to safety and place them in Tusla's care; Tusla must then seek a court order if it intends to keep the child in care.
- [ ] O2 Emergency care order: a short-term court order, made where there is an immediate and serious risk to a child, allowing Tusla to take the child into care at once. It can be made without notice to the parents.
- [ ] O3 Interim care order: a temporary order made while the court considers a full application for a care order, so the child is protected in the meantime.
- [ ] O4 Care order: made by the court on Tusla's application where the child needs care and protection they are unlikely to get at home. The child is removed from the family home and placed in care, for a long or short term (for example with foster carers or in a residential unit); Tusla takes responsibility for the child's care until the order ends. This is the serious step the 2012H scheme calls "removal ... to be placed in care for long or short term".
- [ ] O5 Supervision order: made by the court on Tusla's application so that the child can stay at home while Tusla personnel visit the home periodically to monitor the child's health and welfare (and can give support or directions to the family). It is the less intrusive option because the child is not removed.
- [ ] O6 Care order vs supervision order is the classic comparison: removal from home versus staying at home under visits. Exams award 3-4 marks for each.

Context and links
- [ ] L1 Why the Act matters: it balances the family's right to bring up their child with the child's right to protection; Tusla acts only where a child is at risk and the court decides on evidence.
- [ ] L2 Link to the rights of the child (assumed): protection from abuse and harm is one of the rights of children taught in the notes on the family.
- [ ] L3 Link to the Domestic Violence Act 2018: Tusla can also apply for protection orders on behalf of a child (see the notes on barring orders). One sentence.
- [ ] L4 Exam use: "write an informative note": state what the Act is for (care and protection), the intervention power with the three examples, then care order and supervision order each in a sentence or two.
- [ ] L5 Diagram: none.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "health boards" or "the Health Service Executive" as the agency, because Tusla, the Child and Family Agency, has done the job since 2014; the 2012H marking scheme's wording is superseded.
- Do not use: the lengths of the orders (an emergency care order of eight days, an interim care order of 28 or 29 days, a supervision order of twelve months), because they could not be checked against the Act text in this review and the sources disagree on the interim figure; leave out all durations until confirmed against the Act.
- Do not use: the Children's Act 1997 (joint guardianship for unmarried fathers, named in one scheme) or the Domestic Violence Act 1996, because both are superseded by later law (the Guardianship of Infants Act 1964 as amended in 2015, and the Domestic Violence Act 2018) and are not named in the outline.
- Do not use: the Children First Act 2015 or mandated reporting, because the outline does not name them for this subsection.
- The agency applies to the court; the court makes the order. Do not say Tusla "makes" a care order itself.
- A care order is not adoption, and a child in care is not necessarily in a residential unit: foster care is the most common placement (assumed from Tusla guides). Do not give statistics.
- Supervision does not remove the child. Care does. Students commonly reverse the two.
- Neighbouring subjects: barring orders protect adults and children from a violent person; this Act protects children from neglect and abuse in the family; maintenance protects a child's financial support (first subsection). Preschool inspection also falls under the 1991 Act, but is not asked; leave it out.
- A child's right to protection belongs here only as the purpose; the wider list of children's rights belongs to the notes on the family.

- Compare a care order and a supervision order.
- Name the agency responsible: Tusla (not "health board").
- Expected depth: 14 marks means about six developed points; each order needs "who applies", "what it allows" and "where the child lives".
