# Package: hom-personal-finance-s5 — Insurance

- **loId:** `hom-personal-finance-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-personal-finance` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.3
- **Syllabus scope (outline):** pay-related social insurance, health insurance, household insurance, life assurance.
- **Also examined (outline):** types of life assurance (2025H, 2022H); premium, policy and broker (2017H, 2006H); household insurance (2023O, 2019O). Past papers also ask private health insurance benefits (2021O, 2013O, 2012H) and types of insurance (2008O).
- **Higher Level only:** nothing marked [HL]. Do not mark anything HL.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. Premiums, tax relief amounts, PRSI rates and benefit rates change: leave them out. Housing finance and mortgages are in the Housing subject.

## 1. Must cover

Insurance: the idea and the terms
- [ ] T1 Insurance spreads risk: many people pay premiums into a pool and the insurer pays the few who suffer a loss, protecting a household against a cost it could not meet itself (illness, fire, theft, death, accident).
- [ ] T2 Premium: the sum paid (annually or by instalment) to the insurer to keep cover. Policy: the written contract setting out terms, conditions, what is covered and excluded. Insurer: the company that takes on the risk. Insured/policyholder: the person covered. Claim: a request for payment after a loss. Excess: part of a claim the policyholder pays. Sum assured/insured: the maximum paid out.
- [ ] T3 Broker: a person or firm that advises consumers and arranges cover with several insurers, compared with an agent of one company, or direct purchase; the broker is paid by commission or fee.
- [ ] T4 Factors affecting the premium: the risk (age, health, claims history, location, value, security measures such as alarms), cover chosen, excess chosen; a no-claims bonus lowers car premiums.
- [ ] T5 (assumed) Principles: the policyholder must give full and honest information (disclosure); insurance pays the loss, not a profit (indemnity), and only for events listed in the policy; read the policy before buying; compare quotes.

Pay-related social insurance
- [ ] P1 PRSI is the State social insurance scheme: workers (employees, employers and the self-employed) pay contributions, related to pay, into the Social Insurance Fund. It is compulsory and set by law, not a private choice.
- [ ] P2 Contributions build an entitlement to social insurance benefits, not means tested: Jobseeker's Benefit, Illness Benefit, Maternity Benefit, Carer's Benefit, contributory State pension, widow's or widower's pension, and some health-related benefits (treatment benefit for dental and optical care). Different PRSI classes carry different benefits; low earners may be exempt from paying.
- [ ] P3 Why it counts as insurance: contributions pay for protection against loss of income (unemployment, illness, retirement, bereavement, maternity). Compare with private insurance: compulsory and State-run versus voluntary and private.

Health insurance
- [ ] H1 Private health insurance: a voluntary policy that pays towards hospital, consultant and other medical costs, held alongside the public health system.
- [ ] H2 Benefits: choice of private or semi-private hospital room, cover for consultants' and some GP fees, faster access to treatment, outpatient and day-care cover, convalescence, some dental and optical cover, cover for treatment abroad, peace of mind; tax relief at source (reduces the premium).
- [ ] H3 Choosing: level of cover, premiums, excess and waiting periods, hospital choice, what is excluded, comparing insurers and plans; premiums are set by plan, not by health (community rating), and cost more for people who join late (one line).
- [ ] H4 Providers: examples are Vhi (the largest), Laya, Irish Life Health and Level Health; the regulator is the Health Insurance Authority.

Household insurance
- [ ] I1 Buildings insurance: covers the structure (walls, roof, fixtures) against fire, flood, storm and subsidence; lenders require it for a mortgage.
- [ ] I2 Contents insurance: covers furniture, appliances, clothes and valuables against theft, fire and damage; "all-risks" extension covers items outside the home (phones, bicycles).
- [ ] I3 Combined household policy: buildings and contents together; may include public liability and temporary accommodation.
- [ ] I4 Car insurance: third-party cover is compulsory by law to drive on a public road; third party, fire and theft; comprehensive.
- [ ] I5 Other types the exam accepts: travel, mobile phone, income protection, holiday, public liability, pet insurance.
- [ ] I6 Practical points: insure for the correct value (underinsurance means a reduced payout), keep an inventory and receipts, state security measures, keep the policy up to date, report claims promptly.

Life assurance
- [ ] L1 Life assurance pays a lump sum to dependants (beneficiaries) when the insured person dies; it pays out because death is certain to happen; "insurance" covers events that may or may not occur. Benefits: financial security for dependants, helps clear a mortgage or debts, peace of mind, some policies have a cash-in value (savings element).
- [ ] L2 Term assurance: cover for a set number of years; pays only if death occurs in that period; no payout if the insured survives; cheapest type.
- [ ] L3 Whole-of-life assurance: cover for the whole life as long as premiums are paid; pays an agreed sum on death, whenever it occurs; dearer.
- [ ] L4 Endowment assurance: pays the sum at the end of the term or on earlier death; combines protection and saving.
- [ ] L5 Mortgage protection: a term assurance whose sum assured falls with the mortgage balance, clearing the loan if the borrower dies; lenders normally require it.
- [ ] L6 Convertible term assurance: term cover that can be changed into whole-of-life or other cover during the term without a new health check. Other related cover: serious illness cover and income protection (one line).

## 3. Traps

- PRSI is not private insurance: it is compulsory and State-run. Do not describe benefits as "paid by an insurer".
- Premium is what you pay; the sum assured is what is paid out. Policy is the contract, not the payment.
- Term assurance pays only if death occurs within the term; whole-of-life pays whenever death occurs. Do not say term cover "pays back" premiums.
- Life assurance, not life insurance, in this syllabus's sense (death is certain).
- Buildings insurance covers the structure; contents covers belongings. A tenant needs contents, not buildings.
- Third-party insurance covers damage and injury to other people, not the policyholder's own car.
- A broker is not the insurer.
- Household income deductions and welfare payment categories belong to the Household income subsection; savings schemes to the Saving subsection; mortgages and housing finance to the Housing subject; the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman to the Consumer Rights and Protection subjects.
- Do not use: Aviva, Liberty and Vivas as current health insurers (2012 and 2013 marking schemes), because the market has changed; name Vhi, Irish Life Health or Level Health only.
- Do not use: any premium, average price, market share, tax relief rate, PRSI contribution rate or benefit amount, because these change.
- Do not use: ages, percentages or loading rates for late joining, because they are unconfirmed and change; say only that joining late costs more.
- Do not use: the legal wording of the compulsory insurance provision or section numbers; say only that third-party car insurance is compulsory by law.
- Do not use: "Family Income Supplement" or other welfare payments here, because they belong to the Household income subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain premium and policy (2017H, 2006H); say what a broker does.
- Name three types of insurance a consumer might need: health, house (buildings or contents), car, plus travel and others (2008O).
- Household insurance (2023O, 2019O): why a household needs buildings and contents cover, what each covers, car insurance compulsory.
- Private health insurance: name two advantages (2021O, 2013O, 2012H); name a provider; suggest a method of payment (direct debit, card).
- Life assurance (2025H, 2022H, 2024O, 2016H, 2010O): name and describe one or two types; give one difference (term versus whole of life); two benefits; explain mortgage protection.
- PRSI: define, benefits, who pays (2014H, 2012O); link with the Household income subsection.
- Higher Level answers connect insurance to managing risk in a family budget.
