# Package: hom-food-safety-s8 — National agencies in food safety

- **loId:** `hom-food-safety-s8` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-safety` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.10
- **Syllabus scope:** National agencies in food safety [1.3.10]: a brief outline of the role of national agencies in food safety, to include **[HL]** the Food Safety Authority of Ireland; the Department of Health, under which the FSAI operates; and the official agencies that carry out food controls under contract to the FSAI: the Health Service Executive (its Environmental Health Service and the Public Analyst Laboratories), the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority and the local authorities.
- **Settings:** students gather information on national agencies involved in food safety and build a file.
- **Also examined:** FSAI (2026H, 2023H, 2009H, 2005H; also 2017H, 2015O); Public Analyst Laboratories (2023H); HSE (2019H); Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (2012H); environmental health officers (2008H).
- **Level:** the general idea (state bodies set standards, inspect and enforce) is common to both levels. The named bodies and their roles are Higher Level only: those blocks' headings end " (Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Exams ask "outline the role of X" for 6 or 10 marks, so each body needs three to five distinct, accurate role points. Facts were checked on fsai.ie and hse.ie. The 2005 and older schemes contain role statements that no longer match the legal set-up (see Traps).

## 1. Must cover

The general picture (common to both levels)
- [ ] G1 National agencies protect consumers by setting and enforcing food safety standards, inspecting food businesses, testing food and acting when food is unsafe. The system in Ireland has one central body (the FSAI) that coordinates, and other bodies (official agencies) that do the inspecting.
- [ ] G2 Why it matters: food businesses must be monitored independently, consumers cannot see hygiene standards, and an outbreak or contamination crosses counties and countries. Link to the legislation subsection in one line.

Food Safety Authority of Ireland, FSAI (2026H, 2017H, 2009H, 2005H; 6 marks, three points)
- [ ] F1 [HL] The FSAI was set up by the Food Safety Authority of Ireland Act 1998 as the independent body responsible for food safety in Ireland; it operates under the Department of Health.
- [ ] F2 [HL] Role: coordinates the enforcement of food safety and hygiene legislation across the food chain, through the official agencies; advises the Minister for Health on food safety; protects consumers by ensuring food is safe and food information is accurate.
- [ ] F3 [HL] Works with the food industry to produce safe food, e.g. through guidance and training on hygiene and HACCP systems.
- [ ] F4 [HL] Monitors food businesses and audits the official agencies; takes action (through the agencies) when a premises breaches food regulations.
- [ ] F5 [HL] Handles food alerts, product withdrawals and recalls; works with the agencies on traceability; leads on food incidents.
- [ ] F6 [HL] Provides authoritative advice and information to consumers and industry, publishes food safety information, carries out scientific work, and processes applications for novel foods and new methods of food processing (2017H scheme).
- [ ] F7 [HL] What it does not do: it does not promote Irish food at home or abroad (that is Bord Bia, the Irish Food Board; 2015O asked this as true or false).

Department of Health
- [ ] D1 [HL] The government department responsible for health policy under which the FSAI operates; the Minister for Health is the Minister the FSAI advises and reports to. The Department itself does not inspect food premises.

Official agencies under service contract (the system)
- [ ] O1 [HL] The FSAI does not inspect most food premises itself. It signs service contracts with official agencies, which carry out inspections, sampling and enforcement on its behalf; the four main ones are the HSE, the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority and the local authorities. The FSAI audits their work.

Health Service Executive (HSE) and its Environmental Health Service (2019H 10 marks, 2008H 10 marks for the EHO)
- [ ] H1 [HL] Environmental health officers (EHOs) in the HSE's Environmental Health Service inspect most food premises: restaurants, cafés, shops, hospital kitchens, manufacturers and wholesalers.
- [ ] H2 [HL] Registers new food premises; inspects premises and farms; investigates hygiene complaints; can issue closure orders and enforcement notices where premises are unsafe.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Ensures food safety at public gatherings and events; monitors compliance with food, labelling, additives and contaminants legislation; takes samples; responds to rapid alerts and recalls; investigates food poisoning outbreaks; advises and educates food businesses.

Public Analyst Laboratories (2023H, 6 marks)
- [ ] L1 [HL] Run by the HSE from laboratories in Dublin, Galway and Cork (Dublin confirmed; Galway and Cork from memory, not web-checked); analyse food and water samples taken by EHOs; test food for compliance with food and labelling law (composition, additives, contaminants, microbiological quality); test samples submitted by members of the public, for a fee; results help ensure food sold is safe and wholesome and give the evidence for legal action.

Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, DAFM (2012H 10 marks)
- [ ] A1 [HL] Monitors food safety in meat and meat products, milk and milk products, eggs and egg products, and the use of pesticides (residues).
- [ ] A2 [HL] Enforces EU controls on imports of animal-based foods from outside the EU.
- [ ] A3 [HL] Responsible for the registration and traceability of cattle, pigs and sheep; ensures hygiene procedures from the dairy farm, to the dairy, to the retailer and the consumer.

Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority (SFPA)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Carries out the official food safety controls on fish and shellfish, from production and landing through processing to distribution (not retail).

Local authorities
- [ ] T1 [HL] Carry out official food controls in their own areas under contract to the FSAI, mainly veterinary inspections of food premises handling meat and other animal products, such as small abattoirs.

How the bodies work together
- [ ] W1 [HL] Traceability (one step back, one step forward) lets an unsafe product be traced; a withdrawal removes a product from the supply chain, a recall also asks consumers to return it; rapid alerts pass warnings quickly to the agencies; the FSAI coordinates all of these.

## 3. Traps

- The FSAI coordinates and audits; the official agencies inspect. Do not say the FSAI itself inspects every premises, or that the EHO works for the FSAI (EHOs work for the HSE).
- Do not mix up: FSAI (safe food, law) and Bord Bia (promotes Irish food, quality assurance schemes). The 2015O true/false and the 2007H question depend on it.
- The Department of Health is the sponsoring department; do not give it an inspecting role. Likewise the HSE (health service) is not the FSAI.
- Public Analyst Laboratories analyse; EHOs inspect and take samples. Do not give the laboratories an inspection role.
- Names: "Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine" (current name; the syllabus's older Department of Agriculture, Food and Rural Development and Regional Health Boards are superseded).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the Acts and regulations themselves (food legislation); HACCP (HACCP); how a consumer complains about food or goods (consumer rights chapter, CCPC); the national food industry and Bord Bia's quality assurance (food industry chapter); safefood, the all-island food safety promotion body, is an Irish body named in other chapters; give it one clause only if needed.
- Do not use: "the FSAI issues licences to the food industry" and "enforces all food safety legislation" (2005H scheme), because food premises are registered with, and inspected by, the official agencies and the FSAI coordinates enforcement rather than carrying it out itself.
- Do not use: "the Department of Health, under which FSAI operates" as "the Department of Health and Children" or "Regional Health Boards", because those names are superseded.
- Do not use: percentages of inspections by each agency, a date for the FSAI starting work, or the number of EHOs, because these are changing or unconfirmed.
- Do not use: the detailed local authority and DAFM split of abattoir and meat plant inspections, because it was reported as changing from the end of 2024.
## 4. What exams ask

- Outline the role of the FSAI (2026H, 2017H, 2009H 6 marks; 2005H two ways it supports industry): three points: coordinates and enforces legislation, advises the Minister, protects consumers, works with industry (HACCP), takes action on breaches, processes novel foods, advice and information.
- Role of the HSE in food safety (2019H 10 marks): registers premises, inspects premises and farms, hygiene complaints and closure orders, public gatherings, monitors legislation, alerts and recalls, education.
- Role of an environmental health officer (2008H 10 marks): registers new premises, inspects hospital kitchens, restaurants, shops and manufacturers, handles complaints.
- Role of the Public Analyst Laboratories (2023H 6 marks): analyse food and water; test compliance with law; test public samples for a fee.
- Role of the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (2012H 10 marks): two points at 5 marks: monitors meat, dairy, eggs, pesticides; import controls; animal registration and traceability; hygiene from farm to consumer.
- True/false (2015O): the FSAI promotes Irish food nationally and internationally (false).
