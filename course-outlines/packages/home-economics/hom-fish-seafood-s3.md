# Package: hom-fish-seafood-s3 — Preparation and cooking

- **loId:** `hom-fish-seafood-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-fish-seafood` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.2
- **Syllabus scope:** effects of cooking on fish; moist and dry methods suitable for fish and their underlying principles. No [HL] marks in this subsection.
- **Also examined:** 2022H (one moist method for fish and its underlying principle), 2015O (name three methods of cooking fish and explain how one is carried out), 2010H (recommend one dry and one moist method, state the principle and comment on palatability); 2022O (effects of cooking on fish).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The general theory of heat transfer and each method is taught fully in the cooking methods chapter; here it is applied to fish, at the depth needed to name a method, state its principle and comment on the result.

## 1. Must cover

Preparing fish for cooking (one block)
- [ ] P1 Whole round fish: scaled, gutted and washed; head, tail and fins may be trimmed. Flat fish may be skinned. Fillets are cut from the bone; leaving the skin on helps a delicate fillet hold together.
- [ ] P2 Mussels: scrubbed, the beards removed, any that stay open when tapped discarded; after cooking, any that have not opened are discarded. Prawns are cooked until they turn pink.
- [ ] P3 Wash and dry fish, and keep raw fish and its board and knife apart from cooked food (assumed from the food safety chapter).

Effects of cooking on fish (one block, fact-card list; examiners ask for four or five points)
- [ ] E1 Protein coagulates: the flesh turns from translucent to opaque and firm.
- [ ] E2 The flesh flakes: the thin connective tissue between the muscle fibres changes to gelatine and breaks down quickly, so the flesh separates into flakes. This is why fish cooks fast.
- [ ] E3 Overcooking toughens and dries the fish, because the protein shrinks and squeezes out moisture.
- [ ] E4 Micro-organisms and parasites are killed, so well-cooked fish is safe.
- [ ] E5 Digestibility improves and flavour develops.
- [ ] E6 Some B vitamins and minerals dissolve into the cooking liquid or are lost by heat; use the liquid in a sauce or soup. Fat-soluble vitamins are mostly kept (assumed).
- [ ] E7 Oily fish loses some fat into the pan or grill (and white fish absorbs fat if fried).

Heat transfer (one block)
- [ ] H1 Conduction: heat passes from molecule to molecule through a solid or liquid in contact with the food (a pan on the hob, fat in shallow frying).
- [ ] H2 Convection: heat carried by circulating liquid, steam or air; the hotter material rises and cooler material falls (poaching, steaming, baking, stewing, deep frying).
- [ ] H3 Radiation: heat travels in rays from the heat source to the surface of the food (grilling, barbecuing).
- [ ] H4 Microwave: electromagnetic waves make water molecules in the food vibrate, generating heat inside the food; quick.

Moist methods and their principle (one block; state method, how done, principle, result)
- [ ] M1 Poaching: fish cooked gently in liquid (water, milk, stock or wine) just below simmering point, about 85 °C (80–90 °C), in a saucepan on the hob or in a covered dish in the oven. Principle: conduction and convection through the liquid. Result: moist, easily digested, keeps its shape; may lack flavour, so serve with a sauce. A good method for delicate white fish and for salmon.
- [ ] M2 Steaming: food cooked by steam rising from boiling water, between two plates over a saucepan, in a covered bowl standing in boiling water, or in a steamer. Principle: convection (and the heat released as steam condenses). Result: light, easily digested, few nutrients lost, no fat added; mild flavour.
- [ ] M3 Stewing or casseroling: gentle heat in liquid in a covered container on the hob or in the oven, by convection and conduction. Suits fish stews, chowders and fish in sauce; the liquid is eaten, so nutrients in it are kept.
- [ ] M4 Boiling vigorously breaks up the flesh and loses flavour and nutrients; fish is simmered or poached instead.

Dry methods and their principle (one block; state method, how done, principle, result)
- [ ] D1 Grilling: radiant heat under a grill; the heat seals the surface, retaining flavour, moisture and nutrients. Quick and needs little fat; easily overcooked and dried out, so brush with oil and watch it.
- [ ] D2 Barbecuing: radiant heat on a grid over burning charcoal or gas flames; adds a smoky flavour.
- [ ] D3 Baking: cooking in the dry heat of the oven, by convection; fish may be baked whole, in fillets or wrapped in foil or paper to keep moist.
- [ ] D4 Roasting: cooking in a little fat in a roasting tin in the oven, by convection; suited to firm whole fish or thick pieces.
- [ ] D5 Shallow frying: cooking in hot fat in a pan on the hob; conduction through the fat. Crisp surface and quick, but adds fat and calories.
- [ ] D6 Deep frying: food immersed in hot fat in a deep fat fryer; convection in the fat. A coating of batter or breadcrumbs seals the fish, keeps it moist and crisp; high in fat.
- [ ] D7 Stir-frying: small pieces cooked quickly in a wok in a small amount of oil; conduction and convection.
- [ ] D8 Microwave cooking: quick, no fat, may cook unevenly; cover the dish and allow standing time.
- [ ] D9 Comparison for a "palatability" answer (2010H): grilled fish is quick, low in fat and digestible but easily overcooked; poached fish is digestible and moist but may lack flavour; fried fish is crisp and flavourful but higher in fat and less suitable for low-fat diets.
- [ ] D10 Diagram: none required.

## 3. Traps

- Radiation is the principle for grilling; convection and conduction for poaching. Don't say grilling is "conduction".
- Fish is cooked when the flesh turns opaque and flakes easily; don't wait for it to be dry.
- Don't say the connective tissue "toughens" with cooking: in fish it turns to gelatine and the flesh flakes; the overcooked protein is what toughens.
- The 2022H scheme accepts poaching, boiling, simmering, steaming and stewing as moist methods, but the notes should recommend poaching and steaming; boiling vigorously is unsuitable (M4).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: how to select and store fish and spoilage; preservation methods such as smoking and canning; the general cooking methods chapter's coverage of pressure cooking, boiling and the heat transfer laws for other foods; the nutritive value of fish.
- Do not use: a "safe core temperature" for fish or shellfish, because no scheme states one and official advice varies; say "cook until the flesh is opaque and flakes".
- Do not use: "sealing the surface locks in all the juices", as a general claim, because it is a scheme wording for grilling only and overstates what searing does; keep it as "the heat seals the surface, retaining flavour, moisture and nutrients" for grilling.
- Do not use: "heat destroys bacteria and parasites so fish is always safe", because shellfish and reheated fish still carry risk; say "well-cooked fish is safe".

## 4. What exams ask

- Effects of cooking on fish (6–12 marks): protein sets and turns opaque; flakes; overcooking toughens; microbes killed; flavour and digestibility; B vitamins and minerals lost to cooking liquid.
- Name three methods of cooking fish and explain how one is carried out (20 marks, 4 marks each name or point): baking, grilling, roasting, frying, steaming, microwave, poaching, stewing, barbecuing.
- One moist method suitable for fish with its underlying principle (6 marks): poaching with convection and conduction, or steaming.
- Recommend a dry method and a moist method, state the principle and comment on the palatability of the fish (2010H, 20 marks): grilling, radiant heat, seals the surface, digestible but easily overcooked; poaching, 80–90 °C, conduction and convection, digestible but may lack flavour so serve with a sauce.
