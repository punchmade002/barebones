## 1. Must cover

The B-complex as a group
- [ ] G1 The B-complex is a family of separate water-soluble vitamins that often occur together in the same foods; they are named by number or by name (B1 thiamine, B2 riboflavin, B3 niacin, B6 pyridoxine, B9 folate, B12 cobalamin).
- [ ] G2 Main shared function: coenzymes: they help the enzymes that release energy from carbohydrate, fat and protein, so "vitamin B is necessary for the release of energy" (2011 and 2018 Ordinary). They also keep the nervous system, skin and red blood cells healthy.
- [ ] G3 Being water-soluble, they are not stored in useful amounts (B12 is the exception), so they are needed daily; excess is lost in urine; they are lost into cooking water and in storage.
- [ ] G4 Main food sources of the group: wholegrain cereals and wholemeal bread, fortified breakfast cereals, meat (especially pork and liver), fish, eggs, milk and dairy, pulses, nuts, yeast extract, green vegetables. White flour and polished rice lose much of their B vitamins in milling; wholegrains keep them.
- [ ] G5 RDA: the amount that meets the needs of almost everyone; B vitamin needs rise with energy intake (especially thiamine and niacin, which are linked to energy release). No amounts.
- [ ] G6 [HL] Properties of the group: water-soluble; most are sensitive to heat, light, alkalis or oxidation, and they are lost in cooking water; riboflavin and niacin are the more stable ones; individual properties below.

Vitamin B1: thiamine
- [ ] T1 Sources: wholegrain cereals and wholemeal bread, fortified breakfast cereals, pork and bacon, liver, pulses (peas, beans, lentils), nuts, yeast extract, eggs, potatoes.
- [ ] T2 Functions: a coenzyme in releasing energy from carbohydrate (so its need rises with carbohydrate intake); keeps the nerves working normally and the muscles, including the heart, healthy; supports appetite.
- [ ] T3 Deficiency: beri-beri: loss of appetite, tiredness, weakness, muscle wasting, nerve damage (tingling, numbness, pain, paralysis in "dry" beri-beri); heart failure and swelling (oedema) in "wet" beri-beri. Heavy drinkers are at risk because alcohol reduces absorption and the diet is often poor (can lead to confusion and memory problems). Beri-beri is the vitamin B1 deficiency disease asked in 2004 and 2025 Higher.
- [ ] T4 [HL] Properties: water-soluble; destroyed by heat (prolonged cooking), by alkalis (bicarbonate of soda), by sulphur dioxide (sulphites) used as a preservative, and lost into cooking water; stable in acid conditions.

Vitamin B2: riboflavin
- [ ] R1 Sources: milk and dairy products (the largest contribution in many diets), eggs, liver and kidney, lean meat, yeast extract, green leafy vegetables, mushrooms, fortified breakfast cereals.
- [ ] R2 Functions: helps release energy from carbohydrate, fat and protein; keeps the skin, the lining of the mouth and eyes, and the mucous membranes healthy; helps growth.
- [ ] R3 Deficiency: sore, cracked lips and corners of the mouth (cheilosis, angular stomatitis); a sore, red, smooth tongue; skin problems (greasy, scaly skin around the nose); sore eyes sensitive to light; slow growth in children.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Properties: water-soluble; yellow-green (it gives the yellow colour of some foods and urine); stable to heat and acid in normal cooking, but destroyed by light (ultraviolet), which is why milk is better stored in cartons or opaque bottles than in clear glass; destroyed by alkalis; some is lost in cooking water.

Niacin (vitamin B3, nicotinic acid)
- [ ] N1 Sources: meat (especially liver, poultry), fish, wholegrain cereals, peanuts and other nuts, pulses, mushrooms, eggs, milk, fortified breakfast cereals, yeast extract. The body can also make niacin from the amino acid tryptophan found in protein foods such as milk and eggs, which is why milk and eggs prevent deficiency even though they contain little niacin.
- [ ] N2 Functions: helps release energy from carbohydrate, fat and protein in the cells; keeps the skin, nervous system and digestive system healthy.
- [ ] N3 Deficiency: pellagra, remembered as the four Ds: dermatitis (a skin rash in areas exposed to sunlight), diarrhoea, dementia (confusion, memory loss) and, if untreated, death; also tiredness, weight loss and irritability (accepted by the 2021 Higher scheme). Pellagra was historically found where maize was the main food.
- [ ] N4 [HL] Properties: water-soluble but the most stable of the B vitamins: stable to heat, light, air, acid and alkali in normal cooking; the loss is mainly into cooking water if the food is boiled and drained.

Vitamin B6: pyridoxine
- [ ] P1 Sources: chicken, turkey, pork, fish (tuna, salmon), liver, eggs, bananas, potatoes, pulses, wholegrain cereals, nuts, fortified breakfast cereals.
- [ ] P2 Functions: needed for the use (metabolism) of amino acids and so for protein use and growth; helps form haemoglobin in red blood cells; helps make neurotransmitters (chemicals that pass messages between nerve cells) so it supports the nervous system.
- [ ] P3 Deficiency: rare; skin rashes and sore mouth, anaemia, irritability, depression and confusion; tingling in the hands and feet.
- [ ] P4 [HL] Properties: water-soluble; stable in acid conditions; destroyed by light and by alkali; fairly stable to heat in ordinary cooking, but lost into cooking water, and lost in processing and milling of cereals.

Past-paper matching (deficiency conditions)
- [ ] M1 Match each condition to its vitamin: night blindness is A; rickets is D; scurvy is C; beri-beri is B1; pellagra is niacin (B3); neural tube defects are folic acid; megaloblastic anaemia is B12 (or folate) deficiency; pernicious anaemia is B12 deficiency from lack of intrinsic factor. Give the matched pairs as a fact card.

## 3. Traps

- "Vitamin B" is a group, not one vitamin. A question that says "vitamin B" (2018 Ordinary gap-fill) expects the idea of energy release; a question that names niacin or thiamine expects the individual vitamin.
- Beri-beri is spelled with a hyphen in the Irish marking schemes; accept both, write beri-beri.
- B12 and folate are not covered here, although they belong to the B complex.
- Do not confuse niacin (B3, pellagra) with riboflavin (B2, cracked mouth corners) or thiamine (B1, beri-beri). Do not say pellagra is caused by lack of vitamin C or D.
- Most B vitamins are not stored, but B12 is (liver). Do not say "none of the B vitamins is stored".
- Do not use: any RDA, mg or microgram figure, or the tryptophan-to-niacin conversion ratio, because these are changing or unchecked numbers and no scheme asks for them.
- Do not use: "B vitamins give you energy" in the sense that they themselves supply energy, because they only help release energy from carbohydrate, fat and protein; the energy comes from the food.
- Do not use: that white bread or flour is fortified with all the B vitamins in Ireland, because the fortification rules were not checked; say only that wholegrain products keep the B vitamins that refining removes.
- Neighbouring subsections: B12 and folate; vitamin C; the cereal chapter (B vitamins in the wheat grain, wholemeal versus white flour); metabolism chapter (coenzyme role in energy release).

## 4. What exams ask

- Match deficiency conditions with vitamins (2025 Higher: night blindness A, rickets D, beri-beri B1; 2021 Higher: niacin pellagra, vitamin D rickets or osteomalacia; 2013 Higher; 2004 Higher).
- Complete statements about the B group: "vitamin B is needed for the release of energy from carbohydrate and fat" (2018 Ordinary), "thiamine and energy" (2011 Ordinary), folic acid and neural tube defects (2018 Ordinary).
- Name the two classes of vitamins and give an example of each (the B group is the usual water-soluble example); name two water-soluble vitamins (2005 Ordinary).
- Functions and sources of one named B vitamin within a 6-mark or longer part: expect two functions, one deficiency effect and one or two sources.
- Higher Level properties part: expect solubility in water, loss in cooking water, and the destroyer for each (thiamine heat and alkali, riboflavin light, niacin stable).
- A food-list or menu question (e.g. a breakfast cereal label) can ask which B vitamins are listed and why wholegrain or fortified foods help.
