Fermentation as a preservation method and the preservation principle belong to the preservation chapter; here it is a production tool.

## 1. Must cover

What fermentation is
- [ ] F1 Fermentation: the breakdown of organic substances (usually sugars) by bacteria or yeast in the absence or shortage of oxygen (2009H scheme: "breakdown of organic substances by bacteria or yeast"). By-products include carbon dioxide, energy, acids (lactic, acetic) and alcohol.
- [ ] F2 Two main kinds in food: lactic acid fermentation (bacteria turn sugars such as lactose into lactic acid) and alcoholic fermentation (yeast turns sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide).

Bacteria (lactic acid bacteria)
- [ ] B1 Cheese: a starter culture of lactic acid bacteria is added to milk; they turn lactose to lactic acid, which, with rennet, curdles the milk to form curds and gives flavour. Ripening by further bacteria or moulds develops the flavour.
- [ ] B2 Yoghurt: milk fermented by Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus; lactose becomes lactic acid, so the milk thickens and tastes sour.
- [ ] B3 Probiotic (functional) yoghurts and drinks contain live bacteria that are claimed to benefit the gut; examples of functional foods (2015H scheme).
- [ ] B4 Meat: bacterial starter cultures in salami, pepperoni and chorizo ferment sugars to lactic acid, giving flavour and helping to preserve.
- [ ] B5 Vinegar: acetic acid bacteria (Acetobacter) oxidise alcohol to acetic acid.
- [ ] B6 Bacteria in food supplements (2015H scheme); one phrase.

Moulds
- [ ] M1 Blue-veined cheese: Penicillium roqueforti grows through the cheese giving blue-green veins and sharp flavour; example Cashel Blue (Co. Tipperary), also Roquefort and Stilton.
- [ ] M2 Surface-ripened soft cheese: Penicillium camemberti gives the white rind of Camembert and Brie.
- [ ] M3 Soy sauce: Aspergillus mould (A. oryzae) breaks down soya protein and starch (2015H scheme names Aspergillus for soy sauce).

Yeast
- [ ] Y1 Bread (and sourdough): yeast ferments the sugars in dough, giving carbon dioxide, which makes the dough rise; the alcohol evaporates in baking. Yeast is a biological raising agent (2018O).
- [ ] Y2 Beer: yeast ferments sugars from malted barley into alcohol and carbon dioxide.
- [ ] Y3 Wine: yeast ferments the sugars in grape juice to alcohol. Spirits are distilled from fermented liquids (one clause only if used).
- [ ] Y4 Yeast extracts and food supplements: a source of B vitamins (2008H scheme lists yeast for supplements).

Large fungi and novel foods
- [ ] N1 Edible fungi: mushrooms and truffles are large fungi eaten directly (2008H, 2015H).
- [ ] N2 Mycoprotein (Quorn): a novel protein food made by growing the fungus Fusarium venenatum in large fermenters; used as a meat substitute, high in protein and fibre, low in saturated fat (stable and described qualitatively).
- [ ] N3 Overall benefits of using micro-organisms: flavour, texture, longer keeping time, nutritional value (B vitamins, protein), variety.

## 3. Traps

- Yeast is a fungus (a single-celled one), not a bacterium. Yoghurt and cheese bacteria are lactic acid bacteria, not yeasts. Do not mix them: 2018O asked students to choose "bacteria" for cheese and yoghurt and "yeast" for bread.
- Lactic acid alone does not set the curd in cheese-making; rennet is also used. Write acid together with rennet.
- Quorn is mycoprotein from a fungus, not from yeast or bacteria, and not a plant protein.
- Alcohol made in bread dough evaporates in baking; bread is not alcoholic.
- Vinegar needs two steps: yeast makes alcohol, then Acetobacter makes acetic acid. Give only the second as "Acetobacter" here.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the structure of yeast and moulds (moulds and yeasts); spoilage by yeast and mould (spoilage overview); fermentation as a preservation principle, pickling, and irradiation (preservation chapter); cheese-making stages and bread-making science (dairy and cereals chapters). Keep this subsection to named organism, action, product.
- Do not use: "Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus" as the only bacteria in all yoghurts: say "typically", because some yoghurts add other strains.
- Do not use: a founding date, producer name or milk type for Cashel Blue (only Co. Tipperary and the blue mould are checked), because sources disagree on the date.
- Do not use: "Lactobacillus bulgaricus" as a current scientific name without care; it is the name the exam uses (now classed as a subspecies of Lactobacillus delbrueckii), so give the exam name only.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline three uses of micro-organisms in food production (2015H 12 marks, 2022H 12 marks "assess"): pick three from yeast in bread or beer or wine, lactic acid bacteria in yoghurt and cheese, mould in blue cheese, bacterial cultures in salami, Quorn mycoprotein, soy sauce, edible fungi. Each use needs the organism, what it does and the food.
- Table completion (2014H): lactic acid bacteria -> cheese, yoghurt; Lactobacillus bulgaricus -> yoghurt; yeast -> bread, wine, beer.
- One use each for moulds, yeast and large fungi (2008H): cheese; bread, beer, wine or supplements; mushrooms, truffles or Quorn.
- Two uses (2005H); define fermentation and name two by-products (2009H); fill-in questions at Ordinary Level on cheese, yoghurt and bread (2018O, 2013O).
