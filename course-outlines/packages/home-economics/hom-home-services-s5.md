## 1. Must cover

Heat transfer (assumed science, needed for the principles)
- [ ] H1 (assumed) Heat is a form of energy that moves from warmer to cooler places in three ways. Conduction: heat passes through a solid as particles pass energy on; metals are good conductors; air, wool and polystyrene are poor conductors.
- [ ] H2 (assumed) Convection: in a liquid or gas, the warmed part expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler fluid sinks to replace it, setting up a current. Radiation: heat travels as infrared rays without needing a material, warming the objects it hits, not the air between.
- [ ] H3 (assumed) Solids, liquids and gases expand when heated; different metals expand by different amounts. This is the basis of the thermostat and of the expansion tank.

Thermal comfort
- [ ] TC1 Thermal comfort: the feeling that the temperature of the room is right for the people in it and what they are doing.
- [ ] TC2 Factors: air temperature, temperature of the surfaces round you (walls, windows, floor), humidity, air movement (draughts), clothing, and activity level.
- [ ] TC3 Recommended room temperatures differ by function (marking schemes): sitting room 19 to 23 °C, living room 17 to 21 °C, kitchen 15 to 19 °C, bathroom 16 to 19 °C, bedroom 10 to 16 °C; elderly people need at least 20 °C. Babies, older people and anyone ill need warm, steady temperatures.
- [ ] TC4 Control of thermal comfort: a heating system sized to the house; zoning (separate areas, each with its own control); timers; thermostats; thermostatic radiator valves; insulation and draught-proofing; clothing; controlled ventilation.

Thermostats and controls
- [ ] TH1 A thermostat is a temperature-sensitive switch: it switches the heat source off when the set temperature is reached and on again when it cools.
- [ ] TH2 Principle (bimetal strip): two metals, brass and invar, bonded together. Brass expands much more than invar when heated, so the strip bends away from the contact and breaks the circuit; as it cools it straightens and remakes the circuit. Diagram: a bimetal strip with brass and invar labelled, curved when hot, straight when cool, with contacts.
- [ ] TH3 Uses: room thermostat, boiler thermostat, cylinder thermostat, immersion heater, electric kettle (with its own control), iron, oven, electric blanket, refrigerator and freezer, electric shower.
- [ ] TH4 Room thermostat: placed in a main living area away from direct heat, switches the boiler or heating circuit on and off. Thermostatic radiator valve (TRV): fitted to one radiator, opens or closes to change how much hot water enters it, so each room can have its own level. Cylinder thermostat: keeps stored hot water at the set temperature.
- [ ] TH5 Programmer (timer): switches heating and hot water on and off at set times, so rooms are not heated when empty; mechanical, electronic or smart (phone) control. Timers plus thermostats prevent wasting fuel and overheating. Zoning: each area has its own timer and thermostat.

Heating options and choosing
- [ ] O1 Full central heating: one boiler heats radiators in the whole house and hot water. Partial: only some rooms (e.g. living areas). Background heating: low steady heat through the house with extra from local heaters. Local heating: individual heaters in one room (stove, gas fire, electric heater, open fire).
- [ ] O2 Wet system: boiler, pump, pipes, radiators (fuel: oil, gas, electricity, solid fuel). Dry system: storage heaters (off-peak night electricity heats blocks of fireclay or concrete which release heat during the day, with an afternoon boost) and electric panel or fan heaters.
- [ ] O3 Underfloor heating: pipes (water) or cables (electric) under the floor warm the floor, which heats the room by radiation and convection; leaves walls free; works well with low-temperature heat sources.
- [ ] O4 Heat pump (ground-source or air-source): a refrigerant takes heat from the ground, air or water, a compressor raises its temperature, and the heat is passed to the heating water; runs on electricity but delivers more heat than the electrical energy used; works best in a well-insulated house with underfloor heating or large radiators; qualifies for SEAI grants.
- [ ] O5 Solar heating. Passive: large south-facing windows, absorbed by walls and floors. Active: solar collectors heat a fluid that heats water stored in a cylinder (the wet central heating principle with solar input); biomass (wood pellet or wood chip) boilers are another low-carbon option.
- [ ] O6 Factors to consider when choosing (also 2012H scheme): installation, running and maintenance costs; fuel price and availability; efficiency; effect on the environment; size of house, insulation and number of people (heat output needed); family needs (children, elderly, health); control and convenience; space (boiler, tank, storage for fuel); safety; hot water needs; appearance; grants.

One system of central heating: the wet (small-bore, pumped, indirect) system
- [ ] W1 Components: boiler (fuel: oil, gas, solid fuel, electricity), pump, flow and return pipes (small bore), radiators, a feed and expansion tank in the attic with its overflow pipe, and a hot water cylinder with a heat exchanger coil.
- [ ] W2 Working principle: water heated in the boiler expands, becomes less dense and rises (convection); the pump drives it round the closed loop to the radiators; each radiator warms the room mainly by convection currents in the air with some radiation from its surface; the cooled water returns to the boiler to be reheated; some of the hot water passes through the heat exchanger coil in the cylinder (the cylinder is heated indirectly, hot water in the loop and the domestic hot water do not mix).
- [ ] W3 The expansion tank takes up the extra volume as water expands and tops up the system; the overflow pipe is an escape for steam or excess water. The thermostat and timer control the boiler.
- [ ] W4 Efficiency and convenience: a condensing boiler recovers heat from flue gases; an annual service; controls (programmer, room thermostat, TRVs, cylinder thermostat); heat for the whole house and hot water from one boiler.
- [ ] W5 Environmental impact: depends on fuel (see emissions); fuel-burning gives carbon dioxide; a flue removes gases; a boiler needs ventilation; renewable options (heat pump, solar, biomass) lower emissions.
- [ ] W6 Diagram: closed loop with boiler, pump, flow and return pipes, radiators, expansion tank in the roof space and cylinder, with arrows for flow.

## 3. Traps

- A thermostat switches a heat source; a timer switches by the clock; a TRV controls hot water flow to one radiator. A TRV does not work by the brass and invar strip as such; describe the bimetal principle for the thermostat only.
- Say the strip bends because brass expands more than invar, not because "the strip melts".
- In the pumped wet system the pump circulates the water; convection occurs in the boiler, in the radiator and in the room air. Do not credit the pump's job to convection alone.
- Radiators heat mostly by convection with some radiation; do not say they heat only by radiation.
- Active solar heating collects heat in a fluid; it is not photovoltaic electricity (see energy supplies).
- Background, local and partial heating are different ones: background is low steady heat; local is a heater in one room.
- Insulation and ventilation methods belong to their own subsections; electricity safety and the meter to the electricity subsection; solid fuels and oil as sources to the energy supplies subsection.
- Choose ONE system to describe in an exam, and name its fuel, working principle (transfer methods), control devices, and environmental impact.
- Do not use: "the boiler may be fired by solar energy", because solar collectors feed a cylinder; the boiler burns oil, gas or solid fuel or uses electricity.
- Do not use: "gas boilers are banned in new homes" or any date for such a ban, because Part L does not ban a fuel; it sets performance requirements that effectively exclude fossil boilers in new dwellings, and the policy and dates change.
- Do not use: any boiler efficiency percentage, because efficiencies change with the model.
- Do not use: "keep the heating on at a low setting rather than switching it off" as a general energy-saving rule, because it conflicts with timer and zoning advice; it applies only to frost protection when the house is empty or in very cold weather.

## 4. What exams ask

- Describe one central heating system with fuel, working principle including heat transfer, devices for thermal comfort and the impact on the environment (2006H, 2012H, 2016H; Ordinary Level 2007O recommends a system and gives two reasons). Expect the wet system: boiler, pump, radiators, expansion tank, cylinder; convection and radiation; room thermostat, TRV and timer; fuel's environmental effect.
- Factors in choosing a heating system (2012H): cost, safety, space, convenience, appearance, environment, hot water, house size, storage for fuel.
- Levels of thermal comfort and their control with room examples (2008H, 2012H, 2023H, 2026H): temperatures by room, the six factors, zoning, timers, thermostats, TRVs.
- Principle of a thermostat and uses (2008H, 2023H): bimetal strip, brass and invar, make and break; uses in kettles, irons, ovens, immersions, electric blankets, fridges, room and boiler thermostats, TRVs; a diagram may be requested.
- Timers and thermostats in heating (2019O, 2026O): three benefits, such as heat only when needed, less fuel used, steady temperature, smartphone control, lower bills.
- Heating options (full, partial, background, local, storage, solar, geothermal, biomass) are named as the "type" in the central heating question.
