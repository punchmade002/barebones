## 1. Must cover

Primary structure (both levels)
- [ ] P1 Primary structure is the number and sequence (order) of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.
- [ ] P2 The amino acids are linked by peptide bonds; the chain is a polypeptide (one clause; bond detail is in the composition subsection).
- [ ] P3 (assumed) Different proteins differ in which amino acids they contain and in what order, which is why proteins have different properties.
- [ ] P4 Primary structure decides how the chain can fold later, so changing one amino acid can change the protein's shape and behaviour.
- [ ] P5 Model/diagram: blocks or beads of different colours, one colour per type of amino acid, joined in a row, labelled as amino acids linked by peptide bonds. (Settings item.)

Secondary structure [HL]
- [ ] S1 [HL] The polypeptide chain is further linked and folded by bonds between different parts of the chain, giving the protein a definite shape, often a spiral (coil) or pleated sheet.
- [ ] S2 [HL] The main bond is the hydrogen bond: the hydrogen in one part of the chain is attracted to an oxygen in another part (same chain or a neighbouring chain). Each bond is weak; many together hold the shape.
- [ ] S3 [HL] The Irish schemes also name disulphide links and salt bonds as ways the chains are linked; see T2.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Example: collagen, where polypeptide chains are linked by hydrogen bonds.

Tertiary structure [HL]
- [ ] T1 [HL] The three-dimensional folding of the whole polypeptide chain into a compact unit; the pattern of folding gives the protein its final shape.
- [ ] T2 [HL] Bonds that hold it: disulphide links (two sulphur atoms from two cysteine amino acids, each with an –SH group; can join two parts of one chain or two chains), hydrogen bonds, and salt bonds (attraction between oppositely charged side groups).
- [ ] T3 [HL] The folded shape may be fibrous (long, straight, coiled or zig-zag) or globular (compact, roughly spherical). One line only.
- [ ] T4 [HL] The shape is held by many weak bonds, so heat, acid and whisking can break them; breaking secondary and tertiary structure is denaturation (one clause).
- [ ] T5 [HL] Diagram: a folded chain with the disulphide, hydrogen and salt links between sections labelled.

Comparison
- [ ] C1 Three-row summary: primary = sequence; secondary = local coiling held by hydrogen bonds; tertiary = overall folding held by disulphide, hydrogen and salt bonds. The 2014 scheme asks for exactly these three.

## 3. Traps

- Primary structure is the sequence, not the shape. Shape is secondary and tertiary.
- Peptide bonds are not broken by denaturation; denaturation breaks the weak bonds of secondary and tertiary structure.
- Hydrogen bonds are weak; disulphide links are stronger. Do not call a disulphide link a hydrogen bond.
- Cysteine (with an –SH group) is the amino acid that forms disulphide links.
- Do not add quaternary structure, hydrophobic interactions or protein-folding research.
- Fibrous and globular as protein classes, their examples and foods belong to the classification subsection.
- The denaturation process, its causes and cookery examples belong to the properties subsection.
- Peptide bond formation and hydrolysis belong to the composition subsection.
- Do not use: collagen as the named example for tertiary structure, because the schemes use collagen for hydrogen-bonded chains (secondary); give "fibrous or globular" for tertiary and avoid a conflicting example.
- Do not use: any figure for how many bonds hold a named protein, because no source for such a figure was checked.

## 4. What exams ask

- Ordinary: explain primary protein structure (2 marks): number and sequence of amino acids in the polypeptide chain.
- Higher: describe primary and secondary structure (2006H, scheme 3 points at 4 marks each per structure): primary as sequence joined by peptide links with water released; secondary as further links giving a definite shape (often spiral) with hydrogen bonds, disulphide links and salt bonds, and collagen as the example.
- Higher long question (2014H, 47 marks across classification, supplementary value and structure): structure scored as three points at 5 marks each, with disulphide and hydrogen bonds named and tertiary described as 3D folding into a compact fibrous or globular unit.
- Exam tips to build: say "sequence" for primary; name the bond at each level; for disulphide links name cysteine and the –SH groups; link structure to shape and denaturation in one sentence.
