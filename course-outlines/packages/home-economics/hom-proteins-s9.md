# Package: hom-proteins-s9 — Alternative (novel) protein foods

- **loId:** `hom-proteins-s9` · **chapterId:** `hom-proteins` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.2 (meat, fish, eggs and alternatives)
- **Syllabus scope:** the alternatives to meat as a food commodity: nutritional significance, contribution to the diet, selection, storage, preparation, cooking and processing. Also examined: plant and micro-organism sources and products (2024H, 2012H); stages in manufacture of one (2022H, 2018H); tofu and other vegan protein alternatives (2021H, 2016H, 2018H); protein bars and high-protein products evaluated from data (2026H, 2023O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. No [HL] marks in this subsection. The 6-mark table question (source and product) and the 12–20 mark manufacture question are the two main targets. Manufacture stages are given exactly as the 2018H and 2022H schemes set them (TVP and mycoprotein). Vegetarian and vegan diets belong to the diet and health chapter; protein bars are here because the papers set them as protein alternatives and high-protein products.

## 1. Must cover

Sources and products
- [ ] S1 Novel (alternative) proteins replace meat, fish or eggs: from plant sources and from micro-organisms.
- [ ] S2 Plant source: soya beans. Products: tofu, tempeh, TVP (textured vegetable protein), miso, soya milk, soya yoghurt, soya cheese, soya flour, soya sauce, soya oil.
- [ ] S3 Other plant products: seitan (made from wheat gluten), pulses (lentils, chickpeas, beans), nuts and seeds, quinoa, edamame, hummus.
- [ ] S4 Micro-organism sources: fungi, bacteria, yeast, algae. Product: mycoprotein (from a fungus) sold as Quorn in mince, pieces, burgers and sausages; nutritional yeast (a yeast product used for flavour and as a vitamin B source when fortified).

Soya products: how made
- [ ] P1 Tofu: soya milk is curdled and the curds pressed into blocks; bland, takes flavour from marinades or smoking.
- [ ] P2 Soya milk: soya beans soaked, ground with the soaking water, then strained.
- [ ] P3 Tempeh: cooked soya beans fermented by a mould; firm and chewy.
- [ ] P4 Miso: a paste of soya beans, with rice or other grain, salt and water, fermented.
- [ ] P5 Soya yoghurt: soya milk fermented.

Manufacture (learn at least one fully)
- [ ] M1 TVP: soya beans cleaned and de-hulled; oil extracted; beans ground into flour; carbohydrate removed; vegetable oil, flavouring, seasoning and nutritional additives (vitamin B12, iron, methionine) added; mixture heated and extruded; cut or cubed (mince, chunks); dried; packed and labelled.
- [ ] M2 Mycoprotein: a fungus is grown (fermented) in optimum controlled conditions; the cells are harvested, filtered and drained; a binder (egg albumen in the traditional product) holds the fungal sheets together; flavour and colour are added; textured to resemble meat; sliced, cubed or shredded and steamed to set the binder; packed and labelled.

Nutritional significance and contribution to the diet
- [ ] N1 Soya protein is high biological value; soya products also supply polyunsaturated fat, fibre, B group vitamins, and calcium and iron (calcium especially in tofu set with calcium salts).
- [ ] N2 Mycoprotein is high in protein and fibre and low in saturated fat; plant alternatives contain no cholesterol.
- [ ] N3 Advantages: HBV protein, low saturated fat, fibre, suit vegetarians and vegans, relatively inexpensive, versatile, little preparation, reduce reliance on meat.
- [ ] N4 Cautions: vitamin B12 is not found in plant foods (vegans need fortified foods or supplements); processed products may be high in salt, sugar or additives; soya is a labelled allergen, and Quorn contains egg in some products.
- [ ] N5 Contribution to the diet: sit in the Food Pyramid shelf of meat, poultry, fish, eggs, beans and nuts; can replace meat in meals, as meat extenders, in vegetarian and vegan diets.

Selection, storage, preparation, cooking
- [ ] C1 Selection: read the label (ingredients, allergens, nutrition information, use-by); compare salt, sugar and fat in processed alternatives.
- [ ] C2 Storage: dried TVP and pulses airtight in a cool, dry place; tofu and mycoprotein chilled (or frozen), once opened covered and used within the label's days; follow pack instructions.
- [ ] C3 Preparation: TVP rehydrated in hot stock or water; tofu drained and pressed; pulses soaked and boiled (tinned ready).
- [ ] C4 Cooking: TVP in bolognese, chilli, shepherd's pie; tofu marinated and stir-fried or baked; mycoprotein cooked thoroughly per pack; they take on the flavour of what they are cooked in.

Evaluating protein bars and high-protein products
- [ ] B1 Compare per portion: protein, energy, sugar, saturated fat, salt, fibre; "high protein" on the pack is not the same as healthy.
- [ ] B2 Bars often use milk (whey) or soya protein, which are HBV; some add sugar, chocolate coating and saturated fat; they are portable for sport.
- [ ] B3 High-protein yoghurt gives HBV milk protein with calcium and B vitamins; fruit preparations raise free sugar; full-fat versions have more saturated fat; low-fat may have more added sugar; plain yoghurt with fresh fruit is less processed.
- [ ] B4 EU nutrition claims: "source of protein" needs at least 12% of the food's energy from protein; "high protein" needs at least 20% (Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006).
- [ ] B5 Reading a data table: pick the product with most protein and fibre and least energy, sugar and salt for the stated person (e.g. a young person who plays sport).

## 3. Traps

- The fungus in mycoprotein is grown by fermentation; it is a micro-organism, not a plant. Quorn is a brand name; the generic term is mycoprotein.
- TVP is made from soya (defatted flour), textured by extrusion; it is not tofu. Tofu is curd; tempeh is fermented whole beans; miso is fermented paste.
- Seitan is wheat gluten, so it is unsuitable for coeliac diets; soya is a labelled allergen.
- Not all alternatives are vegan: traditional mycoprotein products use egg albumen as binder; vegan versions use a different binder. Say "check the label".
- Plant-based alternatives are not automatically healthy; ultra-processed versions can be high in salt and additives.
- Vegan, vegetarian and coeliac diets, and nutrient deficiencies in them, belong to the diet and health chapter; HBV/LBV and complementation belong to the biological value subsection; food additives law and labelling law belong to the food legislation subsection.
- Do not use: "Fusarium graminearum" (or "gramineurum") as the Quorn fungus, because the scheme spelling is garbled and the species name has been revised; say "a fungus", unnamed.
- Do not use: "meat, fish and alternatives" as the name of the Food Pyramid shelf, because the pyramid's shelf is named meat, poultry, fish, eggs, beans and nuts.
- Do not use: any figure for protein, salt or energy of named branded products, because they change with recipes; use only figures given in a question's table.

## 4. What exams ask

- Complete a table of source and product (Higher 2012, 2024; 6 marks): plant, soya beans, with TVP, tofu, tempeh, miso, soya milk; micro-organisms (fungi, bacteria, yeast, algae), with mycoprotein/Quorn.
- Name two protein alternatives for a vegan diet and give two advantages (2021H, 2016H, 6 marks): TVP, tofu, tempeh, mycoprotein, seitan, lentils, beans, quinoa; HBV protein, polyunsaturated fat, fibre, B vitamins, calcium, iron; not very expensive, versatile, little preparation.
- Name and describe two novel protein foods (2018H, 8 marks) and outline the manufacture of one (2018H 12 marks; 2022H 20 marks incl. name, stages): five or more stages for TVP or mycoprotein, in order.
- Evaluate protein bars and yoghurts from label data with healthy eating guidelines (2026H 20 marks; 2023O 24 marks): protein per serving, energy, sugar, fat, fibre, salt; HBV protein; recommend one product with reasons.
- A vegan diet's strategies against protein deficiency (2022H): complementary proteins, soya, Quorn.
- Exam tips to build: "Learn the stages of TVP in order"; "Name the micro-organism source as a fungus"; "Compare per portion, not just the protein claim."
