# Package: hom-family-society-s5 — Family functions

- **loId:** `hom-family-society-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-society` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.4
- **Syllabus scope:** "physical, economic, emotional, educational and social functions; how these functions may be adopted or supplemented by other social institutions." Settings: brainstorming all the functions a family carries out and classifying them into the five categories. Also examined: how the State supports the family in each function (2025H, 2024O, 2014H, 2010H). Common to Ordinary and Higher Level; no [HL] items. Past papers set it more often than anything else in this chapter: 2004H, 2006O, 2010H, 2012, 2014H, 2015O, 2019O, 2024O, 2025H.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The examiners ask for "describe three or four functions" and, usually, "explain how the State (or other institutions) helps". Marks go for the function named and described plus the State support named and explained. Note the old papers' vocabulary: "emotional" is also called "nurturing" or "psychological"; "social" is also "socialisation". The notes must use the five names the syllabus gives and mention the alternative terms once. Welfare amounts are not given.

## 1. Must cover

Overview (one block)
- [ ] O1 A family function is something the family does for its members and for society. The five functions: physical, economic, emotional, educational and social. Functions remain constant across societies even when family structures change (2006O wording); other institutions can adopt (take over) or supplement (help with) a function. Classifying activities: one activity may serve more than one function.

Physical function (one block)
- [ ] P1 The family provides for the physical needs of its members: food, warmth, shelter, clothing, rest and a safe environment.
- [ ] P2 Procreation (reproduction): the family continues the population and gives children their first physical home.
- [ ] P3 Protection: the family protects the vulnerable, including young children, older people, the sick and people with disabilities.
- [ ] P4 Supplemented by: local authority housing, GPs and the public health service (HSE, public health nurses), hospitals, schools (school meals), foster care and Tusla.
- [ ] P5 State support: medical card, housing supports from local authorities, home support services for older people, GP and public health nursing, Tusla (child protection and family support), and welfare payments for living costs.

Economic function (one block)
- [ ] M1 The adults earn income to support dependants: children and other dependent family members.
- [ ] M2 Family members pay taxes and spend, contributing to the economy.
- [ ] M3 In some societies, and in Ireland in the past, large families worked together as an economic unit (a farm or business).
- [ ] M4 Managing the household budget: planning income and spending, saving.
- [ ] M5 Supplemented by: banks, credit unions and insurance companies (savings, loans, cover against loss); MABS (Money Advice and Budgeting Service), which helps families deal with debt and plan their budgets.
- [ ] M6 State support: social welfare payments such as Child Benefit, Jobseeker's payments, Carer's Allowance, disability payments, the Working Family Payment (formerly Family Income Supplement), the Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance, the State pension, medical cards, free school books and the school meals programme (all in the schemes). No amounts or thresholds.

Emotional function (one block)
- [ ] E1 The family provides love, security and a place where children develop self-esteem and express emotions and discuss feelings, to become well-adjusted people.
- [ ] E2 Parents support each other emotionally and act as role models; family members learn healthy relationships through daily contact.
- [ ] E3 Also called the nurturing or psychological function in some papers; same content.
- [ ] E4 Supplemented by: school counsellors, youth services, bereavement and relationship counselling, parenting courses, friends and community groups.
- [ ] E5 State support: parenting courses and programmes, family support services and psychological services (HSE, Tusla), and funding of voluntary and community groups providing counselling.

Educational function (one block)
- [ ] D1 The family is the primary educator: passing on beliefs, language, skills and customs through informal learning from birth.
- [ ] D2 Parents give praise, encouragement and a stimulating environment, supporting intellectual development.
- [ ] D3 Parents support formal school work (homework, reading, outings, interest in school) to help children reach their potential.
- [ ] D4 Supplemented by: pre-school, primary, second-level and third-level education, which take over formal teaching.
- [ ] D5 State support: the Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) free pre-school scheme, free education in recognised schools, special needs support (resource hours, special classes, special needs assistants, exam accommodations), third-level access schemes such as HEAR and DARE (2019O scheme), and the National Childcare Scheme.

Social function (socialisation) (one block)
- [ ] S1 Through observation, imitation and stimulation, children learn the norms, values, language, manners and behaviour that society accepts (primary socialisation).
- [ ] S2 Consistent discipline and fair boundaries help children develop a set of values, self-control and respect for others.
- [ ] S3 Supplemented by: schools, which continue socialisation through the hidden curriculum and SPHE, peer groups, youth organisations, sports clubs and the media (secondary socialisation).
- [ ] S4 State support: schools and pre-schools; special provision; if a family fails to socialise a child, the State deals with non-conforming individuals through the justice system (2014H scheme).

Classifying activities (settings, one short block)
- [ ] B1 Brainstorming: list the activities of a family in a week and classify them: cooking a meal (physical, and social if it teaches table manners), reading together (educational, and emotional), paying bills (economic, and educational for children), comforting a child (emotional), teaching manners (social). The aim is to show that a function can be served by one activity and that activities overlap.

How institutions adopt or supplement functions (one block)
- [ ] I1 Adopt (take over fully): foster care, residential care, boarding schools, schools taking over formal teaching. Supplement (support): the examples listed above. Which functions the family keeps and which it shares has changed over time: formal education, health care and many economic activities have moved out of the family; the emotional and social functions remain mainly with the family.
- [ ] I2 Institutions that help: the State (HSE, Tusla, the Department of Social Protection, the Department of Education), schools, credit unions and banks, voluntary organisations, churches and community groups.

## 3. Traps

- Social is not "social life". It means socialisation: learning the norms and values of society. Do not mix it up with the emotional function (love, security, self-esteem).
- Procreation belongs to the physical function in the schemes (reproduction, survival of the species), not the emotional one.
- The economic function is not "the family has money"; it is earning to support dependants, paying taxes and spending.
- Do not name the State support without saying how it helps the function. Exam answers need both: the support named and linked.
- Name a different support for each function; marks are lost for repeating "welfare payments" for all.
- State supports are not family functions; they supplement them.
- Neighbouring subsections: the structures of family belong to "Modern family structures"; the history of how functions have changed belongs to "The historical development of the Irish family"; the physical and psychological needs of children, communication and conflict belong to the chapter on the family as a caring unit; the legal protection of the family (maintenance, the family home, barring orders) belongs to the family law chapter. Do not describe those here.
- Do not use: any figure for Child Benefit, the Working Family Payment, medical card limits, childcare subsidies or income thresholds, because these change each budget.
- Do not use: the Family Law (Maintenance of Spouses and Children) Act 1976 or the Family Home Protection Act 1976 as "State support for family functions", because they are family-law protections for the family (taught in the family law chapter) and are not social supports for a function.
- Do not use: "Community Mothers" or similar named local programmes from old schemes, because their current status is unconfirmed; say "family support services and parenting programmes".
- Do not use: the "One-Parent Family Payment" as an example of support for all families, because it applies only to lone parents and the rules change; mention it only under lone-parent families.
- Do not use: "home help service" as the current name, because the HSE service is now called home support; if the old name is given, say "home support (formerly home help)".
- Do not use: "the State provides free GP care for all", because medical cards and GP visit cards depend on eligibility.
- Do not use: "children until 18, or 23 if in full-time education" as the age limit for dependants, because it is doubtful and could not be verified against the marking schemes.

## 4. What exams ask

- "Describe four (or three) functions of the family" (2004H 16 marks, 2006O 24, 2010H 24 with State support, 2012 20 marks, 2015O 28, 2019O 15, 2024O 20): one named function, then one or two sentences with specific content. Marks per function run from 4 to 8: for example, physical (food, warmth, shelter, clothing, protection of the vulnerable, procreation), emotional (a safe, secure home where children develop self-esteem and express feelings, parents as role models), educational (primary educator, passing on beliefs and customs, praise and a stimulating environment, supporting school), social (learn acceptable behaviour by imitation and observation, consistent discipline), economic (earning to support dependants, paying tax, spending).
- "Explain how the State assists the family with these functions" (2010H, 2012, 2014H, 2024O, 2025H, 5 to 15 marks): one named support per function plus how it helps: economic (Child Benefit and other welfare payments), educational (ECCE, schools, special needs supports), emotional (parenting courses, family support services, counselling), physical (medical cards, HSE services, local authority housing, Tusla), social (schools and the hidden curriculum, the justice system).
- "Explain how one function has been supplemented by other institutions" (2006O, 10 marks): choose one function and name an institution, saying what it does, such as schools for the educational function.
- Link question: the statement that functions remain constant despite changes in structure (2006O). The answer: structures vary but the five functions persist.
- Tip: lay out each answer as function, description, State or institutional support.
