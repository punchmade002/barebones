# Package: hom-family-caring-s2 — Child–parent relationships

- **loId:** `hom-family-caring-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-caring` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.6
- **Syllabus scope:** physical and psychological needs of young children and adolescents and how the family meets them; the rights of children within the family; conflict between adolescents and adults and how it can be dealt with; the importance of good communication within the family. Settings: role-play on communication and avoiding or dealing with conflict. Also examined: 2024H, 2018H, 2016H, 2012H, 2010O. No [HL] marks in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** Sonnet-class model, 2026-11-23. Common to Ordinary and Higher Level. Roles of parents and adolescents are the previous subsection; older people, grandparents and generation conflict are the next.

## 1. Must cover

Physical needs of young children (one block)
- [ ] N1 Food (balanced and suited to growth); shelter and warmth; clothing; sleep and rest; health care (check-ups, immunisation, prompt treatment of illness); hygiene (washing, teeth, toilet training); safety and protection (supervision, safe surroundings, protection from abuse). Each with why it matters.

Psychological needs of young children (one block)
- [ ] N2 Love and affection; security and trust (a dependable, consistent carer); attachment (a strong bond with a consistent carer, the basis of later relationships); praise and encouragement (builds self-esteem); stimulation and learning (play, talk, reading); routine and boundaries (self-control); social contact (learning to relate to others). Each with its effect on development.
- [ ] N3 Why meeting needs matters: well-adjusted, confident adults able to contribute to society; unmet needs lead to insecurity, poor self-esteem, difficulty in relationships (one or two lines; the exam phrases it as "well-adjusted adults").

Needs of adolescents (one block)
- [ ] N4 Physical: growth spurt (more energy, protein, calcium and iron, no amounts); sleep; adequate activity. Psychological and social: independence (increasing freedom with limits), identity and self-esteem, peer acceptance, privacy, emotional support (being listened to without judgement), guidance on risks.

How the family meets these needs (one block)
- [ ] M1 Daily routines (meals, sleep, homework); modelling behaviour and values; teaching self-care, manners and safety; health monitoring and medical care; responsiveness to distress; the family as the first place for language, values and early learning (primary socialisation, assumed).
- [ ] M2 Also examined: the family meets physical, emotional, economic and social needs of members (2021H) belongs to family functions; here confine to children's needs. The State also assists (see N5).
- [ ] N5 State support for children, in brief (2010O and 2005H asked how the State helps): education through schools, Child Benefit (a regular payment to parents for each child, no amount), medical cards, supports for disadvantaged pupils in school, and child-protection services through Tusla. One short block only, no figures.

Rights of children within the family (one block; Also examined 2010O, 2005H)
- [ ] C1 The main rights, in plain terms: life, a name and a nationality; to be cared for by parents and live with them unless this is contrary to the child's interests; protection from abuse and neglect; education and full development (physical, mental, social, moral, spiritual); rest, play and leisure; maintenance (financial support) from parents; to be heard in matters that affect the child, in line with age and maturity; to see both parents where parents live apart (marking scheme 2005H).
- [ ] C2 Source of these rights: the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989, which Ireland ratified in 1992) and the Irish Constitution, which since the Children Amendment (Article 42A) states the State's duty to protect children's rights. Name them in one line without article numbers.
- [ ] C3 Rights come with parents' duties; the State steps in only where a child's welfare requires it. The Child Care Act 1991 gives the State, through Tusla, the Child and Family Agency, powers to protect children at risk (care orders and emergency care orders; the child may be placed with foster carers). One short bullet; the Act itself is taught in the family law chapter.

Conflict between adolescents and adults (one block; Also examined 2004O marking scheme, 2012H)
- [ ] K1 Causes: adolescents seek independence and feel parents are "out of touch"; peer and media influence on values; adolescents given adult responsibilities but not adult freedoms; relationships and friends taking priority over schoolwork and family; parents struggling to let go; curfews, technology, money.
- [ ] K2 Dealing with it: firm but fair, consistent discipline with explained rules; listening and defining the problem; negotiation and compromise, with a trial period and review; an atmosphere where adolescents can talk freely; parents using their experience while still hearing the young person; trust given gradually.
- [ ] K3 Role-play (Settings): a short method for acting out a conflict (e.g. curfew) and trying listening, "I" statements and compromise.

Importance of good communication (one block; Also examined 2026O, 2004O)
- [ ] M3 Members express needs, feelings and opinions and feel heard and secure; problems discussed openly before they become serious; bottled-up feelings lead to resentment and stress; open discussion lets work, spending and care be shared fairly; explaining reasons makes rules acceptable; children learn language, empathy and negotiation; praise from older members builds confidence; secrecy and aggressive criticism cause mistrust and conflict.
- [ ] M4 Skills: active listening (no interrupting, calm language, checking understanding), taking turns, family meetings. Barriers: lack of time, screens, differences in age, tone.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "HSE child and family services work with families to prevent harm", because child protection and family support moved to Tusla, the Child and Family Agency, in 2014. Say Tusla.
- Do not use: "Right to basic privacy and to equal treatment within the family and outside the home" as worded, because it is vague and not a clear right of the child list; say "protection from discrimination" or omit. Privacy is acceptable in the adolescent needs block (N4).
- Do not use: "Children's Allowance" as the current name; Child Benefit is the current payment (the 2005 marking scheme uses the old name).
- Physical needs are not the same as psychological; keep separate lists, and make sure each point says why it matters, since 2010 and 2004 schemes credit explanation.
- Needs, rights and roles overlap: roles (what the parent does) belong to the previous subsection; here frame points as the child's need or right.
- Generation conflict with older people (the next subsection) is a different topic from adolescent-parent conflict; do not mix the dealing-with strategies but note both rely on communication.
- Functions of the family and the other needs of family members (economic, social) belong to the family-structure chapters.
- Child Care Act 1991 detail (care order types, procedure) belongs to the family law chapter (Higher Level there); keep to C3 only.
- Adolescent nutrition: do not give RDA amounts.

## 4. What exams ask

- Physical and psychological needs of young children and how parents provide them; HL wording ties this to well-adjusted adults who contribute to society (2024H, 2016H): food, clothing, shelter, protection, plus love, security, praise, stimulation, with a sentence on the effect of each.
- Four rights of children within the family, each with a short account (2010O, 2005H): life, name and nationality, living with parents, protection from abuse, education, leisure, development.
- Explain how relationships change during adolescence and suggest how conflict with parents is dealt with: independence, peers and media influence, firm but fair rules, communication, negotiation.
- Discuss the importance of good communication within the family (2026O, 2004O): feelings aired, problems solved, mutual respect, shared decisions, building confidence.
- State support for disadvantaged children (2010O) and for rearing children (2005H): education, Child Benefit, medical card, child protection; one example with how it helps.
- Each tip should say: name the need or right, then explain the effect on the child.
