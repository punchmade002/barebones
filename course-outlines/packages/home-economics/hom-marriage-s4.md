# Package: hom-marriage-s4 — When a marriage breaks down

- **loId:** `hom-marriage-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-marriage` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "marriage counselling, family mediation, legal separation, legal nullity, divorce." No Higher Level only items.
- **Also examined:** conditions for divorce under the Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996, as amended by the Family Law Act 2019 (2015H, 2006H); also asked: Family Mediation Service (2008O), options for couples in difficulty (2017H, 2016O, 2005O), separation and divorce (2022O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. Judicial separation is a court order under a separate Act and belongs to the Family Law chapter; here "legal separation" means the separation agreement (deed of separation), with a single clause saying a court can order a judicial separation instead. The 1996 Act is cited with its 2019 amendment: use "two of the previous three years", never the older "four of five".

## 1. Must cover

Marriage counselling
- [ ] C1 Counselling aims to help a couple resolve problems before they become grounds for separation or divorce.
- [ ] C2 It is confidential; the partners may attend together, separately or, where relevant, with their children.
- [ ] C3 Several visits may be needed before the couple reach a resolution. The counsellor facilitates discussion and may offer advice but does not decide for the couple.
- [ ] C4 Providers: Accord and other independent counselling services; counsellors may refer a couple to a specialist for particular problems such as addiction or difficulties in sexual relations.

Family mediation
- [ ] M1 A confidential service in which an impartial, trained mediator helps a couple who have decided to separate or divorce to negotiate their own agreement, rather than having a court decide. The mediator does not take sides or decide.
- [ ] M2 Issues dealt with: parenting arrangements, custody and access, finances and maintenance, the family home and other property.
- [ ] M3 Benefits: encourages communication, keeps children out of conflict, helps partners accept the separation, is less costly, less adversarial and quicker than court. The Family Mediation Service of the Legal Aid Board is State-funded and free; private mediators charge.
- [ ] M4 The outcome is a written agreement that each partner can bring to their solicitor to be made legally binding (assumed).

Legal separation
- [ ] S1 The spouses agree to live apart while remaining married, and set out the terms in a deed of separation, a legally binding written agreement.
- [ ] S2 Typical terms: living apart, care and access arrangements for the children, maintenance, and the family home.
- [ ] S3 A solicitor draws up the deed (each spouse should have their own), and a mediator may help them reach the terms. It can be done without going to court if both agree.
- [ ] S4 The spouses are still married, so neither can remarry; only a divorce or a nullity decree allows that.
- [ ] S5 Where the spouses cannot agree, a court can grant a judicial separation (one clause; the Act is in the Family Law chapter).

Legal nullity and church annulment
- [ ] N1 Legal nullity: a court (Circuit Court or High Court) declares that the marriage was never valid in law. The parties are then free to marry other people.
- [ ] N2 Grounds are limited: a void marriage (for example one party already married or in a civil partnership, within the prohibited degrees, or the formalities not followed), a lack of capacity or free consent, or a voidable marriage (for example one spouse cannot sustain a normal marital relationship, including inability to consummate).
- [ ] N3 Church annulment: a Church marriage tribunal declares the marriage invalid in the eyes of the Church. It has no legal effect in the State: the couple remain legally married, and a civil nullity decree or divorce is still needed to remarry civilly.
- [ ] N4 Do not confuse nullity with divorce: nullity says the marriage never validly existed; divorce ends a valid marriage.

Divorce
- [ ] V1 Divorce is a court decree that legally ends a valid marriage; both spouses are then free to remarry.
- [ ] V2 Introduced after the 1995 referendum, by the Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996.
- [ ] V3 Conditions (Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996 as amended by the Family Law Act 2019): the spouses have lived apart for at least two of the previous three years; there is no reasonable prospect of reconciliation; proper provision has been or will be made for each spouse and any dependent children.
- [ ] V4 The Family Law Act 2019 shortened the period from four of the previous five years to two of the previous three years (in force 1 December 2019).
- [ ] V5 The court can make orders on custody and access, maintenance, the family home and other property, and succession rights (extinguishing a spouse's right to inherit from the other).
- [ ] V6 A decree of divorce is final and allows remarriage. A couple do not have to be legally separated first; they can apply for a divorce directly. The couple can live apart under the same roof. (Assumed, from Courts Service guidance; give as one line.)
- [ ] V7 The couple must also have a link to Ireland: living here permanently, or one of them having lived here for at least one year before applying (assumed; one line).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "four of the previous five years" as the living-apart condition, because it was the pre-2019 rule and 2015 and 2006 marking schemes still show it; the Family Law Act 2019 changed it to two of the previous three.
- Do not use: "Marriage and Relationship counselling services is free of charge" or any cost claim for counselling, because the cost was not confirmed (the 2017 scheme says "free of charge" and may be out of date).
- Do not use: any waiting time, number of sessions, fee or statistic for mediation, counselling, nullity or divorce, because they change.
- Do not use: the old notes' wording that a legal nullity makes the parties free to remarry "without committing bigamy" as the only reason, because it is not needed; say nullity frees them to marry again.
- Do not use: the old notes' statement that a divorce decree "cannot be reversed" as legal doctrine, because it is unconfirmed; say the decree is final and lets each spouse remarry.
- Do not use: a "separation agreement" as the same thing as judicial separation, because judicial separation is a court order.
- Do not use: Family Law Act 1995 grounds for judicial separation (adultery, desertion, unreasonable behaviour) here, because judicial separation belongs to the Family Law chapter.
- Mediation is not counselling: counselling helps a couple decide whether to stay together; mediation helps a couple who have decided to part agree terms.
- Legal separation is not divorce: the spouses remain married and cannot remarry.
- A church annulment is not a legal nullity (N3).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the legal requirements for a valid marriage and void or voidable status (first subsection); pre-marriage preparation (preparing for marriage); the Maintenance, Family Home Protection and Judicial Separation Acts and barring orders (Family Law chapter).

## 4. What exams ask

- "Evaluate, or give an account of, marriage counselling, family mediation and legal separation" (18 marks in 2017H; 24 marks for four options in 2005O; 15 marks for one option in 2016O): for each, name it and give two points. Counselling: confidential, resolve problems before separation or divorce, together or separately, several visits. Mediation: impartial mediator, negotiate own agreement, issues covered, keeps children out of conflict, avoids court. Legal separation: deed through a solicitor, covers children, maintenance and home, still married. "Evaluate" needs a strength and a limit, for example separation lets the couple live apart but they cannot remarry.
- "Explain how the Family Mediation Service helps" (10 marks, 2008O): impartial mediator; negotiate custody, parenting, finances, home and property; communication; children kept out of conflict; helps partners accept the separation.
- "Give an account of legal separation and divorce" (20 marks, 2022O): separation, binding deed through a solicitor, mediation, avoids court; divorce, ends the marriage after living apart two of the previous three years, with provision for the spouse and children.
- "Outline the conditions required for a divorce under the 1996 Act" (3 conditions at 3 marks each, 2015H): living apart, no reasonable prospect of reconciliation, proper provision; award marks for the current "two of three years".
- Occasionally nullity or annulment, and church annulment versus legal nullity (each earns marks for the difference in legal effect).
- Each of these can become an exam tip: name the service or Act, give two distinct points, and state who decides (the couple in mediation and in a separation deed; a court in nullity and divorce).
