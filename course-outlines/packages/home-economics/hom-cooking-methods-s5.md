## 1. Must cover

Selecting pans and cooking equipment (one block)
- [ ] E1 Choose for the job, the quantity, the hob type, durability, ease of cleaning and cost; buy equipment that will be used often and is easy to store.
- [ ] E2 Pan material: aluminium and copper conduct heat quickly and evenly; stainless steel is durable and doesn't react with acid foods but conducts heat less well, so good pans have a base bonded with aluminium or copper; cast iron holds heat well and suits slow cooking but is heavy and rusts; glass and ceramic dishes suit oven cooking and conduct slowly; non-stick coatings allow frying with little fat but scratch and degrade if overheated or scraped with metal.
- [ ] E3 Pan design: a heavy, flat, thick base avoids hot spots and sticking; a close-fitting lid saves energy and keeps steam in; handles should be firm, heat-resistant and (for oven use) oven-safe; the pan base should match the ring size.
- [ ] E4 Induction hobs need a pan with a magnetic base.
- [ ] E5 A set of basic equipment for a practical kitchen: saucepans of different sizes, a frying pan, a roasting tin, casserole, baking tins and trays, mixing bowls, a sieve, colander, wooden spoons, spatula, whisk, grater, measuring jug, scales and measuring spoons, rolling pin, peeler, tin opener, a timer and a thermometer.
- [ ] E6 Measuring: weigh and measure accurately with scales, jugs and spoons; ingredients measured in metric; level spoon measures.

Knives and chopping boards (one block)
- [ ] K1 Types and uses: cook's knife (general chopping), paring knife (small work and peeling), bread (serrated) knife, carving knife.
- [ ] K2 A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one because it needs less force and slips less. Use the claw grip: fingertips curled in, knuckles guiding the blade. Keep the board steady (damp cloth underneath). Carry knives pointing down; never leave them in washing-up water; store in a block or on a rack.
- [ ] K3 Colour-coded chopping boards (raw meat, fish, vegetables, cooked foods) prevent cross-contamination; wash boards well after raw meat; replace badly scored boards.

Safe use in the kitchen (one block)
- [ ] S1 Hob: turn pan handles inwards; use the right size ring; never leave hot fat unattended; keep tea towels and flexes away from the hob; lift lids away from you to avoid steam scalds.
- [ ] S2 Oven: use dry oven gloves (wet cloths transmit heat); place shelves before heating; open the door carefully; use a stable tray for hot dishes.
- [ ] S3 Electrical: dry hands; keep flexes away from water and heat; unplug before cleaning or removing food from blades; don't use a damaged flex or plug; don't overload sockets.
- [ ] S4 Pressure cooker: check seal and valve; never force the lid; let pressure fall before opening. Microwave: microwave-safe containers, no metal, cover and stir. (One line each; principles of these appliances are in the appliances chapter.)
- [ ] S5 General safety: clean spills at once; keep a fire blanket and know how to use it; never use water on a fat fire.

Care and cleaning (one block)
- [ ] C1 Wash in hot soapy water or the dishwasher, rinse, dry and store clean and dry so bacteria and rust do not develop.
- [ ] C2 Don't put cold water into a hot pan (warping, cracking); don't use metal tools or harsh abrasives on non-stick; season or oil cast iron lightly after drying; soak burnt pans before scrubbing; wooden items are not left to soak.
- [ ] C3 Cloths, sponges, tea towels and brushes harbour bacteria; wash or replace them often.
- [ ] C4 Follow the manufacturer's care instructions; store knives safely, pans stacked with protection for non-stick surfaces.

Equipment in practical coursework (one block)
- [ ] W1 Area B assignments ask the student to describe new equipment or a new skill; the planning section lists key equipment. A student should be able to name equipment for a dish, say why it was chosen and how it was used safely and hygienically (link only; coursework is covered in the final subsection).

## 3. Traps

- Appliance theory (working principles of a microwave, refrigerator, mixer, motor appliances, large and small appliance study) belongs to the appliances chapter; here cover only selecting, using safely and caring for equipment.
- Hygiene rules in the kitchen (hand washing, cross-contamination, cleaning schedules) belong to the food safety chapters; mention colour-coded boards and cloths in a sentence only.
- Heat transfer principles belong to the second subsection of this chapter; "conducts heat" is enough.
- Don't say stainless steel is the best conductor; it isn't.
- Don't call all non-stick coatings safe at any temperature; say they are damaged by overheating and metal tools.
- Do not use: any claim about health risks of aluminium or non-stick coatings, because no checked source is in this package; keep to practical care.
