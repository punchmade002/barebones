## 1. Must cover

Classification overview
- [ ] O1 Proteins are classified by what they are made of and where they come from. Two main groups: simple proteins and conjugated proteins. (A third group, derived proteins, formed by chemical or enzyme action on another protein, appears in one Ordinary scheme: mention in one line with caseinogen/casein digested by rennin as the scheme's example, and gelatine from collagen.)
- [ ] O2 Marking schemes also accept classification as animal (high biological value) and plant (low biological value) protein; say this in one line and point to the biological value subsection by title.

Simple proteins
- [ ] S1 Simple proteins are made of amino acids only: on hydrolysis they give amino acids and nothing else.
- [ ] S2 Animal simple proteins come from meat, fish, eggs, milk and dairy foods; plant simple proteins come from cereals, pulses, soya, nuts and seeds. Give a food example for each (poster setting).
- [ ] S3 [HL] Fibrous animal proteins: long strands, tough, insoluble, structural. Examples: collagen (connective tissue of meat and fish), elastin (elastic tissue, meat), myosin (muscle); also keratin (hair, nails).
- [ ] S4 [HL] Globular animal proteins: compact, roughly spherical, most soluble in water. Examples: albumin (ovalbumin in egg white), lactalbumin (milk), myoglobin (meat).
- [ ] S5 [HL] Glutelins (plant): proteins of cereal grains that dissolve in dilute acid or alkali but not water. Examples: glutenin in wheat, oryzenin in rice.
- [ ] S6 [HL] Prolamines (plant): proteins of cereal grains that dissolve in 70–80% alcohol but not water. Examples: gliadin in wheat, zein in maize (corn). Together gliadin and glutenin make gluten when wheat flour is mixed with water.

Conjugated proteins
- [ ] C1 A conjugated protein is a protein part combined with a non-protein part, the prosthetic group; on hydrolysis it gives amino acids plus the non-protein part.
- [ ] C2 Lipoproteins: protein with a lipid, e.g. in cell membranes and egg yolk, blood lipoproteins.
- [ ] C3 Phosphoproteins: protein with a phosphate group, e.g. casein in milk.
- [ ] C4 Nucleoproteins: protein with a nucleic acid, e.g. in chromosomes and cell nuclei.
- [ ] C5 Chromoproteins: protein with a coloured non-protein part, e.g. haemoglobin (prosthetic group haem, which contains iron).
- [ ] C6 Table layout: group, sub-group, example, food source, exactly as the 2014H scheme sets it out (poster/revision table).

## 3. Traps

- Simple does not mean "easy": a simple protein is made only of amino acids; a conjugated protein has a non-protein part.
- Casein is a phosphoprotein (conjugated) in milk; albumin is a simple globular protein. Do not swap them.
- Gliadin is a prolamine and glutenin is a glutelin; together they form gluten. Do not call gluten itself a prolamine.
- Myosin is listed in Irish schemes as fibrous; actin is also a muscle protein (both are covered under sources).
- Haemoglobin is a chromoprotein because of its haem group.
- "Classification" in an exam answer: give both the simple/conjugated groups and (for Higher) the sub-groups; animal/plant, HBV/LBV is also accepted by schemes but belongs to the biological value subsection.
- Which foods contain each protein belongs to the sources subsection; roles in the body belong to the biological functions subsection; the shape differences (fibrous and globular folding) are mentioned in the structure subsection.
- Do not use: lecithin as an example of a lipoprotein, because lecithin is a phospholipid, not a protein, even though the 2014H scheme lists it.
- Do not use: chlorophyll as an example of a chromoprotein, because chlorophyll is a pigment, not a protein, even though the 2014H scheme lists it. Use haemoglobin.

## 4. What exams ask

- Account of protein under "classification" (Ordinary 2013, 2018, 2023, 8 marks, two classes at 4 marks each): simple (animal and plant) and conjugated (protein part combined with a non-protein part, e.g. haemoglobin), or HBV/LBV. Each class needs a one-line definition plus an example.
- Higher 2014 (part of 47 marks): a table of group, sub-group, example and food source: simple animal fibrous and globular, simple plant glutelins and prolamines, and the four conjugated types, each with named examples. Six rows is the usual target.
- Ordinary 2006 style: name two proteins in meat (collagen, myosin, elastin, actin, albumin).
- Exam tips to build: "Define simple and conjugated in one line each"; "Learn one named example and food for each of the four conjugated types"; "Gliadin and glutenin are the wheat proteins; zein is maize."
