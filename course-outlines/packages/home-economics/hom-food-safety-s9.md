# Package: hom-food-safety-s9 — Food legislation

- **loId:** `hom-food-safety-s9` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-safety` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.7
- **Syllabus scope:** Food legislation [1.3.7]: a brief outline of the protection given to the consumer by current national and European food legislation, to include:
  - food hygiene: Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 on the hygiene of foodstuffs, given effect in Ireland by the European Communities (Hygiene of Foodstuffs) Regulations 2006 (amended 2009 and 2010);
  - **[HL]** food labelling: Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 on the provision of food information to consumers, given effect by the European Union (Provision of Food Information to Consumers) Regulations 2014;
  - **[HL]** the general framework of food law: the Food Safety Authority of Ireland Act 1998 and Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 (general food law);
  - **[HL]** official controls: Regulation (EU) 2017/625, given effect by the European Union (Official Controls in Relation to Food Legislation) Regulations 2020.
- **Settings:** students research one piece of legislation and build a shared file.
- **Also examined:** the European Communities (Hygiene of Foodstuffs) Regulations 2006, amended 2009 and 2010 (2025H); food labelling regulations (2010H); a 2008 Ordinary Level question named the superseded Food Hygiene Regulations 1950 to 1989 (do not teach).
- **Level:** food hygiene is common to both levels. Labelling, general framework and official controls are Higher Level only; those blocks' headings end " (Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Use only these instruments (June 2027 list, outline Appendix A). The 2025H question asks what protection the 2006 Regulations give the consumer (12 marks), so that block must be complete. Give section and article numbers nowhere.

## 1. Must cover

How the law fits together (assumed, one block)
- [ ] L0 (assumed) An EU regulation applies directly in every member state; Ireland also makes national regulations (statutory instruments) to give effect to it, set penalties and name the enforcing bodies. The Oireachtas passes Acts such as the FSAI Act 1998. The purpose of all of it is consumer protection: safe, honestly described food.

Food hygiene (common to both levels; 2025H)
- [ ] H1 Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 on the hygiene of foodstuffs lays down hygiene rules for food businesses at all stages after primary production; it is given effect in Ireland by the European Communities (Hygiene of Foodstuffs) Regulations 2006, amended in 2009 and 2010.
- [ ] H2 Sets standards of hygiene for food premises, equipment, food handling, water supply, waste and personal hygiene of handlers.
- [ ] H3 Requires food businesses to put in place and operate a food safety system based on HACCP principles (link to the HACCP subsection).
- [ ] H4 Requires staff to be trained and supervised in food hygiene and to follow hygiene procedures.
- [ ] H5 Requires food businesses to be registered with the relevant competent authority.
- [ ] H6 Allows authorised officers (for example environmental health officers) to inspect, and to close premises or have food destroyed where public health is immediately threatened.
- [ ] H7 How it protects the consumer: food is prepared in clean premises by trained staff under a system that prevents contamination, and unfit food can be taken off sale.

Food labelling (HL; 2010H)
- [ ] B1 [HL] Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 on the provision of food information to consumers, given effect in Ireland by the European Union (Provision of Food Information to Consumers) Regulations 2014. It applies from 13 December 2014 (the nutrition declaration from 13 December 2016).
- [ ] B2 [HL] Main compulsory information on pre-packed food: name of the food; list of ingredients in descending order of weight at the time of manufacture; allergens emphasised in the ingredient list (14 listed allergens); net quantity; date of minimum durability (best before) or use-by date; any special storage conditions or instructions for use; name and address of the food business operator; country of origin or place of provenance where omitting it would mislead (and for certain meats and fresh produce); alcoholic strength for drinks over 1.2% alcohol; a nutrition declaration (energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, salt).
- [ ] B3 [HL] The 14 allergens: cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphur dioxide and sulphites (above a set level), lupin, molluscs.
- [ ] B4 [HL] Use-by (on highly perishable food; unsafe after the date) versus best-before (quality only).
- [ ] B5 [HL] Labelling must not mislead the consumer about the nature, identity, properties, origin or method of production of the food; nutrition and health claims are regulated separately (name only).
- [ ] B6 [HL] How it protects the consumer: informed choice, allergy safety, safe storage and use, and traceability to a named business.

General framework of food law (HL)
- [ ] G1 [HL] The Food Safety Authority of Ireland Act 1998 set up the FSAI and gave it the functions of coordinating food safety law and its enforcement; it names the official agencies that carry out controls under service contracts. It replaced the old Sale of Food and Drugs Acts (the syllabus's 1875 to 1936 Acts) as the framework; do not teach those.
- [ ] G2 [HL] Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, the general food law: sets the general principles and requirements of food law across the EU; food that is unsafe must not be placed on the market; responsibility for safety rests with the food business; food and feed must be traceable one step back and one step forward; it set up the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and the Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed.
- [ ] G3 [HL] Risk analysis has three parts: risk assessment (scientific, by EFSA), risk management (decisions by authorities), risk communication; the precautionary principle may apply where science is uncertain.
- [ ] G4 [HL] Consumer protection: a common high level of protection of health and consumer interests, from farm to fork, with fast withdrawal of unsafe food.

Official controls (HL)
- [ ] C1 [HL] Regulation (EU) 2017/625 sets the rules for official controls: checks by public authorities that food businesses comply with food law at every stage of the food chain. It is given effect in Ireland by the European Union (Official Controls in Relation to Food Legislation) Regulations 2020.
- [ ] C2 [HL] Official controls include inspection, sampling and analysis, and audit; they are regular and risk-based (higher-risk businesses are checked more often) and are carried out without prior warning in most cases.
- [ ] C3 [HL] In Ireland the FSAI is the competent authority with overall responsibility; the official agencies (HSE, DAFM, SFPA, local authorities) carry out controls on its behalf under service contracts (link to national agencies).
- [ ] C4 [HL] Consumer protection: independent checks on all food businesses, with enforcement action (improvement notices, closure, withdrawal) where law is broken.

## 3. Traps

- An EU regulation is not an Irish Act: do not call Regulation 852/2004 or 1169/2011 "Irish laws". The Irish statutory instruments give them effect.
- Do not mix the instruments: hygiene (852/2004) is not labelling (1169/2011); general food law (178/2002) is not official controls (2017/625).
- "Use-by" and "best-before" are different (safety versus quality).
- Do not teach the superseded Food Hygiene Regulations 1950 to 1989, the Labelling of Food Stuffs Regulations 1982 and 1991, the Sale of Food and Drugs Acts, the Health (Official Control of Foodstuffs) Regulations 1991 or Regulation (EC) No 882/2004, even where an older marking scheme names one.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the roles of FSAI, HSE and the other agencies (national agencies); HACCP stages (HACCP); additives and E-numbers (food additives chapter); consumer complaints and the Consumer Rights Act 2022 (consumer rights chapter); food labelling as a consumer tool (consumer studies).
- Do not use: "official controls are carried out by competent authorities, which are the FSAI and the official agencies it contracts" (old notes), because the FSAI is the competent authority and the agencies act on its behalf.
- Do not use: "Reg 1169/2011 requires 'storage conditions needed to keep it safe'" as a general rule; say "special storage conditions and conditions of use where needed".
- Do not use: penalties, fines or section and article numbers, because they change or were not checked.

## 4. What exams ask

- Describe the protection given to consumers by the European Communities (Hygiene of Foodstuffs) Regulations 2006, amended 2009 and 2010 (2025H 12 marks): hygiene standards for premises, HACCP-based system, staff training, registration, closure and destruction of food where health is immediately threatened.
- Food labelling regulations (2010H): items required on a food label and why each protects the consumer (ingredients, allergens, date marks, quantity, business name and address, nutrition declaration, storage).
- Older Ordinary Level short question (2008O) asked one function of the former Food Hygiene Regulations; answer now from the 2006 Regulations (premises hygiene, registration, safe handling, powers to close premises or destroy unfit food).
- Expect a three- or four-mark point each for the framework and official controls instruments at Higher Level: name, what it requires, who enforces it.
