# Package: hom-resource-management-s1 — Family resource management

- **loId:** `hom-resource-management-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-resource-management` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1
- **Syllabus scope:** [2.1] the purpose of resource management; management systems; the family as a managerial unit. No [HL] items in this subsection (common to Ordinary and Higher Level).
  - Also examined: purpose of family resource management (2015H, 2009H, 2005O); how good management systems contribute to a well-run home (2021H, 2006H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. This is the opening subsection of the chapter. It sets up the idea of resources, a management system and the family as a unit. The input, throughput and output model, decision-making, communication and routines are in "Components of management and decision-making". The factors that change how a family manages (life-cycle stage, employment, culture and so on) are in "Factors that affect management". Do not present inputs/throughputs/outputs as a framework here; one passing phrase ("goals, resources and results") is enough.

## 1. Must cover

What resource management is (one block)
- [ ] R1 Family resource management is using the resources a family has wisely and efficiently to achieve its goals. Marking schemes accept this wording for a 2-mark answer, so give it in one clear sentence.
- [ ] R2 A resource is anything a family can draw on to meet its needs and wants. Resources are limited, so choices have to be made about how they are used; that scarcity is why management is needed.
- [ ] R3 Goals are what the family is trying to achieve: short term (this week's meals, an outing) and long term (a home, education, retirement). Goals give management its direction.

Purpose of family resource management (one block; the most-examined item)
- [ ] P1 To use available resources efficiently in order to achieve goals.
- [ ] P2 To improve the quality of family life.
- [ ] P3 To enable each family member to reach their full potential.
- [ ] P4 Supporting detail, not separate purposes: it reduces waste of time, money, food and energy; it gets the best value from limited resources; it avoids conflict and stress by agreeing who does what.
- [ ] P5 One worked example of efficient management, since papers ask for one: a weekly meal plan with a shopping list (saves money and time, reduces food waste); budgeting; planned childcare; sharing household tasks; resolving a conflict by agreeing a timetable. One or two, each clearly linked back to "efficient".

Types of resource (one block)
- [ ] T1 Human resources: the people in the family and what they bring: time, energy, skills, knowledge, abilities.
- [ ] T2 Material (physical) resources: equipment, appliances, food, clothing, furniture and other goods.
- [ ] T3 Economic (financial) resources: income, savings, credit.
- [ ] T4 Environmental resources: the surroundings and community facilities that support family life (space, location, local services such as schools, transport and health care).
- [ ] T5 Resources other than money matter as much as money: a family with little money can still manage well using time, skills, knowledge and good planning. (The detailed treatment of resources other than money is in the next subsection.)
- [ ] T6 One phrase only: the management of the family's money is taught in the household-finance material.

Management systems (one block)
- [ ] M1 A management system is a planned, organised way of using resources to reach a goal or solve a problem. It turns resources (what you have) into results (what you wanted).
- [ ] M2 It is used for everyday areas (meal planning, money and home management, childcare, housework, shopping) and for one-off events and problems (a party, a holiday, moving house, choosing an appliance). Papers list meal planning, money/home management, problem solving and decision-making as uses of the management process.
- [ ] M3 A good system works because goals, standards and responsibilities are agreed in advance, tasks are shared, and the system is reviewed and changed when circumstances change (a new baby, job loss, a move).
- [ ] M4 A system is a cycle, not a one-off: the result is checked against the goal and what was learned improves the next attempt. (Name "feedback" in one phrase; the full treatment is in the next subsection.)

How good management systems contribute to a well-run home (one block; examined 2021H, 2006H)
- [ ] W1 Time is used well: routines make sure meals, laundry and school preparation happen on time with less stress.
- [ ] W2 Money is used well: budgeting and shopping lists reduce impulse buying, waste and debt.
- [ ] W3 Work is shared fairly: all members take part, so nobody is overloaded and each learns skills.
- [ ] W4 Agreed goals and standards give the family a shared direction, reduce conflict and support communication.
- [ ] W5 Energy, water and food are used carefully, which lowers running costs and reduces waste.
- [ ] W6 More time is left for rest, leisure and relationships, so quality of life improves.
- [ ] W7 The family copes better with change and emergencies because it has a plan and some resources in reserve.

The family as a managerial unit (one block)
- [ ] U1 The family is a small group that holds and uses resources together: members contribute income, time and skills, and all benefit. It sets goals, makes decisions, allocates resources and takes responsibility for the results, so it acts as a managerial unit.
- [ ] U2 Roles differ: adults usually plan, earn and coordinate; children and teenagers take on tasks and join decisions as they grow older. Management duties are therefore shared and change over time. (Roles through the life cycle are in "Factors that affect management".)
- [ ] U3 Individual wants must be balanced against the needs and goals of the whole household; the unit works best when decisions take everyone's views into account.
- [ ] U4 The Irish Constitution (Article 41) recognises the family as the natural, primary and fundamental unit group of society. Give as one supporting line only.
- [ ] U5 (assumed) The family and household are defined in the social studies material; use the word "household" without redefining it.

Diagram cues

## 3. Traps

- Resources are not only money. Students often write "resources = money"; make clear that time, skills, knowledge, people and equipment count.
- A "management system" (the planned way of reaching goals) is described later with the input-throughput-output model; do not list inputs, throughputs and outputs here.
- Do not explain budgets, income, expenditure, saving or credit in detail: that is household finance. Economic resources are named, not unpacked.
- Do not describe family types, functions or the sociology of the family; use "the family as a group that manages" only.
- Life-cycle stage, employment pattern, culture and values as influences belong to "Factors that affect management".
- The purpose needs the three points P1-P3; do not give only "to save money".
- Do not use: any household income, spending or time-use figure, because such figures change and are not in the syllabus.
- Do not use: "Article 41 says the family manages its own resources" (or any claim that the Article sets management duties), because it describes the family as a unit group of society only. Use U4 solely to support "the family is a unit".

## 4. What exams ask

- State or explain the purpose of family resource management (2, 6 or 12-mark parts): efficient use of resources to achieve goals; improved quality of family life; full potential of members. The usual 6-mark pattern is two purpose points plus one example of efficient management.
- Give one example of efficient family resource management: meal plan and shopping list, budget, childcare arrangement, shared household tasks.
- Describe how a good management system contributes to a well-run home: planned routines, money and time used well, tasks shared, shared goals, less conflict, more leisure.
- Name the types of resource a family has and give an example of each.
- Describe the family as a managerial unit: the group that sets goals, pools resources and shares decisions and tasks.
- Expected answer style: short points, each with a reason or an example, not a long essay.
