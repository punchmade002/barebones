# Package: hom-appliances-equipment-s1 — Household technology and home management

- **loId:** `hom-appliances-equipment-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-appliances-equipment` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "technological developments available to the household and their contribution to managing the home." Also examined: 2018H, 2018O, 2010O, plus the Higher 2009 "discuss how technology has contributed to the efficient management of the home". No [HL] items. Ordinary and Higher Level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. This is the opening subsection of the appliance chapter: it is about technology in general (appliances, controls, communication, security), not any one appliance. The individual appliance studies are in later subsections.

## 1. Must cover

Why technology matters to home management (one block, lead)
- [ ] T1 Home management means organising time, money, energy and effort to run the household efficiently. Technology contributes by cutting the work, time and energy a task needs and by improving results. (assumed: management of resources is taught in the resource-management chapter; use the idea, do not redefine it.)
- [ ] T2 Appliances reduce the workload: tasks that were done by hand (washing, drying, cleaning, food preparation) are done by machine, so they are less labour-intensive and take less time. Washing machine, dishwasher, vacuum cleaner and food processor are the usual examples.
- [ ] T3 Time saved and a better quality of home life: tasks finish quickly, which frees time for family, leisure, study or paid work; automatic programmes and timers let a task run unattended.
- [ ] T4 Easy-care and low-maintenance products (non-iron and stain-resistant fabrics, non-stick surfaces, smooth wipe-clean worktops) need less cleaning, ironing and upkeep.
- [ ] T5 Increased effectiveness of some tasks: steam cleaners lift dirt a dry cloth leaves behind and help remove bacteria with little or no chemical cleaner; a dishwasher washes at a higher temperature than hands can bear.

Energy and running-cost control (one block)
- [ ] E1 Programmable timers switch heating and hot water on only when needed, cutting fuel use and bills.
- [ ] E2 Zoned heating: separate controls (thermostats or thermostatic radiator valves) heat different parts of the house to different temperatures, so unused rooms are not heated; this gives comfort where needed and lower fuel bills.
- [ ] E3 Microcomputer (microprocessor, "computer chip") controls in appliances such as washing machines, cookers, microwaves and heating systems sense load, temperature or time and adjust the programme, so energy and water are matched to the task.
- [ ] E4 Energy-efficient appliances: microwave ovens heat small portions faster than a conventional oven and use less energy; appliances with a high A to G energy-label class are cheaper to run (the label itself is taught in the selecting-appliances subsection); LED lighting and standby-saving features cut electricity use.
- [ ] E5 Renewable energy at home: solar panels turn sunlight into electricity (photovoltaic) or heat water (solar thermal), reducing use of mains energy.

Home security and safety (one block)
- [ ] S1 Security technology: burglar alarms (sound on forced entry, can be monitored), CCTV (deters and records), movement-sensor security lighting, entry-phone or video doorbell, remotely operated gates.
- [ ] S2 Built-in safety devices protect users without constant attention: automatic cut-outs, thermostats, door interlocks and child locks in appliances; a residual current device (RCD) in the fuse board; smoke and carbon monoxide alarms warn of danger.

Communication, money and information (one block)
- [ ] C1 Communication: mobile phones, email, video calling and messaging keep family members in touch quickly across distances, and let the household get information and services without travelling.
- [ ] C2 Managing money: online and mobile banking (pay bills, check balances at any time), budgeting software or apps that record and categorise spending, internet shopping and price comparison.
- [ ] C3 Home computing: working or studying from home, saving commuting time and cost; the internet as a source of information (recipes, product reviews, consumer information).
- [ ] C4 Remote and "smart home" control: apps that switch heating, lights or appliances on or off from outside the home and check their status.

Costs and limits of technology (one block; useful depth for Higher Level answers, not HL-marked)
- [ ] L1 Initial cost has to be weighed against savings in time, energy and effort over the life of the product; add running costs, repair costs and replacement when machines fail.
- [ ] L2 Dependence on electricity and on working equipment: a power cut or breakdown stops the task; repairs of electronic appliances may need a qualified person; spare parts and servicing must be available.
- [ ] L3 Connected devices hold personal data and need secure passwords and updates; heavy screen use can reduce time together as a family.
- [ ] L4 Environmental effects: manufacture, energy use and disposal of electrical waste (disposal rules are in the selecting-appliances subsection).

Exam shape (one block)
- [ ] X1 Answer pattern: a point, a named technology and the benefit (for example, "timers on heating switch it on only when needed, lowering fuel bills"). Marking is by points at about 4 marks each (a Higher 20-mark question wants five points) or "state three ways" for 6 marks (Ordinary).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the brand name "Skype" (it appears in the 2018 Ordinary scheme), because the service has been retired; write "video calling" instead.
- Do not use: any figure for how much money or energy a technology saves, and any grant or tax relief for solar panels or smart controls, because these change and no checked figure exists.
- Do not use: "steam cleaners kill all germs" or "no chemicals are needed at all", because the claim is too strong; say steam helps remove dirt and bacteria with little or no chemical cleaner.
- Do not use: "Laser card" or "cheque" as current payment methods, because the Laser card ended in 2014 and cheques are rarely used; payment methods belong to the consumer-credit material.
- Do not give working principles or guidelines for use of a particular appliance here: motors, heating elements, refrigeration and the microwave have their own subsections. Mention an appliance only as an example of a benefit.
- Selection criteria, the energy label and sources of consumer information belong to the selecting-appliances subsection; mention the energy label in one phrase only.
- Budgeting, saving and banking products belong to the household-finance material; here online banking and budgeting software are examples of technology only.
- Central heating systems and their controls belong to the Home Design elective; here timers and zoning are examples of efficiency only.
- Do not claim technology only has benefits; Higher answers earn credit for one balanced drawback (cost, dependence, repair, data security).

## 4. What exams ask

- "State three ways technology has contributed to greater efficiency in the home" (Ordinary, 6 marks; 2018O, 2010O). Any three with a short example: appliances saving time and labour; timers and zoned heating lowering fuel bills; steam cleaners for hygiene; security systems deterring burglary; communication (mobile phone, video calling, internet banking) saving time.
- "Discuss how technology has contributed to the efficient management of the home" (Higher, 16-20 marks; 2018H, 2009H). Five developed points; the scheme rewards a spread across workload, time, quality of life, easy-care products, effectiveness, computer-controlled energy saving and security. Each point should name a technology and its effect.
- Expect examples tied to a task: laundry, cleaning, cooking, heating, security, money, communication. Good answers pair a technology with a benefit and, at Higher, add a limit.
- Tips to build: name the technology then the benefit; vary the areas (do not give five kitchen examples); for Higher, add one drawback; keep each point to one developed sentence.
