- **loId:** `hom-lipids-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-lipids` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.4
- **Syllabus scope:** animal, marine and vegetable sources; **[HL]** distribution of saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids in food. Settings: a poster of sources.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The classification itself (what animal/plant/marine and saturated/mono/poly mean) is in "Triglycerides and the classification of lipids"; fatty acid chemistry is in "Fatty acids"; fats and oils as a food commodity (butter, margarine, oils) is in "Fats and oils as a food commodity". This subsection is the lists of foods. Higher and Ordinary Level except the [HL] item.

## 1. Must cover

Animal sources (one block)
- [ ] A1 Meat and meat products: the visible fat on meat (bacon, fatty cuts of beef, lamb, pork), sausages, burgers and pâtés; the marbling inside meat is hidden fat.
- [ ] A2 Dairy foods: butter, cream, whole milk, cheese, ice cream; milk fat also carries vitamins A and D (fat-soluble).
- [ ] A3 Egg yolk (egg white has no fat); the yolk also holds lecithin.
- [ ] A4 Rendered and cooking fats: lard (rendered pig fat), suet (hard fat from around the kidneys of cattle and sheep), dripping (fat that collects from roasted meat).
- [ ] A5 Oily fish are animal foods but are classified as marine (see next block); avoid placing them under animal.

Vegetable (plant) sources (one block)
- [ ] V1 Vegetable oils: olive, sunflower, corn, soya, rapeseed (canola), sesame, groundnut (peanut), plus coconut and palm oil.
- [ ] V2 Fruit and seeds rich in fat: olives and avocados (fruit); nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, peanuts, Brazil nuts) and seeds (sunflower, sesame, flax/linseed).
- [ ] V3 Soya beans (a legume rich in fat and protein); cereals and cereal products supply small amounts (wheat germ, oats, the germ of the grain); chocolate and cocoa butter.
- [ ] V4 Processed plant fats: margarine, vegetable spreads, shortening, and the vegetable oils hidden in biscuits, pastries, crisps, fried and takeaway foods, ready meals.
- [ ] V5 2006O and 2008O schemes accept soya beans, cereals, avocado, nuts, margarine and olive oil as vegetable sources.

Marine sources (one block)
- [ ] M1 Oily fish: salmon, mackerel, herring, sardines, trout (tuna is accepted by the 2008O scheme); the fat is stored in the flesh and is rich in omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids.
- [ ] M2 Fish-liver oils (cod-liver oil), and fish-oil supplements; these also provide vitamins A and D.
- [ ] M3 White fish (cod, haddock, whiting) store their fat mainly in the liver, so the flesh is low in fat; shellfish are low in fat (shellfish accepted as marine source in 2004O).
- [ ] M4 Oily fish and omega-3 link to "Fatty acids"; give the benefit in one phrase only.

Distribution of saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids in food [HL] (one block)
- [ ] D1 [HL] Mostly saturated: butter, cream, cheese, lard, suet, dripping, fat on meat, coconut oil, palm oil, hard cooking fats.
- [ ] D2 [HL] Mostly monounsaturated: olive oil, rapeseed oil, avocado, many nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, peanuts).
- [ ] D3 [HL] Mostly polyunsaturated: sunflower oil, corn oil, soya oil, walnuts and seeds, oily fish.
- [ ] D4 [HL] Almost all foods are mixtures: even butter contains monounsaturated fat; olive oil contains some saturated fat; classification names the largest share.
- [ ] D5 [HL] Dietary meaning (2024H nutritional significance scheme): animal fats are generally saturated and associated with raised LDL; olive oil is monounsaturated; plant and nut oils are polyunsaturated and associated with HDL; the healthy swap is saturated for unsaturated.
- [ ] D6 [HL] Naturally occurring trans fat appears in small amounts in dairy and beef/lamb fat; industrial trans fat is limited in the EU (see Checked facts).

Visible and hidden fat (assumed, supports exam answers on reducing fat intake, 2016O, 2021O)
- [ ] H1 (assumed) Visible fat: fat on meat, butter, cooking oil, spread. Hidden fat: in cakes, pastries, biscuits, chocolate, crisps, fried foods, sausages, cheese, ready meals, takeaways.

Poster of sources (Settings) (one block)
- [ ] P1 Three columns headed animal, vegetable (plant) and marine, with named foods under each, the main fatty-acid type, and one health message per group.

## 3. Traps

- Egg yolk, not egg white, holds the fat.
- Fish: oily fish keep fat in the flesh (marine source, polyunsaturated, omega-3); white fish are low-fat and keep fat in the liver.
- Plant does not mean unsaturated: coconut and palm oils are mostly saturated. Animal does not mean saturated in every case: oily fish and some poultry fat contain much unsaturated fat; keep oily fish in the marine group.
- Butter is a source of fat (animal, saturated), not a "dairy and calcium" example here; the vitamin A in it is a link to "Functions and energy value" (fat-soluble vitamins), one phrase only.
- Margarine and spreads are processed; their production belongs to "Fats and oils as a food commodity". Only list them as plant-based here (the exam schemes accept margarine as a vegetable source).
- Sesame oil is a mixture of mono- and polyunsaturated fat; do not place it in a single class. High-oleic sunflower oil is mostly monounsaturated, so say "standard sunflower oil" if the class is stated.
- Do not use: any percentage fat figure, fatty acid ratio or g per 100 g for a named food, because values vary by product and brand and none is checked here.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name or list dietary sources of lipids under animal, vegetable and marine headings (2004O, 2006O, 2008O, 2022O): at Ordinary Level, one named food per class earns the mark; any of meat, butter, cream, cheese, eggs for animal; olive oil, sunflower oil, nuts, avocado, soya, margarine for vegetable; oily fish (mackerel, salmon, sardines, herring) and fish-liver oil for marine.
- Matching foods to the classification (2013O avocado, sardines, cheese; 2018O salmon, olive oil, butter): quick recall, so notes need clean one-line lists.
- Dietary sources as part of the 28-32 mark Ordinary Level account (2016O, 2021O) alongside classification and functions; and as "name one dietary source" with functions (2022O).
- Higher Level: the nutritional significance of fats and oils (2024H) expects linking sources to type: animal fats saturated and LDL-raising, olive oil monounsaturated, plant and nut oils polyunsaturated and HDL-associated.
- Practical choices: ways to cut fat intake (three ways, 2016O; four factors when selecting foods, 2021O) rely on knowing high-fat foods and lean alternatives (trim visible fat, lean meat, low-fat dairy, avoid fried and processed foods).
- The poster activity supports exam recall rather than being examined.
