# Package: hom-textiles-fibres-s4 — Fabric profiles

- **loId:** `hom-textiles-fibres-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-textiles-fibres` · **syllabusRef:** 5.2 (Elective 2)
- **Syllabus scope:** profiles of one fabric made from natural fibres, one made from manufactured fibres and [HL] one blend, to include fibre production and properties, identification by burning tests and microscopic evaluation, yarn production, and [HL] yarn or filament modification. Settings: observing and feeling fabrics; investigating fabrics for fibre origin, yarn production, construction and design application. Also examined: a fabric profile in almost every Elective 2 question (2026H, 2024H, 2023H, 2021H, 2017H, 2011H, 2010H, 2006H, 2004H); filament modification (2022H, 2008H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Elective 2 only. A profile is the most predictable Elective 2 part (15 marks Higher). The headings change each year (production, properties, identification test, uses, construction, yarn), so notes must give every heading for at least one natural, one man-made and one blend. Fibre classification is the previous subsection; weaving, knitting, finishes, dyeing and performance tests are in the next.

## 1. Must cover

How to answer a profile (one block)
- [ ] A1 Headings seen in past questions: fibre production, fabric properties, identification test, uses, fabric construction technique, fibre composition, yarn production. Name the fabric first (1 mark in older schemes), then give the points under each heading asked.
- [ ] A2 Terms: staple fibre (short) and filament (continuous); yarn (fibres or filaments twisted together); fabric (yarns woven or knitted). (assumed: fibre classification.)

Natural fabric profile: cotton (one block; cotton is the worked default)
- [ ] N1 Production: bolls on the cotton plant open when ripe; fibres picked by hand or machine; ginning removes the seeds; fibres are baled and graded by length, fineness and colour; cleaned and opened; carded (combed into a web then a sliver), optionally combed to remove short fibres; spun into yarn.
- [ ] N2 Properties: strong (stronger when wet), absorbent, cool and comfortable, washable, dyes well; creases, shrinks, burns easily, may mildew, little elasticity.
- [ ] N3 Burning test: burns quickly with a yellow flame, keeps burning when removed, smell of burning paper, soft grey ash.
- [ ] N4 Microscope: flat twisted ribbon with natural twists (convolutions); a central canal (lumen) is visible.
- [ ] N5 Uses: shirts, jeans, towels, bed linen, curtains, T-shirts.
- [ ] N6 Alternative natural fabrics accepted by examiners and the few points needed for each: wool (shearing, grading, scouring, carding, spinning; burns slowly, smell of burnt hair or feathers, black crushable ash; warm, resilient, absorbent, shrinks and felts); linen (flax stem, retting, scutching, combing, spinning; burns quickly, paper smell; strong, absorbent, creases); silk (cocoon, reeling of filaments; burns slowly, burnt-hair smell, crushable black bead; smooth, drapes).

Manufactured fabric profile: nylon (one block)
- [ ] M1 Production: two chemicals (monomers) in equal proportions are heated and join into a long polymer; the molten polymer is extruded through a spinneret, filaments cool and harden, then are drawn (stretched) to line up the molecules and add strength; wound on bobbins.
- [ ] M2 Yarn production: continuous filaments are twisted into filament yarn; yarn may be textured for bulk (see modification).
- [ ] M3 Properties: very strong, elastic, light, abrasion resistant, low absorbency, quick drying, crease resistant; builds static, may be hot to wear, flammable (melts).
- [ ] M4 Burning test: melts and shrinks away from the flame, forms a hard bead, chemical smell.
- [ ] M5 Microscope: smooth, even, round rod-like filaments with no scales or twists.
- [ ] M6 Uses: hosiery, swimwear, lingerie, jackets, carpets.
- [ ] M7 Alternatives accepted: polyester (viscous liquid extruded through spinneret, solidifies in cold air; strong, crease resistant, non-absorbent; burns and melts, sweetish smell, hard bead) and viscose (cellulose treated and dissolved, forced through a spinneret into a bath, weak when wet; burns like cotton).

Blended fabric profile: polyester-cotton (one block, [HL] only)
- [ ] B1 [HL] A blend combines two or more fibres in one yarn (or different yarns in warp and weft) to get the best properties of each. Examples: polyester-cotton, wool-nylon, cotton-Lycra, linen-cotton.
- [ ] B2 [HL] Production: each fibre is produced separately (cotton harvested, ginned, cleaned; polyester extruded and cut into staple), blended before or during carding, then drawn and spun into one yarn. Or fibres can be blended by using one fibre in the warp and another in the weft.
- [ ] B3 [HL] Properties: cotton gives absorbency and comfort; polyester adds strength, crease resistance and quick drying; easier to iron and cheaper than pure cotton (or linen-cotton is cheaper than pure linen).
- [ ] B4 [HL] Identification: burning shows mixed results and is indicative only; the microscope shows both fibre types in the one sample.
- [ ] B5 [HL] Uses: shirts, school uniforms, bed linen, curtains.

Yarn and filament modification (one block, [HL] only)
- [ ] Y1 [HL] Modification changes the yarn or filament to improve fabric properties. Marking scheme examples: blending, crimping, twisting, mercerising, brushing, flame or stain resistance, water repellence.
- [ ] Y2 [HL] Crimping or texturising: the filaments are crimped, looped or curled so the yarn is bulkier, warmer and softer; heat setting fixes the crimp.
- [ ] Y3 [HL] Twisting: more twist gives a stronger, smoother yarn; ply yarns are twisted together.
- [ ] Y4 [HL] Mercerising: cotton under tension is treated with caustic soda, giving more lustre, strength, absorbency and better dye take-up.
- [ ] Y5 [HL] Brushing raises the surface (a warmer, softer fabric).

Fibre identification (one block; an Investigation)
- [ ] I1 Burning test steps: hold a small sample with tongs or forceps, bring it slowly to a flame over a non-flammable surface; observe approach to flame, burning, removal from flame, smell, ash or residue; compare with a known sample (control).
- [ ] I2 Microscope: mount fibres on a slide, view under magnification and compare shape (twist, scales, rod, ribbon) with reference images.
- [ ] I3 Safety: ventilate, keep water and a fire blanket near, small samples, tie back hair.
- [ ] I4 Limits: finishes and dyes change results; blends give mixed results, so use both tests.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Crimplene" or any other brand for bulked yarn, because it is not examined and brand details are unconfirmed; say textured (bulked) polyester.
- Do not use: "nylon is made from a diamine and a dicarboxylic acid" as the only description, because it fits nylon 6,6 only; use "two chemicals joined into a long polymer" and optionally name hexamethylene diamine and adipic acid.
- Do not use: "round cross-section" for every man-made fibre, because shapes vary (viscose is serrated); say round for nylon and polyester only.
- Do not use: a single burning result for a blend, because the result is mixed; say indicative only.
- Mercerising is a treatment of cotton yarn or fabric, but the marking scheme lists it under filament modification; teach it there with that note.
- A blend is not the same as a mixed-fibre fabric made by joining two different fabrics; the fibres are combined in the yarn or in warp and weft.
- Ginning separates fibres from seeds; carding straightens fibres; combing removes short fibres; spinning twists to make yarn. Do not mix them up.
- Fibre classification (previous subsection) and weaves, finishes, dyeing and performance tests (next subsection) are separate. Give production, properties and tests for each named fabric only.
- Burning tests: smell descriptions are subjective; give the scheme words and do not invent detail.

## 4. What exams ask

- Write a profile of one natural fabric (production, properties, burning test, uses or construction) at 15 marks (2011H, 2024H, 2026H, 2021H, 2007H; Ordinary 2016, 2021: 9 marks).
- Write a profile of a manufactured fabric (production, properties, test; 2006H, 2010O, 2019O, 2022O).
- Write a profile of a blended fabric: fibre production for both fibres, how they are blended, properties of both, uses (2010H, 2017H, 2023H, 2004H).
- Explain a burning test for fibre identification with one example (2018H, 6 marks).
- Write a note on blended fabric and on filament modification (2008H); explain filament modification giving two examples (2022H).
