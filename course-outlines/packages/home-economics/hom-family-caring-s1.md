# Package: hom-family-caring-s1 — Roles and responsibilities in the family

- **loId:** `hom-family-caring-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-caring` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.6
- **Syllabus scope:** roles and responsibilities of family members and how they change through the family life cycle; gender issues in family roles; **[HL]** social and economic factors that have changed the roles of family members in recent times; **[HL]** role conflict. Settings: case studies or interviews with older people on how family roles have changed in the last fifty to one hundred years. Also examined: roles of adolescents (2021O, 2007O); parenting roles (2005H).
- **Prepared by:** Sonnet-class model, 2026-11-23. Common to Ordinary and Higher Level except the two [HL] blocks. The chapter's four subsections are: roles (this one), child-parent relationships, older people, special needs. Needs of children, rights, adolescent conflict and communication are the next subsection; grandparents' roles and generation conflict are the third.

## 1. Must cover

The family life cycle (one block)
- [ ] L1 A family passes through stages, and each stage changes who does what. Give the stages in order: couple / establishment; childbearing (expanding); child-rearing (children of school age and adolescents); launching (children leave home); later life (retired couple, one partner alone). Name each with its main roles and demands.
- [ ] L2 Workload and responsibility are highest in the childbearing and child-rearing stages and ease after launching; at later life members may move from giving care to needing it, and adult children take on practical, financial and emotional support (assumed link to older people in the next subsection but give one line).
- [ ] L3 Roles are not fixed: they are shaped by the stage, by each member's age, health, paid work and skills, and by social attitudes. A role is the pattern of behaviour expected of a person in a position (parent, child); a responsibility is a duty that goes with it.

Roles of parents (one block)
- [ ] P1 Provider (food, clothing, shelter, money), nurturer (love, emotional security), educator (skills, language, values), socialiser (norms and behaviour expected in the family and community), protector (safety, supervision, decisions in the child's best interests), manager (budget, meals, schedules, home), disciplinarian (boundaries and consequences, applied consistently and explained).
- [ ] P2 Parenting roles can be shared between two parents or carried by one; parenting style (firm and fair, explained rules) affects how children turn out (one line only).

Roles of adolescents (one block; Also examined 2021O, 2007O)
- [ ] A1 Contributing to the household: cooking, cleaning, shopping, minding younger siblings; managing own schoolwork, time and belongings.
- [ ] A2 Developing independence within agreed limits: choices about friends, leisure, part-time work; respecting family rules and the privacy of others in shared space.
- [ ] A3 Some adolescents give informal care to a sibling or grandparent. Exam answers also expect: sharing in family decisions, and treating roles as growing responsibility, not just chores.

Gender issues in family roles (one block)
- [ ] G1 Traditional segregated roles: men as breadwinners (instrumental roles), women as homemakers and main carers (expressive roles). Say what "segregated" means and contrast with "joint" or shared roles.
- [ ] G2 Today: more women in paid work, yet women often still carry the larger share of domestic work and childcare (the "double shift"/"second shift"); men's roles now include direct childcare, cooking and shared decisions; some fathers are the main carer.
- [ ] G3 Gender stereotyping of children (girls helping in the kitchen, boys outdoors) limits their experience; equal sharing of tasks reduces overload and resentment and models shared responsibility.
- [ ] G4 Adult daughters often provide more of the care of ageing parents than sons.

Social and economic factors that have changed roles [HL] (one block)
- [ ] F1 [HL] Social: more women in paid employment; more lone-parent families; smaller families; greater acceptance of cohabitation, separation, divorce and blended families (new roles such as step-parent); greater gender equality and changed attitudes; weaker extended-family and community support; longer life expectancy (the sandwich generation: adults caring for children and for ageing parents at once).
- [ ] F2 [HL] Economic: high housing, childcare and education costs make two incomes necessary, so the provider role is shared; long working hours and commuting reduce time for family meals, shared activities and housework; unemployment can reverse traditional provider and carer roles.
- [ ] F3 [HL] Technological: labour-saving appliances cut housework time so tasks can be divided differently; mobile phones and the internet allow remote working and contact but also blur work and home time (give each factor with its effect on roles, not just a list).

Role conflict [HL] (one block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] Definition: role conflict arises when the demands of two or more roles held by one person cannot all be met at once.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Types: work-family conflict (a parent needed at work when a child is ill); role overload (employee, parent, carer, homemaker together); conflict between partners over tasks or whose career comes first; intergenerational (caring for an older parent while raising children).
- [ ] R3 [HL] Consequences: fatigue, stress, less quality time, tension between partners, guilt. Ways to reduce it: agreeing the division of tasks, flexible working, shared decisions, help from extended family and services (e.g. childcare).

Investigation (one block, Settings)
- [ ] I1 How to run a short case study or interview with an older person on how roles have changed over fifty to a hundred years: questions to ask (who earned, who cared, who did housework, what children did, family size), and the kinds of change expected (more women in paid work, smaller families, shared tasks, appliances, more mobile adolescents). Present as a method, with no invented findings.

Diagram: none. Optional: a simple flow showing the five life-cycle stages in a row.

## 3. Traps

- Do not mix roles (what a person does) with needs (what a child requires). Children's physical and psychological needs and rights belong to the next subsection on child-parent relationships; give only the matching parent role here.
- Adolescent conflict with parents and how to resolve it belongs to the child-parent subsection; here only the adolescent's roles are taught. Grandparents' roles and generation conflict belong to the older people subsection; only one line on later-life roles here.
- Functions of the family, definitions of the family, universality, family types and the historical development of the Irish family belong to the family-structure chapters (sociological concepts); do not teach them here, only their effect on roles.
- Family law (barring orders, maintenance, separation) belongs to the family law chapter; mention only "lone-parent family" and "separation" as factors.
- Roles of adolescents answers should name roles and responsibilities, not generic teenage "needs".
- Role conflict (one person, many roles) is not the same as generation conflict or conflict between adolescents and parents (two people); keep them apart.
- Do not label HL material in the Ordinary-level blocks: only F1-F3 and R1-R3 are [HL]; the life cycle, parental and adolescent roles and gender issues are common to both levels.
- No Do-not-use lines: every fact in the current notes for this subsection is general and correct. They contain no names, Acts or figures to quarantine.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline (ordinary) or discuss (higher) the roles of adolescents in the family: expect 3-4 roles each with an explanation; contributing to the home, independence, schoolwork, respect for others.
- Describe parenting roles and how they have changed; expected points: provider, nurturer, educator, socialiser, protector; more sharing between parents; fathers more involved in childcare.
- Analyse how social and economic factors have changed roles (HL): usually four to five points, at least one social and one economic, each linked to an effect on who does what, e.g. dual earners leading to shared housework and paid childcare; lone parents carrying all roles; costs forcing two incomes; fewer children; appliances.
- Discuss gender roles: traditional versus current division of work and who carries the unpaid load; stereotyping; equality in the home.
- Explain role conflict and give examples with ways to reduce it (HL).
- The same points are credited in different wording, so a tip should say: name the factor, then state its effect on roles.
