# Package: hom-work-leisure-s8 — Evaluating leisure facilities

- **loId:** `hom-work-leisure-s8` · **chapterId:** `hom-work-leisure` · **syllabusRef:** 6.4
- **Syllabus scope:** [HL] "evaluation of the leisure facilities in the community, to include a cost and value comparison of two facilities." The whole subsection is Higher Level only (Appendix C lists [6.4] "Evaluation of leisure facilities"). Ordinary Level candidates do not meet it in the written paper; the 2015H paper asked students to name and evaluate two leisure facilities popular with retired people.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Neighbours: the definition, value and influences on leisure belong to the previous subsection; the idea of value for money as a consumer is in the consumer chapter, but here it is applied to leisure; do not repeat the evaluation steps for child care options.

## 1. Must cover [all HL]

Types of leisure facility in the community
- [ ] F1 Public (State and local authority) facilities: swimming pools, leisure centres, libraries, parks, playgrounds, sports pitches, walking and cycling trails, community halls, arts centres.
- [ ] F2 Commercial (private, for profit) facilities: gyms and health clubs, cinemas, bowling alleys, golf clubs, cafés and entertainment venues; paid for by fees and charges.
- [ ] F3 Voluntary and community facilities: GAA clubs, soccer, tennis and other sports clubs, youth clubs, active retirement groups, drama and musical societies, community centres; run by volunteers and funded by membership fees, fundraising and grants.
- [ ] F4 Facilities for particular groups: e.g. popular with retired people: golf, bingo, gym or leisure centre classes, library and book clubs, swimming pool, cinema, choir or musical society, drama group, card games (2015H list).

Criteria for evaluating a facility
- [ ] C1 Cost: membership and joining fees, pay-as-you-go, class fees, equipment and clothing, travel, concessions.
- [ ] C2 Value for money: the benefit gained relative to what is paid, depending on how often it is used and whether it suits the user.
- [ ] C3 Location and accessibility: distance, public transport, parking, access for people with disabilities (ramps, lifts, accessible changing rooms), opening hours that suit working or school patterns.
- [ ] C4 Range and quality of facilities and activities: number and variety of activities, equipment and condition; classes suited to different ages and abilities; family provision.
- [ ] C5 Convenience: opening hours, booking arrangements (online booking), crowding, waiting time, childcare on site.
- [ ] C6 Safety, hygiene and staffing: clean changing rooms, qualified instructors and lifeguards, supervision, first aid, security, an atmosphere that suits the user.
- [ ] C7 Benefits: physical, social, emotional and intellectual; chance to meet people and join groups; suitability for the user's age, gender, interests and ability; suitability for individual or family use.

Cost types and the cost comparison
- [ ] K1 Cost types: membership fee (regular), joining fee (once), pay-as-you-go per visit, class and course fees, hidden costs (travel, kit, equipment, locker or towel hire, parking, refreshments); concessions for students, children, older people, unemployed people and families.
- [ ] K2 Total cost for a set period (a year) = fees plus extras plus travel.
- [ ] K3 Cost per visit = total cost divided by number of visits; a facility used often has a lower cost per visit.
- [ ] K4 Pay-as-you-go suits occasional users; membership suits regular users.

Value and the comparison of two facilities (the syllabus's named task)
- [ ] V1 Choose two comparable facilities (for example a local authority leisure centre and a private gym, or a community club and a commercial club) for a named user or family.
- [ ] V2 Compare fees and the full cost, cost per visit for the user's expected use, location and access, hours, range of activities, facilities and condition, safety and staffing, atmosphere and social benefit.
- [ ] V3 Judge value: which offers more for the money for this user, and what would change the decision (more or fewer visits, change in income, a different household member).
- [ ] V4 Give a conclusion for the stated user: e.g. a student wanting cheap exercise against a family wanting varied activities.
- [ ] V5 A cheap facility used rarely may give poor value; a dearer one used often may give better value; value depends on use.

Evaluating one or two facilities (answer format)
- [ ] E1 Name the facility; for each give two developed points (a strength or a limit) as in 2015H: for example, a swimming pool gives low-impact exercise and social contact for retired people; but may involve travel and set sessions.
- [ ] E2 Suggest improvements: extended hours, concessions, better accessibility, online booking and information, more classes for older people or families.

Settings
- [ ] S1 Investigate the leisure facilities in the community: list, visit or check websites, collect fees, and compare two.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use any real or example price for a named business, because fees change and invented figures can look like facts; if an illustrative calculation is used, label it "example only".
- Do not use: "Public facilities are funded mainly by local authority budgets and user charges, and their fees are set by the provider" as written, because funding varies (grants, councils, private operators running some council pools); say "often provided or part-funded by local authorities".
- Do not name specific companies (gym chains, cinema chains).
- Cost is not value: a low price does not mean good value; value compares benefit with cost.
- Evaluation means both strengths and weaknesses and a judgement for a particular user; a list of features is not an evaluation.
- Do not repeat the previous subsection's general benefits of leisure at length; link benefits to a facility.
- Do not treat voluntary clubs as free: they usually charge membership fees.
- Keep ordinary-level wording out of this subsection: it is Higher Level only; put the heading "(Higher Level only)" on every block.
- Do not use the family evaluation of child care here; the criteria are similar but separate.
- Do not describe leisure facilities in other countries.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name and evaluate two leisure facilities popular with retired people (2015H, 12 marks): name two (swimming pool, golf, library, gym classes, choir) and give two evaluative points each (cost, access, social contact, health benefit, suitability for age).
- A cost and value comparison of two facilities for a family or an individual (the syllabus task): list fees and extra costs, compare cost per visit, range, access and hours, give a justified conclusion.
- Evaluate the leisure facilities available in your community: name type, cost, accessibility, range of activities, safety, benefits; strengths and limits; improvements.
- Short parts: state three criteria for choosing a leisure facility; explain the difference between membership and pay-as-you-go; suggest how a facility might improve access for older people or people with disabilities.
