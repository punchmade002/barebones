## 1. Must cover

Biological functions of sugars and starch (one block)
- [ ] B1 Heat and energy: carbohydrate is the body's main and quickest source of energy. Glucose is oxidised in the cells (respiration) to release energy for movement and growth and heat to keep body temperature at about 37 °C (the 2025 scheme). Starch is digested to glucose, so gives the same energy as sugar, more slowly.
- [ ] B2 The brain and nervous system use glucose as their main fuel; red blood cells depend on it.
- [ ] B3 Protein sparing: when enough carbohydrate is eaten for energy, protein is spared for growth and repair instead of being used as fuel.
- [ ] B4 Glycogen store: glucose not needed at once is converted to glycogen and stored in the liver and muscles as a short-term reserve that can be turned back into glucose when blood glucose falls.
- [ ] B5 Fat store: when the glycogen stores are full, extra carbohydrate is converted to fat and stored as adipose tissue under the skin and around organs, where it also insulates the body and protects organs. (Do not teach this as a reason to avoid carbohydrate; the point is that excess energy of any kind is stored as fat.)
- [ ] B6 Name the types: sugars give a quick supply of energy; starch gives a slower, steadier release along with other nutrients in foods such as cereals and potatoes.

Biological functions of dietary fibre (non-starch polysaccharides) (one block)
- [ ] F1 Fibre (mainly cellulose) cannot be digested by humans, so it passes through the digestive system. It absorbs water and adds bulk and softness to the stool.
- [ ] F2 The bulk stimulates peristalsis, the wave-like muscle contractions that move food and waste through the intestine (2025 and 2010 schemes: "movement of food is stimulated by fibre"). Waste leaves faster, so there is less time for toxins to form in the bowel.
- [ ] F3 It prevents constipation and the straining that causes haemorrhoids (piles); it helps prevent diverticular disease (diverticulitis) and reduces the risk of bowel (colon) cancer.
- [ ] F4 Fullness: fibre absorbs water, gives a feeling of fullness and so helps control appetite and body weight.
- [ ] F5 Soluble fibre (pectin; the soluble fibre in oats, beans and lentils) slows the absorption of glucose, helping to keep blood sugar steady, and helps to lower blood LDL cholesterol, reducing the risk of heart disease (2016, 2023 and 2024 schemes).
- [ ] F6 Two kinds, in one line: insoluble fibre (cellulose, wheat bran) speeds the movement of waste; soluble fibre (pectin) slows sugar absorption and lowers LDL cholesterol.
- [ ] F7 Fibre provides little or no usable energy; it does not give energy in the same way as other carbohydrate. Put the point in one line; energy values are in the energy subsection.
- [ ] F8 Bowel conditions to name if asked (2016H, a nine-mark question asks for one bowel disease with account and symptoms): constipation (stools pass too slowly through the large intestine, hard and difficult to pass, discomfort and straining); haemorrhoids (piles); diverticular disease. Give constipation as the model answer: cause (low fibre, too little water), effects (hard stools, straining, bloating, discomfort, risk of haemorrhoids) and prevention (fibre, fluids, exercise).

Culinary functions of sugars (one block)
- [ ] S1 Sweetening and flavouring in cakes, desserts, drinks, sauces and confectionery; improves palatability.
- [ ] S2 Preserving: in jam, chutney, syrup and crystallised fruit, sugar in high concentration draws water out of micro-organisms, so spoilage is slowed.
- [ ] S3 Fermentation: sugar is the food for yeast, which produces carbon dioxide that makes bread dough rise.
- [ ] S4 Colour and flavour: caramelisation and the Maillard reaction brown the surface of baked goods, glazes and crème caramel; sugar glaze on baked goods.
- [ ] S5 Aeration and volume: creamed sugar and fat trap air in cakes; sugar helps egg white hold a stable foam in meringues.
- [ ] S6 Texture: sugar crystals form the structure of fudge and fondant; syrups and icing (a solution of sugar) are used for coating; sugar syrup stops cut fruit in a fruit salad discolouring (2009 scheme).
- [ ] S7 Tenderising and keeping quality: sugar holds moisture (is hygroscopic) so cakes stay moist (optional, one phrase).

Culinary functions of starch and pectin (one block)
- [ ] T1 Starch is a thickening agent: it gelatinises in a hot liquid, thickening sauces, soups, gravies, custards and pie fillings. In a roux the fat separates the starch granules so the sauce is lump-free.
- [ ] T2 Starch binds and coats: it forms the thickening in batters and coatings and gives body to a sauce.
- [ ] T3 Starch is a source of structure in baking: starch in flour gelatinises as a cake, pastry or bread bakes and helps to set the crumb; the elastic structure of bread comes from gluten (protein), not starch.
- [ ] T4 Starch browns on dry heat (dextrinisation), giving crust colour and flavour to toast and baked goods.
- [ ] T5 Pectin is the gelling agent in jam, jelly and marmalade: with sugar and acid it sets the fruit mixture to a gel. It is also used in food manufacture as a gelling agent and stabiliser (additive E440) in confectionery, desserts and yoghurts.
- [ ] T6 Pectin supports shape in cooked fruit and thickens fruit fillings. One phrase.

Assumed knowledge (one block)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Respiration: the process by which cells release energy from glucose using oxygen. Peristalsis, adipose tissue and LDL cholesterol need a one-clause gloss each.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "starch gives structure to batters, pastry and bread" (the old note), because it is vague and partly wrong: the elastic structure of bread and batters comes from gluten, a protein in wheat flour; starch thickens, absorbs liquid and helps set the crumb on baking. Use T3.
- Dietary fibre is not digested; do not say fibre provides energy or "is broken down in the intestine". (Bacteria in the large intestine ferment some; this is not needed.)
- Cellulose, not fat, is the main fibre (lay word "roughage"; teach "dietary fibre"); do not call fibre a vitamin or a mineral.
- Do not say the glycogen store is in the "liver only" or "muscles only": it is liver and muscles (the 2025 and 2010 schemes).
- Do not say carbohydrate is "turned to fat" as the main fate of carbohydrate; the first uses are energy and glycogen; fat only when stores are full.
- Soluble fibre lowers LDL cholesterol, not all cholesterol; do not write "removes cholesterol".
- Fibre does not "clean" or "detox" the bowel; keep to the stated functions.
- Culinary functions list uses (what the ingredient does for the food); the properties subsection explains the chemistry. Do not define gelatinisation or caramelisation in full here; a phrase is enough.
- Type 2 diabetes, tooth decay and obesity from too much sugar belong to the energy value and targets subsection; do not put them in the functions block.
- Digestion and absorption of carbohydrate belong to the digestion and metabolism chapter; insulin and the regulation of blood glucose belong there as well.
- Do not use the old lay term "roughage" as the technical term; say dietary fibre or non-starch polysaccharide.
- Do not name the amount of fibre needed; that is in the targets subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Biological functions of carbohydrate, from two or three marks to long accounts (2026, 2025, 2024, 2019, 2014, 2010, 2009, 2006): heat and energy, protein sparing, storage as glycogen in the liver and muscles, conversion to insulating adipose tissue when stores are full, and for fibre peristalsis, bowel health, fullness, lower LDL, slower glucose release. A good answer gives each function with its reason in a short clause.
- Fill-in or true-false items (2025 O, 2010 O, 2026 O): "sugar and starch give heat and energy" (true); "fibre helps prevent bowel disorders" (true); excess carbohydrate stored as glycogen in the liver and muscles; fibre or cellulose stimulates movement of food through the bowel.
- Benefits of a high-fibre diet (2023H: two benefits; 2016H: evaluate three): slows glucose release, lowers cholesterol and the risk of heart disease, bulk and fullness, faster peristalsis, fewer bowel disorders and a lower risk of bowel cancer. Each benefit needs the reason.
- One bowel disease with account and symptoms (2016H); dietary guidelines to prevent bowel disease (2019O): wholegrain cereals, skins on fruit and vegetables, fresh or dried fruit and nuts and seeds for snacks, whole fruit rather than juice, drink water.
- Uses of sugar in food preparation (2004 O: one use of sugar and one of starch; 2009 O: list three culinary uses of sugar): sweetener, preservative, glaze or colour, aerating, yeast food, icing, syrup; starch as a thickening agent in sauce making.
