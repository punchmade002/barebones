## 1. Must cover

Coronary heart disease diet (one block; common to both levels)
- [ ] C1 Aim: lower LDL cholesterol and blood pressure and reduce strain on the heart and arteries. Recall: CHD is narrowing of the coronary arteries by fatty deposits (one line).
- [ ] C2 Reduce saturated fat: choose lean meat and trim visible fat; low-fat dairy; replace butter and animal fats with mono- and polyunsaturated oils (olive, rapeseed, sunflower); eat more fish and chicken (without skin) in place of fatty red meat; use low-cholesterol products.
- [ ] C3 Eat oily fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines) for omega-3 fatty acids.
- [ ] C4 Increase fibre, especially soluble fibre (oats, beans, lentils, fruit, vegetables), and eat more fruit and vegetables.
- [ ] C5 Reduce refined carbohydrate and sugar, salt (use herbs, spices, lemon juice), and alcohol.
- [ ] C6 Low-fat cooking: grill, bake, steam, boil; avoid frying and cream sauces; skim fat from stock and gravy.
- [ ] C7 Functional foods (2007H menu question): a functional food has a health benefit beyond its basic nutritive value. For CHD: plant sterol or stanol spreads, yoghurts and drinks that lower LDL cholesterol; prebiotic and probiotic foods for digestion (in the scheme); fortified foods. A menu question requires one functional food and its reason.
- [ ] C8 Lifestyle changes (2007H, 2008O): healthy weight, no smoking, little alcohol, regular exercise, reduce stress.
- [ ] C9 A day's CHD menu (3 meals): for example porridge with low-fat milk and fruit; wholegrain sandwich with chicken and salad; grilled oily fish with vegetables and potatoes; low-fat yoghurt or fruit.

Lacto-vegetarian diet (one block; common to both levels)
- [ ] L1 Definition: eats no meat, poultry, fish or eggs; includes milk and dairy products (2016O scheme definition). Other types of vegetarian named by schemes: lacto-ovo (includes eggs and dairy), ovo, pesco; vegan excludes all animal products.
- [ ] L2 Protein: HBV from milk, cheese and yoghurt; LBV from pulses, nuts, seeds, soya beans and soya products (tofu, TVP), and mycoprotein (Quorn-type products); combining plant proteins in a day.
- [ ] L3 Iron: green leafy vegetables, pulses, wholegrains, dried fruit, fortified cereals; vitamin C in the same meal improves absorption (2007O scheme).
- [ ] L4 Calcium: dairy products, soya products, sesame seeds, green leafy vegetables, fortified foods; vitamin D, vitamin C and phosphorus help calcium absorption.
- [ ] L5 Vitamin B12 from dairy products (and fortified foods); vitamin B group from wholegrains; vitamin D from fortified spreads and cereals and sunlight; omega-3 from flaxseed, walnuts, rapeseed oil.
- [ ] L6 Reasons people become vegetarian: religious, cultural, moral, health, economic, environmental (link to the food choices subsection by title).
- [ ] L7 Label care: some cheeses use animal rennet and some meat-free products contain egg, so read the label.
- [ ] L8 A one-day lacto-vegetarian menu following the food pyramid (2016O, 2024O): for example orange juice, porridge with milk, wholemeal toast; vegetable soup with brown bread, yoghurt, fruit; vegetarian lasagne or lentil dish with green vegetables; milk or water.

Vegan diet (one block, Higher Level only)
- [ ] V1 Excludes all animal-derived foods: meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, honey.
- [ ] V2 Protein: pulses, nuts, seeds, soya products (tofu, TVP, soya milk), vegan mycoprotein, seitan, tempeh; eating pulses with grains or nuts over the day supplies all essential amino acids (2004H sources: TVP, soya beans, pulses, nuts).
- [ ] V3 Calcium (2004H): sesame seeds, broccoli, spinach and other greens, fortified soya milk and flour, calcium-set tofu, nuts, vegan cheese, fortified foods or supplements.
- [ ] V4 Iron: pulses, wholegrain, dried fruit, dark green vegetables, fortified cereals, with vitamin C (peppers, tomatoes, berries, citrus).
- [ ] V5 Vitamin B12: no reliable plant source, so fortified foods (soya milk, cereals, yeast extract) or a supplement. Vitamin D from fortified foods and sunlight; omega-3 from flaxseed, walnuts, rapeseed oil.
- [ ] V6 Vegan menu example (2009H): a three-course menu with vegan Quorn-type mince, lentils or beans, vegetables and fruit; explain how it excludes animal products and still supplies protein, iron, calcium and B12 (fortified) and follows the pyramid.
- [ ] V7 Hidden animal ingredients: stock cubes, some spreads, some bread, sweets (gelatine), cheese with rennet; choose labelled vegan products.

Coeliac diet (one block, Higher Level only)
- [ ] G1 Definition (2006H): lifelong intolerance to gluten (the protein in wheat, barley and rye); gluten damages the villi lining the small intestine so nutrients are poorly absorbed (deficiencies of iron, calcium, folate possible).
- [ ] G2 Foods to avoid (2006H): bread, cakes, biscuits, pastries, pizzas and pies made with wheat; processed foods such as sausages, some soups, sauces and gravies; wheat, barley, rye, malt, ordinary pasta and couscous; seitan.
- [ ] G3 Safe staples (2025H): rice, maize (corn), potatoes, buckwheat, soya, lentils and pulses, meat, fish, eggs, dairy, fruit and vegetables; gluten-free versions of bread, pasta and flour.
- [ ] G4 Guidelines (2025H, 2022H): check ingredient lists for hidden gluten (stock, sauces, malt, processed meats); look for the gluten-free declaration and symbol; avoid cross-contamination in the kitchen (separate toaster and utensils); choose high-fibre gluten-free foods (brown rice, beans) as gluten-free breads and cereals can be low in fibre; replace iron, calcium and folate normally supplied by wheat products (fortified or naturally gluten-free sources).
- [ ] G5 A "gluten-free" claim means the food as sold contains no more than 20 mg of gluten per kilogram; wheat and oats used must still appear in the ingredients (Regulation (EU) No 828/2014, FSAI guidance).

Diabetic diet (one block, Higher Level only)
- [ ] D1 Aim: keep blood glucose steady, keep a healthy weight, reduce heart disease risk.
- [ ] D2 Regular meals and snacks at set times, no skipped meals; when insulin is used the carbohydrate eaten is matched to the dose.
- [ ] D3 Low-glycaemic-index (GI) starchy foods for slow release of glucose: wholegrain bread, oats, pasta, pulses; high-fibre foods; plenty of vegetables.
- [ ] D4 Eliminate or limit refined sugar, sugary drinks and sweets; use sweeteners; limit sugary fruit; avoid sugar-laden processed foods.
- [ ] D5 Reduce saturated fat and salt; use unsaturated oils; keep weight healthy and exercise (2009H: three specific guidelines).

How manufacturers and retailers meet special dietary needs (one block; 2009H)
- [ ] M1 Manufacturers: ranges for gluten-free, diabetic, vegetarian and CHD diets; detailed labels and packet recipes; smaller portion sizes.
- [ ] M2 Retailers: specialist shops or sections and shelves for special-diet foods; wider ranges; in-store demonstrations, information and websites.

Labelling for special diets (one block; 2024H, 2023O)
- [ ] B1 Labels give the name of the food and ingredients in descending order of weight, with allergens highlighted; nutrition information (energy, fat, saturates, sugar, salt, fibre); net quantity; use-by or best-before; storage and cooking instructions; origin; GM ingredients.
- [ ] B2 Symbols and claims: gluten-free, vegetarian, vegan, fortified, functional, low-fat or reduced-sugar claims help people with specific needs choose; evaluation: useful but must be read critically (claims can mislead, small print, hidden gluten, "may contain" warnings).
- [ ] B3 Law (one line): allergen and ingredient rules are in Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, given effect in Ireland by the European Union (Provision of Food Information to Consumers) Regulations 2014.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "omega-3 eggs and milk reduce the risk of heart disease", because it overstates the claim; say omega-3 enriched foods supply omega-3 fatty acids.
- Do not use: the crossed-grain symbol described as the official Irish or Coeliac Society of Ireland mark, because no Irish source was confirmed; say "the gluten-free symbol or declaration".
- Do not use: "custard", "margarine" and "bread" as foods that always contain animal ingredients for vegans, because many are vegan; say "check the label for animal ingredients such as gelatine, milk powder, egg, rennet, honey".
- Do not use: brand names (for example for sterol spreads or mycoprotein) as facts; say "plant sterol spreads" and "mycoprotein products".
- Do not use: the claim that all Quorn contains egg; say "some mycoprotein products contain egg white".
- Lacto-vegetarian versus lacto-ovo: marking schemes disagree. One 2009 Ordinary scheme listed eggs as included; the 2016 scheme and a 2021 true/false (the diet includes dairy) treat lacto-vegetarian as no eggs. The notes follow the strict definition (dairy yes, eggs no) and say that some sources also allow eggs. Do not use eggs in a lacto-vegetarian menu.
- A lacto-vegetarian does eat dairy; the 2021 true/false item was false because of that.
- Vegan diets need a B12 source; vegetarians get B12 from dairy.
- Seitan and ordinary couscous contain gluten; vegan "meat" products may too, so vegan and coeliac are different diets.
- Oats: naturally gluten-free but often contaminated; only gluten-free-labelled oats.
- "Diabetic diet" does not mean no carbohydrate; it means low-GI, regular, controlled.
- Do not give amounts, RDAs or grams of fibre or salt.
- Belongs elsewhere: what causes CHD, diabetes or coeliac (diet-related problems); allergy law and detailed label rules (food legislation); recipes and portion costing (meal management).

## 4. What exams ask

- Meal planning guidelines for CHD (three guidelines, 6 marks; four at 16 marks; a day's menu with one functional food and its reason): reduce saturated fat, unsaturated oils, oily fish, fibre, less salt and refined carbohydrate, low-fat cooking, functional foods.
- Define lacto-vegetarian and name two protein alternatives; two specific requirements (protein, iron or calcium with their sources and helpers); a one-day lacto-vegetarian or vegetarian menu; sources of protein, calcium, vitamin B for vegetarians.
- Vegan: sources of protein, calcium and iron (2004H); a vegan menu with how it meets vegan requirements (2009H).
- Coeliac: define it, name three foods to avoid, and discuss three meal-planning guidelines.
- Diabetes: three dietary guidelines (eliminate refined sugar, regular meals, low-GI carbohydrate, fibre).
- Describe measures taken by manufacturers and retailers for consumers with specific dietary requirements; evaluate or explain food labelling for people with specific needs (12 marks).
