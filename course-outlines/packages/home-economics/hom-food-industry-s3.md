# Package: hom-food-industry-s3 — Careers in food and related industries

- **loId:** `hom-food-industry-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-industry` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.1
- **Syllabus scope:** "outline knowledge." Also examined: 2010H, 2010O, 2005H. No [HL] items.
  Common to Ordinary and Higher Level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. "Outline knowledge" means a student can name a
  wide range of careers and say in one clause what each person does, and name the main
  employers and routes in. Past questions only ever ask "list two career opportunities" (2
  marks) or name-and-detail a career as a short part, so breadth with one accurate duty each beats
  depth. The setting (careers offered by a local business) is also in scope.

## 1. Must cover

Careers in production and manufacturing (one block)
- [ ] P1 Primary producers: farmer, fisherman or fish farmer, horticulturist/grower.
- [ ] P2 Miller, butcher, baker, confectioner, cheesemaker, brewer/distiller: craft and
  processing trades, in small businesses or factories.
- [ ] P3 Production operative: runs and monitors processing and packaging lines.
- [ ] P4 Food technologist: develops new products and processes, improves recipes, shelf life and
  quality.
- [ ] P5 Quality control technician: tests raw materials and finished products against
  specifications and food safety standards.
- [ ] P6 Food microbiologist: tests food and surfaces for microbes causing spoilage or food
  poisoning.
- [ ] P7 Food engineer and food scientist (e.g. process, packaging and research): design and
  improve processing plant and products.
- [ ] P8 Agricultural scientist and agricultural adviser: research and advice on crop and
  livestock production and farm efficiency.

Careers in food service, retail and hospitality (one block)
- [ ] H1 Chef: plans menus, prepares, cooks and presents food in restaurants, hotels, hospitals
  and other institutions.
- [ ] H2 Caterer: plans and provides food for events, canteens and institutions.
- [ ] H3 Waiter or waitress, bar staff, receptionist: front-of-house service in hotels,
  restaurants and bars.
- [ ] H4 Hotel or restaurant manager: runs the business, staff, stock and costs.
- [ ] H5 Deli counter assistant, retail and sales assistant, buyer (sources ingredients and
  products from suppliers).
- [ ] H6 Food stylist and food writer or journalist, and marketing and sales roles: promote food
  products to retailers, consumers and export markets (assumed, "related industries").

Careers in health, nutrition, education and regulation (one block)
- [ ] N1 Dietitian: assesses nutritional needs and plans diets for individuals and groups,
  including people with illness or special needs. Dietitian is a protected title: only people
  registered with the Dietitians Registration Board at CORU may use it.
- [ ] N2 Nutritionist: gives advice on healthy eating (public health, sport, industry). Unlike
  dietitian, the title is not protected by law in Ireland.
- [ ] N3 Home Economics teacher: teaches food, nutrition and consumer and family studies at
  second level (a degree and a teaching qualification, registered with the Teaching Council).
- [ ] N4 Environmental Health Officer (EHO): inspects food premises, enforces food hygiene law,
  takes samples, investigates complaints and outbreaks and can serve improvement and closure
  notices. EHOs working on food in Ireland are employed by the HSE.
- [ ] N5 Food safety scientist, public analyst (laboratory testing), and veterinary
  inspectors in the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (official control of meat and
  dairy); sea-fisheries inspectors work for the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority (seafood).

Employers and entry routes (one block)
- [ ] E1 Employers: food manufacturers and processors; retailers and supermarkets; hotels,
  restaurants and caterers; hospitals and schools; state bodies (Bord Bia, Teagasc, BIM, FSAI,
  HSE, Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine); schools and colleges; own business.
- [ ] E2 Entry routes: apprenticeships (e.g. chef), further education and training (PLC and
  other certificate courses), third-level degrees (food science, dietetics, culinary arts, Home
  Economics, environmental health), on-the-job training, and experience through family business.
- [ ] E3 The skills and personal qualities that most food careers need: knowledge of hygiene and
  food safety, teamwork, communication, attention to detail, and an interest in food.
- [ ] E4 Setting: when studying a local business, list the careers it offers (e.g. production
  staff, technician, driver, sales, administrator, chef).

## 3. Traps

- Dietitian versus nutritionist: both give diet advice, but a dietitian is registered and may
  treat illness with diet; do not use the two as synonyms.
- Food technologist (develops products) versus quality control technician (tests products) versus
  microbiologist (tests for microbes): three different jobs, each asked as a pair or list.
- Environmental Health Officer inspects premises; a dietitian does not. EHO is not a "food
  inspector from the FSAI": the FSAI coordinates, the HSE employs the officers.
- Careers in clothing and fashion belong to the textiles elective; keep to food and related
  industries here.
- Agencies and their functions belong to the structure subsection; here name them only as
  employers.
- Do not use: "Local authorities employ Environmental Health Officers to inspect food
  premises", because for food in Ireland the officers inspecting restaurants, retailers and most
  food premises work for the HSE Environmental Health Service; only some functions
  (for example some dairy and meat checks) sit with the Department of Agriculture, Food and the
  Marine, and local authorities have a limited role. Say HSE.
- Do not use: "Forbairt", "Health Boards" or "Department of Health and Children" as employers,
  because they are superseded (a marking scheme lists Forbairt).
- Do not use: fees, salaries, CAO points, number of places or graduates, because they change.
- Do not use: named colleges as the only route; say "third-level degrees" instead, because
  courses and institutions change.
- Do not use: "Environmental Health Officers take samples for testing" as the main duty; it is
  one duty. The main duty is inspection and enforcement of food hygiene law.

## 4. What exams ask

- "List two career opportunities in the food industry" (2 marks) or "name two ... agencies and
  list two careers" (6 marks): any two from chef, waiter, dietitian, Home Economics teacher,
  butcher, baker, confectioner, caterer, food technologist, microbiologist, quality control
  technician, Environmental Health Officer, hotel manager, agricultural scientist, salesperson.
- Occasionally a named career with its details (name 1-2 marks, two duties 3-4 marks): e.g. food
  technologist, dietitian, Environmental Health Officer, chef, quality control.
- Likely tip cards: always give a specific job title (not "working in a shop"); add a one-clause
  duty if the question says "outline" or "state"; vary the two chosen careers (one production, one
  service or health) so the answers are clearly different.
- The careers a local business offers (setting): list roles and the qualifications needed.
