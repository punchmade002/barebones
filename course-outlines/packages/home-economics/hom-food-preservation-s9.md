# Package: hom-food-preservation-s9 — Practical application of two methods of preservation

- **loId:** `hom-food-preservation-s9` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-preservation` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.9
- **Syllabus scope:** two home methods carried out in full (method, suitable foods, steps, packaging, safety). Also examined: 2022H, 2022O, 2010O, 2008H. Not Higher Level only; both levels must be able to describe and carry out two methods.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The syllabus requires two methods of preservation applied in practice. The exam asks for "one method of home preservation" with named headings (name, suitable foods, steps, packaging, principle, safety). The best two for this course are home freezing (of vegetables and fruit) and jam making; both are fully taught in the earlier subsections, so this subsection gathers each into a complete practical account laid out as a sequence the student could write in an exam or carry out in the kitchen, with the safety guidelines. Chutney is an acceptable alternative second method (it is in chemical preservation) and gets a short outline here so a student who has done chutney is covered. The reasons are in the earlier subsections; here give each step with a one-phrase reason.

## 1. Must cover

How to answer a "method of home preservation" question (one block)
- [ ] A1 Structure: name the method; suitable foods; the principle (what condition is removed); the steps in order; packaging and labelling; storage; safety. 2022O: name, suitable foods, steps (24 marks); 2022O part (b): four safety guidelines (16 marks); 2022H: name, underlying principle, suitable packaging (18 marks); 2010O: method, suitable foods, how carried out, packaging (24 marks); 2008H: method and principle (15 marks).
- [ ] A2 The method must be a home method; commercial methods (canning plant, irradiation, spray drying) do not answer these questions.

Method 1: home freezing of vegetables and fruit
- [ ] F1 Name: home freezing. Suitable foods: vegetables (peas, beans, carrots, broccoli), fruit (apples, strawberries), meat, fish, baked foods, soups, sauces, prepared meals. Unsuitable: lettuce and salad foods, bananas, melons, pears.
- [ ] F2 Principle: low temperature stops enzyme action and microbial growth (inactivated); water becomes ice, unavailable to organisms; blanching inactivates enzymes in vegetables; quick freezing makes small ice crystals that protect texture.
- [ ] F3 Steps for vegetables: select fresh, young vegetables; wash, trim, cut; blanch in boiling water for the time for that vegetable; cool at once in ice-cold water for the same time; drain and dry well; portion; pack with air removed; label; switch on fast freeze 3 to 4 hours before; freeze; store at −18 °C.
- [ ] F4 Steps for fruit (strawberries): select sound, ripe fruit; wash quickly, hull, dry; open freeze on a tray in a single layer; pack in portions in bags or rigid containers (leave headspace for fruit in syrup); label; freeze; store. (Fruit is not blanched.)
- [ ] F5 Packaging: moisture-proof and vapour-proof freezer bags, rigid plastic containers with tight lids, foil; air removed; sealed tightly; label with contents, quantity and date.
- [ ] F6 Guidelines: use good-quality food; do not freeze more than about one-tenth of the freezer's capacity in 24 hours; cool cooked food before freezing; place packs against the cold surfaces; rotate stock; thaw safely (fridge) and cook thoroughly; do not refreeze thawed raw food.
- [ ] F7 Effect on the food (examined with method, 2016H): colour, flavour, texture and nutrients well kept; some vitamin C and B1 lost in blanching and drip; slow freezing softens food.
- [ ] F8 Safety guidelines (2022O, four marks each): use knives, graters and processors carefully (cut away from the body, guard on the grater); wash hands, tie back hair, cover cuts; use oven gloves for the hot blanching pan, turn pan handles inward, do not overfill; keep flexes tidy, avoid overloaded sockets and wet hands near electrical appliances; keep raw and cooked foods apart; clean surfaces.

Method 2: jam making
- [ ] J1 Name: jam making (from fruit that is plentiful, 2008H). Suitable fruit: strawberries, raspberries, blackcurrants, plums, gooseberries, apples, blackberries; ripe or just ripe, sound, no mould. Pectin: high in cooking apples, blackcurrants and citrus; strawberries are low, so add lemon juice (and, if needed, pectin or pectin-rich fruit such as apple).
- [ ] J2 Principle: heat destroys micro-organisms and enzymes; sugar preserves by osmosis; pectin and acid set the jam; sealing prevents re-entry of micro-organisms (2024H and 2008H schemes).
- [ ] J3 Preparation: wash jars and lids and sterilise them (oven or boiling water), keep hot; wash and prepare the fruit; put a saucer in the freezer for the setting test; weigh fruit and sugar.
- [ ] J4 Steps: simmer the fruit gently in a heavy-based pan with a little water until soft and pectin is released; add the warmed sugar and stir until completely dissolved; add lemon juice if needed; bring to a full rolling boil and boil rapidly to setting point; test with the cold saucer (a drop wrinkles when pushed with a finger) or a sugar thermometer at about 105 °C; take off the heat; skim the scum with a clean dry spoon; allow to cool slightly so the fruit does not float; pot into hot sterilised jars leaving a small space; wipe the rim.
- [ ] J5 Packaging and storage: seal at once while hot (waxed disc and cover, or twist-top lids) so that a vacuum forms; label with name and date; store in a cool, dark, dry place; refrigerate after opening.
- [ ] J6 Safety guidelines: oven gloves; handles inward; do not overfill the pan (it boils up); keep children and pets away; pour carefully from a jug or jam funnel; do not touch hot sugar syrup (it causes severe burns); clean dry spoon for skimming; sterilised equipment for food safety.
- [ ] J7 Faults and causes: failure to set (too little pectin, acid or sugar; not boiled long enough); crystallised jam (too much sugar or not dissolved); mould or fermentation (too little sugar, unsterile jars, poor seal); fruit floating (potted when too hot); scorching (not stirred or pan too thin).

Alternative second method: chutney (outline)
- [ ] C1 Name: chutney. Foods: apples, tomatoes, plums, onions, marrow; principle: heat destroys organisms; vinegar (acid) and sugar inhibit growth; hot jars sealed; steps: sterilise jars; chop fruit and vegetables; cook slowly with vinegar, sugar and spices until thick; pot hot; seal with vinegar-proof lids; label; mature in a cool, dark place; refrigerate after opening. Safety as for jam.

Other methods a student may choose (one line each; no steps)
- [ ] O1 Home drying of herbs, apples or tomatoes (oven or dehydrator), bottling fruit, pickling vegetables in vinegar: each named with the principle, an example food and the key packaging point.

Choosing the two methods
- [ ] X1 A student should be able to give a full account of two methods, preferably of different types (one low temperature, one heat plus sugar), as required by the syllabus and as the questions ask for "one method".

## 3. Traps

- "Method" answers need the principle and the reasons, not only a list. Marks are given for name, suitable foods, steps, packaging and the principle, one set each; do not run them together.
- Blanching is for vegetables only. Fruit is not blanched; strawberries are open frozen.
- Sterilise jars before filling; do not wash them and then fill them cold with hot jam, which can crack the glass; keep them hot.
- Sugar must be fully dissolved before boiling; boil rapidly to set; do not stir after setting point is reached.
- The two methods must be home methods. Pasteurisation, commercial canning and irradiation do not answer a "home preservation" question.
- Home freezing temperatures: −25 °C fast freeze and −18 °C storage are two different figures.
- Chutney is boiled with vinegar and sugar and does not need pectin; jam needs pectin.
- The underlying reasons for each step are in the freezing, heat-processing and chemical-preservation subsections; do not re-teach long explanations here.
- Food safety in the kitchen (personal and kitchen hygiene, storage and reheating) belongs to the food safety chapter; here give only the guidelines that go with preservation.
- The Food Studies practical coursework (the 20 per cent assignments) belongs to the cooking-methods chapter; do not describe the coursework brief or marking here.
- Do not use: "The sugar is measured in the correct proportion for the fruit and warmed" as a rule for jam in all recipes, because warming sugar is a recipe option rather than a principle; say sugar is added in the proportion in a tested recipe and stirred until dissolved.
- Do not use: "Place the packs against the sides or base of the freezer so that they freeze quickly" as universal, because the instruction depends on the freezer type; say place new packs against the cold surfaces or in the fast-freeze section.

## 4. What exams ask

- Account of one method of home preservation (2022O, 24 marks; 2010O, 24 marks; 2022H, 18 marks; 2016H, 20 marks; 2008H, 15 marks): method name; suitable foods; the principle; steps in order; packaging; effect on the food. Freezing (vegetables, strawberries) and jam making are the usual answers.
- Four safety guidelines when preserving food in the home (2022O, 16 marks): knives and graters, hot pans and jars, hygiene and hair, electrical safety, raw and cooked food apart.
- Short questions on parts of the methods (2024O freezing guidelines; 2011O blanching, pectin, sugar and vinegar; 2018O jam and chutney; 2024H jam principles).
- Higher variants give headings (name, underlying principle, suitable packaging); Ordinary variants give headings (suitable foods, steps).
- Tip content: give each heading its own paragraph and include at least two reasons in the steps; for safety give four distinct guidelines with how each prevents an accident or contamination.
