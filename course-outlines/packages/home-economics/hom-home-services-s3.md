## 1. Must cover

Household supply
- [ ] D1 Route from power station to house: generation; a step-up transformer raises the voltage for the national grid (high voltage means low current and so less heat lost in the cables); step-down transformers and substations reduce it for local networks; a final transformer near the house gives the 230 V used in homes; a service (mains) cable enters the house; a sealed meter box holds the main fuse and the electricity meter; the consumer unit (fuse board) holds a main switch and the circuit breakers. Diagram: a flow chart of the stages with the transformer at each voltage change.
- [ ] D2 The consumer unit divides the supply into separate circuits (sockets, lights, cooker, immersion heater, shower), each with its own protective device, so a fault on one circuit disconnects only that circuit.
- [ ] D3 (assumed) Live, neutral and earth wires: live carries current to the appliance, neutral returns it, earth is a safety wire. Colours: live brown, neutral blue, earth green and yellow.
- [ ] D4 Plugs and sockets: a three-pin plug has a cartridge fuse matched to the appliance (3 A, 5 A or 13 A); the flex is held by a cord grip; the three pins connect live, neutral and earth.

Ring circuit [HL]
- [ ] R1 [HL] A ring circuit is a loop of cable (live, neutral and earth) that starts at the consumer unit, passes through each socket in turn, and returns to the same breaker. Every socket is supplied from two directions.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Advantages: current divides between two paths so a thinner cable than a single radial run can serve many sockets; many sockets can be added; a fault cuts off only that ring. Usually one ring per floor (upstairs and downstairs); lighting and the cooker are on separate circuits.
- [ ] R3 [HL] A spur is a branch from a socket on the ring (feeds an additional socket); the ring has its own protective device (a miniature circuit breaker or fuse) in the consumer unit which trips if the total load is too high. Diagram: a wiring circuit with consumer unit, loop, sockets, one spur labelled.

Electrical quantities, units and costings
- [ ] Q1 Voltage (V): the electrical "push" that drives current; Irish supply 230 V, 50 Hz. Amperage (A): the rate of flow of current. Wattage (W): the rate at which an appliance uses energy; 1000 W = 1 kW.
- [ ] Q2 Wattage = voltage × current (W = V × A); current = W ÷ V. Worked example: kettle 2300 W on 230 V draws 10 A. The same calculation selects the plug fuse (3 A = up to about 690 W, 13 A = up to about 3000 W).
- [ ] Q3 Kilowatt-hour (kWh, the "unit"): energy used by a 1000-watt appliance in one hour. Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h). Cost = kWh × unit rate. Worked example, symbols only: a 2 kW heater for 3 hours = 6 kWh. Use a made-up rate in worked examples, labelled as an example.
- [ ] Q4 The bill: standing charge (fixed daily charge for the network, meter reading and billing, payable even if no electricity is used); unit charge (kWh used × rate); the PSO levy (a charge on all customers, set each year by the energy regulator (CRU), that funds support for renewable energy); VAT. The scheme also lists "meter reading" as a way to find units used: new reading minus old reading.
- [ ] Q5 Tariffs: standard (one rate all day); day and night, or time-of-use (cheaper at night or off-peak, used by storage heaters, immersions and dishwashers); a smart meter records use in intervals and so supports time-of-use pricing. Choosing a tariff means matching use to the cheaper period.
- [ ] Q6 Costing a household's use: add up appliances' kW × hours; identify the biggest users (heating, shower, cooker, immersion).

Safety devices
- [ ] F1 Fuse: a thin wire that is a deliberate weak link; if the current is too high the wire heats and melts, breaking the circuit and preventing fire; it must be replaced. Found in plugs and old fuse boards.
- [ ] F2 Miniature circuit breaker (MCB): a switch that trips (switches off) when current exceeds its rating (overload or short circuit); it can be reset once the fault is cleared; found in the consumer unit.
- [ ] F3 Residual current device (RCD): compares the current in the live and neutral wires; if some leaks to earth (for example through a person) it trips in a fraction of a second; 30 mA is the usual domestic setting; 30 mA RCDs are required on socket circuits. Gives protection against shock, which fuses and MCBs do not.
- [ ] F4 Earth wire (green and yellow): gives a safe path to earth for current from a fault in a metal appliance (kettle, cooker, washing machine); the large current blows the fuse or trips the breaker, and the metal casing cannot stay live to shock the user.
- [ ] F5 Double-insulated appliances (plastic casing, symbol of a square within a square) have no earth wire.

Safe use of electricity (list-form, any four will do)
- [ ] U1 Never mix water and electricity: no wet hands, no appliances in bathrooms except a shaver socket, switches in bathrooms are pull-cords.
- [ ] U2 Do not overload sockets or use many adaptors or long extension leads; avoid trailing flexes; check flexes, plugs and sockets for damage and replace damaged flexes; do not pull a plug out by the flex.
- [ ] U3 Buy appliances with a recognised safety mark and use the right fuse; follow the instructions.
- [ ] U4 Switch off and unplug before cleaning or repair; never repair appliances yourself; use a qualified, registered electrician for wiring.
- [ ] U5 Keep flexes away from hobs and heat; do not cover heaters; check electric blankets for wear and do not fold them or leave them on under someone asleep; use RCDs outdoors; unplug appliances (except fridge and freezer) at night; fit smoke alarms.

Safe use of gas
- [ ] GS1 Gas smell (the added odour): turn off the gas at the meter, open doors and windows, no flames, no switches, call the gas emergency number or leave the house and call from outside.
- [ ] GS2 Incomplete combustion makes carbon monoxide; fit a carbon monoxide alarm; have gas appliances installed and serviced by a registered installer; keep flues and vents clear.
- [ ] GS3 Do not block vents; gas appliances need air; do not use a cooker or oven to heat the room.

Planning electrical services for a room (practical context)
- [ ] P1 Match sockets and switches to use (enough double sockets so extension leads are not needed; switches by the door; sockets at a safe height and away from water); layers of lighting; a heating option with a thermostat or timer; ventilation (extractor fan with humidity switch in a kitchen or bathroom); work done by a registered electrician and certified.

## 3. Traps

- Say kilowatt-hour (kWh), not "kilowatt per hour": the marking schemes sometimes print "per hour" but a kWh is energy (power × time), not a rate.
- Fuse, MCB and RCD are three different protectors: fuse and MCB react to too much current; an RCD reacts to a current leak to earth. Do not say an MCB protects against electric shock.
- A fuse breaks once and must be replaced; an MCB is reset.
- The earth wire does not itself blow the fuse; it carries the fault current so that the fuse or MCB trips.
- Watts measure power, kilowatt-hours measure energy used; the bill is in kWh.
- Ring circuit is Higher Level only; keep the diagram and structure in their own block marked [HL].
- Generation methods and renewable sources belong to the energy supplies subsection; thermostats to heating; extractor fans to ventilation; lamps to lighting.
- Gas safety here is about gas leaks and carbon monoxide in the home; the chemistry of emissions is in the emissions subsection.
- Do not use: "35 A" (or any amp rating) as the protection for a ring circuit (2008 scheme), because the rating is an unconfirmed, changing technical figure; say "the ring's own protective device".
- Do not use: "standing charge is higher in rural areas" (2015 scheme), because it is unconfirmed for current supplier practice.
- Do not use: "a smart meter shows real-time consumption" as a fact about the meter, because it was not confirmed; say it records use in intervals and supports time-of-use tariffs.
- Do not use: the PSO levy as a charge for buying power from "ESB generators" (2008 scheme wording), because the levy is now set by the CRU to fund support for renewables and amounts vary by year; give no amounts.
- Do not use: "a 13 A plug is the largest used", as the notes state in a way that implies a socket limit, because the plug fuse rating is not a limit on the circuit; say plugs carry a fuse of 3, 5 or 13 A matched to the appliance.
