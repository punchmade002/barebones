# Package: hom-social-change-s6 — Unemployment

- **loId:** `hom-social-change-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-social-change` · **syllabusRef:** 6.1
- **Syllabus scope:** item 6, unemployment [6.1]: the impact of unemployment on family life. Chapter-wide also examined: 2026H, 2023H, 2023O (effects of unemployment), 2022H, 2021O, 2018O, 2017H (effects on individuals, families and society; emigration), 2013O (negative effects on young people), 2010O, 2004O (effects on the individual). No [HL] items in this subsection. The outline marks the extent and causes of unemployment [HL] in the Unemployment, Poverty and Social Responses chapter, not here.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. The question is almost always "discuss the effects of unemployment on the individual, the family and society". Definition and extent are assumed knowledge here; causes are taught in the neighbouring chapter. ## 1. Must cover

What unemployment means (one block, assumed)
- [ ] N1 (assumed) Unemployment: people who are available for and willing to work, and are actively seeking work, but have no paid job. Long-term unemployment means being out of work for 12 months or more. (Defining unemployment is in the neighbouring Unemployment, Poverty and Social Responses chapter; give it in one sentence.)
- [ ] N2 (assumed) Why a person becomes unemployed is a separate topic (seasonal, geographical, technical change, changing industry needs, level of demand, redundancy, residual); the notes give at most a one-line pointer in plain words and no list of types. Unemployment black spots: areas where many people are out of work for a long time, often after a major employer closes.

Effects on the individual (one block)
- [ ] N3 Financial: loss of income and a lower standard of living; difficulty paying for food, heating, rent or mortgage; debt.
- [ ] N4 Psychological: loss of status, identity and routine; low self-esteem and confidence; boredom; stress, anxiety and depression (mental health problems); sense of failure.
- [ ] N5 Health and behaviour: poorer physical health; some turn to alcohol, drugs or gambling to cope.
- [ ] N6 Social: isolation from workplace contacts and friends; less social life because of cost; loss of motivation.
- [ ] N7 Skills: skills become out of date and a gap in the CV makes finding work harder, so long-term unemployment feeds on itself; young people lose the chance to gain work experience.
- [ ] N8 Young people: delayed independence, living with parents, difficulty moving out, temptation to emigrate; risk of antisocial behaviour.

Effects on family life (one block)
- [ ] N9 Lower income: reduced standard of living, debt, mortgage or rent arrears, risk of losing the home, reliance on credit or moneylenders, social welfare payments and help from MABS; cuts to savings, holidays, clothes and activities.
- [ ] N10 Relationships: financial strain causes arguments between partners and can lead to relationship breakdown; tension, stress and in some families violence or addiction.
- [ ] N11 Roles: a parent who loses the job may become the main carer at home, and role reversal can reduce their status and authority; the other partner may have to work longer or take work they would not choose.
- [ ] N12 Children: poorer diet, missed school trips, school costs, activities and leisure; stress and behaviour problems; may leave education early or choose college by cost; lack of working role models in long-term unemployed households (cycle of deprivation, covered in the poverty chapter).
- [ ] N13 Emigration and moving: family members may emigrate or move to find work, splitting families, leaving older relatives without support and reducing community populations (past papers ask about the impact of emigration for work on individuals and families).
- [ ] N14 More time together can be a positive effect (more time with children, doing household tasks) but is usually outweighed by stress.

Effects on society and the community (one block)
- [ ] N15 Higher spending on social welfare and lower income-tax and PRSI revenue; less spending by households so local shops and businesses suffer.
- [ ] N16 Loss of skills, output and economic growth; "brain drain" of emigrants.
- [ ] N17 Social exclusion and unemployment black spots; poverty, crime, vandalism and antisocial behaviour can increase in areas of high unemployment; strain on health, mental health and other services; family breakdown and homelessness.
- [ ] N18 Rural and urban effects: emigration and depopulation of areas; a weaker community spirit.

Supports for unemployed people and their families (one block; examiners ask for one named scheme, programme or voluntary organisation)
- [ ] N19 Statutory income supports from the Department of Social Protection, which operates its employment service as Intreo: Jobseeker's Benefit (social insurance, based on PRSI contributions, for a limited period), Jobseeker's Allowance (means-tested), Supplementary Welfare Allowance for urgent needs, and the Working Family Payment for low-paid workers with children. A statutory redundancy payment is paid to eligible employees made redundant (Redundancy Payments Acts).
- [ ] N20 Training and education: Back to Education Allowance (income support while taking an approved course); SOLAS and the Education and Training Boards (further education and training); Springboard+ courses; Community Employment and Tús (temporary work placements in communities) as work experience; Youthreach for young early school leavers.
- [ ] N21 Information and money advice: Citizens Information (free, impartial information on entitlements); MABS, the Money Advice and Budgeting Service (free, confidential help with debt and budgeting).
- [ ] N22 Voluntary organisations: the Society of St Vincent de Paul (charity shops and collections; helps with bills, food, school costs and housing; visits families); the Irish National Organisation of the Unemployed (INOU, advice, advocacy and information for unemployed people); local community and family resource centres (assumed generic). Benefits of voluntary work to the unemployed person (structure, skills, contacts, confidence) and to the community.
- [ ] N23 Answer format: name the scheme or organisation (about 4 marks), who it helps, and two details of what it provides.

Settings (one short block)
- [ ] N24 Case study of a family where a parent loses a job: identify the effects on each member, and the supports they could use.

Diagram: none.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any unemployment rate, number of people out of work, youth unemployment percentage, or recession year (for example from past-paper stems), because they are changing figures.
- Do not use: FÁS as a training or employment body, because it was replaced by SOLAS and the Education and Training Boards in 2013 (the 2007 marking scheme names it).
- Do not use: the Department of Social and Family Affairs or the Combat Poverty Agency as current bodies, because the first is now the Department of Social Protection and the second was dissolved.
- Checked: frictional, structural, cyclical and seasonal unemployment are named in the 2026 Higher Level marking scheme (extent of unemployment). Keep the four types, each with its cause.
- Do not use: JobPath or other named activation contractors, because the arrangements have changed and are unconfirmed.
- Do not use: payment durations, qualifying contributions, the age cut-offs for jobseeker payments or the size of any redundancy payment, because they change.
- Unemployed means seeking work: students, full-time carers and retired people are not counted as unemployed.
- Do not list only negatives about the family: say that effects vary with the length of unemployment, the family's savings and support, and the cause (redundancy against seasonal work).
- Do not confuse a statutory scheme (State-run, paid for by taxes: Jobseeker's payments, Intreo, Community Employment, BTEA) with a voluntary organisation (charity, run by volunteers: SVP, INOU).
- Neighbouring subsections: the extent, causes and definition of unemployment; poverty concepts (absolute and relative poverty, the poverty line, cycle of poverty, poverty trap); statutory and community responses in detail; and co-operatives and cottage industries belong to the Unemployment, Poverty and Social Responses chapter; household budgeting, MABS in depth and welfare payments belong to the Personal Finance chapter; emigration as a rural to urban cause belongs to the settlement subsection; leaving school early belongs to the Education chapter. Give one-line pointers only.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss the effects of unemployment on the individual, the family and society (the most frequent question in this chapter: 2004O, 2010O, 2013O, 2017H, 2018O, 2021O, 2023O, 2023H, 2026H). Expected: financial hardship and debt, loss of status and self-esteem, stress and depression, isolation, family strain and breakdown, effects on children, cost to the State, loss of skills, crime and social exclusion. Each effect needs one sentence of explanation and be assigned to the right level (individual, family, society).
- Describe the effects of unemployment on society (12 marks Higher Level 2022): welfare cost, lost tax, lost output, crime, black spots, emigration.
- Name and give details of one statutory scheme, training programme or course for the unemployed (Back to Education Allowance, Community Employment, Tús, Springboard+, Jobseeker's payments, Intreo).
- Name and give details of one voluntary organisation that supports the unemployed: SVP or INOU, and the benefits of voluntary work to the unemployed person and the community.
- Discuss the impact on individuals and families of emigrating to find work.
- Short questions: define unemployment; name two supports for an unemployed family; name two effects on children.
