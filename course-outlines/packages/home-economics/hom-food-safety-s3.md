# Package: hom-food-safety-s3 — Micro-organisms and the control of microbial spoilage

- **loId:** `hom-food-safety-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-safety` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.8
- **Syllabus scope:** Micro-organisms and the control of microbial spoilage [1.3.8]: the role of micro-organisms in food spoilage; the principles underlying the control of microbial spoilage. (Not marked [HL] in the outline: common to both levels.)
- **Also examined:** conditions for growth of micro-organisms (2026O, 2013O); general causes of food spoilage (2019H, 2009H, 2005O) and names of spoilage micro-organisms (2004O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. This subsection is the overview that links the others: what micro-organisms do to food, what they need, and the five or six principles by which spoilage is stopped. The detailed preservation methods (freezing, canning, drying, jam making, irradiation and so on) belong to the food preservation chapter; here they are named only as examples of a principle.

## 1. Must cover

Spoilage: what it is
- [ ] S1 Food spoilage is any change that makes food unfit or unpleasant to eat: off smell, taste, colour or texture, or growth of micro-organisms. Spoilage micro-organisms mainly make food unpleasant; pathogens (previous subsection) can make food unsafe while it looks and smells normal.
- [ ] S2 Causes of spoilage in general (one block): micro-organisms (bacteria, moulds, yeasts); enzyme action (own subsection); physical damage and bruising; moisture loss (shrinkage, wrinkling, hardening); rancidity of fats; chemical contamination (pesticides, antibiotics, metals). Micro-organisms are the main cause and this subsection covers them. (Assumed: a short list only for the enzyme and rancidity items.)

The three groups of spoilage micro-organisms (OL 2004 asked for two)
- [ ] M1 Bacteria: break down protein (putrefaction: foul smell, slime on meat, fish, eggs); souring of milk (lactic acid bacteria turn lactose to lactic acid, which curdles the milk); can produce toxins.
- [ ] M2 Moulds: visible fluffy, powdery or coloured growth on the surface of bread, cheese, fruit, jam; some produce mycotoxins; spores spread through the air.
- [ ] M3 Yeasts: ferment sugars in fruit juices, jams, syrups and fruit, giving carbon dioxide, alcohol and off-flavours, sometimes bubbling or swollen containers.
- [ ] M4 Which foods are at greatest risk: high-moisture, high-protein foods (meat, fish, poultry, eggs, milk, cooked rice); acidic sugary foods favour moulds and yeasts.

Conditions for growth of micro-organisms (exam: name and explain two to four)
- [ ] G1 Food (nutrients): protein and carbohydrate as an energy and building source.
- [ ] G2 Moisture: reactions and absorption take place in solution; dry foods support little growth; high-moisture foods are high risk.
- [ ] G3 Warmth: most spoilage and poisoning micro-organisms multiply fastest in the danger zone (5 to 63 °C); cold slows growth; freezing stops it without killing; heat kills.
- [ ] G4 Oxygen: aerobic (moulds) need it; anaerobic grow without it (e.g. in sealed packs); facultative adapt.
- [ ] G5 pH: most bacteria prefer near neutral; moulds and yeasts tolerate acid; strongly acidic foods are safer.
- [ ] G6 Time: some bacteria can divide about every 20 minutes under ideal conditions, so a few cells become millions in a few hours.

Principles of control (the syllabus's "principles underlying the control of microbial spoilage")
- [ ] P1 Principle 1, destroy micro-organisms: heat (cooking, pasteurisation, sterilisation, canning, bottling); irradiation (name only; detail in preservation).
- [ ] P2 Principle 2, prevent them reaching the food: hygiene, clean equipment, covering food, sealed packaging, sterile containers, separating raw and cooked food.
- [ ] P3 Principle 3, remove or reduce moisture (water activity): drying, salting, sugar (jam), concentrating; dissolved salt or sugar draws water out of microbial cells by osmosis.
- [ ] P4 Principle 4, lower the temperature: refrigeration slows growth; freezing stops growth without killing, so growth resumes on thawing.
- [ ] P5 Principle 5, change the acidity: pickling in vinegar, lemon juice, chutney, fermentation acids (pH too low for most bacteria).
- [ ] P6 Principle 6, remove oxygen or use chemical preservatives: vacuum packing, canning, modified-atmosphere packs; preservatives such as sulphur dioxide, benzoates, nitrites, natural ones (salt, sugar, vinegar, alcohol, smoke).
- [ ] P7 Combining principles: most foods use more than one (jam: heat, sugar, acid, sealing). Applying each to a named food is the usual exam task.

## 3. Traps

- Spoilage is not the same as food poisoning: spoilage organisms mostly change smell, taste and look; pathogens may not. Do not say all spoiled food is unsafe, or that safe-looking food is safe.
- Freezing does not kill micro-organisms; it only stops growth. Heat is the only home method that destroys them.
- Pasteurisation does not sterilise: it destroys pathogens and many spoilage organisms, not all (sterilisation destroys all micro-organisms and their spores).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: enzymic browning and over-ripening (enzymes and food spoilage); the three bacteria, toxic and infectious types, temperature and oxygen groups (food poisoning bacteria); mould and yeast structure and reproduction (moulds and yeasts); fermentation and its uses (uses of micro-organisms); storage temperatures, reheating and hygiene rules (safe food preparation); detailed methods of preservation and the principle behind each (preservation chapter).
- The old notes' irradiation block (shelf life, ripening, sprouting, effect on colour and texture) is correct but belongs to the preservation chapter; here name irradiation in one clause as a method that destroys micro-organisms.
- Give no pasteurisation or sterilisation temperatures and times; they belong to the preservation and dairy chapters and are not checked here.
- Moulds and yeasts show mostly on the surface, but moulds also grow through open-textured foods such as bread; write "mostly".
- Do not use: "most bacteria prefer a neutral pH, whereas moulds and yeasts tolerate acidic conditions" as an absolute, because some bacteria (lactic acid bacteria) tolerate acid; say "most", and keep lactic acid bacteria as the exception.

## 4. What exams ask

- Conditions for the growth of micro-organisms (2013O 6 marks, 2026O 6 marks): any two or three of food, moisture, warmth, oxygen, pH, time, each with a reason; the OL scheme rewards the reason (moisture because reactions happen in solution; warmth because most multiply in the danger zone).
- Short recall (2004O): two main types of micro-organism that spoil food (moulds, bacteria, yeasts); true/false on cross-contamination and cooking temperatures (2021O).
- Main causes of food spoilage (2019H 20 marks, 2009H 12 marks, 2005O 16 marks): micro-organisms, enzymes, physical damage, moisture loss, rancidity, contamination, with a concrete example of each; 4 marks per cause.
- Principles of control applied to a food: which principle (destroy, exclude, dry, cool, acidify, remove air or add a preservative) and the reason it works.
