# Package: hom-education-s1 — The purpose of education

- **loId:** `hom-education-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-education` · **syllabusRef:** 6.2
- **Syllabus scope:** "The purpose of education [6.2]: as a method of socialisation; in the physical, emotional, moral and intellectual development of the individual; as a preparation for work." No "Also examined" items for this subsection. No Higher Level only material: all of it is common (Ordinary and Higher Level).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Home Economics Scientific and Social syllabus (2001), the one examined in June 2027. This subsection is the "why" of education. The structure of the system (pre-school to adult), the factors behind achievement, equality of opportunity and access initiatives are all in the neighbouring subsections of this chapter. The programmes named here (SPHE, work experience) are named only as examples of how education serves a purpose, not described.

## 1. Must cover

Education as a method of socialisation (one block)
- [ ] S1 Socialisation is the lifelong process by which a person learns the norms, values, beliefs, language and acceptable behaviour of their society so that they can take part in it. The family is the first agent of socialisation; school is a second, major one ("secondary socialisation"; the terms primary and secondary socialisation are defined in the chapter on sociological concepts, so use without redefining). (assumed)
- [ ] S2 Formal socialisation: the planned, stated part of school: the curriculum, timetable, school rules, uniform, codes of conduct, sanctions and rewards. It teaches punctuality, respect for authority and rules, responsibility, and following instructions.
- [ ] S3 Informal socialisation: the unplanned part, often called the hidden curriculum: peer groups, playground rules, friendships, school routines, the school's ethos and the example set by teachers. It teaches sharing, taking turns, cooperation, teamwork and how to get on with others.
- [ ] S4 Education passes on the culture of society: language (English and Irish), customs, history, shared values, and the rights and duties of citizens. It brings together children from different backgrounds and helps them fit into wider society.
- [ ] S5 Exam use: for "education as a method of socialisation" give formal and informal (hidden curriculum) with an example of each, then say what is learned (respect, punctuality, sharing, teamwork, confidence, friendships).

Physical development (one block)
- [ ] PH1 Physical education, games, sport and dance develop gross motor skills (running, jumping, balance, coordination), strength, fitness and general body development.
- [ ] PH2 Practical subjects (Home Economics, Art, Construction Studies, Engineering, Science, and in early years play with building blocks, jigsaws, drawing and writing) develop fine motor skills, manual dexterity and hand-eye coordination.
- [ ] PH3 Lessons in nutrition, hygiene, safety and health (SPHE, Home Economics, PE) give pupils the knowledge to look after their health; school sport can build habits of exercise that last into adult life. Regular activity strengthens bones and muscles and builds cardiovascular fitness.

Emotional development (one block)
- [ ] E1 Starting school moves a child towards emotional independence from parents: they meet other adults and peers, cope with separation, and learn to depend on themselves.
- [ ] E2 Mixing with others develops empathy: understanding and responding to other people's feelings, and expressing one's own feelings appropriately. It supports healthy relationships and friendships.
- [ ] E3 Dealing with success and failure (tests, sport, performances) builds resilience, confidence, self-esteem and maturity. Praise and encouragement from teachers strengthen self-worth.
- [ ] E4 SPHE (Social, Personal and Health Education) teaches emotional literacy, relationships, coping skills and personal safety. It continues into senior cycle as a non-exam programme. Guidance counsellors and pastoral care give support.

Moral development (one block)
- [ ] M1 The school's ethos states its values and the behaviour it expects. School rules, sanctions and rewards teach right from wrong, fairness and accountability for one's actions.
- [ ] M2 Religious education, SPHE and subjects such as History and English raise questions of honesty, fairness, respect and tolerance. Teachers act as role models, and the peer group also shapes morals.
- [ ] M3 Pupils learn to respect the rights, property and opinions of others, and to accept responsibility, so becoming responsible citizens.

Intellectual development (one block)
- [ ] I1 A broad curriculum develops literacy, numeracy, languages, scientific reasoning, creativity and artistic skills, and builds knowledge across many subjects.
- [ ] I2 Thinking skills: analysis, argument, problem solving, research, critical thinking and the ability to apply knowledge. Homework, revision and class tasks train concentration and memory.
- [ ] I3 Examinations and continuous assessment measure progress and give motivation, goals and a sense of achievement. Competition in class, sport, quizzes and extracurricular activities pushes pupils to extend their abilities. Education also builds curiosity and a habit of lifelong learning.
- [ ] I4 Exam use: for 'discuss the purpose of education for the development of the individual' give the four areas in the order physical, emotional, moral, intellectual, each with at least two specific points and a named subject or activity as the example.

Preparation for work (one block)
- [ ] W1 Education gives the basic skills almost every job needs: literacy, numeracy, communication and computer/ICT skills.
- [ ] W2 It gives qualifications (Leaving Certificate, further education and third-level awards) that show employers what a person has learned and that open doors to jobs and higher-paid work. Higher education levels are linked with better employment prospects.
- [ ] W3 School life teaches work habits: respect for authority, punctuality, good attendance, reliability, trustworthiness, responsibility, self-discipline and teamwork. Social skills are learned by working with others.
- [ ] W4 Work experience (Transition Year, LCVP, LCA), career guidance and subjects chosen for a career help students link school to work; vocational and practical subjects teach skills used directly in jobs. (Work experience is detailed in "The provision of education in Ireland".)
- [ ] W5 Education matters more as industry changes: technology and a service-based economy raise the education required for employment, and education prepares people to retrain throughout their working life. (assumed: links to the chapter on work)

## 3. Traps

- Four developmental areas are named in the syllabus: physical, emotional, moral and intellectual. Do not add "social development" as a fifth area for the individual (social learning is covered under socialisation). A past paper (2025 OL) did ask how education contributes to "social and physical development", so social points are given under socialisation and physical under PH1–PH3.
- Keep formal and informal socialisation distinct: rules and timetable are formal; peer groups and the hidden curriculum are informal. Do not describe the hidden curriculum as the written curriculum.
- Do not use: "school is a child's first regular experience of a group outside the family" as a flat statement, because many children attend pre-school or childcare first; say "an early and long experience of a group".
- Do not use: "Junior Certificate" as the name of the current third-year qualification, because Junior Cycle replaced it (the Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement is awarded now). Say "Junior Cycle" and "Leaving Certificate" when listing qualifications.
- Do not describe types of school, programmes (TY, LCA, LCVP details), pre-school or adult education here; they belong to the provision of education subsection. Factors that raise or lower achievement belong to the next subsection; early school leaving and equality belong to the equality subsection.
- Do not describe the unemployment effects or laws protecting young workers here; those belong to the chapters on unemployment and on work and leisure.
- Do not give a sociology definition of "role", "norm" or "agents of socialisation" at length; one clause only.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss the purpose or role of education in the development of the individual, with each of physical, emotional, moral and intellectual development named. Expect marks for one specific point plus an example each, for example manual dexterity through practical subjects, empathy through group work, right and wrong through school rules and ethos, thinking skills through problem-solving subjects. Appears at both levels (2008 HL, 2013 OL, 2021 OL).
- Education as a method of socialisation and as a preparation for work (2024 HL, 20 marks): formal and informal socialisation, behaviour and culture passed on; literacy, numeracy, qualifications, work attitudes and work experience.
- Outline four ways education prepares a young person for work (2019 OL): basic skills, ICT, qualities (respect for authority, punctuality, trustworthiness, responsibility, self-discipline), social skills, work experience.
- How education contributes to social and physical development of children (2025 OL): formal and informal socialisation with examples; physical through PE, practical work and extracurricular activities.
- A typical short question: name the two types of socialisation in school, or give three work-related qualities taught in school.
