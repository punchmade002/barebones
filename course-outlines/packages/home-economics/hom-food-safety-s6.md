# Package: hom-food-safety-s6 — Safe food preparation

- **loId:** `hom-food-safety-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-safety` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.10
- **Syllabus scope:** Safe food preparation [1.3.10]: food storage, reheating procedures, personal hygiene and kitchen hygiene. (Not marked [HL]: common to both levels.)
- **Also examined:** 2025H, 2023O, 2022O, 2021H, 2012H, 2004H; also 2018O, 2011O, 2008H, 2007O.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The exam schemes give temperature figures that differ from current FSAI and safefood guidance in two places (reheating and cooking, see Traps). The notes follow the figures the SEC schemes print, because the schemes decide marks, and teach the reason behind each. Cooking-method detail belongs to the cooking chapter; the HACCP system that applies these rules is in a later subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Why these rules exist (assumed, one block)
- [ ] W1 (assumed) Food poisoning bacteria multiply fastest in the danger zone (5 to 63 °C), so safe preparation keeps food cold, hot or moving quickly through the zone, and stops bacteria spreading from raw to cooked food (cross-contamination).

Food storage (short questions ask for 3 to 6 guidelines)
- [ ] St1 Check use-by and best-before dates and follow storage instructions on the label. Use-by is a safety date for highly perishable food; best-before is a quality date.
- [ ] St2 Store perishable food in the fridge at 5 °C or below (schemes print "below 5 °C" and "0 to 5 °C"; some "2 to 5 °C"). Check fridge and freezer temperature with a thermometer.
- [ ] St3 Store raw meat, poultry and fish on the bottom shelf, on a plate, loosely covered, with shop wrapping removed, so juices cannot drip on cooked or ready-to-eat food (2011O).
- [ ] St4 Keep raw and cooked foods apart and covered or in sealed containers, to prevent cross-contamination, drying out and odour transfer.
- [ ] St5 Cool cooked food quickly before refrigerating or freezing; do not overpack the fridge so cold air circulates.
- [ ] St6 Freezer: fast-freeze at -25 °C (scheme), store at -18 °C; never refreeze thawed food (scheme wording).
- [ ] St7 Thaw frozen food completely before cooking, preferably in the fridge on the bottom shelf.
- [ ] St8 Dry foods: store in a cool, dry, well-ventilated cupboard in vermin-proof containers; keep storage areas clean.
- [ ] St9 Remove fruit and vegetables from plastic wrapping and check for mould regularly. Keep perishable food out of the fridge for as short a time as possible during preparation.

Cooking, reheating and hot holding
- [ ] R1 Cook food thoroughly, with the right time and temperature; turn or stir so heat reaches the centre; meat, poultry and minced meat are cooked right through. The scheme's core temperature for poultry is at least 72 °C (2014H, 2021H); current FSAI guidance of 75 °C is also correct.
- [ ] R2 Reheat food quickly and thoroughly, above 100 °C throughout, and reheat only once (scheme); current FSAI and safefood guidance of piping hot, about 70 to 75 °C at the core, is also correct. High-risk foods such as stews and gravies should be brought to boiling. Cover food when reheating; in a microwave, stir or turn and allow standing time.
- [ ] R3 Serve cooked food immediately; if holding it hot, keep it above 63 °C, and eat it within about 90 minutes (1.5 hours) (scheme).
- [ ] R4 Do not keep food in the danger zone; cool leftovers quickly, refrigerate and use soon.

Personal hygiene (exam: 4 to 6 points)
- [ ] P1 Wash hands in hot water with liquid soap (and a nailbrush for the nails) before handling food, and after the toilet, sneezing or coughing, changing nappies, handling pets or rubbish and handling raw meat; dry on a clean towel or paper towel.
- [ ] P2 Short, clean nails; no jewellery; hair tied back or covered; clean apron or clothing, changed when soiled.
- [ ] P3 Cover cuts, boils and sores with a brightly coloured waterproof plaster, so it can be seen if it falls in food.
- [ ] P4 Do not cough or sneeze over food; do not taste food with fingers or lick fingers.
- [ ] P5 A person with diarrhoea or vomiting must not prepare or handle food for others.

Kitchen hygiene
- [ ] K1 Clean and disinfect work surfaces often and wash equipment in hot water and detergent; clean as you go.
- [ ] K2 Use colour-coded chopping boards and separate utensils for raw and cooked foods; never prepare raw and cooked foods on the same surface.
- [ ] K3 Handle food as little as possible; keep food covered.
- [ ] K4 Kitchen design: floors, walls and work surfaces easy to clean, good lighting, effective ventilation to reduce condensation (which encourages micro-organisms), clean water supply, no access for pets or pests, covered waste bin emptied regularly, clean cloths and dishtowels changed often.
- [ ] K5 Food hygiene (2007O heading): cook thoroughly, thaw fully, buy from reputable shops and check dates and packaging.

## 3. Traps

- Scheme figures differ from current FSAI and safefood guidance (searched): reheating is "piping hot, at least 70 °C" and poultry cooking 75 °C in current guidance, while the SEC schemes print above 100 °C and at least 72 °C. Teach the scheme figures, because the schemes decide marks, and keep to the Do-not-use lines below.
- Freezing does not kill bacteria; thawing allows them to multiply. "Never refreeze thawed food" is the scheme's rule; the exception (raw food that has been cooked after thawing may be frozen) is not examined. Do not add it as a general rule.
- Cross-contamination is the transfer of bacteria from raw to cooked or ready-to-eat food (directly, or by hands, boards, cloths, utensils, juices). Do not confuse it with contamination in general.
- Use-by and best-before are different (safety versus quality); both belong here, with the legal label rules in food legislation.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the bacteria and their growth conditions (food poisoning bacteria); the HACCP stages of purchase, transport, storage, preparation, cooking and serving for a named dish (HACCP); the role of the environmental health officer and the Hygiene of Foodstuffs Regulations (national agencies, food legislation); methods of preservation (preservation chapter); fridge and freezer appliance study (appliances chapter).
- Do not use: "never eat eggs raw" or "certain foods should never be eaten raw" (2004H scheme), because it is not general guidance; the safe statement is that raw and lightly cooked eggs carry a Salmonella risk for vulnerable people.
- Do not use: "reheat for at least five minutes at 100 °C" (2018O scheme), because it is a single-scheme figure that conflicts with current guidance; say "above 100 °C throughout" only.
- Do not use: "poultry cooked to 74 °C for two minutes" (2008H), because the figures disagree across sources; teach "at least 72 °C" as in the 2014H and 2021H schemes, and accept 75 °C as current guidance.
- Do not use: 65 °C as the hot-holding temperature (printed in some schemes), because FSAI guidance and the danger zone use 63 °C.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss the importance of correct storage and cooking or reheating (2025H 20 marks, 2021H 16 marks, 2012H 24 marks across storage, cooking and reheating, kitchen hygiene, 2008H 16 marks): temperatures (fridge, freezer, danger zone, reheat), raw/cooked separation, cooling, thawing, serving promptly, with the reason each prevents food poisoning. Two points per heading at 4 marks each.
- Short guidelines: three headings (storage, personal hygiene, reheating) at OL (2022O 6 marks, 2018O 6 marks); five guidelines for the home (2004H 10 marks); recommendation under personal, kitchen and food hygiene (2007O); how to store raw meat to prevent cross-contamination (2011O); four kitchen hygiene practices (2023O 16 marks).
- True/false (2021O): processing extends shelf life; transfer of bacteria from raw to cooked food is cross-contamination; cooking at 50 °C kills all bacteria (false).
