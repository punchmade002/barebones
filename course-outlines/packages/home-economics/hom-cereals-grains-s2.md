## 1. Must cover

The main cereals and products (one block)
- [ ] C1 Six main cereals eaten as food: wheat, rice, oats, maize (corn), barley, rye. Cereals are seeds of grasses and the staple food in many countries (assumed from the structure subsection).
- [ ] C2 Wheat: flours (white, wholemeal, self-raising, strong), bread, pasta, couscous, semolina, biscuits, cakes, breakfast cereals (Weetabix, shredded wheat).
- [ ] C3 Rice: white and brown rice, rice flour, rice cakes, rice pudding, rice noodles, puffed rice cereal, risotto.
- [ ] C4 Oats: porridge, oatmeal, oatcakes, flapjacks, muesli, granola.
- [ ] C5 Maize (corn): cornflour, cornflakes, popcorn, polenta, corn oil, tortillas; sweetcorn is eaten as a vegetable.
- [ ] C6 Barley: pearl barley (soups and stews), barley flakes, barley water, malt (used for beer and whiskey).
- [ ] C7 Rye: rye bread, rye flour, crispbread.
- [ ] C8 Each answer must be a different product and made from the cereal named; learn at least two per cereal.

Breakfast cereals (one block)
- [ ] B1 Types: flaked (cornflakes), puffed (puffed rice), shredded (shredded wheat), mixtures (muesli, granola). Porridge oats need cooking; ready-to-eat cereals do not.
- [ ] B2 Many are fortified with vitamins and iron; sugar, salt and fat vary between brands, so read the nutrition panel and ingredients list (listed in descending order of weight, so a wholegrain cereal should come first).

Fortification (one block; 2012O, 15 marks: explain it and comment on the increasing consumption)
- [ ] F1 Fortification is the addition of nutrients (vitamins and minerals) to a food, either to replace nutrients lost in processing or to raise the level above that normally present.
- [ ] F2 Examples the schemes accept: vitamins A and D in low-fat milk; folic acid added to some foods to reduce neural tube defects; iron and B group vitamins in breakfast cereals; calcium or vitamin D to help prevent osteoporosis.
- [ ] F3 Why consumption is rising (scheme points): more diet-related illness and fortification attempts to address it; raises the food value of foods; supports healthy eating; an easy way to raise intake of nutrients people tend to lack.
- [ ] F4 Limits: a fortified product can still be high in sugar or salt; it does not replace a varied diet.
- [ ] F5 Manufacturers and special diets (2026H, 16 marks): gluten-free cereal foods from rice, maize and other gluten-free ingredients, made to avoid cross-contamination and carrying a gluten-free declaration; allergens emphasised in the ingredients list; high-fibre and wholegrain lines; lower-sugar and reduced-salt lines; fortified products; vegan products; portion packs. A gluten-free product can still be high in sugar or fat.

Selection and storage (one block; 2018O)
- [ ] S1 Selection: check the best-before date, undamaged sealed packaging, the ingredients list and the nutrition panel.
- [ ] S2 Storage: cool dry place; airtight container once opened; use by the best-before date; away from strong-smelling foods (cereals absorb odours).
- [ ] S3 Why: damp encourages mould and insect pests such as weevils; wholegrain cereals and wholemeal flour keep for less time because the oil in the germ goes rancid.
- [ ] S4 Bread keeps at room temperature in a bin or wrapped, or frozen; it goes stale faster in the fridge.
- [ ] S5 Effects of poor storage: mould, infestation, staleness, rancid flavour, loss of quality.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "folic acid is added to flour by law in Ireland" or "all white flour is fortified here", because flour fortification in Ireland is voluntary (Department of Health answer, 2024) and the mid-2000s plan to make bread fortification mandatory was not carried out. Say some breads and breakfast cereals are fortified.
- Do not use: UK mandatory flour fortification as an Irish rule, because it does not apply here.
- Do not use: "rice paper" as a rice product, because it is a loose answer (wrappers are usually made from rice flour or starch); give rice cakes or rice pudding.
- Do not use: "wholemeal bread keeps as long as white", because wholegrain products keep for less time.
- Sweetcorn is a vegetable; give cornflour, popcorn or cornflakes as the maize product.
- Fortification is not the same as a preservative or flavouring additive; it adds nutrients.
- Neighbouring subsections: flour types (strong, plain, self-raising, cornflour as a flour), extraction rate and gluten belong to the milling subsection; the grain's parts and wholegrain benefits to the structure subsection; staling chemistry and raising agents to the cooking subsection; general food labelling law to the food legislation chapter (only the gluten-free line is needed here).
- Brand names only where a scheme accepts them (Weetabix, Rice Krispies).

## 4. What exams ask

- Name three cereals and a different product from each (6 to 12 marks; 2023O names wheat, rice and maize).
- Explain fortification, give examples and comment on rising consumption (2012O).
- Outline three guidelines for storing cereal products (2018O): cool dry place, airtight once opened, best-before date, wholegrain's shorter shelf life.
- Higher Level: how manufacturers cater for coeliacs and other dietary needs (2026H): gluten-free ingredients, cross-contamination control and declaration, allergen emphasis, reduced sugar and salt, fibre, fortification, with the caution that a special-diet claim does not make a product healthy.
- Choosing breakfast cereals by reading the nutrition panel.
