# Package: hom-work-leisure-s3 — Unpaid and voluntary work

- **loId:** `hom-work-leisure-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-work-leisure` · **syllabusRef:** 6.3.1
- **Syllabus scope:** "the role of unpaid and voluntary work in the community and the benefits to the volunteer and the community." Also examined: 2026H, 2021H, 2018H, 2014H, 2005H (past parts on voluntary work, benefits to the volunteer and to the community, and how voluntary work contributes to social and environmental change). Not Higher Level only; common to both levels.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Neighbours: the basic definition of the three types of work is in the first subsection (one line is repeated here to set up the contrast); national voluntary agencies that support people at risk of poverty (e.g. in a "name one voluntary agency" question) belong to the poverty chapter; community employment schemes and foreign investment belong to the unemployment chapter.

## 1. Must cover

Unpaid work
- [ ] U1 Unpaid work (in the home and home-related): child care, housework, cooking, laundry, caring for an older or disabled relative, DIY, home maintenance, gardening, household management. Done without financial reward, mainly within the household and often out of family duty.
- [ ] U2 Its role: keeps households and the community functioning, saves families the cost of paying for services, supports the paid workforce (children and workers are cared for and fed), and makes family life possible. It is not counted in paid output and has no wage, pension or sick pay of its own, so its value is often overlooked. It is often shared unequally, falling more on women; it may be monotonous; carers can be isolated or stressed.
- [ ] U3 Unpaid carers: people who look after relatives at home (often with State supports such as carer's payments, not given here by amount) save the State and families large costs and give care in a familiar home setting.

Voluntary work
- [ ] V1 Voluntary work: unpaid work done freely, by choice, for others or for a cause outside the household, usually through an organisation. The volunteer gets no wage (expenses may be repaid).
- [ ] V2 Where and what: charities, community groups, sports clubs, schools, parish and church groups, youth organisations and local development groups. Typical activities: charity shops and fundraising; meals on wheels or transport for older people; visiting the housebound; coaching and mentoring young people (club coaches, youth leaders); running local events; community clean-ups; first aid and emergency or rescue services; community care and support groups; environmental work.
- [ ] V3 Contrast with unpaid home work: voluntary is chosen, for people outside the household, usually organised and recognised; unpaid home work is a family duty, done for the household, usually alone and little recognised. Both are unpaid and neither is employment.

Benefits to the volunteer
- [ ] B1 Personal development: self-esteem, confidence, a sense of purpose and achievement; fulfilling one's potential; insight into the lives of others; empathy.
- [ ] B2 Skills and experience: teamwork, communication, organisation, leadership, training; helpful on a CV and as a reference; may lead to paid work or to expertise in an area.
- [ ] B3 Social: new friends, social contact, belonging, less isolation (especially for retired, unemployed people, those new to an area).
- [ ] B4 Wellbeing: active, healthy, a way to use free time well; a good use of time for people who are retired or between jobs.

Benefits to the community
- [ ] C1 Services that would otherwise not be available or affordable: meals for older people, transport, youth clubs, sports coaching, support groups.
- [ ] C2 Support for vulnerable groups: older people, people with disabilities, lone parents, the homeless, people at risk of poverty, new arrivals.
- [ ] C3 Community spirit and social cohesion; people from different backgrounds working for a shared aim; less crime and vandalism where young people are involved in clubs.
- [ ] C4 Local facilities (halls, pitches, parks) built, maintained and improved by volunteer effort and fundraising.
- [ ] C5 Saves the State and local bodies money and complements paid services (it does not replace them).
- [ ] C6 Raises awareness of local issues; encourages participation and active citizenship.

Contribution to social and environmental change (the 2005H question)
- [ ] S1 Social change: campaigning for services and for the rights of groups; helping people to get the supports they are entitled to; supporting inclusion of people with disabilities, minorities, Travellers and migrants; reducing isolation; passing skills on to others so work continues.
- [ ] S2 Environmental change: litter picks and clean-ups, tree planting, conservation work, community gardens and growing schemes, recycling projects, cleaning rivers and beaches, local heritage projects. The Tidy Towns competition is a well-known example of a community improvement effort driven by local volunteers.

Limits (one short block)
- [ ] L1 Volunteer burnout when a few people do most of the work; commitment hard to sustain as people take on paid work or family duties; out-of-pocket costs; volunteers are not employees and have no pay, sick pay or pension; volunteers need training and Garda vetting for work with children or vulnerable adults (the process is Garda vetting through the organisation).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Voluntary work generally carries more social recognition than unpaid work in the home" (the current note), because it is an opinion, not a fact the marking schemes support; the safe contrast is chosen versus duty, and community versus household.
- Do not use any figure for the number of volunteers, value of volunteering or carers, because they change (census and survey figures).
- Do not call volunteering "free labour" or compare it with paid work as a substitute; the point is that it complements paid services.
- Unpaid home work is a type of work, but not voluntary. Do not blur them.
- Work experience placements and internships are unpaid but are not voluntary work as the syllabus means it.
- Benefits to the volunteer and benefits to the community are different lists: a question on one must not be answered with the other. Keep them in separate blocks.
- National voluntary agencies for poverty (SVP in detail, etc.) and community employment schemes (CE, Tús) belong to other chapters; SVP appears only as an example here.
- Do not name charities with details or founding dates other than SVP as an example.

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain or differentiate unpaid work and voluntary work (2005H differentiates voluntary and unpaid; also asked with paid work in 2013H, 2016O, 2024O): voluntary is chosen and for others in the community, often with a charity; unpaid is within the home with no pay and may not be by choice.
- How voluntary work lets a person fulfil their potential (2005H): reward and fulfilment, personal development, new skills, insight into others' lives, becoming an expert, confidence.
- How voluntary work contributes to social and environmental change in the community (2005H): improving services and inclusion, community spirit, litter and conservation projects.
- The benefits of voluntary work for the volunteer and the community (2014H, 2018H, 2021H, 2026H): three or four developed points each, from the lists above.
- Roles of unpaid carers and voluntary organisations in the community; ways to encourage volunteering (training, recognition, expenses).
