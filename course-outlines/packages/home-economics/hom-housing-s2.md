## 1. Must cover

Finance needed to buy a home (one block)
- [ ] F1 The amount of finance required depends on: the price of the house (size, type, location, new or second-hand), the buyer's own savings and deposit, and the extra costs of buying. Amount borrowed = price minus deposit (plus any costs the buyer cannot pay from savings).
- [ ] F2 Extra costs of buying: legal fees (solicitor), stamp duty on the purchase, lender's valuation and survey fees, an application or lender fee, land registry fees, home insurance, and costs of furnishing or repairs. State what each pays for.
- [ ] F3 A deposit is the buyer's own money towards the price; lenders require a minimum percentage deposit, set by Central Bank rules, and lend the balance as a mortgage. Saving a deposit is a main barrier for first-time buyers.
- [ ] F4 A mortgage is a long-term loan to buy property, secured on the property: if repayments are not made the lender can take possession and sell it (repossession). It is repaid with interest, in regular (monthly) instalments over a term of many years.

Sources of finance (one block)
- [ ] S1 The buyer's own savings; a mortgage from a bank or other lending institution; a local authority home loan (government-backed, for those who qualify and cannot get a commercial loan); a credit union loan (smaller amounts, e.g. for a deposit or for home improvement); help from parents or family (a gift or loan towards the deposit).
- [ ] S2 State supports for buyers, named only: the First Home shared equity scheme (the State and participating lenders take a share in a new home, which the buyer can buy back), the Help to Buy incentive for first-time buyers of new homes, and affordable purchase schemes run by local authorities. Give what each is in a line; no amounts or limits (these schemes are also touched on in "Housing provision").

Conditions attached to mortgage approval (one block, the most examined item)
- [ ] C1 Borrowing limit (loan-to-income): the loan is limited to a set multiple of the applicant's gross (before tax) income. The multiple is set by Central Bank rules and changes; give the idea only.
- [ ] C2 Loan-to-value limit: the loan is limited to a percentage of the property's value, so the buyer needs a minimum deposit, with different rules for first-time buyers and others.
- [ ] C3 Secure, continuous employment, and proof of income: payslips, P60, tax and bank statements (the self-employed supply accounts).
- [ ] C4 Good financial record: a clean credit history (no missed repayments, no unpaid debts), a record of regular saving, and reasonable spending habits. Existing debts and commitments reduce what is lent.
- [ ] C5 Term of the loan: normally a long period; an older applicant is offered a shorter term so the loan is repaid before retirement.
- [ ] C6 The property: valued and surveyed by the lender, in good condition and good value as security; the lender checks the title deeds through a solicitor; the buyer must have house insurance (buildings) for the term.
- [ ] C7 Mortgage protection insurance (see below) and proof of identity and address (the lender must confirm who the borrower is).

Mortgage protection (one block)
- [ ] M1 Mortgage protection is a term life assurance policy for the length of the mortgage and the amount of the loan. If a borrower dies during the term, the insurer repays the outstanding balance to the lender, and the family keeps the home.
- [ ] M2 It is required by law for most mortgages: under the Consumer Credit Act 1995 a lender must make sure that the borrower has mortgage protection, with exceptions (for example where cover cannot be obtained because of age or health). The borrower may buy it from the lender's group scheme, an insurer or a broker.
- [ ] M3 Life assurance in general: premiums are paid and a sum is paid to the family when the insured person dies; life assurance can run for life, whereas mortgage protection is for the term only and the sum insured falls as the loan is repaid (a decreasing term policy).
- [ ] M4 Mortgage protection does not pay the repayments if the borrower is ill, disabled or made redundant (income protection is a separate policy).
- [ ] M5 Links by mortgage type: an annuity mortgage and a pension-linked mortgage need a separate mortgage protection policy; an endowment mortgage has life assurance built in.

Types of mortgage (one block)
- [ ] T1 Annuity (repayment) mortgage: each repayment includes part interest and part capital, so the amount owed falls over the term; early repayments are mostly interest; the most common type; the interest rate may be fixed, variable or a combination; needs mortgage protection.
- [ ] T2 Endowment mortgage: only interest is paid on the loan, plus a premium into a life assurance (endowment) policy designed to pay off the loan when it matures; the policy may not produce enough to clear the loan; no separate protection policy is needed; no longer widely available.
- [ ] T3 Pension-linked mortgage: interest is paid on the loan and contributions go into a pension; the loan is repaid from the pension lump sum at retirement; needs mortgage protection; suited to the self-employed because of tax relief on pension contributions; rare today.
- [ ] T4 Tracker mortgage: the interest rate follows the European Central Bank (ECB) rate plus a fixed margin, so repayments rise and fall with the ECB rate.
- [ ] T5 Other types named in marking schemes: interest-only mortgage (only interest is paid for a period, so the capital is still owed) and current account mortgage (savings in a linked account reduce the interest charged). Local authority mortgages and loans are also accepted.
- [ ] T6 The exam asks for "one type" with a name and a description of two to three points; the notes should give annuity, endowment, pension-linked and tracker fully enough to answer on any.

Interest rates (one block)
- [ ] I1 Fixed rate: the rate is set for an agreed period (a number of years), so repayments are the same and the buyer is protected from rises; but the buyer misses out if rates fall, and ending early may carry a charge; the fixed rate is often higher than the variable at the start.
- [ ] I2 Variable rate: the rate changes with the lender's rate, which tends to follow market and ECB rates; repayments fall when rates fall and rise when rates rise, so there is risk for the borrower.
- [ ] I3 Combination (split) rate: the loan is divided into a fixed part and a variable part.
- [ ] I4 Effect of term: a longer term gives smaller repayments but more interest in total; a shorter term gives higher repayments and less total interest.

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Interest as the cost of borrowing; gross against net income; insurance as pooling risk with a premium.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the figures in marking schemes (a 10% deposit, a loan of 3.5 times gross income, a 90% loan-to-value limit, a price ceiling of €220,000, 5% deposits on local authority mortgages), because the Central Bank limits and scheme terms change.
- Do not use: "mortgage indemnity bond" and "mortgage interest relief", because they are old (2008–2014 marking schemes) and no longer apply.
- Do not use: "tracker mortgages are available to new borrowers", because trackers were withdrawn from new lending; keep the tracker as a type students must describe, in the past or general sense.
- Do not use: "variable rates are set by the ECB", because the lender sets its own variable rate; say it tends to follow the ECB and market rates.
- Do not use: "building society" as a main source of finance, because Irish mortgages are now mainly from banks and other lending institutions; say "lending institution".
- Do not use: that an older person always receives a mortgage of a fixed number of years (such as 20–40), because terms vary; say "a long period".
- Do not use: that a current account (offset) mortgage or an interest-only mortgage is a common Irish product, because they are rarely offered; give them one line each as marking schemes name them.
- Do not mix up: mortgage protection (pays the loan if the borrower dies) and home insurance (buildings and contents); the lender requires both. Do not mix up endowment (life policy repays the capital at maturity) with annuity (capital repaid steadily).
- Hire purchase, credit cards, loans for goods and the Consumer Credit Act's rules on APR belong to "Methods of payment and credit" in the personal finance chapter; leave them out here. The existing notes contain a hire purchase block that must be dropped.
- Comparative running costs of buying against renting, and the advantages of each, belong to "Housing choices"; here only the financing is covered.
- The Higher Level label covers the whole subsection: mark every block's heading, and mark nothing outside it.

## 4. What exams ask

- Conditions required to qualify for a mortgage (the most frequent Higher Level item: 6-mark Section A, a 16-mark part, or a 24-mark discussion; seen in 2008, 2011, 2017, 2022, 2025): expected answers are the items in C1 to C7 (borrowing limit, loan-to-value or deposit, secure employment, proof of income, credit history and saving, term and age, property survey, mortgage protection and home insurance). Four developed conditions beat eight named ones.
- Name and describe one type of mortgage (14 to 16 marks; 2012, 2017, 2025): the name earns about 4 marks and the description two or three points; annuity is the safest choice (repayments of interest and capital, the balance falls, protection needed, rate options).
- Short explanations (6 marks): annuity and endowment mortgages (2004); one advantage of a fixed rate and of a variable rate (2007); explain fixed and variable rates (2023).
- Life assurance and mortgage protection (6 marks, 2016): premium, sum paid on death, term only, clears the debt. A 10-mark part (2008) asks what mortgage protection is and why it is necessary: the family would otherwise lose the home or inherit the debt.
- Outline sources of finance for a house purchase (own savings and deposit, lender, local authority loan, credit union, family help).
- Marks follow reasons: say why the lender wants each condition (to be sure the loan can be repaid and the property can be sold if it is not).
