# Package: hom-clothing-selection-s4 — Elements and principles of design in clothing

- **loId:** `hom-clothing-selection-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-clothing-selection` · **syllabusRef:** 5.1, 5.3
- **Syllabus scope:** the elements and principles of design; their application to garment construction and evaluation; evaluating garments or outfits for the relationship between design and function, comfort and aesthetic appeal. Settings: evaluating garments or pictures of garments against the elements and principles. Also examined: using colour and design to flatter body size and shape (2022H, 2014H). No [HL] items marked.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The marking schemes list five elements (colour, pattern, shape, texture, line) and four or five principles (balance, proportion, emphasis, harmony, rhythm/repetition). The "evaluate the outfits shown" question is Section C, Elective 2, and is the largest mark-earner in this chapter (15-18 marks at both levels), so the evaluation method is the centre of this subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Elements of design (one block)
- [ ] EL1 Line: direction or edge created by seams, necklines, hems, pleats, trims, fastenings and pattern. Vertical lines suggest height and slimness; horizontal lines suggest width and shorten; diagonal lines give movement and contrast; curved lines soften.
- [ ] EL2 Shape: the outline of a garment or of its parts (neckline, sleeve, pocket); silhouette; narrow rectangular shapes slim the figure, triangular or A-line shapes widen the hem or shoulders; fitted bodice with a full skirt gives an hourglass shape.
- [ ] EL3 Colour: hue, tone (light/dark) and intensity; warm colours (red, orange) appear to advance, cool colours (blue, green) appear to recede; dark colours reduce apparent size; light and bright colours enlarge. Colour schemes: monochrome or tonal, contrasting, complementary. Colour suits skin, hair and eye tones.
- [ ] EL4 Texture: surface quality seen or felt; shiny/smooth surfaces reflect light and enlarge; matt surfaces absorb light and reduce; rough, thick or heavily textured fabrics look bulkier; smooth fabrics look neater.
- [ ] EL5 Pattern: repeat of line, shape or colour (stripes, checks, florals, geometric prints); small patterns suit small figures, large figures carry large patterns; patterned areas draw the eye (near the face or hem); vertical stripes add height, horizontal add width.

Principles of design (one block)
- [ ] PR1 Balance: even distribution of visual weight; vertical, symmetrical or asymmetrical.
- [ ] PR2 Proportion: relationship of the sizes of the garment's parts to each other and to the wearer (pocket size to garment, collar to neckline, jacket length to skirt length).
- [ ] PR3 Emphasis: a centre of interest that draws the eye (a brooch, contrast trim, belt, bold neckline, bright accessory); one centre of emphasis only per outfit.
- [ ] PR4 Harmony (unity): all parts work together, e.g. colours, fabrics and style that complement each other.
- [ ] PR5 Rhythm or repetition: repeated colour, line or shape that moves the eye through the garment (e.g. a repeated button or stripe).
- [ ] PR6 Say how the principle is shown in the particular outfit; do not only define.

Using elements to flatter body size and shape (Also examined, 2022H and 2014H)
- [ ] FL1 To look taller or slimmer: vertical lines, one colour head to toe or tonal shades, dark colours (navy, black) that absorb light, V-necks, princess seams, a single long line from shoulder to hem, narrow shapes.
- [ ] FL2 To balance or add width: horizontal stripes, light or bright colours, bulky textures, contrasting top and bottom colours to balance shoulders and hips.
- [ ] FL3 Draw attention to a feature or away from another: pattern or bright colour near the face for the upper body, a belt to define the waist, dark colour over areas to de-emphasise.
- [ ] FL4 Colour also flatters hair, eyes and skin (e.g. blues can brighten).
- [ ] FL5 Shape, fit and proportion: choose lines that suit the figure; a short jacket and slim trousers lengthen the leg line.

Application to construction (one block)
- [ ] AP1 Seams and darts create line and shape: darts shape bust, waist and hip; princess seams shape without a waist seam.
- [ ] AP2 Pleats, tucks and gathers add fullness and movement; used at waist, sleeve or skirt.
- [ ] AP3 Pockets, collars, buttons, trims and fastenings add line and emphasis; position them in proportion.
- [ ] AP4 Fabric choice affects the finished design: crisp fabrics hold shape (tailoring), soft fabrics drape; fabric texture and pattern must suit the design lines.
- [ ] AP5 Design application and colour application (named in neighbouring exam parts): dyeing; screen/roller/stencil/transfer printing, embroidery, appliqué.

Evaluating garments and outfits (one block, the heart of the exam question)
- [ ] EV1 Function: does it suit the purpose, weather, activity? (freedom of movement for sport, durability, easy care, pockets, fastenings that work.)
- [ ] EV2 Comfort: fabric (soft, absorbent, breathable, stretch), fit and ease, weather versatility, weight, footwear.
- [ ] EV3 Aesthetic appeal: colour scheme, line, shape, texture, proportion, balance and emphasis working together; accessories.
- [ ] EV4 Design/function relationship: each design feature serves a purpose (a drawcord hood, reflective trim, a zip opening).
- [ ] EV5 Other headings that appear: shape, proportion, design features, current fashion trend, lifestyle, cost, care, suitability for the occasion.
- [ ] EV6 Method: for each heading make a point from the picture, then give the reason (fabric, cut, colour). Spread points across all headings; two points per heading is typical for 15-18 marks.
- [ ] EV7 Diagram cue: label a sketch with the elements and principles used (line, shape, proportion, harmony, emphasis).

## 3. Traps

- Elements are the building blocks (line, shape, colour, texture, pattern); principles are how they are arranged (balance, proportion, emphasis, harmony, rhythm). Do not mix the lists.
- Colour: dark colours do not "shrink the body"; they make it appear smaller. Say "appear".
- Emphasis: one centre only; several competing focal points spoil it.
- Balance has three kinds named in the schemes: vertical, symmetrical, asymmetrical.
- The design process, pattern selection and construction steps belong to "The design process and garment construction"; here, mention a dart or seam only for its effect on line and shape.
- Fabric tests, fibre profiles and finishes belong to the fibres and fabrics material and the textile care subsection.
- Trends as such belong to "Current fashion trends"; here, only the design analysis.
- Do not use: "space" or "form" as additional elements, because the marking schemes list five elements and extra elements invite lost marks for mismatched terms.
- Do not use: "pattern placed near the face distracts from the lower body" as a certain effect, because it is a common styling claim that no scheme states; say pattern draws the eye to where it is placed.

## 4. What exams ask

- Section C, Elective 2 (Ordinary 18 marks, Higher 15 marks): comment on or evaluate outfits shown in a photograph, with set headings: comfort, function, aesthetic appeal, shape, proportion, design features, lifestyle, current trends. Expected: two or three points per heading drawn from the picture (e.g. loose fabric and stretch for comfort; pockets, durable crease-resistant fabric for function; colour, line and contrast for appeal), spread equally.
- Name one design principle and explain how it is shown in the outfit (Higher 2025H, 2023H; Ordinary 2024O for emphasis): emphasis through a contrasting blazer, bright trim or accessory; balance, proportion or rhythm accepted with evidence.
- Name and explain a principle in your own sketched outfit (2014H, 2011O).
- Name three elements and explain how each affects appearance (2009H); apply colour, line and shape in the design of the outfit (2015O).
- Discuss how colour (2022H) or the elements of design (2014H) can be used to flatter body size and shape: darker colours reduce, lighter increase, one colour gives height, contrast balances, line and pattern direct the eye.
- Three key factors in designing an outfit (2013O): rhythm, fabric, proportion, balance, emphasis, harmony, suitability, durability.
- Ordinary Level uniforms and occasion wear (2026O, 2018O, 2021O, 2017O): shape, proportion and design features; comment on function and comfort of uniforms.
