# Package: hom-home-interior-s1 — Elements and principles of design and factors in interior design

- **loId:** `hom-home-interior-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-home-interior` · **syllabusRef:** 4.3
- **Syllabus scope (Elective 1, Home Design and Management):** the elements and principles of design and their application to the home; factors that influence the interior design of the home: aesthetic and comfort factors, ergonomics, family size and circumstances, special needs, cost, environmental awareness. No [HL] marks in this subsection; it is common to Ordinary and Higher Level.
- **Also examined:** design principles applied to a room; colour, pattern and texture; factors in kitchen or room design; evaluating a room or layout.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Neighbouring subsections take the detail of floor coverings, wall finishes, furniture and soft furnishings, and of wood, metal, glass, plastics and fabric. Lighting and ventilation systems belong to the housing and services chapters; here they appear only as a comfort factor.

## 1. Must cover

Elements of design
- [ ] E1 The elements of design are the visual building blocks of a room. The marking schemes list colour, line, shape, pattern and texture (form, the 3-D version of shape, may be added). Students should be able to name three and explain two, each with an example of use in a room.
- [ ] E2 Line: the path the eye follows. Horizontal lines make a room look wider and calmer, vertical lines make it look taller; lines come from skirting, picture rails, curtain and cabinet edges, tile joints and floorboards.
- [ ] E3 Shape and form: shape is the flat outline, form is the 3-D bulk (e.g. a sofa). Rounded shapes look soft and informal, angular shapes crisp and formal.
- [ ] E4 Colour, basics: primary (red, yellow, blue), secondary (orange, green, violet), tertiary (a primary mixed with a neighbouring secondary). Warm colours (red, orange, yellow) advance and make a room feel cosy and smaller; cool colours (blue, green, violet) recede and feel calm and spacious. Pastels feel restful; neutrals are white, cream and similar.
- [ ] E5 Colour, tint/tone/shade: tint = white added, tone = grey added, shade = black added.
- [ ] E6 Colour schemes: monochromatic (one colour in tints, tones and shades); complementary or contrasting (opposite on the colour wheel, e.g. blue and orange); harmonious or analogous (neighbours on the wheel, e.g. green, blue, violet); accent colour (a strong colour in small amounts, e.g. cushions or a feature wall).
- [ ] E7 Uses of colour in a room (the four the schemes list): link rooms, alter the apparent proportions, highlight good features (or disguise poor ones), create atmosphere. Also: pale colours reflect light and make a room look larger; dark colours absorb light and make it look smaller and cosier; aspect matters (warm colours suit a north-facing room with little daylight; cool colours suit a south-facing room); pale ceilings look higher.
- [ ] E8 Pattern: a repeated or decorative design (stripes, checks, florals, geometrics). It adds variety, contrast and interest. Large-scale or bold patterns dominate and can shrink a room; small-scale patterns are calmer. Several patterns work if linked by a common colour, texture or motif. Too much pattern makes a room fussy; too little makes it bland.
- [ ] E9 Texture: how a surface feels, or looks as if it feels, and how light falls on it. Smooth textures (glass, mirrors, glazed tiles, paint, polished steel) look cool and are hygienic, so suit kitchens and bathrooms. Rough or soft textures (carpet, upholstery, brick, textured paint or wallpaper) add warmth and comfort. Matt surfaces absorb light and hide flaws; gloss reflects light and shows marks. Contrasting textures add interest without more colour.

Principles of design
- [ ] P1 Proportion: the size relationship between objects, colours, patterns and textures in a room, and between them and the room. Oversized furniture in a small room crowds it. Scale (size of one item relative to its surroundings) is taught inside proportion.
- [ ] P2 Balance: creates harmony so no feature dominates and colour, pattern and texture are shared out evenly. Formal balance is symmetrical (a pair of lamps either side of a fireplace); informal balance uses different items of similar visual weight.
- [ ] P3 Emphasis: creates a focal point the eye goes to first (a fireplace, window view, feature wall, large picture), made with colour, lighting, strong pattern or furniture placement. One focal point works best; competing ones make the room disjointed.
- [ ] P4 Rhythm: links the room by repeating a colour, shape, style, line or pattern (e.g. a cushion colour repeated in a rug), so the eye moves around smoothly.
- [ ] P5 Applying them: start from the room's function, choose the focal point and colour scheme, arrange furniture and traffic routes, then check each principle. To evaluate a room, name the elements used and say whether each principle is achieved. Diagram: a labelled plan of one room (furniture, windows, doors, radiator, focal point, main traffic route).

Factors that influence interior design
- [ ] F1 Aesthetic factors: occupants' taste, current trends, style of the house, function and décor of the room. Trends date quickly, so neutral bases with changeable accessories last longer.
- [ ] F2 Comfort factors: heating, lighting and ventilation at comfortable levels; comfortable seating and work surfaces at suitable heights; function of the room (working versus relaxing); aspect; space and layout; separating quiet and noisy zones.
- [ ] F3 Ergonomics: designing so that people can move, reach and work with least effort and strain. Work triangle: sink, cooker and fridge at the three points of an imaginary triangle. Work zones in the order of tasks; clear traffic flow; surfaces and storage at heights that avoid bending and stretching; easy-clean surfaces; accessible sockets and switches. Diagram: a kitchen plan showing the work triangle.
- [ ] F4 Family size and circumstances: number, ages and stage of the family set bedrooms, living space and storage; young children (safe, easy-clean surfaces, rounded edges, supervised play space); teenagers (study space, privacy); working from home or a hobby; income and work patterns; future change (attic conversion, a room changing use).
- [ ] F5 Special needs: wheelchair users (wider doorways, turning space, lowered worktops, lowered switches and sockets, level thresholds or ramps); limited mobility (grab rails, raised toilet seat); weak grip (lever handles); poor sight (contrast between floor, walls and furniture, good lighting); non-slip floors; adaptable design.
- [ ] F6 Cost: budget covers purchase, fitting, maintenance and running costs. Spend most on one focal feature; paint and soft furnishings change a room cheaply; buy in stages to a plan; cheap items that wear out cost more over time; weigh cost against quality.
- [ ] F7 Environmental awareness: materials with low impact in production, use and disposal; their origin and transport; sustainably sourced timber; natural, recycled or reused materials and repurposing old furniture; low-VOC paints; durable items; energy-efficient lighting; using daylight and light colours to cut artificial lighting.
- [ ] F8 Trends: interior design trends change and date a room; examined as "identify and comment on two current trends" (2009O). Name trends only in general terms (e.g. open-plan living, neutral schemes, natural materials).
- [ ] F9 (assumed) Interior designer: one advantage (expertise, a coordinated scheme, fewer costly mistakes) and one disadvantage (fees reduce the budget).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "light" as a sixth element of design, because no marking scheme lists it; lighting is examined separately as a system and belongs to the housing and services chapters.
- Do not use: CFL (compact fluorescent) bulbs as an energy-efficient option, because they are being phased out in the EU; write "LED lighting".
- Colour, pattern and texture are the elements most often asked; line and shape come up mainly in clothing questions. The same four principles are used for outfits in the clothing chapters; keep examples here to rooms.
- Scale and harmony are not separate principles in the schemes: scale sits in proportion, harmony is the result of balance. Name the four.
- Do not mix up emphasis (one focal point) with balance (even distribution) or rhythm (repetition).
- Tint is white, shade is black, tone is grey; students swap these.
- Warm colours "advance" visually and make a room look smaller and cosier; they do not change the temperature.
- Ergonomics is the fit between people and layout; it is not the same as aesthetics.
- Choice criteria for floors, walls, furniture and soft furnishings belong to the next subsection on floors, walls, furniture and soft furnishings; materials belong to the subsection on materials used in the home. Lighting, ventilation, heating, energy and insulation belong to the services chapter. Housing styles, location and building regulations belong to the housing chapter.
- Do not state any cost, price or grant figure.

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain two or three elements (usually colour, pattern, texture) with an example of each in a room (2012H, 2022H, 2025O, 2007O). Expected: definition plus use, e.g. contrasting textures add interest, pastel colours relax, linked patterns tie a room together.
- Explain texture and give two uses; give two examples each of smooth and rough texture (2025O, 2007O). Expected: smooth in kitchens and bathrooms, rough or soft in sitting rooms and bedrooms.
- Identify and apply three or four principles in a room (2024H, 2010H, 2005H). Expected: a definition and a room example each, as in P1–P4.
- Factors to consider when planning interior design: choose three or four and discuss each (2012O, 2021O, 2007O, 2014H, 2005H). Expected: a reason and an example per factor, not a bare list.
- Named-factor questions: cost, family size and circumstances, comfort (2025O); cost, family, ergonomics (2024H); aesthetic and comfort, environmental awareness (2023H); function, cost, environmental awareness (2012H); family size, ergonomics, environmental awareness for a kitchen/dining room (2025H); ergonomics, aesthetics, comfort and a family member with special needs for a kitchen/living area (2015H).
- Choosing colour for a sitting room (2008O): room use, size, aspect, overall scheme, light reflectance, features. Colour to create atmosphere (2017O).
- Design and sketch a room and describe its colour scheme, lighting and flooring (2005H), then apply two principles to it.
- Evaluate a given room, kitchen or house plan for a family (2023O, 2022O, 2016O, 2010O, 2026O): strengths and weaknesses for the family's ages, safety, traffic flow, work triangle, storage and future change.
- Trends in modern interior design (2009O): name two and comment.
- One advantage and one disadvantage of using an interior designer (2026O).
