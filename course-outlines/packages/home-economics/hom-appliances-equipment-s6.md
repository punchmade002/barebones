## 1. Must cover

What a microwave oven is (one block, lead)
- [ ] A1 A microwave oven cooks, reheats and defrosts food using microwaves, a form of electromagnetic radiation, generated electrically; it heats the food, not the air in the oven. It is quick and uses less energy for small quantities.
- [ ] A2 Types (2007O, 2014H): conventional (solo) microwave for cooking, reheating and defrosting; microwave with a grill (browns and toasts); combination oven (microwave plus fan oven and grill, can brown and crisp, bake); flatbed (no turntable) models.

Parts and their functions (one block; the most examined point)
- [ ] P1 Transformer: increases the domestic mains voltage (230 V) to the very high voltage the magnetron needs. (It changes voltage, not frequency.)
- [ ] P2 Magnetron: the device that converts electrical energy into microwave (electromagnetic) energy at about 2.45 GHz.
- [ ] P3 Wave guide: a metal channel that guides the microwaves from the magnetron into the oven cavity.
- [ ] P4 Stirrer fan (mode stirrer) and turntable: the stirrer scatters the waves; the turntable rotates the food so that it passes through areas of high and low energy and cooks more evenly.
- [ ] P5 Cavity: a metal-lined (enamelled steel) box that reflects the microwaves so they stay in the oven and bounce around the food.
- [ ] P6 Door and safety: a glass door with a perforated metal screen (holes much smaller than the wavelength, so the microwaves cannot pass out but you can see in); a safety lock or interlock switches the magnetron off when the door is opened; a safety seal around the door; child lock.
- [ ] P7 Controls and timer: power level and cooking time; the timer stops the magnetron; an interior light, a ventilation fan to cool the magnetron.
- [ ] P8 Diagram: a labelled section of a microwave oven showing the transformer, magnetron, wave guide, stirrer fan, cavity, turntable and door.

The working principle (one block)
- [ ] W1 When switched on, the transformer steps the mains voltage up and the magnetron produces microwaves.
- [ ] W2 The wave guide carries them into the cavity; the metal walls reflect them, and the stirrer and turntable spread them so the food is hit evenly.
- [ ] W3 The microwaves pass through glass, ceramic and most plastics and paper, are absorbed by food (water, and also fats and sugars) and are reflected by metal.
- [ ] W4 In the food, the microwaves make the water molecules vibrate rapidly; the friction produces heat inside the outer layers of the food. The microwaves penetrate a few centimetres only; the heat then spreads towards the centre by conduction, which is why standing time is needed.
- [ ] W5 The oven switches off when the timer finishes or the door opens. Food does not become radioactive.
- [ ] W6 Food does not brown in a microwave because the surface does not reach a high enough temperature; browning needs a grill or combination mode or a browning dish.

Guidelines for use and care (one block, 2024O, 2014H)
- [ ] G1 Containers: use only microwave-safe glass, ceramic, paper or suitable plastic; never use metal, foil or dishes with metallic trim because they reflect microwaves and cause sparks (arcing) and can damage the oven.
- [ ] G2 Preparation: pierce skins (potatoes, sausages, egg yolks, tomatoes); do not cook whole eggs in their shells (steam pressure makes them burst); cut food into even pieces; cover food (lid, microwave-safe film with a vent) to hold in moisture and prevent splashing.
- [ ] G3 Arrangement: arrange food in a circle with thick parts towards the outside; do not pile it up; stir, turn or rearrange partway so the heat spreads evenly.
- [ ] G4 Timing: follow the times in the instructions; start with short times, add more; allow standing time so heat spreads by conduction; make sure the food is piping hot all through, especially reheated meals, poultry and minced meat, to destroy bacteria.
- [ ] G5 Defrosting: use the defrost setting or low power, turn and break up the food, and cook straight away after defrosting.
- [ ] G6 Safety: never run the oven empty; do not use if the door, seal or hinges are damaged; take care with steam when removing covers and with hot containers; use oven gloves; keep children from using it unsupervised; beware of the heating of liquids in the container (stir before use).
- [ ] G7 Care and cleaning: unplug before cleaning; wipe the cavity, door seal and turntable with a damp cloth and mild detergent after spills; do not use abrasives; steam-clean a bowl of water and lemon juice to loosen food; keep the vents clear; follow the manufacturer's instructions.
- [ ] G8 Selecting a microwave (2023O): initial and running cost; wattage and capacity; type (solo, grill, combination); pre-set programmes and features; size and space; ease of cleaning and use; safety features; brand and guarantee and after-sales service.

Modern features (one block)
- [ ] M1 Variable power levels and automatic defrost by weight; pre-set or sensor programmes (vegetables, popcorn, reheat) and "quick start"; digital display, clock, timer; memory settings.
- [ ] M2 Grill and fan oven combination, convection, crisping dish; inverter technology for steady low power; steam function in some models.
- [ ] M3 Child lock; keep-warm; interior light; self-cleaning or easy-clean interiors; temperature probe; smart or app control; low standby use.

Energy efficiency and contribution to modern cooking (one block; 2005H, 2009H)
- [ ] E1 Cooking is faster so less energy is used for small and medium quantities; no preheating; only the food is heated; less heat is given out into the kitchen; a combination of reheating and defrosting saves time.
- [ ] E2 Food can keep more colour, flavour and some nutrients when cooked for a short time with little or no added water; no added fat is needed for many foods.
- [ ] E3 Useful for busy lifestyles (reheating, ready meals, defrosting), and simple and safe to use for children, older people and people living alone; the container can often serve as the serving dish, reducing washing-up.
- [ ] E4 Limits: does not brown or crisp (unless combined), uneven heating, not good for large joints or baking in the same way, can overcook edges; a conventional oven can be more economical for several large dishes.

Underlying working principles (Higher Level only; one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] H1 [HL] Microwaves are electromagnetic waves of frequency about 2.45 GHz (wavelength about 12 cm). They are non-ionising radiation.
- [ ] H2 [HL] The transformer works by electromagnetic induction: an alternating current in the primary coil induces a higher alternating voltage in the secondary coil, which has more turns; it is a step-up transformer. The high voltage is rectified and fed to the magnetron.
- [ ] H3 [HL] The magnetron is a vacuum tube in which electrons moving in a magnetic field set up oscillations in resonant cavities, which give out microwaves.
- [ ] H4 [HL] Water, fat and sugar molecules are polar (a positive and a negative end). The microwaves' electric field alternates billions of times per second, so the polar molecules rotate back and forth; the friction between molecules turns the movement into heat.
- [ ] H5 [HL] The metal screen in the door has holes much smaller than the wavelength, so the waves are reflected and do not leave the oven; the door interlock is wired so the magnetron cannot work with the door open.
- [ ] H6 [HL] Diagram: polar water molecules aligning with the alternating field at two moments.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "the transformer increases the frequency" (it appears in the 2008H and 2019H schemes), because a transformer changes voltage and not frequency; say it increases the voltage.
- Do not use: "microwaves penetrate 2 to 4 cm" or any exact penetration depth, because the depth depends on the food and no single figure is confirmed; say "a few centimetres".
- Do not use: "water is driven to the surface, which stops browning" as the reason for no browning; the surface does not get hot enough to brown because the evaporating water keeps it near 100 °C.
- Do not use: "220 V" as the domestic voltage (the 2008H scheme), because the supply is 230 V.
- Do not use: the wattage or price of a named model, because these change.
- Do not use: "microwaves cook food from the inside out"; they heat the outer few centimetres and conduction carries the heat inward.
- Microwave is not a heating-element appliance and has no element; the motor in a microwave drives only the turntable and the fan. Keep it separate from the heating-element and motor subsections.
- Microwaves do not make food radioactive and do not heat the container directly, though the container is warmed by the hot food. Some plastics are not microwave-safe; use only labelled microwave-safe containers.
- Metal is reflective: small amounts of metal (foil trim) cause arcing; do not tell students that all metal can be used.
- Food safety rules for reheating (piping hot, cool food quickly) belong to food safety; here only the guideline that reheated food must be hot all through.
- The physics (induction, polar molecules, resonant cavities) is [HL] and must be in a separate block marked "(Higher Level only)"; at Ordinary Level give the plain working principle.
- Energy label and the selection criteria belong to the selecting-appliances subsection; the microwave is not given an energy label in this chapter.

## 4. What exams ask

- Parts and functions, 6 marks (2008H, 2019H, 2026H Higher; 2014O Ordinary matching question): transformer, magnetron, wave guide, turntable. One sentence each. The transformer raises the mains voltage for the magnetron; the magnetron converts electricity to microwaves; the wave guide directs microwaves into the cavity; the turntable rotates the food for even cooking.
- Guidelines for use (2024O, three guidelines, 6 marks): any three from containers, piercing skins, arranging food, stirring or turning, covering, timing and standing time; each with a reason.
- Selecting a microwave (2023O, three guidelines): cost, wattage, features, space, safety, ease of cleaning, guarantee and service.
- Full study (2026O, 2014H, 2009H, 2007O, 2005H): type (conventional, grill, combination); working principle with the parts in order; construction; guidelines for use; modern features (variable power, defrost, programmes, grill); energy efficiency. 26-38 marks at Higher.
- Evaluate the microwave's contribution to modern cooking (2005H, 12 marks): four points: quick and energy efficient, retains colour and nutrients, good for reheating and busy lifestyles, safe and simple to use; add a limit (no browning).
- Ordinary 2026 also asked care and cleaning and the complaint procedure for a faulty microwave and environmentally friendly disposal; the complaint procedure and consumer rights belong to the consumer-rights material, and disposal is in the selecting-appliances subsection.
- Tips to build: put the parts in the order the energy flows (transformer, magnetron, wave guide, cavity); give the reason for each guideline; for Higher add the molecules-vibrating explanation.
