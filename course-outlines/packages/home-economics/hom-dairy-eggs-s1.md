# Package: hom-dairy-eggs-s1 — Milk: nutritional significance and contribution to the diet

- **loId:** `hom-dairy-eggs-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-dairy-eggs` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.2
- **Syllabus scope:** "Milk: nutritional significance and contribution to the diet" [1.3.2]: composition; nutritive and dietetic value; types of milk (whole, low-fat, fortified and others). No [HL] marks in this subsection.
- **Also examined (outline):** whole, skimmed, semi-skimmed and fortified milk (2014O, 2010O); milk in young children's diets (2021H); choosing milk from a nutrition table (2022O). Also asked in 2024O (nutritive and dietetic value of milk; three ways dairy foods fit a healthy diet) and 2007O (why three servings from the milk, cheese and yoghurt group).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23, from the outline, the current notes, the marking schemes in the exam bank and a small number of web checks. Heat treatments, packaging and labelling of milk belong to the milk-processing subsection; cream, butter and yoghurt, and cheese, have their own subsections. Protein, lactose, calcium and the vitamins are taught in full in the nutrient chapters; here they are used, not redefined.

## 1. Must cover

Composition (one block)
- [ ] C1 Milk is a complete food for the young mammal and a useful source of most nutrients for people; it is widely available, so it is a staple of the Irish diet.
- [ ] C2 Water: about 87% of milk. Gives hydration. (Marking schemes 2021H, 2024O.)
- [ ] C3 Protein: about 3.5% in whole milk. High biological value (HBV): it contains all the essential amino acids. Mostly casein, with the rest whey proteins. Roles: growth and repair, and making hormones and enzymes.
- [ ] C4 Fat: about 3.5% to 4% in whole milk, mostly saturated, in small globules (so easily digested). Carries the fat-soluble vitamins A and D. Cholesterol is present.
- [ ] C5 Carbohydrate: lactose (milk sugar, a disaccharide) is the only significant carbohydrate, about 4–5% (2021H scheme); gives energy. Lactose also helps calcium absorption. Almost no starch.
- [ ] C6 Minerals: calcium and phosphorus in useful amounts (bones and teeth); traces of magnesium, potassium and sodium; little iron.
- [ ] C7 Vitamins: A and D (in the fat, so highest in whole milk), and B group vitamins, including riboflavin (B2) and B12. Skimmed milk keeps the B vitamins and loses A and D.
- [ ] C8 Nutrients milk lacks or is low in: vitamin C, fibre, iron. (Examiners ask which nutrient is absent, so list these together.)
- [ ] C9 Energy: milk supplies energy from lactose and fat; the more fat, the more energy.

Nutritive value, written as an exam answer (one block)
- [ ] N1 How to give "nutritive value": the nutrients milk contains and what each does (protein: growth and repair; fat and lactose: energy; calcium and phosphorus: bones and teeth; vitamins A, D, B group; water), plus what it lacks (vitamin C, iron, fibre). Name the nutrient, its function and the milk fact.

Dietetic value (one block)
- [ ] D1 How to give "dietetic value": how milk suits different people and diets, with a reason. Points:
  - growth and repair for children, adolescents and pregnant women (protein, calcium);
  - bone and tooth development and maintenance, including older people and nursing mothers (calcium, phosphorus, vitamin D);
  - easily digested, so it suits invalids, convalescents and older people;
  - energy for active people (lactose and fat);
  - low-fat milks suit low-cholesterol and weight-watching diets;
  - versatile: drinks, cereal, sauces, soups, puddings, baking; inexpensive, widely available, no preparation, nothing wasted;
  - can be fortified.
- [ ] D2 Limits to state: not suitable as the only food (no vitamin C, fibre; little iron); whole milk is high in saturated fat and not ideal for those cutting cholesterol or calories; lactose intolerance (a person lacking the enzyme lactase cannot digest lactose; lactose-free milk exists); milk allergy is a different condition (a reaction to the milk protein). (Allergies and intolerances are taught in the chapter on special diets; one line only.)
- [ ] D3 Healthy eating: the Department of Health healthy eating guidelines and Food Pyramid have a shelf for milk, yoghurt and cheese. Recommended servings: three a day for most adults; five a day for 9 to 18 year olds. Low-fat choices are advised, and cheese in small amounts. (The 2007O scheme gave the reasons as HBV protein, calcium, vitamins A, D and some B, and versatility and low cost.)
- [ ] D4 Ways of including dairy in a healthy diet (2024O, 2022O): milk on cereal or porridge, in smoothies and milky drinks, in soups, sauces, batters, puddings and baking; yoghurt as a snack, on cereal, in smoothies, as a dip or dressing; cheese in sandwiches, salads, pasta, pizza, soups, on potatoes; cream in soups, mash, sauces and desserts. Give three different ways with a nutritional reason.

Milk in young children's diets (one block)
- [ ] Y1 The nutritional significance for children: protein for growth and repair; calcium and phosphorus (with vitamin D) for developing bones and teeth; energy and fat-soluble vitamins A and D from the fat; B vitamins; water. (2021H scheme.)
- [ ] Y2 Whole milk suits young children because they need the energy and vitamins A and D in the fat; skimmed milk is not suitable as a young child's main milk. Safefood advice: full-fat milk for ages 1 to 2; skimmed milk not suitable as a main drink under 5. Cow's milk is not given as the main drink to babies under 12 months (HSE).
- [ ] Y3 Milk is low in iron and has no vitamin C, so balance it with iron-rich foods (meat, fish, eggs, fortified cereals) and with vitamin C and fibre foods; too much milk can crowd out other foods. Milk can be fortified.

Types of milk (one block)
- [ ] T1 Whole milk: full fat, about 3.5% to 4% fat; the most energy and vitamin A; the most popular type.
- [ ] T2 Semi-skimmed milk: part of the fat removed; compared with whole milk it has less energy, fat and saturated fat and vitamin A, and slightly more protein, carbohydrate and calcium per 100 ml (2010O scheme).
- [ ] T3 Skimmed milk: 0.1% to 0.4% fat; most of the fat is removed, taking vitamins A and D with it; suits low-fat and low-cholesterol diets; protein, lactose and calcium remain.
- [ ] T4 Low-fat milk: a reduced-fat milk (in practice the same idea as semi-skimmed). Say "low-fat or semi-skimmed" and give the fat level only as "reduced".
- [ ] T5 Fortified milk: nutrients added to full-fat, low-fat or skimmed milk, for example vitamins A and D, folic acid, calcium, protein or omega-3 fatty acids. Fortification adds nutrients that are low or were lost in processing; it can restore vitamins A and D to low-fat milk.
- [ ] T6 Other types to name: long-life (UHT), sterilised, evaporated, condensed and dried milk (heat-treated or concentrated types, taught in the milk-processing subsection); lactose-free milk; goat's and soya (plant) milks as alternatives (one line each, no figures).
- [ ] T7 Differences to give in a "compare whole, skimmed and fortified" answer: fat content, energy, vitamins A and D, and suitability (2014O).

Choosing a milk from nutrition data (one block, 2022O)
- [ ] K1 Method: read per 100 ml values. Recommend for a teenager: lower fat and energy (weight control and heart health), protein (growth and repair), calcium (bones and teeth), vitamin B12 (red blood cells, nerves, making use of folate and fat). Give four reasons, each with the number from the table and the function. Do not use any figure that is not in the table you are given.
- [ ] K2 Energy and fat decide suitability for slimming or low-cholesterol diets; protein and calcium decide suitability for growth; compare like with like (per 100 ml).
- [ ] K3 Diagram or table cue: a comparison table of whole, semi-skimmed and skimmed milk (fat, energy, vitamins A and D, calcium) is a good revision aid.

Assumed knowledge (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) HBV protein, essential amino acids, lactose as a disaccharide, saturated fat, fat-soluble vitamins, calcium functions and the function of each B vitamin are taught in the nutrient chapters; use the terms and give each a short gloss.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Pasteurisation is named as a heat treatment but not described here.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the numbers from the 2022O nutrition table (for example 160 mg calcium, kcal values, brand names such as "Super Milk"), because they belong to that question's table. Teach how to compare, not those figures.
- Do not use: a fat percentage for semi-skimmed or "low-fat" milk, because the marking schemes give none and the figures differ by label; say "reduced fat".
- Do not use: "low-fat milk sits between whole and skimmed as its own separate type with its own fat level", because in Irish shops low-fat and semi-skimmed are effectively the same product; treat them as one reduced-fat type.
- Milk is a good source of protein and calcium, not of iron or vitamin C. A statement that milk is a "complete food" means it supports a young mammal, not that an adult can live on it.
- Skimmed milk loses vitamins A and D with the fat; it does not lose calcium or protein. Semi-skimmed and skimmed milk are not "fortified" unless the label says so.
- Fortified is not the same as processed by heat; fortification adds nutrients, heat treatment extends shelf life. Heat treatments (pasteurisation, UHT, sterilisation), homogenisation and the stages of production belong to the milk-processing subsection.
- Cream, butter and yoghurt, and cheese, belong to their own subsections; do not give their composition here.
- Lactose intolerance (cannot digest the sugar) differs from milk protein allergy (immune reaction to the protein).
- Calcium requirements or amounts: do not give them; the 2022O question asked calcium functions and sources, which belong to the minerals chapter.

## 4. What exams ask

- "Nutritive value and dietetic value of milk" (2024O, 2014O; a 20-mark part, so four points at 5 marks each, at least one on nutritive and one on dietetic value). Expected: HBV protein for growth and repair; fat (saturated) and lactose for energy; vitamins A, D and B group; calcium and phosphorus for bones and teeth; water; lacks vitamin C and iron. Dietetic: suits children, adolescents, pregnant women, older people, invalids; easily digested; versatile; inexpensive; low-fat versions for weight and cholesterol control.
- "Nutritional significance of milk in young children's diet" (2021H, 20 marks): the composition points with the children's reason for each, whole versus skimmed, that milk is low in iron and lacks vitamin C so other foods must be added, and fortification.
- "Difference between whole, skimmed and fortified milk" (2014O, 12 marks, one point per type); "three differences between whole and semi-skimmed" (2010O); "dietetic value of semi-skimmed milk" including who it suits and who it does not (children under five).
- "Recommend a milk from a table" (2022O, 20 marks): four reasons, each tied to a figure in the table and a function (growth, bones, energy, red blood cells). Also "interesting ways of including dairy in a teenager's diet" (12 marks) and "factors in buying and storing dairy to cut food waste" (buy from a clean shop, check dates and intact packs, refrigerate at 4 °C, rotate stock, use covered containers away from strong odours).
- Short Section A parts: name two nutrients in skimmed milk (2013O), true/false items on nutrients, and why three servings a day of milk, cheese and yoghurt are recommended (2007O).
- Menu questions (2014O): plan a day's menu for a teenage girl needing more calcium, with milk, yoghurt and cheese placed in the meals and a reason for each; current healthy eating guidelines must be followed.
