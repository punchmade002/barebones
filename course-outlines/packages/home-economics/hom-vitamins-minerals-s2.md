## 1. Must cover

Water-soluble features (assumed from the previous subsection, one block)
- [ ] W1 (assumed) Water-soluble vitamins (the B group and C) dissolve in water, are not stored in useful amounts (exception: B12, stored in the liver), and any excess is lost in urine, so they are needed regularly in the diet. They are lost into cooking water and are easily destroyed during storage, preparation and cooking.
- [ ] W2 RDA: the daily amount that meets the needs of almost everyone in a group; it varies with age, sex, pregnancy and breastfeeding (smokers are advised more vitamin C). State the idea; give no amounts.

Vitamin C: ascorbic acid
- [ ] C1 Sources: citrus fruit (oranges, lemons, grapefruit), blackcurrants, kiwi fruit, strawberries, rosehips, peppers, broccoli, sprouts, cabbage, tomatoes, new potatoes, peas; also fruit juices and smoothies (fresh) and fortified drinks. The scheme accepts any fruit or vegetable, a juice, or a supplement. Fresh, raw and in-season produce has most.
- [ ] C2 Functions (an examiner asks for two or three): needed to make collagen, the protein of connective tissue, so it keeps gums, skin, blood vessels, cartilage, bones and teeth healthy and helps wounds heal; aids absorption of iron (converts ferric iron, Fe³⁺, in plant foods to the ferrous form, Fe²⁺, which is absorbed more easily; it does not turn it into haem iron); marking schemes also accept "aids calcium absorption"; antioxidant (neutralises free radicals); supports the immune system; prevents scurvy.
- [ ] C3 Deficiency: scurvy after a long lack of vitamin C. Weak collagen causes bleeding, sore, swollen gums and loose teeth, easy bruising, slow wound healing, painful joints; poorer iron absorption can cause anaemia (tiredness, pale skin); lowered resistance to infection.
- [ ] C4 [HL] Properties: white crystalline solid with an acid (sour) taste, very soluble in water; unstable: destroyed by heat (prolonged cooking), by oxidation in air, by light, by alkalis (e.g. bicarbonate of soda), by contact with copper or brass pans, and by the enzyme oxidase released when plant cells are cut or bruised; lost into cooking water or by soaking; lost during long storage and dehydration; it is an antioxidant (reducing agent).
- [ ] C5 Retaining vitamin C in practice (the 2012 Ordinary and 2005 Ordinary parts): buy fresh and use quickly; eat raw where possible; wash before cutting, do not soak; prepare just before cooking; cut in large pieces; cook in a little boiling water for a short time with the lid on, or steam, microwave or stir-fry; do not use bicarbonate of soda or copper pans; serve at once (not kept hot); use the cooking water in soups and sauces. One line only; detail is in the vegetables chapter.

Vitamin B12: cobalamin
- [ ] B1 Sources: only animal foods contain it naturally: liver and kidney, meat, fish and oily fish, eggs, milk, cheese and yoghurt. Plant foods have none naturally; vegans rely on fortified foods (some breakfast cereals, plant milks, yeast extract) or supplements.
- [ ] B2 Functions: works with folate to make healthy red blood cells; needed to make DNA, and so for cell division; keeps the nerve cells healthy by maintaining the myelin sheath around nerve fibres; used in the use of fatty acids and amino acids.
- [ ] B3 Absorption: B12 is bound by intrinsic factor, a protein made in the stomach lining, and absorbed in the ileum (end of the small intestine). The liver stores it, enough for years.
- [ ] B4 Deficiency: anaemia in which the red blood cells are too large and immature (megaloblastic anaemia: tiredness, weakness, pale skin, breathlessness); damage to nerves (tingling, numbness in hands and feet, poor balance, memory problems). Causes: a strict vegan diet without fortification or supplements, or failure to absorb B12 because of a lack of intrinsic factor (pernicious anaemia, usually an autoimmune condition) or stomach or gut disease; older people absorb less.
- [ ] B5 [HL] Properties: water-soluble; a red crystalline compound containing the mineral cobalt (hence cobalamin); stable to heat in ordinary cooking but destroyed by light and by strong acids and alkalis; some loss into cooking water.

Folate and folic acid
- [ ] FO1 Folate is the natural form (vitamin B9); folic acid is the synthetic form used in supplements and fortified foods. Sources of folate: dark green leafy vegetables (spinach, broccoli, sprouts, cabbage), asparagus, pulses (chickpeas, lentils, beans), oranges, liver, yeast extract, wholegrain and fortified breakfast cereals.
- [ ] FO2 Functions (an examiner asks for two or three): formation of red blood cells (with B12); making DNA and RNA, so cell division and growth, including the growth of the foetus; the formation of the neural tube, the early brain and spinal cord, in the first weeks of pregnancy; protects against neural tube defects such as spina bifida and anencephaly.
- [ ] FO3 Deficiency: megaloblastic (folate-deficiency) anaemia, with tiredness, weakness, pale skin, breathlessness and sore tongue; neural tube defects in the baby when the mother is deficient in early pregnancy; poor growth.
- [ ] FO4 Advice: women who may become pregnant are advised to take a folic acid supplement before conception and in early pregnancy (the HSE advice; the exact dose is not given here), as well as eating folate-rich foods; the neural tube forms before many women know they are pregnant.
- [ ] FO5 [HL] Properties: water-soluble; destroyed by heat, light and oxygen (prolonged cooking and storage); lost into cooking water; synthetic folic acid is more stable than natural folate, which is why it is used in fortification.

## 3. Traps

- Vitamin C helps absorb iron by changing ferric (Fe³⁺) iron to the ferrous (Fe²⁺) form. Do not say it "converts non-haem iron to haem iron" (one scheme wording says this loosely; the chemistry is ferric to ferrous).
- Vitamin C does not directly strengthen bones; it makes the collagen framework that bone and teeth are built on. Do not write "vitamin C cures or prevents colds".
- Folate and folic acid are not two different vitamins; folic acid is the synthetic form. B12 and folate deficiency give the same type of anaemia (megaloblastic) but only B12 deficiency damages nerves.
- Pernicious anaemia is a specific condition: B12 deficiency because intrinsic factor is missing. A vegan with a B12-poor diet has B12-deficiency (megaloblastic) anaemia, not strictly pernicious anaemia.
- Scurvy is the vitamin C disease; beri-beri is B1; pellagra is niacin; rickets is D. Past papers test this matching.
- Do not use: "vitamin C prevents or shortens the common cold", because the evidence does not support it and no scheme says so.
- Do not use: "pernicious anaemia results whenever B12 is lacking", because it is specifically failure to absorb B12 through lack of intrinsic factor.
- Do not use: "vitamin C converts non-haem iron to haem iron", because the change is from the ferric to the ferrous form of non-haem iron.
- Do not use: any RDA, supplement dose (for example the micrograms of folic acid advised), or store duration in days or years beyond "several years", because these are changing figures and no scheme for this topic gives them.
- Do not use: that cooking destroys all the vitamin C in vegetables, because the loss depends on method and time; steaming, microwaving and short cooking keep most.
- Neighbouring subsections: iron and anaemia from iron deficiency, and the full list of absorption factors (mineral subsections); other B vitamins (thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, B6); fat-soluble vitamins; preparing and cooking vegetables to keep nutrients (vegetables and fruit chapter).

## 4. What exams ask

- State two or three functions of vitamin C and name a source (2004, 2009, 2015, 2018, 2024 and 2026 papers): collagen and wound healing, iron absorption, antioxidant, immunity, healthy gums and blood vessels; citrus, blackcurrants, peppers, kiwi.
- Give an account of vitamin C under sources, functions and effect of deficiency (2012, 2017, 2025 Ordinary); Higher adds properties (2019, 2023): water-soluble, crystalline acid, unstable to heat, air, light, alkali, oxidase, copper, dehydration. Deficiency answer: scurvy, bleeding gums, bruising, slow healing, anaemia, infections.
- True or false and gap-fill: vitamin C essential for iron absorption (2010 Ordinary, 2021 Ordinary); citrus fruit as a vitamin C source (2022 Ordinary); ascorbic acid is vitamin C (2019 Ordinary).
- How vitamin C helps iron absorption (2004 Higher): changes ferric iron to ferrous (a reducing agent). Name a nutrient vitamin C helps absorb (2017 Ordinary): iron (also calcium and protein appear in the scheme).
- Ways of retaining vitamin C when preparing and cooking (2005, 2012): use fresh, avoid soaking, cook briefly, minimum water, lid on, no bicarbonate or copper pan.
- State two or three functions of folate or folic acid (2005, 2010, 2016) and two sources: red blood cells, DNA and RNA synthesis, foetal development, neural tube defects prevented; green leafy vegetables, pulses, liver, fortified cereals. Gap-fill: neural tube defects prevented by folic acid (2018 Ordinary).
- B12: functions and deficiency appear within Higher "properties/functions" parts and in vegan diet questions; expect red blood cell formation, nerve myelin, pernicious anaemia, vegan sources.
