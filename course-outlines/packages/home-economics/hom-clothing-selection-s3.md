# Package: hom-clothing-selection-s3 — Current fashion trends

- **loId:** `hom-clothing-selection-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-clothing-selection` · **syllabusRef:** 5.1
- **Syllabus scope:** critical evaluation of current fashion trends (male and female) with reference to colour, shape, line, proportion, pattern, texture, influences, accessories and other factors. Settings: magazine pictures and videos as stimulus for discussion. Also examined: athleisure, vintage and pre-loved clothing, social media and trendsetters (2026O, 2023H, 2022O, 2021H, 2017H); accessories (2016H, 2006H); a sketched outfit reflecting a current trend (most Elective 2 questions). No [HL] items marked.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Fashion changes, so the notes must teach the method of evaluating any trend and use particular trends only as labelled examples. The elements and principles themselves (what line, colour and so on are) are in "Elements and principles of design in clothing"; why people follow trends is in "Influences on clothing".

## 1. Must cover

How to evaluate a trend (one block)
- [ ] E1 Critical evaluation: describe the trend, then judge it using the headings the exam gives: colour, pattern, line, shape, proportion, texture, accessories, comfort, function, aesthetic appeal, influences, cost, suitability for the wearer and the occasion. Give a strength and a weakness.
- [ ] E2 Name and describe a trend in terms of the elements: colour (bright, neon, tonal, monochrome, colour panelling); shape or silhouette (oversized, boxy, high-waisted, cropped); line (vertical, horizontal, diagonal); proportion (relation between parts, e.g. a short jacket over a long dress); pattern (geometric, floral, retro, bold prints); texture (shiny, matt, sequins, embroidery, distressed or frayed).
- [ ] E3 Male and female trends both count; evaluate at least one of each, or choose one.
- [ ] E4 Seasonal examples examined: summer trends (light leggings, loose T-shirts, crochet knits, denim jackets, cut-outs, cargo trousers, oversized jackets, 2024O); casual wear (bright and fluorescent colours, colour panelling, geometric prints, 2013H); teenagers (ripped denim, bomber jackets, chunky jewellery, 2017O). Treat each as an example of its year.

Influences on current trends (one block)
- [ ] I1 Social media: spreads an image internationally within hours; influencers and bloggers display outfits, give samples, and their feedback encourages or discourages purchases; repeated exposure turns a garment, colour or vintage look into a trend; trends increase sales.
- [ ] I2 Celebrities and public figures; musicians, dance and music styles; sports and world events.
- [ ] I3 Designers and fashion houses, seasonal collections and fashion weeks; ready-to-wear copying runway looks.
- [ ] I4 Environmental issues: sustainable or circular fashion, durable natural or recycled fabrics, repair, upcycling; fast fashion criticised for waste.
- [ ] I5 Travel and culture; global and ethnic influences.
- [ ] I6 Past decades returning: retro and vintage styles and bold retro patterns are revived (2023H).

Athleisure and leisure wear (one block)
- [ ] A1 Athleisure (leisure wear): sportswear worn for everyday life: leggings, joggers, hoodies, zipped jackets, trainers; stretch, breathable, easy-care fabrics.
- [ ] A2 Strengths: comfortable, versatile, durable, affordable, adaptable, allows movement, supports posture and a healthy active lifestyle, casual and trendy, promoted by influencers; works as a wardrobe staple in neutral colours.
- [ ] A3 Weaknesses: too informal for some workplaces and occasions; tight leggings and tops do not suit every body shape; may lead to casual dress becoming the default.
- [ ] A4 Examined as: evaluate athleisure as a wardrobe staple (2021H), discuss the emergence of leisure wear (2024H), comment on an outfit (2017H), suitability of the outfit shown (2022O, 2024O).

Vintage and pre-loved clothing (one block)
- [ ] V1 Vintage: older garments from a past era; pre-loved: second-hand clothing; bought in charity shops, vintage shops, online marketplaces and swaps.
- [ ] V2 Reasons for popularity: cheaper than new; unique, individual look; sustainability and the reaction to fast fashion; influencers and celebrities promote it; the economic climate; up-styling and up-cycling (patches, appliqué, dyeing, shortening, customising denim with studs or embroidery).
- [ ] V3 Patterns, influences and accessories (2023H): bold, eye-catching retro statement patterns; influences include contemporary style, sustainability and up-cycling; accessories draw attention, brighten and tie an outfit together.
- [ ] V4 Restyling a garment to follow a trend (2025H, 2015H): add belts or embellishment, alter hem or trouser length, add layers or shoulder pads, crop or deconstruct a jacket, distress or recolour jeans, change buttons, patches or linings.

Accessories (one block, 2016H, 2006H, 2012O)
- [ ] X1 Accessories: belts, bags, scarves, hats, gloves, jewellery, sunglasses, footwear, ties, cufflinks, pocket squares.
- [ ] X2 What they do: complete the look, create a focal point or emphasis, add or repeat colour, update or modernise an outfit cheaply, change an outfit's formality, draw attention to a feature (a belt shows off the waist), balance colour and proportion.
- [ ] X3 Footwear contributes to the overall look, complements the outfit and can create a focal point (2016O).
- [ ] X4 Choosing one: name the accessory and give a reason in terms of emphasis, colour coordination, formality or completing the look.

Sketching an outfit that reflects a trend (Elective 2 link)
- [ ] K1 Sketch front view (back if needed); label design features (line, shape, proportion, harmony); give colour and fabric; choose a trend and say why.
- [ ] K2 Fabric reasoning: absorbent, breathable, stretch, easy-care, shape-retaining (e.g. cotton-elastane jersey for fitted tops; polyester/cotton for durable jackets and joggers).
- [ ] K3 Aesthetic appeal: colour, line, balance and emphasis working together; say how the outfit suits wearer and occasion.
- [ ] K4 Diagram: front-view fashion sketch of an outfit with labelled features.

## 3. Traps

- Do not present a list of "current trends" as fixed; the 2027 exam may show any trend. Teach how to describe and evaluate rather than a memorised list. The notes may give a few worked examples but say they are examples.
- Do not use: capri pants, peplum skirts, cocoon shoulders, collar necklaces and fascinators as "current" trends, because they come from an older (c. 2013) set of schemes and read as dated; use them only as past-trend examples if at all.
- Do not use: "herringbone, zig-zag, polka dot" as examples of "bold retro" patterns without saying they are named in 2013 schemes; the point examined is that retro patterns are revived.
- Do not use: "Orla Kiely", brand or influencer names in this subsection, because names belong to the industry subsection and risk becoming dated.
- Elements and principles (definitions, flattering effects) are taught in "Elements and principles of design in clothing"; use the terms here only to describe a trend.
- Fibre production, burn tests and fabric properties belong to the fibres and fabrics material; mention a fabric only to justify a choice.
- Fast fashion and sustainability appear here as influences and criticism; the detailed environmental effects of clothing production are not in this syllabus subsection.
- Athleisure is not "sportswear for sport": it is sportswear styling for everyday wear.
- "Vintage" (older era) and "pre-loved" (any second-hand) differ; both are examined.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name and describe three (2026H), or two (2024O, 2017O) current trends; say what the trend looks like (colour, shape, line, fabric) and why it suits the wearer.
- Sketch and describe an outfit that incorporates one current trend (2026H, 2016H), then give fabric and aesthetic appeal (2026H, 2016H). Expected: labelled sketch (5 marks) and a description of the trend and its suitability.
- Evaluate current fashion trends in casual wear by colour/pattern, line/shape and accessories (2013H); critically evaluate male or female fashion of the past decade (2016H), noting the "mash-up", boho/global and music-subculture influences.
- Evaluate social media's contribution to trends (2026O, 2022O): speed of spread, influencers' samples and reviews, effect on sales, encouraging or discouraging purchases.
- Discuss the popularity of pre-loved or vintage clothing (2026O, 2023H): cost, individuality, sustainability, reaction to fast fashion; with pattern, influences, accessories.
- Evaluate athleisure as a wardrobe staple or the emergence of leisure wear (2021H, 2024H, 2017H, 2022O).
- Name and discuss two accessories currently popular with trendsetters (2023O); suggest one accessory with a reason (2025O, 2021O, 2022O); discuss accessories in completing an outfit (2016H, 2012O) or footwear (2016O).
- Outline three factors influencing current trends (2025H): celebrities and influencers, designers, environmental issues, music and dance, travel and culture, world events.
- Restyle a garment to reflect a trend (2025H, 2015H): examples above.
