# Package: hom-food-safety-s7 — HACCP and ISO 9000

- **Syllabus scope:** HACCP and ISO 9000 [1.3.10] **[HL]**: hazard analysis in food processing operations.
- **Also examined:** stages of a HACCP system for a named dish (2021H, 2014H, 2008H); benefits (2010H, 2004H); also 2016H (hazard and control measure terms).
- **Level:** the whole subsection is Higher Level only. Every block heading ends " (Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The exam almost never asks the seven principles by name; it asks the stages of a food's journey (purchase to reheating) with a hazard and a control measure at each, worth 24 marks. The notes must therefore be centred on that worked example, with the principles and ISO 9000 as shorter blocks.

## 1. Must cover

What HACCP is
- [ ] H1 [HL] HACCP = Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point: a preventive food safety system that identifies hazards at each stage from raw material to consumer and controls them at critical points, rather than relying on testing the finished product.
- [ ] H2 [HL] Terms (2016H asked hazard and control measure with an example each): a hazard is anything that could contaminate food or harm the consumer; a control measure is an action that prevents, removes or reduces a hazard; a critical control point (CCP) is a step at which control is essential and can be monitored (e.g. cooking); a critical limit is the value that separates safe from unsafe (e.g. minimum core temperature).
- [ ] H3 [HL] Three types of hazard: biological (bacteria, moulds, viruses), chemical (pesticide residues, cleaning chemicals, metal residues, allergens), physical (glass, metal, bone, stones, pests, hair).
- [ ] H4 [HL] Who uses it: caterers, restaurants, food manufacturers, and food businesses generally; the Hygiene of Foodstuffs Regulations require food businesses to operate a food safety system based on HACCP principles (link to food legislation, one line).

The seven principles (Codex)
- [ ] P1 [HL] Conduct a hazard analysis.
- [ ] P2 [HL] Determine the critical control points.
- [ ] P3 [HL] Set critical limits for each CCP.
- [ ] P4 [HL] Set up monitoring procedures for each CCP.
- [ ] P5 [HL] Set corrective actions for when a critical limit is not met.
- [ ] P6 [HL] Set up verification procedures (confirm the system works).
- [ ] P7 [HL] Keep records and documentation.

Stages in a HACCP system for a hot main course (2008H, 2021H 24 marks), each with hazard and controls
- [ ] S1 [HL] Purchase: hazards are damaged goods, bacterial or chemical contamination, pests; controls: reputable shop, check packaging, dates, temperature, signs of pests, separate raw and cooked foods in the trolley, foods within date and with quality assurance marks.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Transport: hazard is bacterial growth if food warms or is delayed; controls: cool bag or refrigerated bag for perishables, cover food, minimise time, store promptly.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Storage: hazards are bacterial growth and cross-contamination; controls: fridge below 5 °C, freezer at -18 °C, raw below cooked and covered, check vegetables for mould, clean sealed containers.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Preparation: hazards are contamination, bacterial multiplication, cross-contamination; controls: antibacterial soap and hot water for hands, handle food as little as possible, colour-coded boards and separate utensils, thaw fully (preferably in the fridge, bottom shelf), keep perishables chilled until needed, clean equipment in hot water and detergent.
- [ ] S5 [HL] Cooking: hazard is surviving bacteria (food poisoning); control: cook thoroughly, turn or stir, core temperature for poultry at least 72 °C (2014H, 2021H; current FSAI guidance of 75 °C is also correct); use a thermometer.
- [ ] S6 [HL] Serving and holding: hazards are cross-contamination and multiplication of surviving bacteria; controls: serve immediately on clean plates with clean utensils, hold above 63 °C and no longer than about 90 minutes.
- [ ] S7 [HL] Reheating and storing leftovers: hazard is growth during storage and survival of bacteria; controls: cool quickly, cover, refrigerate, reheat once only, above 100 °C throughout (scheme; current guidance of piping hot, about 70 to 75 °C at the core, is also correct).
- [ ] S8 [HL] Another dish (2014H barbecue): same stages with extras: cook poultry and sausages right through, no pink or red juices, do not use the same plate or tongs for raw and cooked food, keep raw meat separate from salads, boil a raw-meat marinade before using it as a sauce, keep perishable salads and coleslaw in the fridge until needed.

Benefits of HACCP (2010H 3 benefits, 2004H 12 marks)
- [ ] B1 [HL] Identifies and eliminates or reduces hazards, so food is safer and outbreaks are prevented.
- [ ] B2 [HL] Focuses workers on safety at every step.
- [ ] B3 [HL] Meets legal requirements and gives written records as evidence of due care.
- [ ] B4 [HL] Records help business plan for future safety, time and cost; less waste and fewer recalls.
- [ ] B5 [HL] Increases consumer confidence; system reviewed and evaluated regularly.

ISO 9000
- [ ] I1 [HL] ISO is the International Organization for Standardization, which publishes international standards. ISO 9000 is a family of standards for quality management systems: documented procedures, records, regular review, continual improvement to meet customer needs.
- [ ] I2 [HL] ISO 9001 is the standard in the family that a business is audited and certified against; certification by an independent body shows customers a consistent quality system.
- [ ] I3 [HL] Difference: HACCP is a food safety system (controls hazards); ISO 9000 is a general quality management system for any business, covering safety only indirectly. A food business can use both (a food company holding ISO certification can use HACCP as its food safety system).

## 3. Traps

- HACCP is about hazards and critical control points, not "Hygiene and Cooking Control Procedure". Do not mix "critical control point" (a step) with "critical limit" (a value).
- ISO 9000 is a quality management standard for any organisation, not a food-safety standard, and it is not part of HACCP. The food-specific ISO standard (ISO 22000) is not on this syllabus; do not introduce it.
- The exam's HACCP answer follows the dish's journey: purchase, transport, storage, preparation, cooking, serving, reheating. Do not answer with the seven principles when asked to describe the stages for a dish.
- A hazard and a control measure must be paired; a hazard alone or a control alone earns half.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the storage, reheating and hygiene rules themselves (safe food preparation); the bacteria (food poisoning bacteria); the Hygiene of Foodstuffs Regulations (food legislation); the FSAI's role in HACCP guidance (national agencies).
- Do not use: "core temperature 74 °C for two minutes" (2008H scheme), because it differs from the later 72 °C figure.
- Do not use: "hold above 65 °C", because the course figure is 63 °C.
- Do not use: "poultry must reach at least 72 °C to destroy bacteria" as a stand-alone food-safety law; it is the scheme's HACCP critical limit, used in the exam example. Current FSAI guidance for poultry is 75 °C (searched), and a student giving 75 °C is also correct.
- Do not use: a date or founder for HACCP or ISO, because none is checked here.
- Do not use: "the system should be reviewed" as principle 8; there are seven principles, and review belongs to verification (principle 6).

## 4. What exams ask

- Describe the stages in a HACCP system for a hot main course or barbecue (2008H 24 marks, 2014H 24 marks, 2021H 24 marks): six or so stages at 4 marks, each with a hazard and its control; the scheme requires a reference to preparation and one to cooking.
- Outline three benefits of HACCP (2010H 6 marks); explain how HACCP can benefit a catering business in preventing food poisoning outbreaks (2004H 12 marks): identifies hazards from purchase to serving, controls them at critical points, monitoring prevents contamination, reviewed and evaluated.
- Explain the terms hazard and control measure with an example each (2016H 6 marks).
- ISO 9000 has not appeared as a standalone question in the food safety questions; a consumer-studies marking scheme mentions the National Standards Authority of Ireland (NSAI) implementing the ISO 9000 scheme. Give one block, name NSAI as Ireland's national standards body in one clause, and keep it short.
