# Package: hom-marriage-s1 — Definition of marriage

- **loId:** `hom-marriage-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-marriage` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "Marriage" [3.1.5]; the outline gives no further wording for this subsection. No Higher Level only items.
- **Also examined:** marriage in Irish law and its legal requirements and conditions for validity (2024O, 2022H, 2015H, 2014O, 2005O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to Ordinary and Higher Level. This subsection defines marriage and sets out what makes a marriage valid in Irish law. The rights and duties of spouses and cultural variations are in the next subsection; what happens when a marriage ends (nullity, divorce) is in the last. The legal requirements are the most examined item here: exams ask for "four conditions" and for a definition. Answers must use current law, not the older marking-scheme wording (see Traps).

## 1. Must cover

What marriage is
- [ ] D1 Definition: marriage is a legally recognised union of two people who freely agree to it, entered into by a civil or religious ceremony, and to the exclusion of all others. It brings legal rights and duties for both spouses (the detail is in the next subsection; give one line here).
- [ ] D2 Marriage is both a social institution (recognised by society) and a legal status (recognised and protected by the State). It is a voluntary commitment intended to be lasting.
- [ ] D3 "To the exclusion of all others" means monogamy: a person may be married to only one person at a time. Marrying again while the first marriage is still valid is bigamy, a crime. (One clause only; polygamy and bigamy are developed in the cultural-variations subsection.)
- [ ] D4 (assumed) Living together as a couple and remaining faithful are part of what marriage involves; the 2015 marking scheme's definition includes both.

Marriage in Irish law
- [ ] L1 The Constitution (Article 41) pledges the State to guard the institution of marriage, on which the family is founded, with special care, and to protect it against attack. (Assumed link to the constitutional protection of the family.)
- [ ] L2 After the marriage equality referendum (22 May 2015) the Constitution says marriage may be contracted in accordance with law by two persons without distinction as to their sex. The Marriage Act 2015 gave this effect: same-sex couples marry on the same legal terms as opposite-sex couples (in force from 16 November 2015).
- [ ] L3 Civil partnership (Civil Partnership and Certain Rights and Obligations of Cohabitants Act 2010) gave same-sex couples rights and duties similar to marriage but is a different legal status. Civil partnership is no longer available to new couples; existing ones remain valid, and civil partners who marry each other end their civil partnership.
- [ ] L4 A marriage can be a civil ceremony (registrar, or a civil solemniser) or a religious ceremony (priest, minister or other person registered as a solemniser); a secular or humanist ceremony is possible with a registered solemniser. Each has the same legal effect once registered.

Legal requirements for a valid marriage in Ireland (exams: "four conditions")
- [ ] R1 Age: both people must be at least 18.
- [ ] R2 Free to marry: neither may be already married or in a civil partnership.
- [ ] R3 Consent: both must consent freely; consent obtained by force, threat or pressure is not valid. Both must also understand what they are doing: a person who cannot understand the nature of marriage cannot validly marry (capacity).
- [ ] R4 Prohibited degrees: the couple may not be too closely related (by blood or, in some cases, by marriage or adoption).
- [ ] R5 Notice: both must give notice of the intention to marry, in person, to a civil registration service (the registrar of the area) at least three months before the wedding, with the documents it requires (for example identification and a declaration that there is no impediment).
- [ ] R6 Formalities: the ceremony is conducted by an authorised person (a registrar or a registered solemniser), at a venue the registrar has approved (for a civil ceremony) or a place suitable for the religion, with two witnesses present and the required declarations made.
- [ ] R7 Registration: the couple and the witnesses sign the register at the ceremony and the marriage is registered; a marriage certificate can then be obtained.
- [ ] R8 A marriage that fails the conditions may be void or voidable (below); the court process (legal nullity) is in the last subsection of this chapter, so refer to it in one clause only.

Void and voidable marriages (one short block)
- [ ] V1 Void: never valid in law, for example because one person was already married or in a civil partnership, they were within the prohibited degrees, one was under 18, or the formalities were not followed. A court decree is still sought to settle the status.
- [ ] V2 Voidable: valid until a court annuls it, for example because one spouse did not freely consent, or because one spouse is unable to sustain a normal marital relationship. Exams also mention that a marriage that cannot be consummated (sexual relations after the ceremony) can be annulled.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the definition "a union between a man and a woman", because the 2015 marking-scheme wording is superseded by the Marriage Act 2015; same-sex couples may marry.
- Do not use: "over 16 with parental or guardian consent" as an age exception, because the minimum age is now 18 for everyone (the 2005 and 2014 marking schemes are out of date).
- Do not use: "partners must be of opposite sex" as a condition, because the Marriage Act 2015 removed it.
- Do not use: a "licence" for the temporary registration of a venue such as a hotel, beach or garden, because that wording from the 2015 marking scheme is dated and unconfirmed as current law. Say only that the venue must be one the registrar approves (civil) or suitable for the religion, and that the registrar inspects it in advance.
- Do not use: "failure to consummate" as a flat ground for annulment, because the legal ground is inability to sustain a normal marital relationship; say "inability to consummate" if consummation is mentioned.
- Do not use: any fee for notice, ceremony or certificate, because fees change.
- Do not use: the old notes' claim that a voidable marriage is annulled "for failure to consummate" in the void/voidable block without the "inability" wording (same reason as above).
- Civil partnership is not marriage; mention it briefly and do not say it is "the same as marriage".
- Notice is three months. A court can shorten it in exceptional cases; do not teach that exemption.
- Common mistake: saying only the two witnesses sign; the couple sign as well (and the solemniser).
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: rights and responsibilities of spouses, inheritance, arranged marriage, polygamy and customs (cultural variations); pre-marriage courses (preparing for marriage); counselling, separation, the nullity process, church annulment and divorce (when a marriage breaks down). Judicial separation, maintenance, the family home and barring orders belong to the Family Law chapter.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define marriage as it exists in Irish law (about 5 marks): a union recognised by the State, of two people, excluding all others, with living together and faithfulness. Marks go for "legally recognised", "exclusion of all others" and one of living together or faithfulness.
- Outline or discuss four conditions for a valid marriage (about 4 to 5 marks each): the usual four are age 18, voluntary consent, free to marry (not already married), and three months' notice to the registrar. Each should be a short sentence with a reason, for example consent must be voluntary because a marriage made under pressure is not valid.
- Other accepted conditions: two witnesses, a registered or approved venue and authorised solemniser, not within the prohibited degrees, registration of the marriage.
- Occasionally, why marriage is popular today: security, a caring setting for children, and the choice of civil, religious or same-sex marriage. This is a discussion of choice, not law.
- Each requirement in the notes should be a short fact-card-ready sentence with its reason, saying the three months is before the wedding and the notice goes to the registrar of the area. An exam tip can tell students to give four distinct conditions and not repeat the same one in different words.
