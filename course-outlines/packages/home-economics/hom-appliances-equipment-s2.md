# Package: hom-appliances-equipment-s2 — Selecting a large and a small household appliance

- **loId:** `hom-appliances-equipment-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-appliances-equipment` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.5

Also examined: energy labelling (2026O, 2022O, 2018H, 2012H, 2004O); refrigerator star rating (2007H); quality and safety marks (2011H, 2008O); sources of consumer information (2017O, 2009O, 2014H); selection factors for large and small appliances (2006O, 2008O, 2013O, 2017O, 2021O, 2023O); protecting the environment when choosing, using and disposing of appliances (2014H, 2021O, 2025H, 2026O). ## 1. Must cover

Selection criteria (one block)
- [ ] C1 Initial cost: compare purchase price, delivery and installation across shops and brands; value for money is quality against price, not simply the lowest price.
- [ ] C2 Running cost: energy and water used over the appliance's life; the energy label shows this. A cheap model can cost more over its lifetime.
- [ ] C3 Size, capacity and space: the appliance must suit the household size and fit the space (room for doors to open, ventilation, plumbing and a power point). Examples: kilograms of laundry for a washing machine; litres of fresh and frozen food for a fridge; litres for a kettle.
- [ ] C4 Brand and reliability: an established, reputable brand, good reviews, available spare parts.
- [ ] C5 Guarantee and after-sales service: length and cover of the guarantee, availability of repairs, servicing and parts, delivery and installation.
- [ ] C6 Ease of use and cleaning: clear controls, removable parts, smooth surfaces, dishwasher-safe parts, cord storage.
- [ ] C7 Safety features and safety marks: cut-outs, interlocks, child lock, cool-touch surfaces, safety marks (see M1-M4).
- [ ] C8 Family needs and modern features: features should be ones the household will use; programmes, timers, smart controls.
- [ ] C9 Payment method: cash, debit card, credit card, credit agreement, and the total cost of credit (one line only; credit belongs elsewhere).
- [ ] C10 Large and small compared: large appliances (cooker, fridge, washing machine, dishwasher, tumble dryer) are expensive and long-lived, usually delivered and installed, so cost, energy efficiency, size and servicing weigh most; small appliances (kettle, toaster, iron, food processor, mixer) are cheaper and portable, so storage, cleaning and cord length weigh more. Apply the criteria to one large and one small example.

Design and construction (one block)
- [ ] D1 Design: how the appliance looks and works: style and colour to fit the kitchen, shape, controls within easy reach and clearly marked, handles and grips, stability (non-slip feet).
- [ ] D2 Construction: materials and build quality: stainless steel, heat-resistant or cool-touch plastic, glass, enamelled steel; sealed joints and smooth surfaces that are easy to clean and durable; strong flex and plug.
- [ ] D3 Design for safety and efficiency: insulation, interlocks, automatic cut-off, energy-saving settings, quiet running.

Energy efficiency and the energy label (one block)
- [ ] F1 The EU energy label lets consumers compare the energy efficiency of appliances at a glance; the scale is A (most efficient) to G (least efficient). Efficient appliances cost less to run and do less harm to the environment.
- [ ] F2 The label also shows annual energy use in kilowatt-hours (kWh) and, depending on the appliance, capacity, noise in decibels and water use. A refrigeration label shows the fresh and frozen volumes in litres. The current label has a QR code linking to the product's entry in the EU product database.
- [ ] F3 Appliances that carry it: refrigerating appliances (fridges, freezers, fridge-freezers), washing machines, washer-dryers, dishwashers and, since 2025, tumble dryers. Two named appliances are enough for "name two".
- [ ] F4 Efficiency in use is also the consumer's job: full loads, eco programmes, lower temperatures, switching off standby, the correct size.
- [ ] F5 (assumed) Energy in kWh equals power in kW multiplied by hours used; Higher answers can use this to compare running costs.

Star rating for frozen-food storage (one block)
- [ ] R1 The star rating applies to the frozen-food compartment of fridges and fridge-freezers and to freezers, not to the fresh-food section. It tells how cold the compartment is and how long frozen food keeps.
- [ ] R2 One star: -6 °C, about one week. Two stars: -12 °C, about one month. Three stars: -18 °C, about three months. Four stars: -18 °C or lower, up to a year, and fresh food can be frozen in it.
- [ ] R3 Star rating is not energy efficiency: use the energy label to compare running cost.

Quality and safety marks (one block)
- [ ] M1 CE mark: the manufacturer's declaration that the product meets EU health, safety and environmental requirements (2011H: goods comply with European safety standards). It is not independent testing.
- [ ] M2 Double insulation symbol: a square inside a square. The appliance has two layers of insulation, so it needs no earth wire and its plug has no earth connection. Common on plastic-bodied small appliances.
- [ ] M3 Irish Standard mark of the National Standards Authority of Ireland (NSAI): shows the product has been certified to a standard.
- [ ] M4 An ISO 9000 quality mark (2008O): shows the maker's quality-management system has been assessed. Marks show a standard has been met; they do not guarantee performance.
- [ ] M5 A manufacturer's guarantee or a retailer's own-brand label is not an independent mark.

Sources of consumer information (one block)
- [ ] I1 Independent: consumer magazines and websites that test and compare products; the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC) for consumer rights and advice; Citizens Information; the European Consumer Centre Ireland for cross-border purchases; libraries.
- [ ] I2 Commercial: manufacturers' leaflets and brochures, product labels and packaging, sales staff, advertisements, retailer websites. Useful but can be biased.
- [ ] I3 Personal: word of mouth, online user reviews (read critically), in-shop demonstrations.
- [ ] I4 Benefits of sourcing information before buying (2009O): compare price, installation and running cost; find the most energy-efficient model; compare features, design, size and ease of operation; reduce the influence of advertising; avoid a costly mistake.

Environmentally responsible choice, use and disposal (one block)
- [ ] V1 Choosing: the best energy class affordable, the correct size, repairable and durable, eco and low-water programmes, little packaging.
- [ ] V2 Using: full loads, eco and short cycles, lower temperatures, switch off standby, defrost freezers, do not leave fridge doors open, descale and maintain.
- [ ] V3 Disposing: repair, sell or donate while it works; under the WEEE rules a retailer must take back an old appliance of the same type when a new one is bought, and electrical waste can go to a civic amenity site; never in the household bin (crossed-out wheelie bin symbol).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the A+++ to D energy-label scale, because it was replaced by the A to G scale (March 2021 for most products).
- Do not use: "ovens carry the A to G energy label" in a list, because it was not confirmed; keep to refrigerating appliances, washing machines, washer-dryers, dishwashers and tumble dryers.
- Do not use: "the double insulation symbol is two overlapping squares"; it is one square inside another.
- Do not use: the "Irish Quality Control Association" as the body awarding an ISO 9000 mark (2008O scheme), because no such body was confirmed in a search; say an ISO 9000 mark shows the maker's quality system has been assessed, and name NSAI only for the Irish Standard mark.
- Do not use: "Consumer Choice" magazine as a current source, because its continued publication was not confirmed; write "consumer magazines and websites that test products".
- Do not use: "Director of Consumer Affairs" as a source, because the office has been replaced by the CCPC; do not give the Ombudsman as a source of product information.
- Do not use: the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980 (named in the 2007O, 2009O and 2025H schemes), because the Consumer Rights Act 2022 replaced it for goods bought from late November 2022; rights of redress belong to the consumer-rights material.
- Do not use: "Laser card" or "cheque" as current payment methods, because the Laser card ended in 2014 and cheques are rarely used.
- Do not use: any price, running cost, wattage or kWh figure for a named model, because these change.
- The star rating is not the energy label; a four-star freezer compartment is not the same as the most efficient appliance.
- Star ratings apply only to the freezer compartment, never to the fresh-food section.
- The CE mark is not a quality mark awarded after independent testing.
- The working principle and guidelines for use of the motor, heating-element, refrigeration and microwave appliances belong to their own subsections.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline three factors for selecting a large or small appliance (Ordinary 6 marks and 16-20 marks): initial and running cost, size and space, brand and reliability, guarantee and after-sales service, energy rating, ease of cleaning, safety, design, family needs. Each factor needs a clause saying why it matters.
- Energy labelling (2004O, 2011O, 2012H, 2018H, 2022O, 2026O): purpose (compare efficiency at a glance, choose a lower running cost and less environmental harm); the A to G scale with A best; the information shown; two appliances that carry it. For 6 marks give two linked points.
- Refrigerator star rating (2007H, 12 marks): the four ratings with temperature and storage time, and that fresh food can be frozen only at four stars.
- Quality and safety marks (2008O, 2011H): what a named mark tells the consumer (European safety standards; double insulation; ISO 9000 quality).
- Sources of consumer information (2009O, 2014H, 2017O): name two or three, say what each offers and one limit; benefits of getting information before buying.
- Environmental responsibility when choosing, using and disposing (2014H, 2021O, 2025H, 2026O): choosing efficient and correctly sized; using full loads and eco settings; disposing through take-back or a civic amenity site.
- Tips to build: link each factor to a reason; give the label scale direction; say what the star rating does not show.
