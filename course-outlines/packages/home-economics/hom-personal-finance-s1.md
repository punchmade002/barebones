# Package: hom-personal-finance-s1 — Household income

- **loId:** `hom-personal-finance-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-personal-finance` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.3
- **Syllabus scope (outline):** the household as a financial unit within the economy; household income and social factors (age, sex, social class, culture); wages, salaries, pensions, social welfare allowances and benefits as sources of income.
- **Also examined (outline):** gross and net income, PAYE, PRSI, USC and tax credits (2022H, 2018H, 2014O, 2005H); social insurance and social assistance payments (2024H, 2009H); named welfare payments such as the Working Family Payment, Supplementary Welfare Allowance and Child Benefit (2023H, 2023O); contributory and non-contributory State pensions (2026H).
- **Higher Level only:** nothing in this subsection is marked [HL]. Do not mark anything HL.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. Amounts (tax rates and bands, credits, PRSI and USC rates and thresholds, welfare and pension rates, income limits) are left out: explain how each works.

## 1. Must cover

The household as a financial unit
- [ ] H1 A household is a group sharing a home and its income and spending decisions. As a financial unit it earns income, spends, saves, borrows and pays tax, and it is the basic unit in the economy.
- [ ] H2 Its role in the economy: it spends on goods and services (this supports the businesses and the jobs that supply them); it pays taxes and PRSI that fund public services and welfare payments; it buys property (stamp duty is a State revenue); it supplies workers; and it teaches children how to manage money.
- [ ] H3 Households usually manage their own finances independently, but may need State help (welfare payments, MABS) when income falls short of need.

Sources of household income
- [ ] I1 Wages: pay for hours worked or output, often weekly, usually manual or hourly work.
- [ ] I2 Salaries: a fixed yearly sum paid in regular instalments, usually non-manual or professional work.
- [ ] I3 Pensions: income after retirement from the State (see State pensions below), an employer's occupational scheme or a personal pension fund. Money paid into a pension while working is a voluntary deduction (I11).
- [ ] I4 Social welfare allowances and benefits: payments from the State to people who are unemployed, ill, disabled, caring, retired, widowed or raising children.
- [ ] I5 (assumed) Other sources: self-employment profit, rent from property, interest on savings, gifts or maintenance payments. One line only.

Social factors affecting household income (age, sex, social class, culture)
- [ ] F1 Age: pay generally rises with experience and promotion, then falls at retirement unless a pension has been planned; young people can earn part-time income.
- [ ] F2 Sex (gender): equal-pay law requires equal pay for the same work, yet women are still under-represented in senior management and more likely to work part-time or take career breaks for caring.
- [ ] F3 Social class (socio-economic group): education and qualifications open access to better-paid work; early school leaving and low qualifications lead to low pay or unemployment.
- [ ] F4 Culture: cultural attitudes affect whether women do paid work, which occupations are seen as suitable and which spending priorities a household sets.
- [ ] F5 Employment pattern (Also examined, 2019H style): how many household members are in paid work; whether hours are full-time, part-time, job-sharing, flexible or seasonal. One line on disability or illness (reduces earning capacity and adds costs).

Gross and net income; compulsory and voluntary deductions
- [ ] G1 Gross income: income earned before any deductions. Net income: take-home pay after deductions. Budgets are planned on net income.
- [ ] G2 Compulsory deductions: PAYE, PRSI, USC. Voluntary deductions: pension contributions and AVCs, health insurance, union subscription, savings schemes, social club, holiday fund.
- [ ] G3 PAYE (Pay As You Earn): income tax deducted by the employer from each payment of wages or salary and paid to the Revenue Commissioners (the State). The tax rises with income through a standard rate and a higher rate; the employer uses the details Revenue gives (credits and rate band).
- [ ] G4 Tax credit: an amount subtracted from the tax calculated on gross income; it reduces the tax payable (it does not reduce income before tax is worked out). Examples of credits: personal credit, employee (PAYE) credit. Name them without amounts.
- [ ] G5 PRSI (Pay Related Social Insurance): compulsory contribution, a percentage of pay, paid by the employee (deducted) and separately by the employer; it funds social insurance benefits and entitles the contributor to them. Low earners may be exempt from paying; self-employed people pay it too. Contributions build the record that decides entitlement (see S1).
- [ ] G6 USC (Universal Social Charge): compulsory charge on gross income at rising rates in bands; low earners below a threshold are exempt. It is separate from income tax and is not reduced by tax credits.
- [ ] G7 What the deductions pay for: PAYE and USC go to the State for public services (health, education, roads, welfare); PRSI funds the social insurance payments.

State welfare payments: social insurance and social assistance
- [ ] S1 Social insurance payments: paid to people who have paid enough PRSI contributions; not means tested; the person has "earned" them. Examples: Jobseeker's Benefit, Illness Benefit, Maternity Benefit, Carer's Benefit, contributory State pension.
- [ ] S2 Social assistance payments: paid from taxes to people who do not qualify for insurance payments or have no contributions; means tested. Examples: Jobseeker's Allowance, Disability Allowance, Carer's Allowance, non-contributory State pension, Supplementary Welfare Allowance.
- [ ] S3 Means test: the State assesses the claimant's (and usually partner's) income and capital against a limit; payment is reduced or refused as means rise.
- [ ] S4 Universal payment: paid to everyone who qualifies on one condition, without a means test. Child Benefit: monthly payment for each qualifying child, universal, not means tested.
- [ ] S5 Working Family Payment (WFP): weekly payment for employees on low pay with children; means tested against a family-size income limit; formerly called Family Income Supplement (FIS).
- [ ] S6 Supplementary Welfare Allowance (SWA): means-tested payment for people whose means cannot meet their basic needs, including a temporary payment while a claim for another payment is processed.
- [ ] S7 State pensions: contributory State pension: based on PRSI record, not means tested, so other income does not remove it. Non-contributory State pension: means tested social assistance for someone without enough contributions, also subject to residence conditions.
- [ ] S8 Other examples a student may give when asked for "a social welfare payment": Carer's Allowance, Disability Allowance, Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance, Fuel Allowance, Maternity Benefit.

## 3. Traps

- Gross is before deductions, net is after. Students swap them.
- PRSI is a payment into social insurance, not a tax in the PAYE sense, and is not the same as private insurance (private insurance is in the Insurance subsection).
- Tax credits reduce the tax bill; they do not reduce gross income. USC is not reduced by credits.
- Social insurance = contributions, not means tested. Social assistance = means tested. Child Benefit is neither a contribution nor a means-tested payment: it is universal.
- The contributory pension is not means tested; the non-contributory is. Do not call the non-contributory pension "free": it is means tested.
- Do not describe pension contributions as a compulsory deduction.
- Do not describe the benefits of PRSI in detail here beyond naming payments; the insurance role of PRSI is covered in the Insurance subsection.
- Budgeting, essential and discretionary spending and MABS belong to the next subsection (Household expenditure and budgeting); payment methods, credit and hire purchase to the Methods of payment subsection; types of savings to the Saving subsection.
- Do not use: "income levy", "health levy" and "pension levy" as compulsory deductions (named in the 2009 marking scheme), because they are superseded; the USC replaced the income and health levies in 2011 and the pension levy has ended.
- Do not use: "Family Income Supplement (FIS)" as the current payment name (2011 marking scheme), because it was renamed Working Family Payment; write Working Family Payment, "formerly FIS" at most once.
- Do not use: Disability Allowance as a social insurance payment (the 2009 marking scheme lists it that way), because it is means tested social assistance.
- Do not use: any euro figure, rate, band, threshold, income limit, pension age or weekly-hours condition (including the exemption thresholds for PRSI and USC and the Working Family Payment limits), because these change every year.
- Do not use: the claim that employees receive a yearly notice of credits and cut-off point, because the procedure has changed (Revenue now supplies payroll details to employers); say only that Revenue works out the credits and rate band.
- Do not use: "old age pension" as the only name; write "State pension (contributory)" and "State pension (non-contributory)", with "old age pension" as the older name at most.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define gross and net income and name two compulsory deductions (PAYE, PRSI, USC) and two voluntary ones: Ordinary Level 6-mark short answers, many years. Net income is the money a household budgets with.
- Explain PAYE (income tax deducted by employer, goes to the State, depends on earnings; name two uses of the money such as health and education), PRSI (compulsory, shared by employer and employee, related to pay, funds social welfare benefits, classes) and USC, tax credit (reduces the tax owed; credits subtracted after tax is calculated).
- Social insurance versus social assistance with one example of each: contributions and no means test versus means test; named payments.
- True/false and one-line items on Child Benefit (universal), Working Family Payment (low-paid employees with children), Supplementary Welfare Allowance (means tested).
- Describe the contributory and non-contributory State pension (PRSI record and no means test versus means test).
- Higher Level essays (20-24 marks) on factors affecting household income: age, gender, social class and culture, with a reason for each; employment pattern and number of earners as extra factors. Strong answers give an example per factor and a consequence for the household.
- The role of the household as a financial unit in the economy (10 marks): spending that sustains jobs, tax and stamp duty, teaching money management, usually independent but may need State aid.
- Name three social welfare payments available to individuals: any three valid names.
