## 1. Must cover

Production of flour (one block; 2026H "informative account" for 18 marks)
- [ ] M1 Intake and cleaning: wheat is sampled, graded and may be blended to give the quality needed. Cleaning removes dust, stones, chaff and metal using sieves, air currents and magnets.
- [ ] M2 Conditioning (tempering): a controlled amount of water is added and the grain rests. The bran toughens so it separates in large pieces, and the endosperm softens.
- [ ] M3 Break rolling: corrugated rollers open the grain and release the endosperm without crushing the bran.
- [ ] M4 Sifting: plansifters (sieves) separate bran, germ and endosperm particles by size.
- [ ] M5 Reduction rolling: smooth rollers grind the endosperm into fine flour, with sieving after each pass.
- [ ] M6 Finishing: flour is blended for consistent quality (gluten strength), may be treated with permitted improvers, fortified where used, and tested (moisture, gluten, colour) before packing.
- [ ] M7 Extraction rate: the percentage of the grain that ends up in the flour. White flour is about 75% (2018 scheme), wholemeal flour 100% (all three parts). Wheatmeal is between.
- [ ] M8 Diagram: a flow chart of the stages in order.
- [ ] M9 Stone-ground flour: grain ground between millstones rather than rollers; the schemes accept it as a flour type.

White and wholemeal flour compared (one block; 2018O, 9 marks)
- [ ] W1 White: bran and germ removed, endosperm only; lighter colour and texture; less fibre and fewer B vitamins and minerals; longer shelf life.
- [ ] W2 Wholemeal: whole grain, brown, higher in fibre, B vitamins and minerals; denser baked products; shorter shelf life because of the germ's oil.

Types of flour and uses (one block; 2021O, 2013H, 2010O)
- [ ] T1 Plain white (cream) flour: pastry, cakes (creamed or whisked), biscuits, sauces and roux, pizza, scones, batters.
- [ ] T2 Self-raising flour: plain flour with a raising agent (baking powder) added; cakes, sponges, scones, biscuits.
- [ ] T3 Strong flour: made from hard wheat, high in gluten-forming protein; yeast breads, pizza dough, puff pastry; traps gas for a good rise.
- [ ] T4 Wholemeal flour: brown bread, scones, muffins, wholemeal pastry.
- [ ] T5 Wheatmeal (brown) flour: part of the bran removed; brown bread and soda bread.
- [ ] T6 Cornflour: a fine starch from maize; thickening sauces, custards and gravies.
- [ ] T7 Gluten-free flours (rice, maize and other blends): baking for people with coeliac disease.
- [ ] T8 Each answer must pair a named flour with a different use.

Gluten in flour (one block, assumed from the protein chapter where the wheat proteins are named; one short block)
- [ ] GL1 Gluten forms when the proteins gliadin and glutenin in wheat flour are mixed with water. It is elastic and traps gas, giving bread structure.
- [ ] GL2 Kneading develops gluten (strong, elastic dough); rubbing in fat and light handling limit it (tender pastry). Strong flour forms more gluten than plain.

Flour as a processed food (one block; 1.3.5, 2026H "packaging and labelling")
- [ ] P1 Packaging: strong, sealed, food-grade bags (paper or plastic) that protect against moisture, insects, contamination and odours, and are easy to handle and store.
- [ ] P2 Label: name of the food (type of flour), ingredients list, net quantity, nutrition information, best-before date, storage instructions, name and address of the business, batch or lot code, and allergens (wheat and gluten) emphasised, e.g. in bold. The label may carry cooking or recipe guidance and any added nutrients.
- [ ] P3 Purpose of labelling: safe choice, comparison, traceability (batch code allows recall) and storage guidance.
- [ ] P4 Why it counts as an extensively processed food in the syllabus's wording: the whole grain is cleaned, separated, ground, blended, treated and packed, producing a refined ingredient with a different nutrient profile.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "flour is fortified by law in Ireland" or "fortified where required", because flour fortification here is voluntary; write "may be fortified".
- Do not use: chlorine bleaching of flour, because it is not permitted in the EU; "treated with permitted improvers" is enough and no named chemical is needed.
- Do not use: 70% or 80% extraction figures, because only 75% (white) and 100% (wholemeal) are in a scheme and extraction varies by mill.
- Wholemeal is not made by adding bran back to white flour unless stated on the label; the milled stream may be recombined, but do not teach it as a standard step.
- Cornflour is a starch, not a wheat flour; do not say it contains gluten. Self-raising flour is a plain flour with raising agent added, not a different wheat.
- Do not give milling machinery brand names or capacities.

## 4. What exams ask

- Give an informative account of the production of flour (Higher Level, 18 marks): the stages in order with the reason for each (cleaning, conditioning, break rolling, sifting, reduction rolling, blending and testing), then packaging (what protects the flour) and labelling (what the label states and why).
- Name three flours with a different use for each; or give a use for self-raising, wholemeal and plain flour.
- Explain the difference between white and wholemeal flour (bran and germ removed or kept, extraction rate, fibre, vitamins, colour, shelf life).
- Explain gluten and why strong flour is used for bread and plain flour for pastry.
