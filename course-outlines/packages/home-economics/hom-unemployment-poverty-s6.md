# Package: hom-unemployment-poverty-s6 — Statutory responses

- **loId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty` · **syllabusRef:** 6.7
- **Syllabus scope:** "Social welfare assistance and benefits; **[HL]** initiatives encouraging foreign investment; schemes to reduce expenditure for low-income families" [6.7]. **Settings:** small groups investigate one statutory service and present a synopsis. Foreign investment is Higher Level only; the rest is common.
- **Also examined:** statutory initiatives to create employment (2026H, 2022H, 2015H, 2012H, 2009H; also 2006O, 2018O and 2023O in their wording as a scheme for or programme training the unemployed); schemes for low-income families (2023H, 2017H, 2008H); statutory responses to poverty (2006H, 2021H, 2010H); a named welfare payment (2023O Jobseeker's Benefit, 2021H Jobseeker's Allowance, 2023H Working Family Payment).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Checked: Citizens Information-style descriptions of payments, SOLAS, Enterprise Ireland, IDA Ireland, Local Enterprise Offices, the 2006H-2026H schemes. Marking schemes up to 2012 still name bodies and payments that have since been replaced; those are listed under Traps. Use only current names.

## 1. Must cover

Social welfare assistance and benefits (one block)
- [ ] W1 Social welfare payments come from the Department of Social Protection. Two routes: social insurance (benefit) payments depend on paid PRSI contributions and are not means-tested; social assistance payments are means-tested (the household's income and means are assessed) and need no contribution record, being paid on the basis of need. Money for the first comes from PRSI contributions; for the second from general taxation.
- [ ] W2 Jobseeker's Benefit: social insurance payment for an unemployed person with enough PRSI contributions (the 2023O named scheme). Jobseeker's Allowance: means-tested payment for an unemployed person who does not qualify for Jobseeker's Benefit or whose benefit has ended (2021H). Claimants must be unemployed, capable of work, available for full-time work and genuinely seeking work.
- [ ] W3 Supplementary Welfare Allowance: means-tested payment for people with no or very low income whose basic needs are not met (assumed in a "social welfare assistance" answer; also pays exceptional needs).
- [ ] W4 Child Benefit: a payment for each qualifying child, paid regardless of parents' income (universal, not means-tested).
- [ ] W5 One-parent family supports: means-tested payments for lone parents, recognising that one adult carries the whole cost of the household.
- [ ] W6 Working Family Payment (formerly Family Income Supplement): a means-tested, tax-free weekly payment for low-paid working parents with children. It is an in-work payment that eases the poverty trap.
- [ ] W7 How to answer "name and give details of one": name the payment, say who it is for, say how it is paid (contributions or means-test) and what it provides; two details at 4 marks each.

Schemes that reduce expenditure for low-income families (one block)
- [ ] L1 Medical card: free GP visits and many health services; eligibility depends on income and, in some cases, medical need (HSE).
- [ ] L2 Fuel Allowance: weekly payment during the winter to help with heating costs of qualifying low-income households (Department of Social Protection).
- [ ] L3 Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance: an annual payment toward school uniforms and shoes for children of qualifying families.
- [ ] L4 Housing supports: local authority (social) housing at rents related to income, and the Housing Assistance Payment (HAP), a payment through the local authority that helps tenants with rent in the private rented sector; HAP is income-assessed (the rent contribution is based on income).
- [ ] L5 Household Benefits Package: help with electricity or gas and a free television licence for qualifying older people and some people with disabilities.
- [ ] L6 Education supports: the school meals programme and free school book schemes (the 2017H scheme lists school meals and a school books grant scheme); the free pre-school place (ECCE) reduces childcare costs.
- [ ] L7 MABS (Money Advice and Budgeting Service): free, confidential budgeting and debt advice for people in debt, funded by the State through the Citizens Information Board.
- [ ] L8 How to answer: name the scheme, say who qualifies (low income or special need) and what it pays for or removes; two details at 4 marks each (2017H).

Statutory initiatives to create employment (one block; examined in six papers 2006-2026)
- [ ] J1 SOLAS: the State's further education and training authority; it funds, plans and coordinates further education and training, working with the Education and Training Boards (ETBs). It provides apprenticeships, skills training and upskilling for unemployed people (2022H and 2026H schemes).
- [ ] J2 Enterprise Ireland: State agency that helps Irish companies increase their sales and exports, so creating jobs; it gives advice, grants and market support (2009H and 2012H schemes).
- [ ] J3 Local Enterprise Offices (LEOs): the "first stop shop" for people starting or growing small businesses; they give training, mentoring, advice and grants and encourage job creation. They replaced the county enterprise boards.
- [ ] J4 Intreo: the Department of Social Protection's public employment service; it gives job-seekers advice, job-matching and referral to training.
- [ ] J5 Community Employment (CE) and similar schemes: temporary part-time work placements on local community projects for long-term unemployed and other eligible people (details in the community responses subsection where they overlap).
- [ ] J6 Other schemes: Vocational Training Opportunities Scheme (VTOS), Back to Education Allowance and Youthreach (an ETB programme for early school leavers) support return to education and training; mention only as extras.
- [ ] J7 How to answer: name the body, say what it is, give two functions with how they create jobs or improve employment prospects.

Initiatives encouraging foreign investment [HL] (one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] F1 [HL] Foreign direct investment (FDI): overseas companies setting up or expanding operations in Ireland. IDA Ireland is the State agency responsible for attracting it and for helping foreign companies already here to expand (2009H and 2012H schemes).
- [ ] F2 [HL] The attractions Ireland offers, as listed in the schemes: a skilled, young, flexible and educated workforce, a low corporation tax rate (12.5%; 15% for the largest multinational groups), grant assistance, good telecommunications, access to the EU single market, and an English-speaking workforce.
- [ ] F3 [HL] How it helps: direct jobs in the foreign firms, indirect jobs in suppliers and services, tax revenue, new skills and technology; IDA Ireland also aims to spread investment to regions outside the main cities.
- [ ] F4 [HL] Limits: heavy dependence on a few large firms (a plant closure removes many jobs), investment tends to favour cities and larger towns.

## 3. Traps

- Social insurance is not means-tested; social assistance is. Child Benefit is universal and is neither. The Fuel Allowance, medical card and Back to School Allowance are means-tested (an income test) supports, not insurance payments.
- A "statutory" body is set up by law or run by the State (SOLAS, Enterprise Ireland, IDA Ireland, the Department of Social Protection, the HSE, local authorities). Voluntary bodies (SVP, Simon, Focus Ireland) belong to the community responses subsection, and the Combat Poverty Agency is covered there as a former body.
- The poverty trap and how payments are withdrawn are explained in the previous subsection; here the payments are described, not the trap.
- Do not use: FÁS (the training authority replaced by SOLAS), County Enterprise Boards (replaced by Local Enterprise Offices), Jobstart, Back to Work Allowance, Rent Allowance, Family Income Supplement as a current name, mortgage allowance scheme, and RAPID, because the 2006H-2012H schemes name them but they are replaced or ended; use SOLAS, Local Enterprise Offices, Housing Assistance Payment and the Working Family Payment.
- Do not use: the National Anti-Poverty Strategy (NAPS) or the National Action Plan for Social Inclusion as the current policy, because both are past strategies; the current national strategy is the Roadmap for Social Inclusion 2026-2030 (published May 2026). If named, name only the Roadmap and say it is the Government's whole-of-government strategy to reduce poverty.
- Do not use: any payment rate, weekly amount, income limit, number of contributions, qualifying hours, age limit, the length of time Jobseeker's Benefit is paid, or the duration of Fuel Allowance, because they change with each budget.
- Do not use: SOLAS as the body running community-based employment (CE) or long-term unemployed programmes, because Community Employment and Tús are Department of Social Protection schemes, and SOLAS funds Community Training Centres, apprenticeships and ETB further education and training.
- Do not use: Pathways to Work or other named employment strategies, because the current strategy and its dates are unconfirmed.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name and give details of one statutory initiative aimed at creating employment (Higher, 10-12 marks, name 2-5 marks then two details at 4-5 marks each; 2026H, 2022H, 2015H, 2012H, 2009H): SOLAS, Enterprise Ireland, a Local Enterprise Office, IDA Ireland. A strong answer states the body's function and then two ways it creates or supports jobs.
- Name and give details of one scheme to reduce expenditure for low-income families (Higher, 10 marks; 2017H, 2023H, 2008H): medical card, Fuel Allowance, Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance, HAP, School Books and meals schemes.
- Name and describe a social welfare assistance or benefit (12 marks, 2021H) or a statutory scheme that supports unemployed people (8 marks, 2023O): Jobseeker's Benefit or Allowance with the contributions/means-test difference.
- Outline how the State has responded to eliminating poverty (10 marks, 2010H): welfare payments, housing, medical card, back-to-school allowance, foreign investment initiatives, community and education programmes. A list answer with brief details works.
- Foreign investment [HL]: IDA Ireland and why multinationals come, and the risk.
- Tips: name first (a named scheme earns the first marks); for each detail say who, what, why; do not name replaced bodies even if an old marking scheme does.
