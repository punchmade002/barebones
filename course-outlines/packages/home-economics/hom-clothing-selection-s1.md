# Package: hom-clothing-selection-s1 — Textile care

- **loId:** `hom-clothing-selection-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-clothing-selection` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.6 (core, both levels)
- **Syllabus scope:** "the basic scientific principles underlying the care of fabrics relative to their general properties." Settings: simple scientific tests comparing shrinkage, water-repellence and crease recovery of a variety of fabrics. Also examined: fabric care symbols (Ordinary Level Section A most years: 2025O, 2023O, 2022O, 2021O, also 2018O, 2016O, 2013O); detergent components, namely surfactants, enzymes and fluorescents (2013H). No [HL] items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23, from the outline, the 2013-2025 marking schemes, and a web check of the care-symbol standard. "Basic scientific principles" means: why a fibre type behaves as it does in water, heat and detergent, and what the three simple tests show. Fibre and fabric profiles, fabric finishes (including fire-retardant finishes), construction and identification burn tests belong to the textiles chapter's fibres-and-fabrics material; here they appear only as far as they explain a care need.

## 1. Must cover

Fibre type decides care (one block)
- [ ] C1 A fabric's care needs come from its fibre, its yarn and its construction. Water, heat, friction and detergent act differently on each fibre family.
- [ ] C2 Cellulose fibres (cotton, linen): absorb water readily, are strong when wet (so can be washed hot and often), crease easily, and tend to shrink on first washing.
- [ ] C3 Protein fibres (wool, silk): absorb moisture, are weakened by alkaline detergent and high temperatures; silk is damaged by alkaline detergents; wool is washed cool (about 30 C, as the label states); wool felts and shrinks if agitated in hot water. Use a mild or wool-specific detergent, a low temperature and gentle action.
- [ ] C4 Manufactured (synthetic) fibres such as polyester and nylon: absorb very little water, dry quickly, keep their shape, resist creasing; they attract static, and melt or glaze under a hot iron or high tumble-dry heat.
- [ ] C5 Blends and mixtures: the most delicate fibre in the blend sets the wash temperature and ironing heat (e.g. a wool/polyester mixture is washed and ironed as wool).
- [ ] C6 Construction matters too: a tight weave resists water and wind better than a loose knit; knitted fabric stretches and can sag or shrink.

Shrinkage (one block)
- [ ] S1 Shrinkage is a permanent reduction in a fabric's length or width after washing or drying.
- [ ] S2 Relaxation shrinkage: fibres stretched during spinning, weaving and finishing relax back in water; most marked in cotton, linen and viscose.
- [ ] S3 Felting shrinkage (wool): the surface scales on wool fibres interlock under heat, moisture and agitation; the fabric thickens and shrinks and this cannot be reversed.
- [ ] S4 Heat shrinkage: washing or tumble drying hotter than the fabric tolerates, especially in knitted and thermoplastic fabrics.
- [ ] S5 Percentage shrinkage = (original length − new length) ÷ original length × 100.
- [ ] S6 Prevention: follow the care label, wash at the stated temperature, avoid hot tumble drying; pre-shrunk or shrink-resistant finished fabric; (assumed) wash a test sample of fabric before cutting out a garment.

Water repellence (one block)
- [ ] W1 Water repellence is how well a fabric resists wetting. Three things give it: close weave or knit (small gaps between yarns), a fibre that absorbs little water (polyester, nylon), and a water-repellent finish that makes water bead and roll off.
- [ ] W2 Water-repellent (showerproof) fabric resists wetting but will let water through in heavy or prolonged rain; waterproof fabric (coated or laminated, seams sealed) stops water passing. Breathable fabrics let perspiration vapour out while keeping rain out.
- [ ] W3 Washing and wear reduce a finish, so a repellent garment may need re-proofing; cotton and wool, which absorb water, are not naturally repellent.

Crease recovery (one block)
- [ ] R1 Crease recovery is how well a fabric springs back after being folded or crushed.
- [ ] R2 Poor recovery: cotton, linen and viscose (they creases easily and need ironing); good recovery: wool (elastic fibre, creases hang out), polyester and nylon.
- [ ] R3 Crease-resistant finishes on cotton or linen improve recovery and cut down ironing; blending with polyester also helps.
- [ ] R4 Care link: damp garments can be shaken and hung, steam relaxes wool creases, and ironing temperature must match the fibre.

Simple scientific tests (one Investigation block)
- [ ] T1 Shrinkage test: mark and measure a square on each sample, wash and dry under identical conditions, remeasure and calculate percentage shrinkage.
- [ ] T2 Water-repellence test: place drops of water on each sample (flat or on a slope), time or observe whether drops bead and roll off or soak in and darken the fabric.
- [ ] T3 Crease-recovery test: crumple equal-sized samples in a closed fist for one minute, release, and compare how smooth each is after a set time.
- [ ] T4 Fair test: equal-sized samples, same conditions, same timing, one variable (the fabric); repeat to get reliable results and record in a table; name a fibre or fabric per sample.

Care symbols (one block, with a Diagram cue)
- [ ] L1 Care labels use a fixed set of symbols in a fixed order: washing, bleaching, drying, ironing, professional care. They are international (standard ISO 3758; GINETEX is the European association behind them).
- [ ] L2 Wash: washtub = may be machine washed; number or dots in the tub = maximum temperature; bars under the tub = gentler action; hand in tub = hand wash only; cross over tub = do not wash.
- [ ] L3 Bleach: triangle = bleach may be used; crossed triangle = do not bleach.
- [ ] L4 Drying: square with a circle = tumble drying allowed (dots = heat setting); crossed tumble-dry symbol = do not tumble dry; square with a horizontal line = dry flat; square with one vertical line = line dry; drip dry (examined as "drip dry" in 2023O and 2018O) = hang to drip without wringing.
- [ ] L5 Iron: iron symbol, one dot = cool, two dots = warm or medium, three dots = hot; crossed iron = do not iron. (Examined: "hot iron", "medium iron".)
- [ ] L6 Dry cleaning: a circle (letters inside tell the cleaner which solvent process); crossed circle = do not dry clean.
- [ ] L7 Meaning to the consumer: choosing a garment and caring for it correctly, avoiding shrinkage, colour loss and damage; reading the label before buying.
- [ ] L8 Diagram: draw and label the washtub (30/40 degrees or dots), hand wash, triangle with cross, tumble dry, dry flat, iron with two dots and circle (dry clean). Candidates in the examined years were asked to state the meaning of two shown symbols for 6 marks.

Detergent components (one block, examined Higher Level 2013)
- [ ] D1 Surfactants: lower the surface tension of water so it wets the fabric; one end attracts water, the other attracts oil and grease; they lift greasy dirt off the fibre, break it into small particles, and hold it suspended so it is rinsed away and does not settle back.
- [ ] D2 Enzymes (in "biological" detergents): proteins that speed up the breakdown of stains: protease for protein stains (blood, egg), amylase for starch, lipase for fat. They work in warm, not hot, water and are destroyed by high temperatures.
- [ ] D3 Fluorescent whitening agents (optical brighteners): absorb ultraviolet light and re-emit it as blue-white light, making white and light fabrics look whiter and brighter. They do not clean or remove stains.
- [ ] D4 Purpose-matching: a mild detergent for wool and silk; a biological detergent is not for wool or silk, whose protein fibres enzymes can attack. (assumed)

## 3. Traps

- Fibre profiles, fibre production, burn tests and fabric finishes (waterproofing, mercerising, flame retardant, anti-static) belong to the textiles chapter's fibres and fabrics material; mention a finish only as a cause of a care property.
- Stain removal, wash-load preparation, laundry routine and clothing storage are Junior Cycle household-skills content and are not on this syllabus. Do not add them.
- Washing machines and tumble dryers as appliances (working principles) belong to the appliances chapter, not here.
- Water-repellent is not waterproof; breathable is not the same as water-repellent.
- Surfactants lift soil; enzymes digest stains; fluorescents only change appearance. Candidates often swap these.
- Felting is the shrinkage of wool; relaxation shrinkage is mainly cellulose; both are irreversible but have different causes.
- Do not use: "fluorescent agents are unsuitable for coloured fabrics", because that claim is unsourced and not what the marking scheme says (it says they brighten whites).

## 4. What exams ask

- Ordinary Level Section A (6 marks, most years): state what two care symbols tell the consumer. Pairs asked include: hand wash only with do not bleach; dry flat with do not bleach; drip dry with do not tumble dry; hot iron with drip dry; hand wash only with medium iron. Expected: the plain meaning in a few words plus where useful the reason (e.g. wool or delicates).
- Higher 2013: explain the purpose of two of surfactants, enzymes, fluorescents. Expected: surfactants reduce surface tension so water wets the fabric and dirt is dislodged and dispersed; enzymes break down protein-based (and other) stains; fluorescents make whites look brighter by converting UV to visible light.
- 2005 Ordinary: give reasons why two properties matter for care: crease resistant (easier washing and ironing, less creasing when worn); strong when wet (can be washed often without stretching or tearing); colour-fast (colour does not run or fade).
- Performance tests (Higher 2025): name two tests and explain the procedure for one. Crease test: crumple equal samples in a fist for a minute and compare. Other tests named in the scheme: abrasion, absorbency, stretch, colourfastness, water resistance, washability. The procedure answer needs equal samples, the same conditions and a comparison of results.
- Coursework settings: shrinkage, water-repellence and crease recovery tests; expected: fair-test method, percentage shrinkage and a conclusion on suitability for a garment.
