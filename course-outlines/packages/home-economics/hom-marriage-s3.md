# Package: hom-marriage-s3 — Preparing for marriage

- **loId:** `hom-marriage-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-marriage` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "facilities and services available to those preparing for marriage." No Higher Level only items.
- **Also examined:** pre-marriage courses (2025O, 2017H, 2014O, 2009H; also 2008O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. The syllabus wording is broad ("facilities and services"); exams concentrate on pre-marriage courses (what they cover and how they help) and, once, on the role of the school. The practical steps a couple must take by law (notice to the registrar) sit in the first subsection and are only linked here. Counselling for couples already in difficulty belongs in the last subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Pre-marriage courses
- [ ] P1 A pre-marriage course is a course for engaged couples, run before the wedding, that helps them understand what married life involves and prepares them for it.
- [ ] P2 Providers: Accord (the Catholic Church's marriage and relationship service) runs pre-marriage courses in centres around Ireland. Churches and other religious bodies run preparation for their own members. A couple should check what their church requires before a religious wedding.
- [ ] P3 Content: communication, conflict resolution (managing disagreements), relationships, family planning, child-rearing and parenting, financial responsibility and budgeting, the legal side of marriage, buying or renting a home, and roles and expectations.
- [ ] P4 Problems that can damage a marriage: the course prompts couples to discuss issues such as addiction (alcohol, drugs, gambling) and where to get help early.
- [ ] P5 Personal qualities: couples examine qualities that help a marriage (honesty, openness, tolerance, commitment).

How pre-marriage courses help (exams: "benefits", "discuss how")
- [ ] H1 Explain the expectations and realities of marriage, so couples test their assumptions before committing.
- [ ] H2 Encourage openness and honest communication between the partners.
- [ ] H3 Provide a forum to discuss expectations about roles, money, housing and children, and to find out whether the couple agree.
- [ ] H4 Give practical advice on money, housing, parenting and family planning.
- [ ] H5 Build conflict-management skills that reduce the risk of later breakdown, and show couples where help is available.
- [ ] H6 Meeting other engaged couples gives support and shows that others have the same questions (assumed; a reasonable benefit, keep to one line).

Other facilities and services
- [ ] O1 The civil registration service (registrar of the area): couples give notice of intention to marry at least three months before the wedding, supply documents, and arrange the ceremony and venue approval; the registrar then registers the marriage. (One short block; the legal requirement is in the first subsection.)
- [ ] O2 The school: in Home Economics, students study the roles and responsibilities of partners in marriage and the laws relating to marriage; in SPHE, relationships and marriage are discussed. This is the school's role in preparing young people for marriage.
- [ ] O3 Religious bodies provide preparation and may require it before a religious wedding.
- [ ] O4 Information and advice services: Citizens Information, solicitors (for legal questions such as property and wills), financial advice services such as MABS for budgeting (assumed; name them without detail), and the family's own support.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the old notes' bullet that co-educational schools "help students develop healthy interpersonal relationships between boys and girls, which supports later marriage", because it is not a syllabus point or marking-scheme point and rests on an assumption about single-sex schools.
- Do not use: any course length, cost, location or "free of charge" claim for Accord courses, because costs and availability change and have not been checked.
- Do not use: "Relationships Ireland" or any other organisation name not in this package, because the name and services were not confirmed.
- Do not use: "Accord is run by the State", because it is a Catholic Church agency; say it is a marriage and relationship support organisation and that it is Catholic-founded.
- Do not use: the claim that pre-marriage courses are compulsory, because they are only required by some religious bodies (do not say which).
- Pre-marriage course is not marriage counselling: the first prepares couples before the wedding; counselling helps couples already in difficulty (last subsection).
- Do not teach the legal conditions for validity here beyond the registrar's role (first subsection).
- Common mistake: giving only the topics (what is covered) when asked to "discuss how courses help"; explain the benefit of each topic.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the rights and responsibilities of spouses, and customs (cultural variations); counselling, mediation, separation and divorce (when a marriage breaks down).

## 4. What exams ask

- "Discuss how pre-marriage courses can help couples prepare for marriage" (about 10 to 18 marks; 4 benefits at 4 marks each in 2017H, 3 or 4 points in other years). Expected points: they explain the reality and expectations of marriage; they develop communication and conflict-resolution skills; they give advice on finance, housing, family planning and parenting; they let couples discuss roles and expectations; they help couples recognise problems such as addiction and seek help; Accord is the named provider. The best answers say what a topic is and how it helps (for example: finance, so the couple can plan a budget and avoid money disputes).
- "Outline the role of the school and of marriage preparation courses in preparing for marriage" (2014H, 6 marks each): school: Home Economics studies the roles and responsibilities of partners and the laws on marriage; SPHE discusses relationships. Courses: as above.
- "Outline the benefits to a young couple of attending a pre-marriage course" (2008O): same list.
- Only brief mention of the registrar's notice appears in this subsection's questions; the legal requirement is asked under the definition subsection.
- Each benefit above becomes an exam tip: pair every topic with the problem it prevents.
