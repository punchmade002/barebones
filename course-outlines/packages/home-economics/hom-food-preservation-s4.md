# Package: hom-food-preservation-s4 — Dehydration

- **loId:** `hom-food-preservation-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-preservation` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.9
- **Syllabus scope:** principles and methods, including accelerated freeze drying. Also examined: canning and dehydration of fruit and vegetables (2021H). No Higher Level only items marked. Ordinary and Higher Level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. A short subsection: the principle (removing available water), home and commercial methods, accelerated freeze drying (named in the syllabus, so it needs a clear account of the process), the effects on food and storage. Dehydration is examined mainly as a six-mark effects question and as one of the heat or water-removal methods in "name a method" questions. Drying as a home method for a practical account is possible but rare; the notes give enough for it. Sugar and salt "drying" by osmosis belong to chemical preservation; smoking is also in that subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Principle (one block)
- [ ] D1 Dehydration (drying) removes most of the water from food. Micro-organisms need available water to grow and enzymes need it to act, so with most water removed growth and enzyme action stop. Micro-organisms are inactivated, not killed; they can grow again if the food takes in moisture.
- [ ] D2 Fresh fruit and vegetables are mostly water (about 70 to 95 per cent), so dried foods are much lighter and smaller: cheaper to store and transport.
- [ ] D3 Dried food must be stored airtight and dry, because it takes in moisture from the air and then goes mouldy.
- [ ] D4 Blanching vegetables (and sulphuring or dipping fruit in a lemon juice or ascorbic acid solution) before drying inactivates enzymes that would cause browning and off-flavours during storage.

Home drying methods (one block)
- [ ] M1 Sun drying: food spread on trays or racks in strong, dry heat; slow and weather-dependent, exposes food to dust, insects and moisture; poorly suited to the Irish climate; common for fruit in hot countries (raisins, apricots, sun-dried tomatoes).
- [ ] M2 Oven drying: thin pieces on trays at a very low temperature with the door slightly open so moisture escapes; takes several hours.
- [ ] M3 Electric dehydrator: warm air circulated by a fan over trays at a controlled low temperature; even, reliable drying.
- [ ] M4 Air drying: herbs tied in small bunches and hung in a warm, dry, airy place away from direct sunlight; also beans and pulses left to dry on the plant.
- [ ] M5 Preparation for drying: select sound, ripe produce; wash; cut into thin even pieces; blanch vegetables; arrange in a single layer.
- [ ] M6 Tests and storage: dried fruit leathery and pliable with no moist spots; dried vegetables brittle; condition by keeping loosely in a jar for a few days (condensation inside the jar means more drying is needed); store in airtight, moisture-proof containers in a cool, dark, dry place.
- [ ] M7 Foods suited to drying: fruits (apples, apricots, pears, bananas, grapes as raisins), vegetables (onions, carrots, tomatoes, peas, beans), herbs (mint, parsley, thyme), pulses and cereals, meat and fish (dried, salted or smoked: stockfish, jerky).

Commercial drying methods (one block)
- [ ] C1 Spray drying: liquid food (milk, coffee, eggs, soup) sprayed as fine droplets into a stream of hot air; water evaporates in seconds and the powder falls; used for powdered milk, instant coffee, infant formula.
- [ ] C2 Drum (roller) drying: a thin layer of liquid or purée spread on a heated rotating drum, dried and scraped off as flakes; used for instant mashed potato, baby cereals.
- [ ] C3 Tunnel or cabinet drying: trays or a conveyor belt of food pass through a tunnel of hot air; used for fruit and vegetables.
- [ ] C4 Accelerated freeze drying (AFD): food is frozen quickly, placed in a vacuum chamber and gently heated; the ice changes straight from solid to vapour without melting (sublimation) and is drawn off, leaving a dry, porous food that keeps its shape. It is packed airtight, often with nitrogen. Used for coffee, fruit pieces, vegetables in instant soups, camping and ready meals, herbs.
- [ ] C5 Why AFD is valued: very good retention of colour, flavour, shape and nutrients (little heat, no liquid water moving to the surface); rehydrates quickly and fully; very light; long shelf life. Why it is limited: the equipment and energy are expensive, so the food costs more.
- [ ] C6 Diagram cue: a labelled sketch of AFD in three stages (frozen food; vacuum chamber with ice sublimed to vapour; condenser and dry product).

Effects of dehydration on food (one block; examined 2021H, 6 marks)
- [ ] E1 Nutrients: some water-soluble vitamins (C and B group) are lost; vitamins A and E are reduced (heat and oxidation; the 2021H scheme says "may lose B vitamins, A and E"); minerals, fibre, protein and carbohydrate are kept; dried fruit is concentrated in sugar and energy, so portions are small.
- [ ] E2 Colour, texture and flavour: colour darkens or fades; texture becomes leathery, chewy or brittle; flavour becomes concentrated; dried foods shrink and wrinkle.
- [ ] E3 Rehydration: most dried foods are soaked or cooked in water before use; spray-dried and freeze-dried foods rehydrate easily; the soaking water contains vitamins and can be used.
- [ ] E4 Advantages (use for evaluation questions): long shelf life without refrigeration; light and compact; no freezer needed; cheap to transport and store; concentrated flavour. Disadvantages: vitamin loss, changed colour and texture, slow, needs correct storage, some foods do not rehydrate well, high sugar in dried fruit (and dental decay if eaten sticky).

## 3. Traps

- Drying does not kill micro-organisms; it stops their growth. Rehydrated or damp dried food can spoil.
- Dehydration is not the same as freezing; freeze drying uses freezing only as the first step. AFD is "accelerated freeze drying", not "freezing".
- Sublimation (solid to gas) is not evaporation and not melting; say "the ice turns directly into vapour".
- "Dehydration" in the human body (lack of water, from the water and body chapters) is a different meaning; here it means removing water from food.
- Sugar and salt preserve by osmosis, not by removing water by evaporation; salting and smoking belong to chemical preservation. Salted and dried fish should be named only as a link.
- Canning is paired with dehydration in the 2021H question; do not give the canning detail here (it is in heat processing).
- Packaging for dried foods (airtight, moisture-proof) is named only to explain why storage matters; packaging materials belong to the food industry chapter.
- Do not use: "onions and garlic are cured in a warm, dry, ventilated place until their outer skins are papery" as a drying method, because curing for storage is a harvest practice, not dehydration of the edible part.
- Do not use: "Water-soluble vitamins ... Colour darkens ... because of oxidation and browning reactions; blanching helps prevent this" as a statement that blanching stops all darkening in sun-dried fruit; blanching applies to vegetables, and fruit is treated with acid or sulphur dioxide; say vegetables are blanched and fruit is dipped in lemon juice.
- Do not use: "Minerals and fibre are retained, because drying does not destroy them" for sulphured dried fruit without qualification; say minerals and fibre are not destroyed by drying.
- Do not use: sun drying as a normal home method in Ireland, because the climate is too damp and cool; say it is used in hot, dry countries.

## 4. What exams ask

- Effects of dehydration on fruit and vegetables (2021H, 6 marks, with canning): loss of B vitamins, A and E, change in colour, texture and flavour, prevention of microbial and enzyme activity, high sugar in dried fruit. Two or three effects at 3 marks.
- Drying as a method in short questions (2026O matching; 2021O matching; 2007O name a method; 2004O name three methods with an example): "removing moisture from food", examples carrots, tomatoes, onions, fruit and vegetables, meat and fish.
- Higher: describe accelerated freeze drying, or explain why dried foods keep (water unavailable).
- Possibly as a method for a surplus of home-grown produce (2008H scheme lists drying among other methods): name, principle, steps.
- Tip content: always say micro-organisms cannot grow because water is unavailable; for effects, name vitamins by group (C and B lost, A and E reduced).
