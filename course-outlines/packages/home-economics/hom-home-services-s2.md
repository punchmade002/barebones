## 1. Must cover

Emissions from burning fuels [HL]
- [ ] M1 [HL] Burning a carbon fuel (gas, oil, coal, turf, wood) in boilers, stoves and fires gives off gases and particles. What comes out depends on how completely the fuel burns.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Complete combustion (plenty of air, well-adjusted burner): carbon dioxide and water vapour. Example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
- [ ] M3 [HL] Incomplete combustion (too little air, blocked flue, dirty burner): carbon monoxide (CO) and soot or smoke as well. Carbon monoxide is colourless, odourless and poisonous: it attaches to haemoglobin in the blood so less oxygen is carried (headache, dizziness, nausea, death). Prevention: servicing, ventilation, flues clear, a CO alarm.
- [ ] M4 [HL] Carbon dioxide: a greenhouse gas; builds up in the atmosphere, traps heat, causes global warming and climate change (rising temperatures and sea levels, extreme weather, changes to crops and species).
- [ ] M5 [HL] Sulphur dioxide (SO₂): from sulphur in coal, oil and turf; dissolves in rainwater to make acid rain, which damages trees and crops, acidifies lakes and kills fish, corrodes metal and wears limestone buildings and statues; also irritates the lungs.
- [ ] M6 [HL] Nitrogen oxides: formed when the air is heated in a burner or engine; contribute to acid rain and smog and irritate the lungs.
- [ ] M7 [HL] Smoke and particles (from coal, turf, wood and badly adjusted oil burners): smog, dirty buildings, breathing problems (asthma, bronchitis); smokeless fuel and efficient stoves reduce them.
- [ ] M8 [HL] Fuel comparison, per unit of heat: coal and turf give most CO₂ and smoke, oil less, natural gas least; electricity gives no emissions in the house but may do at the power station. The "fuel to pollutant to effect" chain can be asked as a diagram. Water vapour is also given off and can cause condensation (see ventilation).

Identifying energy inefficiencies
- [ ] I1 Why look: heat is the largest energy use in the home; wasted energy costs money and adds to emissions.
- [ ] I2 (assumed) Heat moves from warm to cold by conduction, convection and radiation (explained in the heating subsection); in a house heat is lost through the building fabric (roof, walls, windows, doors, floors) and through air leakage.
- [ ] I3 Heat loss routes to look for: uninsulated attic, walls and floors; single glazing; gaps round doors, windows, letterbox, chimney and floorboards (draughts); thermal bridges such as lintels.
- [ ] I4 Heating inefficiencies: an old or unserviced boiler, no timer, room thermostat or thermostatic radiator valves, rooms heated when not in use, thermostat set too high, open fire losing heat up the chimney, radiators blocked by furniture or curtains.
- [ ] I5 Hot water: uninsulated cylinder and pipes, immersion left on, long hot showers or baths, hot water heated at the wrong time, dripping hot taps.
- [ ] I6 Electricity and appliances: old filament bulbs, lights left on, appliances left on standby, half loads in washing machines and dishwashers, old or low-rated appliances, tumble dryer used instead of line-drying, fridge door left open.
- [ ] I7 Cooking: using more water or heat than needed, no lids, large pan on a small ring (or the reverse), opening the oven door, preheating too long, cooking one dish in a large oven, using an oven where a microwave, toaster or slow cooker would do, boiling too much water in the kettle.
- [ ] I8 The investigation (practical context): walk through the house with a checklist; test for draughts with the back of the hand or a smoke pencil; record room temperatures, how long heating runs, controls available, how hot water is heated, cooking habits, standby appliances; compare meter readings or fuel use over a set time; list the faults, rank them by how much energy each wastes, and recommend. Safety: do not use a naked flame to test for gas leaks.

Strategies to improve efficiency and cut emissions
- [ ] S1 Insulation: attic, cavity or solid walls, floors; double or triple glazing; draught-proofing doors, windows, floorboards, letterbox, chimney; lined curtains. Methods are in the insulation subsection.
- [ ] S2 Heating: a programmer or timer, room thermostat, thermostatic radiator valves, a cylinder thermostat, zoning; an efficient condensing boiler serviced yearly; a heat pump; closing doors; a stove in place of an open fire; background heating where it suits.
- [ ] S3 Hot water: insulate (lag) the cylinder and pipes, time the immersion, shower rather than bath, fix dripping taps, solar thermal panels, set cylinder thermostat correctly.
- [ ] S4 Lighting and appliances: LED lamps, switch off unused lights, sensors and timers, appliances with a high energy-label rating, full loads, eco programmes, switching off standby, line-drying clothes. Lighting detail is in the lighting subsection.
- [ ] S5 Cooking: lids on pots, pan matched to ring, boil only the water needed, use a microwave, pressure cooker or slow cooker, fill the oven, avoid opening the oven door, cook several items together.
- [ ] S6 Renewable and low-carbon energy: solar thermal and PV, wood-burning stove with controlled air supply, heat pump, switching from coal or oil to gas or smokeless fuel, choosing a renewable electricity supplier.
- [ ] S7 Reducing emissions specifically: burn less fuel; change from coal to a cleaner fuel; service boilers so combustion stays complete; smokeless fuel; do not burn rubbish; fit a CO alarm; keep flues clear.
- [ ] S8 Building Energy Rating (BER): a certificate that rates a dwelling's energy performance on a scale from most efficient to least efficient, like the energy label on appliances; carried out by a registered assessor; shows where to improve; a BER is required when a home is sold or rented and must appear in adverts; SEAI runs the system. See Traps for the scale.
- [ ] S9 Designing an energy-efficient house (practical recommendation): compact shape and few corners (less outside wall), south-facing living rooms with large windows (passive solar gain), small north windows, high levels of insulation in attic, walls and floor, airtight construction with controlled ventilation, double or triple glazing, a porch or lobby, zoned heating with controls, an efficient or renewable heating system, rainwater use, LED and sensor lighting, orientation and shelter from wind.
- [ ] S10 Balance: improvements are ranked by cost and effect (draught-proofing and attic insulation are cheap and effective; a heat pump or external wall insulation costs much more); an airtight house needs controlled ventilation; SEAI grants help but amounts are not given here.

## 3. Traps

- Combustion products: do not say burning gas gives off only "carbon dioxide"; water vapour is also made, and carbon monoxide appears if combustion is incomplete.
- Carbon dioxide causes global warming; sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides cause acid rain; do not swap them.
- Poor ventilation does not make carbon monoxide; it helps incomplete combustion by starving the fire of air. Ventilation is in its own subsection.
- Burning wood: renewable, but still releases carbon dioxide and, if the stove is poor, smoke.
- Efficiency versus conservation: efficiency is getting the same result from less energy (insulation, LED); conservation is using less (switch off, shorter showers). Give examples of both.
- The principles of insulation, ventilation, thermostats, lighting and electricity safety belong to their own subsections; here give the strategy and one reason.
- Fuel types and the sustainability of sources belong to the energy supplies subsection.
- Do not use: "ozone" or "ozone layer damage" as an effect of burning fuels in the home (2004 scheme lists "ozone"), because ozone-layer loss comes from CFC gases (old fridges), not combustion; low-level ozone as a smog component is beyond the notes.
- Do not use: "acid rain with a pH of 4.1" (2018 scheme figure) or any other pH, because it is an unverified, variable figure.
- Do not use: "wood is carbon neutral" as a statement, because it depends on replanting and wood burning still emits CO₂ and particles.
- Do not use: "BER scale A1 to G" or "triple A" as the only scale, because the scale was simplified on 24 May 2026 (A0, A to G); say "A (most efficient) to G (least efficient)" and, if the bands are named, give the new ones.
- Do not use: "choose A+++ appliances", because the EU label no longer has plus grades; say "the highest rating on the energy label".
- Do not use: any percentage share of heat lost through roof, walls, windows or floor, because the share varies with the house and the figures are unsourced.
- Do not use: "choose a shower over a bath" as a flat rule without noting it saves water only for a short shower (a long or power shower can use as much), because the claim is conditional.

## 4. What exams ask

- Emissions: give an account of emissions from burning fuels and the environmental effects (Higher, 15 marks, three points at 5 marks); describe the impact of burning fossil fuels with one emission, effects and one strategy (2023H); methods of reducing harmful emissions (2007H); effects of fuel emissions: greenhouse effect and climate change, acid rain, smog (2004H). Expect gas name, source fuel, effect, and a way to cut it.
- Identify three inefficient uses of energy (or electricity) in the home and assess or recommend a strategy for each (2024H, 2008H). Expect pairs such as heat loss and insulation, unregulated heating and thermostats and timers, hot water and lagging and timers, standby and switching off, bulbs and LEDs.
- Strategies to improve energy efficiency (Ordinary, 15 marks, three strategies): insulation, boiler servicing, zoned heating, LED, lagging, immersion timers, showers, standby, appliance rating (2023O); three ways to improve energy efficiency in a kitchen (timers, thermostats, efficient appliances, full loads, 2016O); three actions when building a house (insulation, solar, geothermal, lagging, zoning, thermostats and timers, 2005O); two ways to conserve energy (2009O, 2012O).
- Write an informative note on the BER (2017H, 2014H): what it is, who assesses it, the scale, what is assessed (windows, walls, insulation, heating), compulsory for sale or rent, advises on improvements.
- Influence of energy efficiency on house design (2019O, 2021H): glazing, insulation, zoning, renewables, thermostats, timers, appliances.
- Design recommendations for an energy-efficient house and a small investigative study of the home (practical context).
