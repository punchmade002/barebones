# Package: hom-unemployment-poverty-s2 — Causes of unemployment

- **loId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty` · **syllabusRef:** 6.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "Causes of unemployment" [6.5] **[HL]**: seasonal, geographical, technical, difficulty in adapting to the changing requirements of industry, level of demand for products and services, residual. The whole list is Higher Level only as the outline gives it.
- **Also examined:** groups who find it hard to get work (2024H, 2007H) (this item carries no separate [HL] mark in the outline); causes asked with named headings (2022H: changing requirements of industry, geographical location, level of consumer demand; 2012H: geographical location, the global economy, level of demand; 2009H "analyse the causes", where the scheme also names recession, high wages reducing competitiveness, and residual unemployment).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Marking schemes 2009H, 2012H, 2022H and the group questions were used. The current notes are sound on the six causes; one claim about refugees' right to work is wrong and a few "belongs elsewhere" items are listed in Traps.

## 1. Must cover

Seasonal and geographical causes [HL] (one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Seasonal unemployment: work that depends on the time of year, so people are laid off in the off-season and rehired later. Examples: tourism and hospitality, farming and fishing, construction (weather). It is predictable and temporary.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Geographical unemployment: jobs are concentrated in some regions (usually the larger cities and their commuter areas) so people in other areas find little local work. Moving costs money and means leaving family, so people stay out of work.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Unemployment black spots: areas that lost their main employer (for example a factory closure) and keep high unemployment for years. Other firms and shops that depended on the workers' spending close too, which adds job losses.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Rural depopulation: the young and skilled move away in search of work; shops, schools, post offices and services close, so fewer jobs remain locally. This is a cause and an effect, which links to the neighbouring effects subsection.

Technical change and changing industry requirements [HL] (one block)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Technical (technological) unemployment: machines, automation, computers and robots replace workers. Examples: automated production lines and robots in manufacturing, self-service checkouts, online and self-service banking replacing bank staff.
- [ ] T2 [HL] Difficulty adapting to the changing requirements of industry: the economy moves from traditional industries (farming, fishing, manufacturing) to services, information technology and pharma/science. Workers with old skills struggle to retrain, particularly older workers and those with few qualifications. Many jobs now ask for qualifications.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Globalisation (the global economy): firms may move production to countries with lower labour costs, and a world recession cuts demand for Irish exports and so jobs. Ireland is an open economy that depends on world trade and foreign investment (the foreign-investment initiatives are a statutory response and belong to another subsection).

Level of demand for products and services [HL] (one block)
- [ ] D1 [HL] Firms hire to meet demand. When demand falls, they cut output and staff. In a recession consumers spend less, so firms reduce production and make workers redundant; unemployment then lowers incomes and spending further (a downward spiral).
- [ ] D2 [HL] Demand for Irish goods and services also falls when cheaper imports are bought instead, when consumers shop online from abroad, or when people take cheaper foreign holidays instead of holidaying at home.
- [ ] D3 [HL] High wage and other costs can make Irish firms uncompetitive against rivals abroad, so they lose sales and jobs (the 2009H scheme includes high wages reducing competitiveness).
- [ ] D4 [HL] National and international recession as the main cause of the post-2008 rise; economic growth reverses it (this links to the extent of unemployment in a neighbouring subsection).

Residual unemployment [HL] (one block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] Residual unemployment: the "hard core" who remain out of work even when the economy is growing and vacancies exist. Causes: poor skills or education, long time out of work, ill health or disability, age, discrimination, living far from jobs. It is why growth alone does not bring unemployment to zero.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Long-term unemployed people make up much of the residual group; time out of work makes employers less willing to hire them.

Groups who find it hard to get work (one block; not marked [HL] in the outline)
- [ ] G1 Examiners ask the student to name two or three groups and give a reason for each group's high unemployment. Groups the schemes accept: Travellers and ethnic minorities (discrimination, lower qualifications); migrants and asylum seekers (language, recognition of qualifications, restrictions on the right to work for people still waiting for a decision); people with disabilities or long-term illness (employers' doubts, access and transport barriers); homeless people (no address, clothing or hygiene facilities); people with criminal records (employers' mistrust); early school leavers (lack of qualifications and literacy); the long-term unemployed (out-of-date skills); residents of unemployment black spots (few local jobs).
- [ ] G2 Reasons the schemes use: discrimination, employers' ignorance of the person's abilities, lack of skills or qualifications, mistrust, language barriers, no permission to work, and transport or caring barriers. Pair each group with a reason that really fits it.

## 3. Traps

- Seasonal is predictable and short; residual persists in a boom; technical is caused by machines, not by falling demand; keep the three apart. A student who writes "recession" as a technical cause loses the point.
- Frictional unemployment is not one of the six syllabus causes; it was introduced under defining unemployment. Do not add it here as a seventh cause.
- The effects of unemployment (crime, depression, loss of tax) belong to the effects subsection; the statutory responses (SOLAS, Enterprise Ireland, IDA Ireland) belong to the responses subsection. This subsection only mentions rural depopulation as a cause and does not describe the schemes.
- Early school leaving and low qualifications are a cause of poverty too; the poverty chapter's causes subsection covers that. Here they appear only as a group who find it hard to get work.
- Do not use: "refugees ... may not have permission to work or a valid work permit" (current notes), because a person granted refugee status has the right to work; only asylum seekers still waiting on a decision face restrictions, and the rules and waiting times have changed. Say "asylum seekers may face restrictions on the right to work while their application is decided; refugees and migrants may face language barriers and non-recognition of foreign qualifications".
- Do not use: any claim that automation "has removed X jobs" or any firm name or closure date as a checked example, because none is checked.
- Do not use: "Travellers' unemployment rate is ...", or any group rate, because rates change and none is checked.
- Do not use: "high wages are the cause of unemployment" as a flat statement, because the scheme treats high wage costs as one factor reducing competitiveness, not the main cause; say "can".

## 4. What exams ask

- Analyse or discuss the causes of unemployment in Ireland (Higher Level, 20-24 marks, four to five points at 5 or 6 marks): the strongest answers give four distinct causes from the six, each with a one-line explanation and an Irish illustration (automation in manufacturing, recession cutting construction work, cheaper foreign holidays hurting tourism). The 2009H scheme also credits recession and high wages.
- Discuss named causes (2022H: changing requirements of industry, geographical location and level of consumer demand; 2012H: geographical location, the global economy and level of demand): one developed point per heading plus one more.
- Identify two or three groups who find it hard to get work and give a reason for each (18 marks): a group plus a fitting reason for every point.
- Define seasonal, technical or residual unemployment in a few words.
- Tips: name the cause, explain how it removes jobs, then give an Irish example; use the heading words the question gives; do not list causes without explanation.
