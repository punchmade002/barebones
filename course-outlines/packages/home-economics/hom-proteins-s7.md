# Package: hom-proteins-s7 — Biological value

- **loId:** `hom-proteins-s7` · **chapterId:** `hom-proteins` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.2
- **Syllabus scope:** complete and incomplete protein; complementation (supplementary value). Settings: planning and preparing simple meals that illustrate protein complementation. Also examined: high and low biological value foods (2017O, 2007O, 2021O, 2012O, 2024O); explain high biological value protein (2006H); low-cost protein foods and value for money and safety when buying protein foods (2026H, 2014H, 2013O, 2006H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. The buying-protein material is examined repeatedly as a 18–20 mark discussion, so it is included as its own block, but only as purchasing advice for protein foods. General shopping, budgeting and labelling law belong to the consumer chapters.

## 1. Must cover

High and low biological value
- [ ] B1 Biological value (BV) measures how well a protein food supplies the essential amino acids the body needs, in the right proportions, for growth and repair.
- [ ] B2 High biological value (HBV, complete) protein contains all the essential amino acids in the right amounts. Sources are mainly animal foods: meat, fish, poultry, eggs, milk, cheese, yoghurt. Soya beans (and soya products) are the main plant exception.
- [ ] B3 Low biological value (LBV, incomplete) protein lacks, or is low in, one or more essential amino acids. Sources are mainly plant foods: cereals (wheat, rice, maize), pulses (peas, beans, lentils), nuts, seeds. Peas, beans and lentils are LBV (a frequent true/false).
- [ ] B4 Examples a student can sort: eggs, fish, meat, milk = HBV; peas, rice, wheat, maize = LBV.

Complementation (supplementary value)
- [ ] C1 Complementation is the ability of one protein food to make good the deficiency of another: when two LBV foods lacking different essential amino acids are eaten together (in the same meal or the same day), together they supply all the essential amino acids.
- [ ] C2 Worked example: bread is low in lysine but supplies methionine; beans supply lysine but are low in methionine; beans on toast gives a complete protein.
- [ ] C3 Other pairs: hummus with pitta bread, dhal with chapatti, rice with lentils, lentil soup with wholemeal bread, bolognese with beans.
- [ ] C4 Adding a small amount of an HBV food (milk, cheese, egg) to a cereal or pulse also raises its value, e.g. macaroni cheese, rice pudding, cereal with milk.
- [ ] C5 Importance: vegetarians and vegans can meet protein needs from plant foods; cheaper meals; pulses stretch meat (meat extenders).
- [ ] C6 Meal-planning activity: plan a simple meal showing complementation, and say which two foods complement each other and why.

Value for money when buying protein foods
- [ ] V1 Compare unit price (per kg or per 100 g) and the usable portion, since bone, fat and gristle are waste.
- [ ] V2 Cheaper cuts of meat are as nutritious as dear cuts and suit stewing and braising; fish is cheaper in season (e.g. mackerel); eggs, milk, cheese, tinned fish, pulses, TVP, tofu and mycoprotein are low-cost protein foods.
- [ ] V3 Own-brand products, special offers and bulk buying save money if the food is used in time or frozen in portions; plan meals and use a shopping list; use pulses or TVP to extend mince.
- [ ] V4 Healthy eating link: low-cost protein foods fit the Food Pyramid's shelf of meat, poultry, fish, eggs, beans and nuts (two servings a day).

Buying protein foods safely
- [ ] S1 Buy from a clean, reputable retailer; check use-by date, undamaged sealed packaging, non-bulging cans, labels and traceability/quality assurance information.
- [ ] S2 Chilled protein foods at or below 5 °C, bought last and carried in a cool bag; frozen foods solid, stored at −18 °C; avoid thawed and refrozen packs.
- [ ] S3 At home: refrigerate quickly, keep raw and cooked foods apart, defrost in the fridge, cook thoroughly.
- [ ] S4 Quality signs: fresh fish has firm flesh, bright eyes, red gills; meat has fresh colour and smell.

## 3. Traps

- HBV is not "better for everyone": plant proteins with complementation give a complete mix. Do not say plant foods cannot supply protein.
- Soya is a plant food with HBV protein; peas, beans and lentils are LBV on their own.
- Complementation does not require eating foods at the exact same moment; the same meal is the exam answer.
- Biological value strictly measures how much of the absorbed protein the body uses; the Irish schemes define it by essential amino acid content. Use the schemes' wording.
- Essential amino acids themselves belong to the composition subsection; deamination and protein as energy to the energy subsection; novel protein foods (tofu, TVP, Quorn) to the alternative protein subsection; food safety law and labelling to the food safety chapters.
- Do not use: any specific price, price per kilogram or "saves X%", because prices change.
- Do not use: "a protein's BV is a number such as 100 for egg", because no figure has been checked and none appears in the schemes.

## 4. What exams ask

- Tick HBV or LBV for named foods (2017O: eggs, fish HBV; peas LBV) and true/false: "meat contains high biological value protein" (2021O true), "eggs are low biological value" (2024O false); name three HBV and three LBV sources (2007O).
- Explain high biological value protein (2006H): contains all essential amino acids, generally from animal sources.
- Explain supplementary/complementary value with an example (2014H, 8 marks, two points at 4): foods lacking an amino acid combined to give a complete set, e.g. beans on toast, hummus with pitta, dhal with chapatti; the bread/beans methionine and lysine table is acceptable.
- Recommend low-cost protein foods and say how each fits a healthy eating plan (2014H, 9 marks): cheaper cuts, offers, pulses, hummus, Quorn, tofu, TVP, dairy, eggs, nuts, quinoa; used as meat substitutes or extenders in main meals, salads or snacks.
- Strategies or four ways to cut the protein food bill and ensure safety (2026H 20 marks, 2013O 20 marks, 2006H 18 marks).
- Exam tips to build: "Name two foods that complement and say what each lacks"; "Give both economy and safety points when asked for six factors"; "HBV = all essential amino acids".
