# Package: hom-work-leisure-s6 — Child care

- **loId:** `hom-work-leisure-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-work-leisure` · **syllabusRef:** 6.3.2
- **Syllabus scope:** "family requirements for child care and the options available; [HL] evaluation of two types of child care option." Settings: investigating the child care options in the area. Also asked on past papers: benefits of pre-school education (2005H); naming and evaluating or analysing one option (2023H, 2025H). Ordinary Level covers requirements and the options; evaluating two options is Higher Level only [HL], and the outline marks only that part.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23, with the regulation of early years services checked against Tusla, gov.ie and the statute book. Neighbours: role overload and the effects of dual-earner families belong to the previous subsection; pre-school education as part of the education system (special needs, ECCE in the education chapter) is covered only as a child care option here; the Child Care Act 1991 child protection and welfare provisions belong to the family law chapter.

## 1. Must cover

Family requirements for child care (examiners ask for a list or a summary)
- [ ] Q1 Working pattern: both parents or a lone parent in paid work, hours worked, shift work or irregular hours.
- [ ] Q2 Availability of relatives or friends (grandparents) who could mind the child.
- [ ] Q3 Cost: what the family can afford; any State subsidy for which the family qualifies (see Checked facts).
- [ ] Q4 After-school and holiday cover: school finishes before work does, school holidays are long.
- [ ] Q5 Availability and convenience: place free, open when needed, practical to get to.
- [ ] Q6 Hours and flexibility: early starts, late finishes, changing shifts.
- [ ] Q7 Proximity to home, school or work.
- [ ] Q8 The child's age and any special needs or health needs.
- [ ] Q9 Reliability: what happens if the carer is ill or the service closes.
- [ ] Q10 Quality: safety and hygiene, staff qualifications, the activities provided, the ratio of staff to children, and parents' trust and the child's happiness.

Child care options (Ordinary and Higher Level: describe at least three; name and give the main features of each)
- [ ] T1 Crèche or day nursery (full day care): a centre with trained staff caring for groups of children in a premises used for that purpose, usually open all working day; structured routines and play.
- [ ] T2 Childminder: a person who minds a small number of children in the minder's own home; home setting, personal attention, often flexible hours.
- [ ] T3 Nanny: carer employed by the family, usually in the family home, caring for the children of one family; the family is the employer.
- [ ] T4 Au pair: a young person, often from abroad, living with the host family and helping with children and light household duties in exchange for board and pocket money; not a trained carer. Limited hours and suitable for older children or as support.
- [ ] T5 Relative or friend: informal, usually low cost or free, familiar and trusted; depends on the helper's health and availability.
- [ ] T6 After-school group (and holiday or summer camps): collects children from school and cares for them until parents finish work; may offer homework help and activities.
- [ ] T7 Playschool or playgroup (sessional): pre-school sessions of a few hours, mainly play-based, for children under school age.
- [ ] T8 Montessori school: pre-school method that encourages self-directed learning through structured materials, following the educational ideas of Maria Montessori.
- [ ] T9 Naíonra: Irish-language pre-school where children learn through Irish by play.
- [ ] T10 Pre-school under the national free pre-school scheme (ECCE): State-funded pre-school hours in the year or years before primary school in registered services; the family does not pay for the funded sessions. Mention in one line; no ages or hours.
- [ ] T11 Employer or workplace crèche: care provided at or near the workplace by the employer.

Regulation and quality (one block)
- [ ] G1 Early years services (crèches, playschools, Montessori, naíonraí, after-school) must be registered with Tusla, the Child and Family Agency, and are inspected by Tusla's early years inspectorate under the Child Care Act 1991 (Early Years Services) Regulations 2016. This is why parents should check registration and inspection reports.
- [ ] G2 Childminders: new regulation under the Child Care (Amendment) Act 2024 and the Child Care Act 1991 (Early Years Services) (Childminding Services) Regulations 2024 brings childminders into Tusla registration. Registration opened on 30 September 2024 and is currently voluntary; mandatory registration is due after a transition period, but the deadline is being reviewed, so do not state an end date. Registered childminders can take part in the National Childcare Scheme. Parents should ask whether the childminder is registered, has training, Garda vetting, references, insurance and emergency cover.
- [ ] G3 The National Childcare Scheme (NCS) is the State's scheme that subsidises the cost of registered child care according to the family's circumstances. Say this in one line; amounts and rates are not given.

Benefits of pre-school education (2005H)
- [ ] P1 Children become more socially aware through play with other children, form relationships outside the home, learn to share and take turns, become more independent and confident.
- [ ] P2 Intellectual development: language, number, listening, problem-solving, creativity; readiness for primary school.
- [ ] P3 Physical development: active play, coordination, fine motor skills; emotional development: separating from parents, self-esteem.
- [ ] P4 Also helps working parents (child cared for in a safe, structured setting).

Evaluating two child care options [HL]
- [ ] V1 Choose two (typically a crèche and a childminder, as in 2019H; any two work). Set criteria from the family's requirements: cost, hours and flexibility, location, safety and hygiene, staff qualifications and ratio, reliability and cover, activities and learning, suitability for the child's age and needs.
- [ ] V2 Crèche: advantages: fixed reliable hours, trained staff, staff cover when one is absent, structured activities, group play, inspected and registered. Disadvantages: fixed hours, less flexible for shift workers, cost, group exposure to infections, less individual attention, child may be unsettled by a large group, travel at set times.
- [ ] V3 Childminder: advantages: home setting, small number of children, individual attention, flexible hours, may collect from school, suited to babies and young children, often cheaper for several children. Disadvantages: one adult so illness or holidays leave no cover, activities may be less planned, quality depends on the minder (check registration, training, vetting, insurance), fewer facilities.
- [ ] V4 Other pairs: au pair (low cost, flexible in the home, but untrained and short-term) against after-school group (structured, registered, but only after school); playschool or Montessori or naíonra (educational and sociable, but only a few hours each day, so another arrangement is needed).
- [ ] V5 Method for a "recommend and analyse" question (2023H, 2025H): name the option; say which family requirements it meets and which it does not; give a judgement for a stated family.

Settings
- [ ] S1 Investigation: research the options in the area (type, cost, hours, registration, activities); compare in a table.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the Child Care (Pre-School Services) Regulations 1996 or 2006 or the HSE as the inspecting body, because the 2016 Regulations replaced them and Tusla is the registering and inspecting body.
- Do not use: the Community Childcare Subvention scheme or the Childcare Support Scheme as the current subsidy, because the National Childcare Scheme replaced them in 2019.
- Do not use any weekly or hourly fee, subsidy rate or ratio (for example child-to-adult ratios by age), because they are changing figures.
- Do not use: "a childminder minds children in the minder's own home, and parents need only check insurance" as complete advice, because registration with Tusla is now part of what parents should check (see G2). The old note's checks of experience, training, safety and insurance remain correct and should stay.
- A nanny is employed by the family in the family's home; a childminder is self-employed in the minder's home; an au pair is not a trained nanny and is not an ordinary employee. Do not blur them.
- Do not describe child care as only for working parents; a pre-school is also educational.
- Do not give long lists of options with no evaluation at Higher Level; do give a balanced pair of advantages and disadvantages for each of the two chosen.
- Pre-school education as a policy area (special needs, free year in the education chapter) is not covered beyond one line.
- Role overload and role conflict belong to the previous subsection.
- Do not use "the Child Care Act 1991" as the source of the child care subsidy; it is the registration and child protection law.

## 4. What exams ask

- Summarise the principal factors influencing a family's child care requirements (2019H, 12 marks): the ten requirements, a short phrase for each.
- Name and evaluate or analyse one child care option for working parents (2023H, 2025H): name the option, then cost, hours and flexibility, location, safety and hygiene, qualifications and ratio, reliability, activities, suits the child's age and needs. Always give strengths and weaknesses and say which family it suits.
- Evaluate two options (2019H, 18 marks): a crèche and a childminder compared by the same criteria, with one overall conclusion.
- Benefits of pre-school education (2005H): social, intellectual, physical, independence and confidence.
- Possible Ordinary Level: name and describe two types of child care; why a family may choose a childminder or a crèche.
