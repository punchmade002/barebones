# Package: hom-family-caring-s4 — Family members with special needs

- **loId:** `hom-family-caring-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-caring` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.6
- **Syllabus scope:** **[HL]** the response of the family unit to a member with special physical, mental or emotional needs, to include statutory and voluntary services available. Settings: a short investigative study of how a family might respond. Also examined: challenges for the family (2026H, 2021H, 2016H, 2007H); named voluntary organisations, e.g. the Rehab Group (2026H, 2007H). The whole subsection is Higher Level only; every block heading ends "(Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** Sonnet-class model, 2026-11-23. Disability education policy and special education are only referred to here; adult care of older people is the previous subsection; the Child Care Act and family law are in other chapters.

## 1. Must cover

Meaning of special needs (one block)
- [ ] D1 [HL] Special needs are physical, mental or emotional needs that need support beyond the usual family routine. Give examples across the three kinds: physical (mobility, sensory impairment, long-term illness), mental (intellectual disability, learning disability, autism, mental health difficulty), emotional (behavioural or emotional difficulty). Can be from birth or acquired through accident, illness or age. Use respectful language.

How the family responds (one block; Also examined 2007H, 2021H, 2016H)
- [ ] R1 [HL] Care at home: daily personal care, supervision, transport; reorganising work and routines.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Adapting the home: ramps, wider doorways, grab rails, downstairs bedroom, equipment.
- [ ] R3 [HL] Encouraging independence and helping the member integrate: self-care, communication, mobility, decisions, with safe limits.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Working with professionals and organisations: doctors, therapists, teachers, social workers; planning supports together.
- [ ] R5 [HL] Claiming all available assistance and grants for equipment, adaptations and income support.
- [ ] R6 [HL] Emotional support, protecting dignity and self-worth; treating the member as a full family member; giving siblings attention too.
- [ ] R7 [HL] Advocacy and lobbying for better services and rights.

Difficulties and challenges for the family (one block; Also examined 2026H, 2021H, 2016H, 2007H)
- [ ] C1 [HL] Limited employment and educational opportunities for the member.
- [ ] C2 [HL] Social isolation and prejudice towards the member and the family.
- [ ] C3 [HL] Shortage of help and respite, leaving carers exhausted; stress, fatigue, fear for the future (who will care when parents cannot).
- [ ] C4 [HL] Limited mobility; inaccessible transport and buildings.
- [ ] C5 [HL] Inadequate State financial support, loss of income when a parent gives up work, extra costs of care, equipment and transport.
- [ ] C6 [HL] Delays or shortages in medical, therapy and educational services and assessment.
- [ ] C7 [HL] Siblings given added duties and less parental attention; strain on relationships; challenging behaviour in some conditions.
- [ ] C8 [HL] Explain each challenge with its effect, since marking schemes credit development.

Statutory services (one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Statutory services are provided or funded by the State. Health, disability and social care services through the HSE (therapies such as physiotherapy, speech and language and occupational therapy; public health nurses; respite and day services; assessment of need).
- [ ] S2 [HL] Disability Act 2005: gives the right to an assessment of health and education needs and to a service statement; families can request this assessment.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Education: special educational needs supports in schools, with resource teaching and special needs assistants, coordinated by the National Council for Special Education (NCSE), under the Education for All Children with Special Educational Needs Act 2004 (EPSEN Act).
- [ ] S4 [HL] Social welfare supports: Domiciliary Care Allowance for a child with a severe disability, Disability Allowance for adults, Carer's Allowance and the Carer's Support Grant (a yearly payment to carers that may fund respite); no amounts or means-test details; medical card for those who qualify; housing adaptation grants through the local authority.

Voluntary services (one block; Also examined 2026H, 2007H)
- [ ] V1 [HL] Voluntary organisations are independent, non-profit bodies funded partly by the State and by fundraising and donations, giving services, support and advocacy alongside the State: respite, support groups, training, advocacy, information.
- [ ] V2 [HL] The Rehab Group: a non-profit organisation providing education, training, employment and social care to promote equality for people with disabilities. Its training arm is the National Learning Network; Rehab Enterprises provides employment. Name those and the purposes only.
- [ ] V3 [HL] Other organisations the notes may name, one line each: Enable Ireland (services for children and adults with physical and neurological disability), Irish Wheelchair Association (support and housing and transport for wheelchair users), Down Syndrome Ireland, Inclusion Ireland (advocacy for people with intellectual disability), AsIAm (autism).
- [ ] V4 [HL] Why voluntary services matter: they fill gaps in State provision, offer peer support, and campaign for rights.
- [ ] V5 [HL] Investigation (Settings): a short case study of a family with a member with a physical disability and how they use both kinds of service; method only, nothing invented as fact.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "The Department of Health sets national policy for the health and disability services delivered by the HSE" as the policy owner of disability, because government departments' disability responsibilities have been reorganised more than once and the current department was not confirmed; say "the Government and the HSE" or just "the HSE".
- Do not use: "Rehab Group training programmes include certified courses in catering and computing", because the specific courses were not confirmed in search; say the National Learning Network offers training and qualifications for people with disabilities.
- Do not use: the name RehabCare or "Rehab Lotto" or any Rehab fundraising detail beyond "recycling and fundraising", because the Rehab Group's structure and fundraising have changed; the sub-brands were not confirmed.
- Do not use: any amounts, pay rates or eligibility ages for allowances and grants; they change.
- Mark every block "(Higher Level only)"; there is no Ordinary Level content in this subsection, so do not add an Ordinary block.
- Statutory = State; voluntary = independent non-profit; do not call a charity statutory. Do not say voluntary bodies receive no State funding.
- Do not describe diagnoses or medical detail; the syllabus is about the family's response.
- Care of older people, independence and nursing homes are the previous subsection; special education law and the Child Care Act belong to other chapters; mention in a phrase only.
- Do not assume the special-needs member is a child: the exam asks about "a member" and the 2016H version asked about parents of a child, so cover both.

## 4. What exams ask

- Identify and discuss the difficulties or challenges the family of a member with special needs may face (2007H, 2016H, 2021H, 2026H): employment loss, isolation, extra cost, fatigue, siblings, access, service delays, challenging behaviour, each with an effect.
- Discuss how a family might respond (2007H): home care, adapting the home, independence, professionals and organisations, claiming grants, emotional and financial support, lobbying.
- Name and outline the role of a voluntary organisation such as the Rehab Group (2007H, 2026H): who it serves, services, funding, aim of equality.
- Statutory versus voluntary services: examples of each and what each offers.
- Tips: for "challenges" give the problem and then its consequence; for "voluntary organisation" name it first, then its services and purpose.
