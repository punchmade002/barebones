# Package: hom-textiles-fibres-s5 — Fabric construction, finishes, colour, design and testing

- **loId:** `hom-textiles-fibres-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-textiles-fibres` · **syllabusRef:** 5.2 (Elective 2)
- **Syllabus scope:** fabric construction techniques (two; [HL] three); finishes (two; [HL] three); colour application (two methods); design application (two methods); [HL] fabric performance testing (two tests). Settings: simple scientific tests on fabrics for performance characteristics. Also examined: knitted fabric (2019H, 2009H, 2005H); functional finishes (2014H, 2011O, 2010H, 2007O, 2005H, 2018O, 2023O, 2025O); colour and design application (2026O, 2016H, 2010O, 2009O, 2006H, 2004O); performance tests (2025H, 2008H, 2005H); a construction technique for a named fabric (2007H, 2004O, 2009O, 2016O, 2021O, 2022O).

## 1. Must cover

Weaving (one block, with a diagram)
- [ ] W1 Woven fabric: two sets of yarn interlaced at right angles on a loom. Warp = lengthwise yarns held under tension; weft = crosswise yarns passed over and under the warp.
- [ ] W2 Selvedge: the firm edge where the weft turns back; it stops fraying.
- [ ] W3 Plain weave: weft goes over one, under one warp yarn, alternately; simple, firm, hard wearing (calico, cotton shirting).
- [ ] W4 Twill weave: weft goes over two or more warps then under, each row stepping across to form a diagonal line; drapes well and is hard wearing (denim).
- [ ] W5 Satin weave: long floats of yarn on the surface give a smooth, lustrous face; less durable and snags.
- [ ] W6 Diagram: interlacing grids for plain, twill and satin weaves (the warp and weft over or under at each crossing).

Knitting (one block; Higher 2019 and 2005 ask a 9-mark explanation)
- [ ] K1 Knitted fabric: made from a series of interlocking loops of yarn, by hand with needles or by machine.
- [ ] K2 Weft knitting: loops run across the fabric, usually from one yarn; warp knitting: machine-made, the yarn zigzags along the length.
- [ ] K3 Looping methods: plain (jersey), rib, purl.
- [ ] K4 Properties: stretchy and flexible (depends on the yarn and the looping), warm because loops trap air, drapes well, comfortable, can be made without seams or in shaped pieces; may snag, sag or shrink.
- [ ] K5 Uses: jumpers, hats, scarves, socks, T-shirts, blankets, throws.

Third technique: felting and non-woven (one block, [HL] only)
- [ ] F1 [HL] Felt: wool fibres are matted together by heat, moisture and pressure so the scales lock; no yarn is needed.
- [ ] F2 [HL] Bonded non-woven fabric: a web of fibres is held together by adhesive, heat or needle punching. Uses: interlinings, felt hats, insulation.
- [ ] F3 [HL] Crochet, lace and macramé are other constructions that appear in marking schemes; one line.

Finishes (one block)
- [ ] I1 Why finishes are used: to change appearance, handle or performance; prevent creasing and shrinkage; add water repellence or stain resistance; increase durability and safety; improve wear; prevent loss of colour.
- [ ] I2 Crease-resistant finish: resin cross-links cellulose fibres (cotton, linen) so they resist wrinkling; less ironing.
- [ ] I3 Water-repellent / waterproof finish: makes fabric repel water (raincoats, tablecloths).
- [ ] I4 Stain-resistant finish: spills sit on the surface and wipe off (jackets, upholstery, tablecloths).
- [ ] I5 Anti-static finish: reduces static cling in synthetic fabric (dresses, linings); fabric conditioner does this at home.
- [ ] I6 Flame-retardant finish: reduces flammability (see the safety subsection; nightwear).
- [ ] I7 Mercerising: cotton treated with caustic soda; stronger, lustrous, more absorbent (bed sheets, jeans).
- [ ] I8 [HL] Third finish: calendering (heated rollers give a smooth, glossy or embossed surface) or a shrink-resistant finish.

Colour application (one block, with a diagram)
- [ ] D1 Dyeing: colour is applied to fibre, yarn or fabric. Yarn dyeing gives stripes, checks and tweeds; piece dyeing colours the made fabric one colour.
- [ ] D2 Match dye to fibre: reactive dyes for cotton and linen; acid dyes for wool, silk and nylon; disperse dyes for polyester and acetate.
- [ ] D3 Printing: screen printing (stencil on a mesh screen blocks areas, ink pushed through with a squeegee; one screen per colour); roller printing (engraved rollers, one per colour, fast continuous, long runs); stencilling (a cut stencil and sponge, simple, suits children's clothing); transfer printing (design printed on paper, heat transfers to polyester).
- [ ] D4 Resist methods: tie-dye (tying stops dye reaching areas) and batik (wax stops dye).
- [ ] D5 Diagram: a screen-printing stage (screen, stencil, squeegee, ink, fabric on a padded table).

Design application (one block)
- [ ] E1 Appliqué: shaped pieces of fabric stitched onto a background (logos, jackets).
- [ ] E2 Embroidery: decorative thread stitching by hand or machine (cross stitch, chain stitch).
- [ ] E3 Quilting: top, wadding and backing stitched together through all layers; patchwork: pieces sewn together to make a design.
- [ ] E4 Smocking: fabric gathered into pleats and held by stitches; stretch and decoration, children's wear.
- [ ] E5 Printing (screen, roller, stencil, transfer), embossing (heated engraved roller presses a raised design) and beading or sequins also count as design application in schemes.

Performance testing (one block, [HL] only)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Aim: compare fabrics for suitability for a use, e.g. a school jacket or a uniform. Fair test: equal-size samples, grain in the same direction, same conditions, same weight and time, repeat and average.
- [ ] T2 [HL] Crease recovery: fold or crumple equal samples (in the fist for one minute, or folded under a set weight), release, and compare; measure the angle of recovery or compare on a scale. Best recovery is the closest to flat.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Absorbency: drop a set volume of water on the fabric and time how long it takes to soak in (towels).
- [ ] T4 [HL] Colourfastness: wash a sample with white fabric; compare colour change and staining with a grey scale.
- [ ] T5 [HL] Shrinkage: measure, wash and dry, measure again, work out the percentage change.
- [ ] T6 [HL] Others named in schemes: abrasion, insulation, stretch, pilling or snagging, tearing, water resistance, flame resistance.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "hand-knitted fabric has greater stretch than machine-knitted", because it is unsupported; say stretch depends on the yarn and the looping.
- Do not use: "fluorocarbon" as the stain-resistant or water-repellent chemical, because these finishes are being restricted and the name is not examined; say a finish that repels water or stains.
- Do not use: disperse or acid dyes as suitable for cotton (the 2006H scheme lists them for cotton), because they suit polyester and acetate, and wool, silk and nylon; cotton takes reactive dyes.
- Do not use: "a stain-resistant finish keeps fabric breathable" or any claim on breathability, because it varies by finish and is unconfirmed.
- Waterproof (coated, blocks water) and water repellent (water beads and runs off) are not the same; use the examiner's word waterproofing for either.
- Printing and tie-dye appear under both colour application and design application in schemes; give each its own use but do not use the same method for both parts of one answer.
- Purl is a looping method in the scheme; do not say it is a different kind of fabric.
- A finish is applied to the fabric after it is made; dyeing yarn is done before weaving or knitting.
- Fibre and yarn production belong to the fabric-profiles subsection; fire retardants and Proban belong to the safety subsection (give one line); care labels and shrinkage in washing belong to the clothing chapter.
- Higher Level: three construction techniques, three finishes, performance testing; Ordinary: two constructions, two finishes and two colour methods only.

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain how knitted fabric is constructed (9 marks) and give its properties and uses (2019H, 2005H).
- Name and describe a fabric construction technique suitable for a named fibre (weaving or knitting; 2007H, 2004O); describe warp, weft, selvedge.
- Explain why finishes are used on fabric (6 marks, 3 x 2) and name and describe one finish with an example of its use (Ordinary most years).
- Name and describe one colour application method and one design application method suitable for cotton or children's clothing (2016H, 2009O, 2006H, 2004O).
- Name and describe a performance test, or two tests, and give the procedure for one (2008H, 2025H, 2005H): fair test, measurement and conclusion.
- A functional finish to apply to a jacket fabric (2005H, 2010H) with its purpose.
