# Package: hom-proteins-s8 — Energy value

- **loId:** `hom-proteins-s8` · **chapterId:** `hom-proteins` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.2
- **Syllabus scope:** contribution to the total energy value of the average diet; protein as a supplementary energy source; [HL] deamination. Also examined: deamination (2026H 14 marks, 2024H 6 marks, 2015H 6 marks); energy per gram of protein (2024O true/false).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The common level is: protein gives 17 kJ (4 kcal) per gram, and is a supplementary energy source because its main jobs are growth and repair. Deamination is [HL]. The overall energy balance, BMR and calculating energy from a meal belong to the energy chapter; this subsection teaches the protein part.

## 1. Must cover

Energy value of protein
- [ ] E1 Protein provides 17 kJ (4 kcal) per gram. For comparison carbohydrate gives 16 kJ (3.75 kcal), fat 37 kJ (9 kcal) per gram, so fat gives more than twice the energy of protein per gram.
- [ ] E2 Protein is a supplementary (secondary) energy source: its main roles are growth and repair; it is used for energy when carbohydrate and fat are too low, or when more protein is eaten than the body needs.
- [ ] E3 Protein-sparing: enough carbohydrate and fat in the diet leave protein free for growth and repair (one line).
- [ ] E4 Eating protein more than needed does not build extra protein: surplus amino acids cannot be stored as protein.

Contribution to the average diet
- [ ] D1 Protein is one of the three energy-giving nutrients (with carbohydrate and fat; alcohol also gives energy). The protein's share of a day's energy is only a part of the total; the rest comes mainly from carbohydrate and fat.
- [ ] D2 The energy from protein in a food = grams of protein x 17 kJ (or x 4 kcal). Worked example: 25 g protein = 425 kJ or 100 kcal. Total energy of a food = sum of protein, carbohydrate, fat (and alcohol).
- [ ] D3 Protein foods differ in the energy that comes with the protein: lean meat and white fish give protein with little fat; full-fat dairy, fatty meat and nuts bring more fat and so more energy; pulses add carbohydrate and fibre.
- [ ] D4 Healthy eating link: choose lean protein foods; excess energy from any source, including protein foods, is a route to weight gain (one line).

Deamination [HL]
- [ ] A1 [HL] Deamination is the breakdown of surplus amino acids in the liver, since the body cannot store protein.
- [ ] A2 [HL] The amino group (–NH₂) is removed and converted to ammonia, which is toxic.
- [ ] A3 [HL] The liver converts ammonia to urea (combined with carbon dioxide), which is much less toxic; urea passes into the blood, is filtered by the kidneys and leaves in urine. Enough fluid helps excretion.
- [ ] A4 [HL] The rest of the molecule (the carbon skeleton, with the carboxyl group –COOH) is oxidised in respiration to give energy (heat and energy). When energy is in surplus, it can be turned to glucose and stored as glycogen, or to fat in adipose tissue.
- [ ] A5 [HL] Sequence summary: excess amino acids -> liver -> NH₂ removed -> ammonia -> urea -> kidneys -> urine; carbon part -> energy.
- [ ] A6 [HL] Diagram or flow chart cue: the sequence above as a flow chart.

## 3. Traps

- Protein is a supplementary energy source, not the main one. Do not say protein is the body's main fuel.
- Surplus amino acids are not stored as protein. For Ordinary true/false, "excess protein is stored as adipose tissue" was marked false (2016O). Do not write "excess protein is stored as fat" as a bare statement. If the fat route is given at Higher Level, it is the carbon skeleton, after deamination, in a state of energy surplus (the 2026H scheme), like any surplus energy.
- Ammonia is toxic; urea is the safer product. Students reverse this.
- The nitrogen leaves as urea in urine; the carbon part is the energy source. Do not say the amino group provides the energy.
- Deamination happens in the liver, not the kidney or stomach; the kidneys only excrete the urea.
- Digestion of protein into amino acids, absorption and BMR/energy balance belong to the digestion and metabolism chapter and energy chapter.
- Do not use: a percentage for how much of the average Irish diet's energy protein supplies, because no percentage was confirmed and such figures change; say protein supplies part of the total energy, and give the calculation instead.
- Do not use: "protein is used mainly for energy" with no qualification, because it is used mainly for growth and repair.

## 4. What exams ask

- Energy per gram: true/false that 1 g protein provides 4 kcal (2024O); "state the energy value of protein per gram"; calculate the energy from a stated mass of protein.
- Explain deamination (Higher 2015, 2024, 6 marks; 2026, 14 marks): excess amino acids; liver; amino group to ammonia to urea; kidneys and urine; carbon part oxidised for energy; (2026) carbon skeleton to glucose/glycogen or fat; excess not stored as protein; fluid needed. Marks go for the sequence and for naming ammonia, urea and the liver.
- Part of "functions of protein" answers: a source of energy, supplied when carbohydrate and fat are low.
- Exam tips to build: "Name the liver, ammonia, urea and kidneys"; "Give 17 kJ or 4 kcal per gram"; "Amino acids are not stored as protein."
