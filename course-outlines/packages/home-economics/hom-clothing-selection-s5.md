# Package: hom-clothing-selection-s5 — The design process and garment construction

- **loId:** `hom-clothing-selection-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-clothing-selection` · **syllabusRef:** 5.3
- **Syllabus scope:** knowledge and application of the design process to construct a garment using a range of prescribed processes (commercial patterns may be used); selection and **[HL]** modification of a commercial pattern to meet a specific need; the Elective 2 coursework (100 marks, 10%) from the SEC's 2027 brief (M47/48B): design and sketch an outfit for a young person to wear on holiday (knitted, crocheted or machine stitched), set out its design specifications, make and evaluate one garment from it including two of buttons and buttonholes, darts or shaping, a pocket; individual work under teacher supervision; all sources, including AI-generated material, referenced; completion date 6 April 2027. Design folder 25 marks (analysis and research 15, implementation 4, evaluation 6); garment 75 marks (design aspects 15, skills and processes 50, finish 10). Notes teach how each process is made and the design process; they do not write the coursework. Settings: a fashion sketch for a brief; testing fabrics for suitability (absorbency, shrinkage, water-repellence, crease recovery). Also examined: design process steps (2021H); pattern modification (2022H, 2018H, 2008H); pattern selection and envelope information (2025O, 2019O, 2009O).
- **[HL] mark:** only the modification of a commercial pattern is Higher Level only; selecting and using a pattern are common to both levels.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Fabric tests are explained in the textile care subsection; flattering design is in the elements and principles subsection; the clothing industry is in the final subsection.

## 1. Must cover

The design process (one block, examined 2021H)
- [ ] D1 Seven steps in order: define and analyse the brief; investigate and research; generate possible ideas; present the ideas; refine the design; implement (make); evaluate.
- [ ] D2 What each step involves: the brief (who, purpose, occasion, budget, constraints); research (magazines, trends, fabrics, patterns, key requirements); several sketches; choosing and justifying one; adjusting fabric, trim and details; planning and making; evaluating the result against the brief (fit, finish, comfort, function, appearance, what to improve).
- [ ] D3 The design specification: a list of what the garment must be and do (wearer, use, fabric needs, size/fit, care, cost, time, key features).

Sketching and describing a design (one block)
- [ ] SK1 A fashion sketch shows line, shape, proportion, colour and fabric; front view (and back view where needed); labelled features.
- [ ] SK2 The description covers colour, design, line, proportion, shape, pockets, seams, neckline, sleeves, fabric, finishes and accessories (2023H scheme); every feature described should be visible in the sketch.
- [ ] SK3 Diagram: a labelled front-view fashion sketch.

Choosing fabric and notions (one block)
- [ ] FA1 Match fabric to purpose and design: drape for flowing skirts, crisp fabric for tailoring, stretch for fitted or sportswear, easy-care for everyday wear; and to the wearer's budget.
- [ ] FA2 Test suitability: absorbency, shrinkage, water-repellence, crease recovery (see the textile care subsection for the methods); wash a sample before cutting.
- [ ] FA3 Notions (zips, buttons, interfacing, lining, thread, elastic, trims) chosen to suit fabric weight, colour and care.
- [ ] FA4 Interfacing gives body and support at collars, cuffs, waistbands and buttonholes.

Selecting a commercial pattern (one block, both levels)
- [ ] PS1 Choose by skill level (beginners: simple pattern, few darts and seams; easy-sew range), by design and purpose, by cost and value (several garments from one pattern), by availability of suitable fabric, by buyer preference, and by size from measurements.
- [ ] PS2 Sizing: skirts and trousers by hip measurement; dresses, shirts, coats and jackets by bust or chest.
- [ ] PS3 The pattern envelope: front and back views, suitable fabrics, fabric quantity by size and style, notions required, standard body measurements and finished garment measurements, number of pieces, plaid or diagonal matching advice, size range. (assumed) Buy the pattern before the fabric.
- [ ] PS4 Explain the importance of one envelope item: e.g. fabric quantities stop over- or under-buying; suggested fabrics avoid unsuitable choices; finished measurements show ease and fit.

Body measurements (one block)
- [ ] BM1 Bust or chest: fullest part, tape level, comfortably snug; waist: natural waistline; hips: widest part; nape to waist: from the prominent bone at the base of the neck to the waistline; inside leg and shoulder where needed; take measurements over light clothing, tape level and not tight.
- [ ] BM2 Compare measurements to the pattern chart to choose size.

Laying out and cutting (one block, 2012O)
- [ ] LC1 Follow the pattern instructions; fold fabric as directed; pin pieces on; place straight grain parallel to the selvedge; place "fold" pieces exactly on the fold; allow for nap and pattern direction and match checks/stripes; keep fabric flat, cut accurately, transfer markings.
- [ ] LC2 Diagram: pattern pieces on fabric showing grain line parallel to the selvedge and a piece on the fold.

Plan of work for construction (one block, 2015H)
- [ ] PW1 Order of work: lay out and cut; transfer markings; pin and tack (fit check); sew darts and seams in order, pressing as you go; insert fastenings; attach facings, collars and hems; final press; evaluate.
- [ ] PW2 Pressing: removes creases, defines darts, flattens seams, prevents puckering and improves the finished look (2025O, 2015O schemes).
- [ ] PW3 Careful finishing: neaten seam allowances, clip curves, trim corners.

Modifying a commercial pattern (Higher Level only)
- [ ] MP1 Why: the wearer does not match the standard measurements, a different length or width is wanted, a different style or preference.
- [ ] MP2 Lengthen: cut across the pattern at the marked alteration lines, spread the pieces, insert tissue paper, redraw the seam and fitting lines.
- [ ] MP3 Shorten: fold along an alteration line by half the amount to be removed and redraw the lines.
- [ ] MP4 Width: similar folds or slashes alter waist, hip, bust or sleeve width.
- [ ] MP5 Check the modification on the pattern (match seams, keep the grain line) before cutting.

Elective 2 coursework (2027 brief)
- [ ] CW1 Facts in the scope above: 100 marks (10%); design folder 25, garment 75; mark splits; completion 6 April 2027; individual work, supervised, sources and AI material referenced.
- [ ] CW2 Brief: holiday wardrobe expressing values, interests and personality; design and sketch an outfit for a young person to wear on holiday (knitted, crocheted or machine stitched); set out design specifications; make and evaluate one garment from it including two of (i) buttons and buttonholes, (ii) darts or shaping, (iii) a pocket.
- [ ] CW3 What the folder follows: analysis and research (requirements, constraints, theme, sketches, justification, proposed action); implementation (work sequence, the mandatory processes); evaluation (garment, difficulties and fixes, modifications, outcome against the brief).

The three prescribed processes (one block)
- [ ] PR1 Buttonholes: mark position and length (button diameter plus its thickness) on the right front for a woman's garment (left for men), at right angles to the edge for horizontal buttonholes; reinforce with interfacing; stitch by machine with a buttonhole foot or by hand (buttonhole stitch); cut open last with a seam ripper or sharp scissors without cutting the stitching.
- [ ] PR2 Buttons: marked through the buttonhole position; sew with a thread shank so the fastened layers lie flat; reinforce on thin fabric.
- [ ] PR3 Darts: transfer markings (tailor's chalk or tacking); fold right sides together matching the marks; stitch from the wide end to the point; tie or backstitch the point; press bust and waist darts flat then towards the centre or downward.
- [ ] PR4 Other shaping: gathers, pleats, tucks, princess seams, elastic.
- [ ] PR5 Patch pocket: mark position; turn and stitch the top hem or facing; press seam allowance under; pin and topstitch to the garment; reinforce the top corners.
- [ ] PR6 In-seam pocket (an alternative): pocket bag stitched into a seam, opening hidden.
- [ ] PR7 Interface or reinforce at stress points; press as each process is made.

## 3. Traps

- The design process is a sequence; do not reorder (many answers jump from research to construction).
- Commercial pattern modification is [HL]; selection and envelope information are not.
- Selection by size uses hip for lower-body garments and bust/chest for upper-body garments; do not give waist as the sizing measure.
- "Easy-sew" is a range of simple patterns; "zero waste" is a design approach mentioned in the 2025O scheme but not part of the outline; mention only in a phrase if at all.
- Do not write the coursework: notes teach the processes and design-process headings.
- Fabric tests are taught in the textile care subsection; here only say that tests inform fabric choice.
- Design elements and flattering design belong to the elements and principles subsection.
- Fabric finishes and design application (printing, appliqué) belong to the fibres and fabrics material.
- Do not use: "the garment must be textile based, show a clear interpretation of the theme, and show progression from Junior Cycle textile skills", because that wording is not in the outline's brief and is unconfirmed.
- Do not use: coursework mark splits other than those in the outline, or any prior-year brief, because the 2027 brief is the one examined.
- Do not use: "Stage 6: evaluate fit and finish" as the final step of a plan of work to be examined without saying evaluation follows the garment; in the design process, evaluation is step seven.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline the steps of the design process (2021H, 10 marks): the seven steps with a few words on each.
- Factors in selecting a commercial pattern (2025O, 2019O, 2015O, 2012O): beginners choose simple patterns with few darts/seams; size by hip or bust/chest; personal preference; cost; value for money; suitability for purpose; materials and notions needed.
- Explain the importance of one item on the pattern envelope (2019O): quantity of fabric, finished measurements, suitable fabrics, notions, views.
- Importance of pressing during construction (2025O, 2015O): improves appearance, removes creases, defines darts, opens seams, stops puckering.
- Body measurements needed and plan of work for a garment (2015H): nape to waist, bust, waist, hip; stages in order.
- Laying out a pattern (2012O): list stages: follow instructions, pin, grain parallel to selvedge, fold lines on the fold, etc.
- Modify a commercial pattern (2022H, 2018H): describe lengthening or shortening, giving reasons for modification; explain suitable reasons (non-standard figure, length, width, preference).
- Coursework follow-up: describe how to make a buttonhole, dart or patch pocket; evaluate the finished garment (fit, finish, comfort, function, appearance, changes).
- Sketch and describe a design for a brief (many years): front view, labelled features, fabric.
