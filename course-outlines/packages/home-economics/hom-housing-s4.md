# Package: hom-housing-s4 — Housing provision

- **loId:** `hom-housing-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-housing` · **syllabusRef:** 4.1.2 (Elective 1, Home Design and Management)
- **Syllabus scope:** the housing requirements of families, single people, older people, people with disabilities and homeless people; evaluation of housing provision in Ireland today — **[HL]** distribution of housing (rented or owner-occupied, private or social, urban or rural), quality of accommodation, comparative costs of buying and renting, **[HL]** adequacy of provision to meet varying requirements; social housing provision (local authority, voluntary and co-operative housing); provision of local amenities and services (schools, shops, community centres, transport, play areas, street lighting, refuse collection).

Quality of accommodation and the comparative costs of buying and renting are not marked [HL], so they are for all levels. (The existing notes wrongly mark "quality of accommodation and comparative costs" Higher Level only; fix that.)
- **Settings:** a group housing study of a local area (townland, village, housing estate).
- **Also examined:** specific housing needs of groups (2011O, 2013H, 2016H, 2018H, 2025H); comparative costs of buying and renting (2005H, 2014H); adequacy of housing provision (2005H, 2007H, 2010H, 2013H, 2017H, 2026H); social housing (2010H, 2013H, 2025H); local amenities and services (2026H).

## 1. Must cover

Housing requirements of different groups (one block per group or one block with a point list)
- [ ] G1 Families: enough bedrooms for all members, storage, a well-equipped, ventilated and well-lit kitchen, a living room, more than one bathroom in a larger family, a hot-press; families with young children need a safe, enclosed garden and safe stairs; teenagers need study space; a garage or shed for car, garden and sports equipment; closeness to schools, childcare, shops, parks and public transport. Higher Level examples tie these to "school-going children".
- [ ] G2 Single people: smaller, lower-cost accommodation (apartment, bedsit, small townhouse, a room in a shared house); closeness to work or college and public transport; privacy and security; space to study.
- [ ] G3 Older people: single-level living, or a ground-floor bedroom and bathroom; a stairlift where there are stairs; level or ramped entrance; a home that is warm, well insulated and easy to heat; safe lighting and non-slip floors; alarm or emergency call systems; closeness to shops, health services, public transport and community and family support; sheltered housing or accommodation designed for older people.
- [ ] G4 People with disabilities: the adjustments depend on the disability. Mobility: ramp access, wide doorways and hallways, lowered worktops, light switches and sockets at reachable heights, raised toilet with grab rails, accessible bathroom or shower, lift or stairlift. Hearing: flashing lights linked to doorbell and alarms. Sight: audible or tactile alarms and good lighting. Communication or alarm systems for emergencies.
- [ ] G5 Homeless people: regular (not nightly queue) shelter such as a hostel or emergency accommodation with a bed, washing and laundry facilities, privacy and dignity, a safe place to store possessions, communal space (kitchen, day room) to reduce isolation; then rent-assisted private accommodation or local authority housing; serviced halting sites for Travellers who live in mobile homes or caravans; the aim is a permanent home with support (the Housing First approach is named in "Housing choices").
- [ ] G6 Say who each requirement serves, and why (a ramp lets a wheelchair user enter; a flashing doorbell alerts a person who cannot hear).

Distribution of housing (Higher Level only)
- [ ] D1 By tenure: owner-occupied (with a mortgage or owned outright), private rented, and social rented (local authority and approved housing bodies). Owner-occupation is the largest tenure in Ireland; private renting and social housing are smaller shares.
- [ ] D2 By sector: private housing is built by developers and individuals for sale or rent on the open market; social housing is provided by local authorities and approved housing bodies for people who cannot afford a home.
- [ ] D3 By location: urban areas have higher density, more apartments and higher demand; rural areas have more one-off houses and bungalows, and in some places vacant homes with fewer jobs and services; strong demand in cities and commuter areas pushes prices and rents up.

Quality of accommodation (all levels)
- [ ] Q1 Quality is judged by structure and repair, insulation and heating, damp and ventilation, lighting, space for the household, safety and facilities. Older stock is often poorly insulated, costly to heat and may cause fuel poverty; new homes must meet the Building Regulations.
- [ ] Q2 The Building Energy Rating (BER) certificate rates a home's energy efficiency from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient). (Detail in "House design and building standards".)
- [ ] Q3 Rented housing must meet legal minimum standards (Housing (Standards for Rented Houses) Regulations 2019), enforced by local authorities.
- [ ] Q4 Poor-quality housing harms health (damp, cold, overcrowding) and school and work performance.

Comparative costs of buying and renting (all levels)
- [ ] CC1 Buying, initial costs: deposit, legal fees, lender, valuation and survey fees, stamp duty on the purchase, furnishing. Ongoing: mortgage repayments, mortgage protection and home insurance, property tax, maintenance and repairs, service charges for apartments. Gains: ownership, equity, possible rise in value, security.
- [ ] CC2 Renting, initial costs: a deposit (a month's rent is the usual) and rent in advance, and an agency fee where an agent is used. Ongoing: rent, contents insurance, utilities. The tenant has no repair costs but builds no equity; rent is "dead money".
- [ ] CC3 The balance: renting needs less capital and gives mobility; in high-demand areas the rent can be as much as a mortgage repayment on a similar home, so the cost of renting for years can exceed the cost of buying. Buying needs a deposit many cannot save.

Adequacy of provision (Higher Level only)
- [ ] AD1 Adequacy means whether housing matches need: enough homes, affordable, secure, suitable, accessible, in good condition, and near work, schools, transport and services, for each type of household.
- [ ] AD2 Strengths: a wide variety of house types and tenures, social housing through local authorities, approved housing bodies and co-operatives, cost rental, rent supports (HAP and RAS), supports for first-time buyers, adaptation grants, better standards through Building Regulations and the BER.
- [ ] AD3 Weaknesses: high prices and rents in cities, waiting lists for social housing, homelessness and long stays in emergency accommodation (which lacks the stability of a home), poor access for some people with disabilities and older people, poor stock in places, vacant property, and uneven distribution between urban and rural areas.
- [ ] AD4 Groups at risk of inadequate provision: low-income families, lone parents, single people (lower priority for social housing), homeless people, Travellers, older people, people with disabilities, students and new arrivals (refugees and asylum seekers).
- [ ] AD5 Ways to improve: more social, affordable and cost rental homes; renovating vacant property; mixed, serviced developments; adaptation grants; planning for need.

Social housing provision (all levels)
- [ ] SH1 Local authority housing: rented homes for households on a lower income who cannot afford a home; allocated by need through a social housing assessment and a waiting list; priority to families, people with disabilities, older people and others in greater need, so single people can wait longer.
- [ ] SH2 Voluntary housing: provided by approved housing bodies (non-profit voluntary organisations, often for older people, people with disabilities or homeless people); the tenant's landlord is the approved housing body.
- [ ] SH3 Co-operative housing: a group of members who pool their resources to provide homes, share costs and manage the housing, run on non-profit principles. A local authority may provide a low-cost site to a co-operative whose members are on its waiting list.
- [ ] SH4 Rent supports and schemes (named and described in one line each): the Housing Assistance Payment (HAP), where the local authority pays the private landlord and the tenant pays a contribution; the Rental Accommodation Scheme (RAS), where the local authority arranges long-term private rented homes for households with a long-term need; cost rental, homes let at a rent based on cost rather than profit, for households above social housing income limits; affordable purchase and the First Home shared equity scheme for buyers.
- [ ] SH5 Adaptation grants help older people and people with disabilities to adapt a home so they can stay in it. Specific provision exists for homeless people, older people and Travellers.
- [ ] SH6 Demand exceeds supply, so there are waiting lists.

Local amenities and services (all levels)
- [ ] AM1 Schools, childcare and playgrounds; shops, post office, banks, health services and pharmacy; community centres and meeting places; public transport; parks, sports grounds and play areas; street lighting; refuse collection; and services for older people and people with disabilities.
- [ ] AM2 State why each matters: access to work and services, safety (lighting), hygiene (refuse), health and exercise (parks), social contact and a sense of community (community centres); an estate without transport and services isolates residents.
- [ ] AM3 Setting: a local-area study (townland, village or estate) recording which amenities exist and which are missing.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: percentages of private, social and rented housing, waiting-list totals, and homelessness counts from papers or earlier notes (for example "81%" or "896 families"), because they are old and change.
- Do not use: the "tenant purchase scheme and affordable housing schemes are no longer available" point from the 2013 and 2017 marking schemes, because affordable purchase now exists (local authority affordable purchase) and the position has changed.
- Do not use: the shared ownership scheme (buy 40%, rent 60% from the local authority) from the 2010 marking scheme, because it is an old scheme; use First Home shared equity if shared equity is asked.
- Do not use: mortgage interest relief, "rent supplement" or "rent allowance" as current supports, because HAP replaced rent supplement.
- The existing notes mark "Quality of accommodation and comparative costs" Higher Level only. The outline does not: remove that label from that block.
- Do not confuse social housing (let at subsidised rent by local authorities and approved housing bodies) with affordable housing (sold at below-market price) or cost rental (let at cost rent to those above social housing income limits).
- Distribution means where and in what tenure homes are; adequacy means whether they meet need. Keep the two Higher Level blocks separate.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the national housing plan and renting against buying as a household choice (housing choices); mortgage types (housing finance); design of homes and the BER certificate (house design and building standards); planning and building (location, planning and professional services).

## 4. What exams ask

- Housing requirements of groups: "give an account of the housing requirements of families, single people and older people" (Higher Level 2013); "people with disabilities and homeless people" (2016); "homeless people" (2018, four points); "families with school-going children and people with disabilities" (2025); "two features for an older person with limited mobility" (Ordinary Level 2011). Six to eight distinct requirements, each tied to its group, earn the marks.
- Adequacy of housing provision (Higher Level, 12 to 24 marks): expect points on owner-occupied, private rented and social sectors, a named strength and weakness of each, and the groups not well served (single people, homeless people, older people, Travellers, students); 2026 asked for "adequacy" in the wider sense of affordability, location and quality.
- Social housing (2010 "name and give details of one type of social housing"; 2013 evaluate adequacy; 2025 discuss provision): local authority, approved housing body and co-operative housing, supports such as HAP, RAS, cost rental, allocation by need, waiting lists, adaptation grants.
- Comparative costs of buying and renting (Higher Level 15 marks, 2005 and 2014): initial costs, ongoing costs and a balanced conclusion.
- Importance of local amenities and services in a development (2026, 15 marks): link each amenity to a need and the effect on the community.
- Answers are rewarded for naming the sector or group, then saying how provision does or does not meet its need.
