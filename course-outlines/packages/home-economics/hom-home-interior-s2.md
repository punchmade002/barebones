# Package: hom-home-interior-s2 — Floors, walls, furniture and soft furnishings

- **loId:** `hom-home-interior-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-home-interior` · **syllabusRef:** 4.3
- **Syllabus scope (Elective 1, Home Design and Management):** selection, properties and uses of flooring and floor coverings, wall finishes, furniture and soft furnishings. No [HL] marks in this subsection; it is common to Ordinary and Higher Level.
- **Also examined:** floor coverings; wall finishes; furniture; soft furnishings.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The elements and principles of design and the general interior-design factors are in the previous subsection; the properties of wood, metal, glass, plastics and fabric as materials (Higher Level) are in the next. Fire-safety labelling of furniture is a consumer-law topic and is not covered here.

## 1. Must cover

Floors and floor coverings
- [ ] FL1 Solid floor: a concrete slab on a hardcore layer, with a damp-proof layer (course or membrane) and a thin concrete or fine screed finish; usually on the ground floor of modern homes.
- [ ] FL2 Suspended floor: joists (wood, concrete or steel) covered with tongue-and-groove boards or planks; an air space beneath lets air circulate and prevents dampness; a damp-proof course can be added; usually on upper floors. Boards can be sanded and varnished. Gaps and loose boards are fixed before a new covering is laid.
- [ ] FL3 Floor coverings are grouped hard, semi-hard and soft. For each covering the student gives a name, a short description (what it is made of), properties, and an advantage and a disadvantage.
- [ ] FL4 Hard: ceramic tiles (clay, minerals and water; glazed or unglazed; many sizes and shapes; hard-wearing, easy to clean, stain resistant, water resistant, but hard and cold underfoot and can crack or chip). Quarry tiles (unglazed clay, hard-wearing, for hallways and kitchens). Terracotta and glazed stone tiles. Flagstones or cut stone (natural colours, uneven texture, hard-wearing, cold, chip). Slate (fine-grained foliated metamorphic rock; hard-wearing, easy to clean, slightly absorbent, uneven texture, dark colours; the scheme gives it as warm underfoot). Marble and travertine (limestone-type natural stone cut into tiles and slabs, flecks of colour, chip easily, cold underfoot). Stone carpeting (gravel-based mixture poured on the subfloor and sealed when set). Mosaics (glass, metal, resin and stone in various textures and colours; decorative, hard-wearing). Safety flooring (slip-resistant vinyl for bathrooms, hospitals; hard-wearing, hygienic).
- [ ] FL5 Semi-hard: wood strip flooring (tongue-and-groove strips of hardwood such as oak, beech or maple, or softwood such as pine or spruce; solid or laminated with a plastic veneer); block parquet (hardwood blocks in herringbone pattern); mosaic parquet (blocks in a basket-weave pattern). Properties: attractive, durable, low maintenance, most sold ready sealed; noisy, cold underfoot, marked and scratched easily, can be expensive; softwood dents more than hardwood.
- [ ] FL6 Vinyl: polyvinyl chloride (PVC), a plastic developed from petroleum, with resin, plasticiser, pigment and filler, put together under heat and pressure on a backing; sheets or tiles; patterns can be marbled, flecked, embossed, veined or striped. Properties: durable (varies with thickness), quiet, easy to clean, hygienic, stain resistant, resistant to water, acids and alkalis, huge range of colour and design, foam-backed vinyl soft and warm underfoot; scorches or melts with heat; marked by heels or furniture.
- [ ] FL7 Linoleum: natural, from linseed oil, cork dust and resin on a jute backing; resilient, quiet, easy care. Cork: granules of cork-oak bark bonded with polyurethane resin, or sliced directly from bark; sold sealed or unsealed; warm and quiet underfoot, not hard-wearing. Coir: coconut fibre softened in water, beaten, spun and woven; tough, hard-wearing. Sisal: from a dark green spiky plant (agave); hard-wearing, easy to clean, anti-static.
- [ ] FL8 Soft: carpet (soft, warm and resilient underfoot, insulates against heat and sound, many colours and patterns, durable; needs regular vacuuming, dirties easily, can be expensive). Woven carpet with fabric pile woven onto a hessian backing: Axminster (cut pile, almost unlimited colours and designs) and Wilton (continuous strand, limits the number of colours, thicker). Tufted carpet (tufts inserted into a woven backing, held with adhesive, foam backed). Fibre blends: 100 per cent wool, 80 per cent wool and 20 per cent nylon, acrylic, polypropylene. Carpet tiles (sealed-edge squares backed with PVC or rubber; stain resistant; a damaged tile can be replaced; limited colours and patterns; graded light, medium, heavy and general). Underlay (cushioning, insulation, longer carpet life). Rugs (loose, movable, need a non-slip backing).
- [ ] FL9 Factors when choosing a floor covering (the schemes list these): function of the room and traffic flow, durability and quality, cost (purchase, laying, upkeep), ease of cleaning and hygiene, warmth and sound insulation, safety (non-slip), condition and type of the subfloor, décor and aesthetics, size of the room, flooring in adjoining rooms, occupants.
- [ ] FL10 Matching coverings to rooms: hallway (ceramic, quarry tile or flagstone, or wood or vinyl), kitchen (tiles, vinyl, linoleum), bathroom (ceramic, slate, vinyl or safety flooring: durable, water resistant, non-slip), sitting room (wood, carpet, vinyl), bedroom (carpet), home office (carpet or carpet tiles). Diagram: cross-sections of a solid floor and a suspended floor.

Wall finishes
- [ ] W1 Paint: water-based emulsion (matt, silk or soft sheen vinyl): easy to apply, quick drying, low odour, inexpensive, wide colour range, durable, washable. Solvent-based gloss or satin: hard-wearing, washable, copes with high condensation, but strong smell and slow drying. Textured paint (plastic fibres give a textured finish). Specialised paint (e.g. flame retardant, mould resistant for bathrooms). Matt paint absorbs light and hides flaws; gloss and silk reflect light and wipe clean.
- [ ] W2 Wallpaper: general-purpose (design printed on paper, varies in thickness, hides faults); embossed (design stamped into the paper, can be painted); woodchip (paper with wood chips, hides faults, paintable); flock (raised velvet-like pattern); vinyl or washable (plastic-coated, withstands condensation, durable, strong, wipeable, huge choice of designs, camouflages faults, makes rooms cosier). Lining paper: plain paper hung before paint or wallpaper for a smooth even surface.
- [ ] W3 Tiles: ceramic or porcelain wall tiles; hard-wearing, waterproof, easy to clean and hygienic, glazed or unglazed, many colours, sizes and patterns; need a sound wall, waterproof adhesive and grout; costly to install and can feel cold; used behind sinks and cookers, around baths and in showers.
- [ ] W4 Other finishes: wood or medium-density fibreboard panelling (texture and warmth); stencils and motifs to decorate a painted wall.
- [ ] W5 Factors when choosing wall finishes (the schemes list these): function of the room and its conditions (moisture in kitchens and bathrooms), décor and trends, durability (traffic flow, age of occupants), size and shape of the room and condition of the walls (cracks, damp, uneven plaster need treating), maintenance and cleaning, budget (materials, labour, preparation), light reflectance, texture and finish, ease of application, coverage, aesthetics, environmental impact (low-VOC paints), non-toxic products for children.
- [ ] W6 Matching finishes to rooms: a young child's bedroom (silk vinyl emulsion: washable, easy to apply, quick drying, inexpensive, motifs and stencils can be added); a kitchen (washable vinyl paint, washable vinyl wallpaper, tiles); a bathroom (tiles, moisture-resistant washable paint, washable vinyl wallpaper); a living room (water-based or textured paint, washable or embossed wallpaper).

Furniture
- [ ] FU1 Factors when choosing furniture: style or type (antique, traditional, contemporary) and décor; function of the room; space available (measure room, doors and stairs); budget and cost against quality; construction and materials (solid timber is more durable than chipboard; frame and joints); durability and safety; comfort and ergonomics (seat height and depth suit the user); personal preference; free-standing or built-in; availability; safety labels, quality marks and guarantees; applying principles of design such as proportion and balance.
- [ ] FU2 Furniture for particular uses: a bedroom (storage, size, durable, safe); an office (desk size and shape, chair, built-in drawers, shelving; functional and aesthetic guidelines).
- [ ] FU3 Storage furniture: built-in wardrobes (hanging rails, drawers, shelving; custom built to suit the space; cannot be moved); shelving and bookcases (open, cheap, wood or metal); modular units (wall unit with shelving, cupboards, display or desk; good in confined spaces); wall units, filing cabinets, TV cabinets, furniture chests, toy boxes, storage boxes and baskets; free-standing flat-pack units (home assembly, usually cheaper). Reasons for choosing: avoid clutter, conceal items, display photographs and trophies, find items easily, improve the appearance of the room.

Soft furnishings
- [ ] SF1 Soft furnishings include curtains, blinds, cushions, throws, rugs, bed linen, upholstery fabrics, bean bags, pouffes, lamp shades and arm covers.
- [ ] SF2 Fabric suitability: curtains (drape well, pre-shrunk, fade resistant, flame resistant, durable); cushions (strong, cleanable, stain resistant); bed linen (smooth, closely woven, absorbent, easy to launder, shrink resistant); upholstery (durable, comfortable, resilient, stain resistant, hard-wearing). Fabrics named in the schemes: cotton, linen, wool, polyester, velvet, silk.
- [ ] SF3 Curtains and blinds: lined curtains insulate a window and reduce draughts and heat loss, give privacy and can add width to a window; roller blinds roll up neatly and suit small windows; Venetian blinds have adjustable slats to control light and privacy; blinds suit roof (Velux) windows.
- [ ] SF4 How soft furnishings enhance a room (the schemes list these): add colour, pattern and texture; create warmth, comfort and cosiness; form a focal point or add interest; give privacy and insulation; economically update or transform a room's style.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "natural fibres such as wool absorb moisture from the air, which helps reduce condensation", because it is not in any scheme and is misleading; condensation is reduced by ventilation and insulation, not by fabrics.
- Do not use: the Fire Safety (Domestic Furniture) Order 1988 (and its 1995 amendment) or its permanent label wording, because it was revoked in July 2026 (S.I. 357/2026) and is replaced by the EU General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988, even though the 2022H and 2026H schemes still name it. Furniture fire safety and labelling belong to the consumer-protection chapter; here say only "choose furniture and upholstery that is fire resistant and carries safety labels".
- Slate's warmth underfoot comes from the 2018H scheme; most sources call stone cold. Give it as the scheme does, but give "hard-wearing, easy to clean" as its main points.
- Marble and travertine are cut natural stone; do not say they are manufactured.
- Laminate and "laminated wood" flooring: write "laminated, with a plastic veneer" as the scheme does; do not describe its core in detail.
- Coir is "tough, hard-wearing"; sisal is "hard-wearing, easy to clean, anti-static". Do not give "easy to clean" to coir.
- Gloss and silk finishes reflect light and wipe clean; matt absorbs light and hides flaws but marks more easily. Students reverse these.
- Carpet cost varies with fibre and quality; the schemes mark carpet as "can be expensive", not always dearer than hard floors. Give no price.
- Solid versus suspended: solid = concrete, ground floor; suspended = joists and boards with an air space, upper floors. Students reverse these.
- Properties of the materials themselves (wood types, plastics, glass, metal, fabric fibres) belong to the materials subsection; give here only the properties that make a covering suitable. Colour, pattern and texture theory belongs to the elements-of-design subsection. Lighting, heating and ventilation of the room belong to the services chapter.
- Do not state prices, grants or running costs.

## 4. What exams ask

- Solid and suspended floors: explain each (2013H). Expected: materials, usual location, how dampness is prevented.
- Describe two or three floor coverings with properties, or a hard and a semi-hard, or semi-hard and soft (2018H, 2013H, 2007H, 2010O). Expected: name, description, two properties each, advantage and disadvantage.
- Recommend a floor covering for a named room (bathroom 2016O, kitchen 2005O, hallway 2007H, home office 2022H, sitting room 2010O) with reasons: durable, water resistant, non-slip, warm, easy to clean, sound-absorbent as relevant.
- Factors when choosing floor coverings (2018H, 2016O, 2007H, 2005O): function and traffic, cost, cleaning, aesthetics, safety, subfloor, warmth and noise.
- Factors when choosing wall finishes (2026O, 2019H, 2013O, 2006O): function, durability, maintenance, cost, condition of walls, light reflectance, décor. Expect one reason each.
- Name and describe two or three wall finishes and give two properties each, for a given room (2026O bathroom, 2019H kitchen/living room, 2014H living room, 2008H, 2006O child's bedroom).
- Factors when choosing furniture, including for a bedroom, office or living room (2018O, 2016H, 2015O, 2009H, 2007O); name and describe two or three items of storage furniture with reasons (2015O, 2018O).
- Soft furnishings: recommend two, with reasons (2017O, 2008O); recommend one fabric and justify it (2010H); explain how soft furnishings enhance a room (2021O).
- A long-answer combination: recommend a floor covering and a wall finish for a family room and justify each by durability, safety, cost and maintenance.
