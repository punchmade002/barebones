# Package: hom-family-law-s1 — The Family Law (Maintenance of Spouses and Children) Act 1976

- **loId:** `hom-family-law-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-law` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.7
- **Syllabus scope:** "The Family Law (Maintenance of Spouses and Children) Act 1976" [3.1.7], as part of the protection available for families under current Irish family law. Common to Ordinary and Higher Level: no [HL] mark on this item.
- **Also examined:** 2018H (outline the protection available for families under this Act, 10 marks, 2 points at 5 marks each); 2012O (explain how the Act protects the family, 10 marks, 2 points at 5 marks each). The Act is also named as one of several Acts in a Section B "how does Irish family law protect family members when a marriage breaks down" question (12 marks) and in a "rights and responsibilities of a couple in marriage" question (20 marks); the same facts serve there.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The Act is still in force (as amended) and keeps its syllabus name (outline, Appendix A). Divorce, judicial separation and deeds of separation belong to neighbouring notes; here they appear only as the contexts in which maintenance is claimed. Sources checked: Citizens Information (maintenance orders and agreements), the 2012O/2018H marking schemes.

## 1. Must cover

What the Act does
- [ ] M1 The Act gives a legal right to maintenance: regular financial support paid by one person for the benefit of a dependant. It protects a dependent spouse and dependent children when a relationship breaks down. Open with a one-sentence definition of maintenance; examiners expect it.
- [ ] M2 A dependent spouse is one who does not have the financial resources to support themselves. The marking schemes write "spouse/civil partner" in this phrase; the notes may say "spouse or civil partner" as the schemes do, but should not name any other Act for civil partners.
- [ ] M3 Maintenance can be sought for the dependent spouse and for the dependent children, so the Act protects the whole family, not one person. Mutual duty: spouses owe each other support according to their means (assumed).
- [ ] M4 A dependent child is: under 18; or 18 to under 23 and in full-time education; or a child of any age with a disability who cannot support themselves. The age limits are the detail examiners reward.
- [ ] M5 Maintenance can be sought whether the couple are living together or apart. For a child, the marital status of the parents does not matter: the parents of a child born outside marriage owe the child support too.
- [ ] M6 What maintenance is for: the everyday costs of the dependant (food, clothing, housing and other necessities). The amount depends on the means and needs of the people involved: the court considers what the paying person can afford and what the dependant needs (assumed).
- [ ] M7 Who may be asked to pay: the other spouse, for a spouse's maintenance; either parent, for a child's (assumed). The payer can be ordered to pay whether or not they live in the same house.

How maintenance is obtained
- [ ] H1 Step 1: the parties try to agree the amount and how often it is paid, ideally written down (a maintenance agreement). It is a private arrangement.
- [ ] H2 Step 2: if they cannot agree, the dependant applies to court. The marking scheme says the District Court or the Circuit Court, "depending on amount".
- [ ] H3 Step 3: the court hears the application and, if it grants it, makes a maintenance order setting out how much is paid and how often. A court order is enforceable by the court; an informal agreement is not enforced in the same way (assumed).
- [ ] H4 The court can vary (change) an order if circumstances change, for example if the payer's income falls or the child's needs grow (assumed).

Enforcement
- [ ] E1 If payments stop, the court can make an Attachment of Earnings Order: an amount is deducted from the payer's wages and paid directly to the dependant. Name the order exactly; it earns a mark.
- [ ] E2 Why it matters: the dependant does not have to chase the payer, and the money arrives before the payer can spend it. (Where the payer is self-employed, other enforcement is used; no detail needed.)

Fitting the Act into the wider picture
- [ ] F1 Maintenance can be claimed during a separation or after one, so the dependant's support is protected whatever the legal status of the marriage (assumed link; no detail on separation or divorce here).
- [ ] F2 The Act deals with money (income); it does not deal with the home, violence or children at risk. Each of those has its own Act, taught in the next subsections. Say this in one line so students keep the Acts apart.
- [ ] F3 Exam use: a 10-mark "explain how the Act protects the family" answer needs two developed points: (i) maintenance for a dependent spouse and dependent children when a relationship breaks down, including who counts as dependent; (ii) how it is obtained and enforced (agreement, then court order, then Attachment of Earnings Order).
- [ ] F4 Diagram: none.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the District Court's weekly or lump-sum limits on maintenance, because these are changing figures and the outline excludes them.
- Do not use: how maintenance is treated for income tax or for social welfare means tests, because those rules change and belong to other chapters.
- Do not use: any claim that an unmarried partner can claim maintenance for themselves under this Act, because the Act gives a partner's own claim to spouses only (civil partners have separate provisions). The only unmarried-couple point to make is that a child's maintenance is claimable whether or not the parents are married.
- Maintenance is regular support, not a one-off payment or a gift. An Attachment of Earnings Order is not a fine or penalty; it is a way of collecting what is owed.
- The test is dependency (M2, M4), not who has "custody". Do not make custody the condition of the right.
- Do not mention the "Family Law Act 1976 section 22 barring order": it is superseded (see the notes on barring orders).
- Divorce (ends the marriage; Family Law (Divorce) Act 1996 as amended by the Family Law Act 2019), judicial separation and deeds of separation belong to later subsections and to the notes on marriage breakdown. One clause is enough here: maintenance can continue alongside them.
- Neighbouring subject: the Family Home Protection Act covers the home, not income; keep "support" and "home" apart.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline or explain how the Act protects the family (10 marks, two developed points): who is covered (dependent spouse and dependent children), that the right exists whether couples live together or apart, and the route (agreement, then a District or Circuit Court order).
- State the meaning of maintenance, a dependent spouse and a dependent child. Students lose marks by leaving out the age limits for a dependent child.
- Name the enforcement tool: the Attachment of Earnings Order, with what it does (deducts from wages and pays the dependant directly).
- In wider "legal protection when a marriage breaks down" questions, name the Act with one line: it provides maintenance for dependants.
- In "rights and responsibilities of a couple", the financial duty to support a partner and children is credited under this Act.
- Expected depth: two developed points, each in one or two sentences, with the Act's full name written once.
