# Package: hom-housing-s6 — House design and building standards

- **loId:** `hom-housing-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-housing` · **syllabusRef:** 4.2 (Elective 1, Home Design and Management)
- **Syllabus scope:** factors that influence house design — aesthetic and environmental factors, present and future family requirements, energy efficiency, ergonomics, initial and maintenance costs, technological developments; **[HL]** regulation of house-building standards — the Building Regulations and building control (certificates of compliance required under the Building Control (Amendment) Regulations 2014), structural defects warranties such as HomeBond's, and grant provision such as SEAI home energy grants.
- **HL marks:** only the regulation block (Building Regulations, building control certificates, structural defects warranties, SEAI grants) is [HL]. Everything else, including the BER certificate, is for all levels.
- **Also examined:** the BER certificate (2014H, 2017H); legislation regulating building standards (2006H, 2011H, 2018H); evaluating a house plan for a stated family (2015O, 2017H, 2026O); design factors (2026H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Insulation methods, heating systems, ventilation and water supply are in the energy-efficient home and services chapter; interior design is in the interior chapter. Here the design factors are given at the level of the house as a whole.

## 1. Must cover

Aesthetic and environmental factors (one block)
- [ ] A1 Aesthetic: the elements of design (colour, pattern, shape, texture, line) and principles (proportion, balance, harmony) used to make an attractive home that does not date quickly; a design that suits its surroundings.
- [ ] A2 Aspect and orientation: living rooms south-facing for daylight and solar heat, bedrooms and utility rooms placed by use and the sun's path; passive solar design uses layout, orientation and glazing to gain and keep heat without machinery.
- [ ] A3 Environmental: durable, sustainable and locally sourced materials; low energy used in making and running the house; fit with landscape and neighbouring buildings; slope and drainage of the site affect layout and foundations.

Present and future family requirements (one block)
- [ ] F1 Present: the number and ages of family members set bedrooms, bathrooms, living space and storage; study space for teenagers; home-office space; rooms planned for use (for example a utility room); safe design for young children.
- [ ] F2 Future: room to extend, convert the attic or add a ground-floor bedroom; flexible rooms (a playroom that becomes a study); accessibility for ageing or a disability (wide doors, level access, accessible bathroom).
- [ ] F3 Evaluating a plan: look at the layout for the family: relationship of kitchen, living and bedrooms; use of space; safety and supervision for children; storage; light and aspect; flexibility; accessibility; cost. Name a strength and a weakness and a modification with a reason (as Ordinary Level 2026 asks).

Energy efficiency and the BER certificate (one block)
- [ ] E1 Design features that cut heat loss and fuel use: insulation in roof, walls and floor; double or triple glazing; draught proofing; compact shape; passive solar gain; a well-controlled heating system (zones, thermostats, timers); renewable energy such as solar panels or a heat pump; efficient lighting (LED) and appliances. Each feature with its effect.
- [ ] E2 Building Energy Rating (BER): a certificate that rates a home's energy performance, like the energy label on an appliance. The rating scale runs from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient); the very top band is now A0 (a home that uses no fossil fuel for heating). A registered BER assessor inspects the home and calculates the rating, looking at the area of rooms and windows, thickness of walls, insulation, the heating system, and floor and wall types, and issues an advisory report on improvements.
- [ ] E3 A BER is required by law when a home is built, sold or rented, and the rating must be shown in every advertisement for sale or rent. A certificate lasts ten years unless the home is upgraded. A better BER means lower running costs, a more comfortable home and often higher value.

Ergonomics and technological developments (one block)
- [ ] G1 Ergonomics: designing the space to suit how people move and work. Kitchen work triangle (sink, cooker and fridge); circulation space; counter heights; switches and sockets at reachable heights; storage within easy reach; safe stairs; accessible layout for people with limited mobility.
- [ ] G2 Technological developments in design: energy-saving lighting (LED, sensor lights); zoned heating with programmers and smart thermostats; underfloor heating; solar thermal and solar photovoltaic panels; heat pumps; heat-recovery ventilation; smart home controls; alarm and security systems linked to a phone; electronic gates and garage doors; modern methods of construction (factory-built timber frame or panels).

Initial and maintenance costs (one block)
- [ ] K1 Initial costs: site and servicing, the builder, materials, professional fees (architect, engineer), legal fees, planning and lender costs, stamp duty on a purchase, and furnishing.
- [ ] K2 Maintenance costs: cleaning, decorating, repair of roof and gutters, servicing of the heating system, replacing worn parts.
- [ ] K3 Choices trade initial cost against running cost: durable, low-maintenance materials (brick, uPVC windows) and good insulation cost more at the start and save over the life of the house; a cheap build can cost more in repairs and heating.

Regulation of house-building standards (Higher Level only)
- [ ] B1 Building Regulations: legal standards for the design and construction of buildings. They cover (by technical part) structure, fire safety, resistance to moisture, materials, sound, ventilation, hygiene, drainage, heat-producing appliances, stairs and guards, conservation of fuel and energy (Part L) and access for people with disabilities (Part M). They are made under the Building Control Act 1990.
- [ ] B2 Building control: local authorities, as building control authorities, enforce the regulations and inspect building work.
- [ ] B3 Building Control (Amendment) Regulations 2014: new building works must be notified to the building control authority with a commencement notice, with certificates of compliance on design, and an assigned certifier and a builder who undertake to inspect and build in line with the regulations. On completion, a certificate of compliance on completion, signed by the builder and the assigned certifier, is lodged with the building control authority and recorded on a public register before the building is opened or occupied. The aim is to hold designers, builders and certifiers responsible for compliance.
- [ ] B4 Structural defects warranty (such as HomeBond's): registered builders' new homes are inspected during construction and a structural defects warranty or insurance covers the cost of repairing major structural defects for ten years from completion. Lenders often require such a warranty (or equivalent latent defects insurance) before lending on a new home.
- [ ] B5 Grants: SEAI (Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland) home energy grants support the cost of insulation, heating upgrades such as heat pumps, and solar panels in existing homes. State the aim, not amounts.
- [ ] B6 Why standards matter: safety, comfort, durability, energy saving, a lender may require them, and resale value.
- [ ] B7 Marking schemes accept any two or three regulation methods (Regulations, a warranty, the BER); each needs its name, who runs it and what it does.

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] AK1 (assumed) Local authority; planning permission is separate from building control (see "Location, planning and professional services").

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Building Regulations Act 1991" (named in 2006, 2011 and 2018 marking schemes), because there is no such Act: the Building Regulations are made under the Building Control Act 1990.
- Do not use: "National House Building Guarantee Scheme" as a name or as a government scheme, because HomeBond is a company giving structural warranties (not a department scheme); the outline names "structural defects warranties such as HomeBond's".
- Do not use: that HomeBond protects the buyer's deposit if the builder fails, because it was not confirmed (older marking schemes say so); give structural defects cover only.
- Do not use: the HomeBond cover limit, excess, or the five-year cover for water ingress and smoke, because they are changing figures.
- Do not use: "triple A rated" or the sub-ratings A1 to G (B1, B2, C3 and so on), because the BER scale was simplified on 24 May 2026 to A0, A, B, C, D, E, F and G; the marking schemes of 2014 and 2017 name the old scale.
- Do not use: compact fluorescent lamps as an energy-saving choice, because they have been phased out of sale in the EU; say LED and sensor lights.
- Do not use: grant amounts, scheme names or eligibility (SEAI grants change often).
- The BER is an energy rating (A to G), not a building safety certificate; the certificate of compliance on completion is a different document (compliance with the Building Regulations).
- A BER certificate is not a structural warranty; a structural defects warranty does not guarantee energy performance.
- Do not mark the whole subsection [HL]: the existing notes correctly mark only the regulation block.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: planning permission and professional services (location, planning and professional services); insulation methods, heating systems, ventilation and solar energy in detail (the energy-efficient home chapter); room design, colour, texture and lighting (the interior chapter).

## 4. What exams ask

- Factors that influence house design, Higher Level 2026 (15 marks): family requirements (size, stage, present and future needs), technological developments, and aesthetic and environmental factors, each developed with examples.
- BER informative note (2014H and 2017H, 12 to 15 marks; three points at five marks): a rating scale from A (most efficient) to G, compulsory for homes built, sold or rented, shown in advertisements, assessed by a registered assessor, what the assessor measures, and the advice on improvements. Older schemes mention "triple A"; the notes use the current scale.
- Regulation of building standards (2006H, 2011H, 2018H; 14 to 16 marks): the Building Regulations and building control by local authorities, certificates of compliance, a structural defects warranty such as HomeBond's (registered builders, inspections, ten-year cover on major structural defects), and the BER. Name each, who runs it and what it does; say why standards matter (safety, sustainability, lenders require them).
- Evaluate a given house plan for a stated family and suggest modifications (Ordinary Level 2015, 2026 and Higher Level 2017): strengths (kitchen position, space, garden access, single storey for young children), weaknesses, two modifications for future needs with reasons.
- Energy-efficient design features (Ordinary Level 2025, six marks for three features): insulation, glazing, zoned heating with thermostats and timers, passive solar design, each with how it saves energy.
- Marks go to the feature, the reason it saves energy or money, and the link to the family's situation.
