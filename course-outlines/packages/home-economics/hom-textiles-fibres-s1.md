# Package: hom-textiles-fibres-s1 — Textiles as a household resource

- **loId:** `hom-textiles-fibres-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-textiles-fibres` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.6 (core)
- **Syllabus scope:** the use of textiles as a resource for household and clothing purposes; selection criteria: fitness for purpose and suitability in use, determined by fabric properties, cost, personal choice and aesthetic appeal. Also examined: functions and uses of household textiles (2025O, 2023O, 2021O, 2009O); fabric properties such as absorbent, flammable, crease-resistant, colourfast (2026O, 2012O, 2010H, 2007O); choosing and recommending fabrics for curtains and other items (2022O, 2018H, 2017O, 2009O, 2006O); selection criteria assessed at length (2024H). No Higher Level only items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. This subsection is the core (all students) overview. Fire safety is the next subsection; fibre classification and fabric profiles are Elective 2 and come later. Care symbols and textile care belong to the clothing chapter.

## 1. Must cover

Why textiles matter at home (one block)
- [ ] T1 A textile is a material made from fibres or yarns (woven, knitted or otherwise made into fabric). Textiles are a household resource: they are bought, used, cared for and replaced, so choosing well saves money and effort. They serve household purposes (bedding, curtains, carpets, towels, upholstery) and clothing purposes. (assumed: fibre, yarn and fabric as words; fibres are taught in later subsections.)
- [ ] T2 Textiles usually do several jobs at once (a curtain gives privacy, decoration and some warmth).

Functions and uses of household textiles (one block; a list examiners ask for)
- [ ] F1 Warmth and heat insulation: blankets, duvets, throws, thick curtains trap still air and reduce heat loss.
- [ ] F2 Sound absorption (sound insulation): carpets, rugs, curtains, upholstery.
- [ ] F3 Privacy and light control: curtains, blinds, net curtains.
- [ ] F4 Comfort: cushions, mattress covers, upholstery, bed linen.
- [ ] F5 Protection: oven gloves, aprons, carpets protecting floors, mattress protectors.
- [ ] F6 Absorbency: towels, tea towels, dishcloths, bath mats (washing and drying).
- [ ] F7 Decoration and aesthetics: curtains, cushions, wall hangings, bedspreads, rugs add colour, pattern and texture.
- [ ] F8 Hygiene: washable textiles can be laundered. Safety (fire-resistant finishes) is a further function named in a marking scheme.

Selection criteria (one block; examiners often ask for three or four)
- [ ] S1 Fitness (suitability) for purpose: the fabric's properties match the job, e.g. absorbent for a towel, good drape and fade resistance for curtains.
- [ ] S2 Suitability in use: stands up to the wear, washing and conditions of the room (kitchen, child's bedroom, living room).
- [ ] S3 Cost: price against budget and expected life (durability); wool and silk cost more than most other fibres.
- [ ] S4 Personal choice: likes and dislikes, colour and style, comfort, preference for natural or easy-care synthetic fabrics, current trends.
- [ ] S5 Aesthetic appeal: appearance, drape, weight, texture, lustre, colour, pattern.
- [ ] S6 Care and cleaning: washable or dry clean, ironing needed, fade and stain resistance, shrinkage.
- [ ] S7 Safety: flame-retardant finish, fire-resistance label, loose or pile surface avoided (detail in the next subsection; one line here).
- [ ] S8 Durability, finishes and sustainability are accepted in a 12-mark Higher answer; give one line each.

Fabric properties (one block; define in plain words, each with an example)
- [ ] P1 Absorbent: takes in and holds moisture (cotton, linen: towels). It also takes up dye easily.
- [ ] P2 Flammable: ignites and burns readily; a hazard (cotton burns easily); not wanted in household textiles.
- [ ] P3 Crease resistant: resists wrinkling in wear and washing, returns to a smooth shape, less ironing (polyester, wool; cotton and linen crease unless finished).
- [ ] P4 Colourfast: colour does not run in washing or fade in light, because the dye is fixed to the fibre.
- [ ] P5 Durable / hard wearing: resists abrasion, tearing and wear.
- [ ] P6 Drape: how a fabric hangs in folds (curtains, loose garments).
- [ ] P7 Other properties examiners accept: warm, strong, washable, easy to dye, shrink resistant, stain resistant, quick drying, resilient.
- [ ] P8 Static: synthetic fibres build up static electricity so fabric clings; fabric conditioner reduces it (2019O). Wool can shrink in hot washing (2019O).

Matching fabrics to items (one block)
- [ ] M1 Towels: cotton, absorbent, strong when wet, stands hot washing. Tea towels: linen (absorbent, strong, quick drying, creases).
- [ ] M2 Curtains: polyester (drapes well, washable, fade resistant, resists creasing, dries quickly) or cotton (easy to launder and dye, crisp, strong when wet).
- [ ] M3 Blankets: wool (warm, insulating, absorbent, resilient; shrinks and felts if washed carelessly). Carpets: nylon or wool (hard wearing, resists abrasion).
- [ ] M4 Bed linen: cotton or polyester-cotton (washable, strong, easy to iron). Upholstery fabric is in the next subsection.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "natural fibres cost more than most synthetic fibres", because cotton and linen are not dear; say wool and silk cost more, as the scheme does.
- Do not use: brand names, store names or any price figure, because they date and are not examined.
- Fire safety detail (Proban, upholstery labels, fabric structure and fire risk), fibre origins and processes (fibre classification, fabric profiles) and care symbols or stain removal (clothing care) belong to their own subsections or chapters; give one line only.
- "Flammable" and "inflammable" mean the same; use flammable.
- "Suitability for purpose" and "suitability in use" differ: purpose = the job; use = the wear and conditions. Give both under fitness.
- Absorbency is about moisture only; do not use it to mean warm.
- Irritant, flammable and windproof are not properties wanted in household textiles (2012O distractors).

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline or describe three or four functions of household textiles, each with an example item (most years at Ordinary Level Section A; also a four-function part in Section B).
- Explain how suitability for purpose, personal choice and cost influence selection (3 x 2 marks); factors for choosing household textile items; assess selection criteria for a 12-mark Higher answer (developed points on cost, properties, aesthetics, care, safety, trends, durability, sustainability).
- Explain absorbent, flammable, crease resistant or colourfast with an example fabric for one.
- Suggest two fabrics for living room curtains, or two household textiles with one property each; recommend a fabric for a stated item with a reason.
- Tick which properties are desirable in household textiles.
- Complete statements: static (conditioner), shrink (wool), Proban (flame retardant).
