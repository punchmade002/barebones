# Package: hom-unemployment-poverty-s1 — Defining unemployment

- **loId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty` · **syllabusRef:** 6.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "Defining unemployment" [6.5], and **[HL]** "knowledge of the extent of unemployment in Ireland" [6.5]. The definition is common to Ordinary and Higher Level; the extent of unemployment is Higher Level only.
- **Also examined:** the definition is asked every few years as a short question (2023O 6 marks, 2022H 6 marks, 2018O 6 marks, 2006O 6 marks, and 2009H with "comment on the extent"). Higher Level data questions give a table of unemployment rates over several years and ask the student to comment on the trend and explain it (2017H, 2012H). Explaining the trend uses the causes in a neighbouring subsection; the "comment on the table" skill belongs here.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Checked: CSO Labour Force Survey background notes (ILO definition, two-week availability, four-week search, 15-74 age band, long-term = one year or more). ## 1. Must cover

The definition (one block)
- [ ] D1 Unemployment: a person of working age who is without paid work, available for work and actively seeking work. All three conditions must hold. The marking schemes accept two or three of these (some say "willing" for "available"), so give all three.
- [ ] D2 Available for work: could start a job within a short time; the CSO's standard measure uses two weeks. Give "normally within two weeks".
- [ ] D3 Actively seeking work: has taken specific steps to find work, such as applying for jobs, answering advertisements, contacting employers or registering with Intreo, the Department of Social Protection's public employment service.
- [ ] D4 Who is not counted: students in full-time education, people in full-time home duties, retired people, and people unable to work through illness or disability, because they are not available for, or not seeking, work. A part-time worker who wants more hours counts as employed in the standard measure (underemployed; one line).
- [ ] D5 Paid work is the test (assumed): unpaid work such as volunteering or caring at home does not make a person employed. Paid and unpaid work are examined elsewhere in the social studies; do not redefine them here.

Measuring unemployment (one block)
- [ ] M1 (assumed) Labour force: everyone in employment plus everyone unemployed. Unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ labour force × 100. A rate compares years or regions better than a raw number because the labour force changes in size.
- [ ] M2 The Central Statistics Office (CSO) Labour Force Survey is a household survey that applies the International Labour Organization (ILO) definition and gives the "standardised unemployment rate", the official measure. It is published monthly and quoted seasonally adjusted.
- [ ] M3 The Live Register is the Department of Social Protection's count of people claiming jobseeker payments (signing on). It includes some part-time, casual and seasonal workers, so it is not the official unemployment figure. Data questions quote both (2012H gave claimant numbers for Jobseeker's Benefit and Jobseeker's Allowance beside the standardised rate).

Types by duration (one block)
- [ ] T1 Short-term unemployment: a brief spell between jobs or after leaving education. Frictional unemployment is the short-term unemployment caused by the normal time taken to move from one job to another (assumed term; the syllabus's six causes are in a neighbouring subsection).
- [ ] T2 Long-term unemployment: out of work for one year or more. Skills, habits and confidence erode with time, which makes re-entry to work harder (one line; residual unemployment is a neighbouring subsection).
- [ ] T3 Young people have tended to have higher rates of unemployment than older workers, especially in downturns (2018O's stem was a newspaper line on youth unemployment). One sentence, no figures.

Extent of unemployment in Ireland [HL] (one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] E1 [HL] Extent means how many people are unemployed, as a rate and as a number, and how that changes over time. It follows the economic cycle: it rises in recession and falls in growth.
- [ ] E2 [HL] The pattern since the 2000s: low unemployment in the boom years of the 2000s; a sharp rise after the downturn that began in 2008, with construction and related professions worst affected and retail and service industries also hit; a peak of roughly 15% in the early 2010s; a steady fall as the economy recovered and jobs returned in construction, retail, services and multinational firms.
- [ ] E3 [HL] Emigration rose during the downturn (the 2012H scheme names it). It held the measured rate down, so the rate alone understates the damage.
- [ ] E4 [HL] Distribution: unemployment is not spread evenly. It differs by region, by age (higher among young people), by education (higher among people with few or no qualifications) and between unemployment black spots (areas that lost their main employer) and areas with plenty of work. Long-term unemployment forms a hard core that remains when the overall rate falls.
- [ ] E5 [HL] How to comment on a table of rates: state the trend (rise, peak, fall), name the period of sharpest change, link it to the economy (recession, then recovery) and add a cause or effect. The 2017H and 2012H schemes give a mark for referring to the table itself.
- [ ] E6 [HL] The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 caused a sudden temporary rise in joblessness, and the State paid a temporary pandemic unemployment payment. One sentence, optional, no figures.

## 3. Traps

- Unemployed is not the same as "not working": students, home-makers, retired people and sick or disabled people who are not seeking work are outside the measure.
- The Live Register is not the unemployment rate. Do not call it "the number of unemployed".
- Payment names are Jobseeker's Benefit and Jobseeker's Allowance, not "unemployment benefit"; their detail belongs to the statutory responses subsection and appears here only as the Live Register's main payments.
- Seasonal, technical, geographical and residual causes belong to the causes subsection; the effects belong to the effects subsection. Only frictional and long-term types are named here.
- Do not use: any rate, peak or Live Register total other than "roughly 15% in the early 2010s", because rates change monthly and the figures in past papers (the 2006-2016 rate tables, the 327,000 peak, the 14.4% rate quoted for October 2011) belong to those papers.
- Do not use: "recent graduates, foreign workers and young people were among the groups hit hardest after 2008", because the schemes name construction and related professions, and retail and service industries, as the sectors worst hit; the group claim is unconfirmed. Say only that young people generally have higher rates.
- Do not use: a named region as having the highest or lowest rate (the 2012H scheme names the border region and south-east as highest and the mid-east as lowest), because regional rankings change; say that rates differ between regions.
- Do not use: "more men than women are unemployed" as a present fact (2006O asked why for 2005), because the gap followed the collapse of male-dominated construction and is not a standing pattern.
- Do not use: "registering with Intreo shows a person is seeking work" as a rule by itself, because the CSO test is specific steps to find work; registering is one such step.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define unemployment (6 marks, Ordinary and Higher): no paid job, available for work, actively seeking work; two to three points.
- Comment on a table or news extract of unemployment rates (Higher Level, 20 marks, four points at 5 marks each): one point on the trend in the table, then extent, causes and effects; linking the rise to the 2008 downturn and the fall to economic recovery is the core of the answer.
- Discuss the extent and distribution of unemployment in Ireland (Higher Level, 20 marks in 2026H): change over time with the economic cycle, and variation by region, age, education and black spots.
- Distinguish the Live Register from the standardised rate (used inside data questions).
- Define short-term and long-term unemployment in a few words.
- Tip material: write all three conditions; always give a direction and a reason for extent; do not quote numbers unless the question supplies them.
