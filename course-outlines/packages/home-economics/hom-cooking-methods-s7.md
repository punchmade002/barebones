## 1. Must cover

Weighting and structure (one block)
- [ ] W1 The Food Studies practical coursework is worth 20% of the total marks for every candidate; the written paper carries the rest (the textiles coursework for Elective 2 candidates is separate and is covered elsewhere).
- [ ] W2 Each assignment is marked out of 80, recorded in the SEC's digital Food Studies Practical Coursework Journal under four headings.
- [ ] W3 Marks for each assignment: investigation (analysis and research) 32; planning and preparation 8 (ingredients with quantities and costing, key equipment); implementation 28 (the procedure followed, key factors considered, safety and hygiene); evaluation 12 (of the implementation and of the assignment's specific requirements). Total 80.
- [ ] W4 Number of assignments: for 2027, candidates present two of the five assignments the SEC set, and where more than two are presented the two highest-scoring count (SEC Assessment Arrangements 2027). The assignment brief itself still prints the standing instruction to complete four; the Arrangements document overrides it. The completion date was 2 November 2026.

Areas of practice (one block)
- [ ] A1 Every assignment sits in one of five areas: A Application of Nutritional Principles; B Food Preparation and Cooking Processes (new skills or equipment); C Food Technology; D Properties of a Food; E Comparative Analysis including Sensory Analysis.
- [ ] A2 No Area C assignment was set for 2027.

What the investigation covers in each area (one block)
- [ ] I1 Area A: the nutritional needs investigated, appropriate meal-planning guidelines, and suitable or modified dishes.
- [ ] I2 Area B: the preparation process, skill or equipment, the key points for success, and dishes that illustrate it.
- [ ] I3 Area C: the processing method and its principles, containers, packaging and labelling, and suitable foods or dishes.
- [ ] I4 Area D: the definition of the property, the principles associated with it, and dishes that illustrate it.
- [ ] I5 Area E: the testing technique and its possible outcomes, the foods or products tested, and the conditions to control.
- [ ] I6 In every area, sources of information are cited.

Rules (one block)
- [ ] R1 Dishes must meet the requirements of the assignment; a dish may not be repeated, or partly repeated, across assignments.
- [ ] R2 Fresh foods should be used instead of convenience foods.
- [ ] R3 Practical work may be done in pairs, or in groups for Area E (sensory testing); the written record must be the candidate's own.
- [ ] R4 All sources must be referenced, including AI-generated material.

The five 2027 assignments (one block, with the linked theory named by subject)
- [ ] T1 Assignment 1 (Area A): challenges older people face in keeping a healthy diet; their nutritional needs and meal-planning guidelines; two-course main-meal menus; prepare one main course. Theory: meal planning for older people and special diets.
- [ ] T2 Assignment 2 (Area A): causes and effects of iron deficiency; nutritional needs and meal-planning factors that reduce the risk; two-course main-meal menus; prepare one main course. Theory: minerals (iron) and meal planning.
- [ ] T3 Assignment 3 (Area B): commercially prepared filo and puff pastry (brands, forms, ingredients, cost, sweet and savoury dishes); key points for success with one; prepare one dish; evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of the chosen pastry. Theory: pastry in the cereals chapter.
- [ ] T4 Assignment 4 (Area D): types of marinade and dishes using each; benefits of marinating and its principles; key points for success; prepare one marinated dish; evaluate whether the marinade achieved its purpose. Theory: meat tenderising.
- [ ] T5 Assignment 5 (Area E): descriptive rating tests and their use in the food industry; the range of commercial granola (brands, ingredients, nutritional content, cost); a descriptive rating test on two similar brands with six agreed sensory attributes and star diagrams; comparison of the two sensory profiles. Theory: sensory analysis in the food additives and processing chapter.
- [ ] T6 Each of this chapter's other subsections supplies skills for the practical: cooking methods, soups and sauces, equipment, recipe balance and presentation (link in one line).

## 4. What exams ask

The coursework itself is not examined on the written paper, but its themes are (older people's meal planning, iron deficiency, pastry, tenderising and marinades, sensory analysis). Expect students to need:
- the 20% weighting and the 32/8/28/12 mark split, and the number of assignments;
- which area an assignment falls in and what the investigation for it must cover;
- the rules (no repeated dishes, fresh foods, own written record, sources referenced);
- how each assignment links to theory they revise elsewhere.
