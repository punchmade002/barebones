## 1. Must cover

Classification (one block; 2010H "name two types and one difference")
- [ ] C1 Pastry is classified by how the fat is combined with the flour and how it rises: short (fat rubbed in; shortcrust, sweet shortcrust, rich shortcrust, biscuit and cheese pastry), flaked or laminated (fat in pieces or layers; rough puff, flaky, puff), choux (cooked paste, raised by steam), suet (suet and self-raising flour, steamed or baked) and filo (very thin sheets layered with fat).
- [ ] C2 Differences the schemes accept: ingredients, proportions, method, characteristics or appearance.
- [ ] C3 Dishes (2024O, 2019O): shortcrust - apple tart, quiche, mince pies; rough puff or puff - sausage rolls, vol-au-vents, pie toppings; choux - eclairs, profiteroles; filo - samosas, strudel; suet - steamed pudding.

Shortcrust pastry (one block, one of the two types)
- [ ] S1 Ingredients: plain flour, pinch of salt, fat in half the weight of the flour (butter, margarine, or a mix), cold water to bind; sugar for sweet shortcrust.
- [ ] S2 Method: sieve flour and salt; rub in cold fat with the fingertips until like fine breadcrumbs (lifting the mixture to add air); add cold water gradually and mix with a knife; bring together lightly; rest in a cool place; roll lightly on a floured surface; bake in a hot, preheated oven.
- [ ] S3 Why it works: rubbing in coats the flour with fat, which limits gluten and makes the pastry short (crumbly); too much liquid or handling toughens it.
- [ ] S4 Cooking: bake hot (about 200 degrees Celsius, gas 6) until golden; blind bake a case for a filling that is not cooked, to prevent a soggy base.
- [ ] S5 Presentation: tarts, quiche, pies, mince pies; edges crimped or fluted; glazed with beaten egg or milk, or dusted with icing sugar; neat, evenly browned, crisp and not shrunk.

Rough puff pastry (one block, one of the two types)
- [ ] R1 Ingredients: plain flour, pinch of salt, hard fat (butter or block margarine) in a high proportion (about three-quarters of the flour), cold water, a little lemon juice optional.
- [ ] R2 Method: cut cold fat into cubes and mix lightly with flour (the lumps stay); bind with cold water; roll into a rectangle; fold in three and turn a quarter; repeat the rolling and folding several times, resting and chilling between turns.
- [ ] R3 Why it rises: the layers of dough and fat; in a hot oven the water in the dough and fat turns to steam and lifts the layers; the fat melts into the dough and the layers set, giving a flaky, light pastry.
- [ ] R4 Cooking: very hot oven (about 220 degrees Celsius, gas 7 to 8), preheated; do not open the door early.
- [ ] R5 Presentation: sausage rolls, vol-au-vents, pie lids, Eccles cakes; glazed with egg wash (not on the cut edges, so layers can rise); even risen layers, sharp edges.

Principles for success (one block; 2026O, 2022H, 6 marks)
- [ ] P1 Keep ingredients, hands, utensils and kitchen cool so the fat does not melt.
- [ ] P2 Weigh accurately; correct proportions of flour to fat.
- [ ] P3 Sieve the flour to introduce air.
- [ ] P4 Rub in lightly with the fingertips; do not overwork; handle as little as possible.
- [ ] P5 Add cold water gradually, to avoid sticky or tough pastry.
- [ ] P6 Rest or chill the dough so the gluten relaxes and shrinkage is reduced.
- [ ] P7 Roll lightly in one direction, evenly, without stretching.
- [ ] P8 Bake in a preheated hot oven; blind bake where needed; place on a hot baking tray.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "spring rolls" as a filo dish, because spring rolls use a separate spring roll wrapper though a scheme accepted it; give samosas or strudel.
- Do not use: "suet pastry is a short pastry" and also a separate type in the same list, because the current notes list it twice; treat suet as its own type (fat is shredded suet, not rubbed in).
- Do not use: "knead the dough" for shortcrust, because kneading develops gluten and toughens it; say "bring together lightly".
- Do not use: croissants as a puff-type pastry dish, because croissants are a laminated yeasted dough, not puff pastry.
- Do not use: "puff pastry is leavened by baking powder", because it is raised by steam and trapped air in layers.
- Do not use: exact oven temperatures or fat percentages as scheme requirements, because the schemes only say "hot oven" and "accurate proportions"; give them only as approximate.
- Do not use: "all-butter pastry is always better", because the schemes accept butter or margarine or lard.
- Shortcrust and rough puff differ in method: rubbing in versus folding and rolling; do not mix the two methods.
- Neighbouring subsections: gluten structure and flour types in the milling subsection; steam and air as raising agents, and dextrinisation, in the cooking subsection; the general classification and methods of cakes and sauces elsewhere in the cooking-methods chapter.
- The syllabus asks for two types only; do not teach the full method for all five.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name two types of pastry and a different dish from each (2024O, 2019O).
- Name two types and state one difference between them (2010H: ingredients, proportions, method, characteristics, appearance).
- Outline three guidelines to follow for light, successful pastry (2026O, 2022H): cool ingredients and kitchen, accurate weighing, sieving, light handling and fingertip rubbing in, cold liquid added gradually, resting, rolling lightly, preheated hot oven, blind baking.
- Longer questions: give an account of preparing one named pastry with the principles for success, how it is cooked and presented.
- Explain why a pastry is tough, shrunk, soggy or heavy (overhandling, too much water, no resting, oven too cool).
