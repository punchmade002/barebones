# Package: hom-consumer-rights-s5 — Voluntary and statutory consumer protection bodies

- **loId:** `hom-consumer-rights-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-consumer-rights` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2.3
- **Syllabus scope:** voluntary and statutory consumer protection bodies, e.g. the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission, Citizens Information, the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman, the Advertising Standards Authority. Also examined: 2024H, 2022H, 2019H, 2016H, 2015H, 2013H (and Ordinary papers 2014, 2007, 2005). No [HL] items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Ordinary and Higher Level. Papers name bodies that have since been renamed or replaced (Director of Consumer Affairs, National Consumer Agency, Comhairle, Financial Services Ombudsman); for June 2027 teach only the current ones below. The Acts these bodies enforce are in the consumer protection legislation subsection; how a consumer uses them in a complaint is in the complaints subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Statutory against voluntary (one block)
- [ ] D1 A statutory body is set up by law (an Act of the Oireachtas) and has legal powers, funded by the State. A voluntary body is independent or industry-run, with no legal powers, and works by information, advice, research, lobbying or self-regulation.
- [ ] D2 The usual route: complain to the trader first, then use the body that fits the problem.
- [ ] D3 Exam wording: "name a statutory body" or "name a voluntary body" needs the body in the correct class plus its role.

Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC, statutory)
- [ ] S1 Established by statute in 2014 (it took over the work of the National Consumer Agency and the Competition Authority).
- [ ] S2 Enforces consumer protection law, including the Consumer Protection Act 2007; investigates unfair and illegal trading practices, including misleading advertising and pricing; can prosecute.
- [ ] S3 Informs consumers of their rights through its website, helpline and campaigns, including on personal finance.
- [ ] S4 Enforces product-safety rules for non-food products.
- [ ] S5 Promotes competition and advises government on consumer issues.
- [ ] S6 It does not resolve individual disputes between a consumer and a trader.

Citizens Information and the Citizens Information Board (statutory)
- [ ] C1 The Citizens Information Board is the statutory body; it funds and supports the Citizens Information Centres and services.
- [ ] C2 Functions (two to be given in exam): provides free, confidential information and advice on rights and entitlements, including consumer rights; supports local Citizens Information Centres; runs the phone service and website; also covers social welfare, housing and health information.

Ombudsmen (statutory)
- [ ] O1 The Office of the Ombudsman investigates complaints about the administrative actions of public bodies (government departments, the HSE, local authorities).
- [ ] O2 The Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman (FSPO) investigates complaints about financial service providers (banks, insurers, credit unions, pension providers). It replaced the Financial Services Ombudsman and the Pensions Ombudsman in 2018.
- [ ] O3 Both are free for the complainant and are a last resort: the consumer first uses the organisation's own complaints procedure.

Other statutory bodies accepted in schemes
- [ ] T1 Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI): set up under the Food Safety Authority of Ireland Act 1998; sets and monitors food safety standards and informs consumers; official agencies inspect on its behalf.
- [ ] T2 Central Bank of Ireland: regulates financial service providers and protects consumers of financial services (the schemes accept it).
- [ ] T3 National Standards Authority of Ireland (NSAI): sets and certifies standards (the schemes accept it).
- [ ] T4 European Consumer Centre Ireland: advises consumers on cross-border purchases within the EU; run by the CCPC as part of an EU network (schemes list it as statutory or voluntary; give the role).
- [ ] T5 The small claims procedure is a court route, not a body (see complaints subsection).

Voluntary bodies
- [ ] V1 Consumers' Association of Ireland (CAI): independent, non-profit; researches and tests goods and services and publishes findings in Consumer Choice; gives objective information and advice; lobbies government; represents consumers on bodies; founded 1966.
- [ ] V2 Advertising Standards Authority (ASA; the body appears as the Advertising Standards Authority for Ireland, ASAI, in older material): self-regulatory, set up by the advertising industry; promotes high standards through a code (advertisements to be legal, decent, honest and truthful); investigates complaints from the public; where a complaint is upheld the advertiser is asked to amend or withdraw the advertisement.
- [ ] V3 Trade associations: look after members' interests; may have codes of practice and complaints procedures.
- [ ] V4 Citizens Information Centres, as local services, are accepted as voluntary in some schemes (they are staffed partly by volunteers).

Matching the body to the problem
- [ ] M1 Misleading price or advertising claim: CCPC (the law) or the ASA (the code).
- [ ] M2 Complaint about a bank or insurer: the provider first, then the FSPO.
- [ ] M3 Complaint about a public service: the Office of the Ombudsman.
- [ ] M4 Where to find consumer information: Citizens Information, the CCPC, CAI.
- [ ] M5 Faulty goods from an EU trader online: European Consumer Centre Ireland.
- [ ] M6 Unsafe or mislabelled food: FSAI.

## 3. Traps

- Statutory and voluntary are the two classes asked. Do not put the ASA or CAI under statutory, and do not put the CCPC, Ombudsman or Citizens Information Board under voluntary.
- The CCPC enforces the law; the ASA enforces a code of its own industry. Give the ASA as complaint handling and self-regulation, not prosecution.
- The Ombudsmen deal with complaints about public bodies and about financial providers; they do not deal with a faulty product from a shop.
- The Acts enforced are in the legislation subsection; the step-by-step complaint is in the complaints subsection.
- Do not use: the Director of Consumer Affairs, Office of Consumer Affairs or National Consumer Agency as a current body (2006, 2007 and 2014 schemes), because the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission replaced them.
- Do not use: Comhairle (2007 scheme), because it is the former name of the Citizens Information Board.
- Do not use: the Financial Services Ombudsman or the Pensions Ombudsman as separate bodies, because the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman replaced both.
- Do not use: "the ASA can force an advertiser to withdraw an advertisement", because it has no legal powers and relies on industry compliance; write that it asks the advertiser to amend or withdraw.
- Do not use: a prosecution count, a levy figure or the number of consumer complaints for any body, because they change.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name one statutory (or one voluntary) body and give its role (6 marks). Full marks need a correct name plus two points of role.
- Name three statutory bodies (6 marks): the CCPC, Office of the Ombudsman, FSAI, Citizens Information Board, Central Bank, NSAI, European Consumer Centre.
- Outline two functions of the Citizens Information Board or the role of the Consumers' Association of Ireland (6 marks; 11 marks at 3 for the name plus two roles).
- Name and give details of one statutory agency that protects consumers (10 marks): the CCPC with enforcement, investigation, information, product safety and advice to government.
- Matching a complaint to a body (bank to FSPO, advert to the ASA).

## Answer pattern for "name a body and give its role"

Give the full name (not the acronym alone), say whether it is statutory or voluntary if asked, then two or three roles in separate sentences. For the CCPC: enforces consumer law and the Consumer Protection Act 2007; investigates unfair and illegal trading including misleading advertising; informs consumers through its website, helpline and campaigns; enforces non-food product safety. For the Consumers' Association of Ireland: researches and tests goods and services; publishes findings in Consumer Choice; gives objective information and advice; lobbies government and represents consumers. For the Citizens Information Board: free and confidential information on rights and entitlements; funds and supports local centres; phone service and website. For the Advertising Standards Authority: sets and promotes the advertising code; investigates public complaints; asks advertisers to amend or withdraw advertisements that break the code.
