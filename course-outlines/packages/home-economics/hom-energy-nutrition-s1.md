# Package: hom-energy-nutrition-s1 — Energy requirements and the role of energy

- **loId:** `hom-energy-nutrition-s1` · **chapterId:** `hom-energy-nutrition` · **syllabusRef:** 1.2.1
- **Syllabus scope:** factors determining energy requirements; the role of energy in the body: basal metabolic rate, growth, physical activity. Also examined: BMR and the factors affecting it (2026H, 2023H, 2021H; also 2019O, 2014H, 2009O, 2006H); factors that affect the energy requirements of adults, teenagers or a named individual (2026O, 2023O, 2019O, 2016O, 2013O, 2009O, 2007O, 2005O); functions of energy in the body (2010H).
  No item in this subsection is Higher Level only. Both levels answer the same questions; Higher Level answers are expected to explain how each factor changes the requirement.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. This subsection explains what energy is for and what decides how much a person needs. Balancing intake against output, obesity and BMI are in "Energy balance". The kJ values of the nutrients are in "Energy value of the nutrients". The Food Pyramid, RDAs and menus are in "Dietary guidelines".

## 1. Must cover

What energy is and how it is measured (one block)
- [ ] E1 Energy is what the body needs to carry out every function: breathing, circulation, nerve and brain activity, growth and repair, movement and keeping warm. It comes from the food and drink we take in.
- [ ] E2 Units: energy is measured in kilojoules (kJ) or kilocalories (kcal; often called "calories"). 1 kcal is about 4.2 kJ. Food labels give both.
- [ ] E3 (assumed) Carbohydrate, fat and protein are the nutrients that release energy in the body; alcohol also supplies it (values are in "Energy value of the nutrients"). Energy not needed straight away is stored: a small store as glycogen in the liver and muscles, the large store as fat in adipose tissue.
- [ ] E4 Functions of energy (2010H asks for three): basal metabolism (keeping the body working at rest), growth, and physical activity. Marking schemes also credit the working of the internal organs and the production of heat to maintain body temperature. These three headings form the structure of the notes.

Basal metabolic rate (one block)
- [ ] B1 BMR is the minimum amount of energy the body needs to stay alive when completely at rest (every Higher Level scheme 2014 to 2026 uses this wording in essence).
- [ ] B2 What it pays for: breathing, the heartbeat and circulation of the blood, the work of the brain, liver, kidneys and other internal organs, and maintaining body temperature. Also the continuing renewal of cells.
- [ ] B3 How BMR is measured (2019O, 2009O): the person lies at complete rest, awake, in a warm room, about 12 hours after eating (fasting). State all three conditions: rest, warmth, fasted.
- [ ] B4 BMR is the largest single part of the energy a person uses in a day (for most people, more than activity). Do not give a percentage.
- [ ] B5 Factors that affect BMR, each with a reason:
  - Age: BMR per kilogram is highest in infancy and childhood because of rapid growth, and it falls with age as lean tissue is lost.
  - Gender: men generally have a higher BMR than women of the same weight because men have a higher proportion of muscle to fat (2007O scheme wording).
  - Body size and weight: the larger the body, the more tissue to maintain and the higher the BMR (2007O).
  - Body composition: lean (muscle) tissue is more active than fat, so a muscular person has a higher BMR than a person of the same weight with more fat.
  - Hormones: the thyroid gland controls the speed of metabolism; an overactive thyroid raises BMR and an underactive one lowers it (2021H lists hormones).
  - Health: fever, infection and recovery from injury raise BMR.
  - Pregnancy and lactation: raise the requirement (see total requirement below).
  - Climate: a cold climate raises it as the body works to stay warm; a hot climate has a smaller effect.
- [ ] B6 Activity does not change BMR; activity is a separate part of the total.

Growth (one block)
- [ ] G1 Energy builds new tissue (muscle, bone, blood, organs). Infants and young children need a lot of energy for their size because they grow fast.
- [ ] G2 Adolescent growth spurts raise the requirement sharply (2016O, 2013O and 2019O all credit "growth spurts"). Teenage boys generally need more than girls because of greater body size and muscle.
- [ ] G3 After growth stops, adult energy is needed for maintenance and repair of tissue, not new growth.

Physical activity (one block)
- [ ] A1 Physical activity is the most variable part of daily energy use, and the one a person can change most easily.
- [ ] A2 The energy used depends on the type, intensity, duration and frequency of activity.
- [ ] A3 Occupation: a manual worker (builder, farm worker) or an athlete uses far more than someone in a desk job. Name one example of each (2019O, 2013O, 2007O credit occupation).
- [ ] A4 Leisure: walking, cycling, sport, gardening. A sedentary lifestyle (little activity, much sitting) lowers the requirement and means less food energy is needed (2024O, 2023O, 2005O credit "sedentary lifestyle" or "activity level").

Total daily energy requirement and the other factors (one block)
- [ ] T1 Total daily energy use is BMR plus the energy for physical activity plus the small amount used to digest, absorb and process food (the thermic effect of food). The third part is not named in marking schemes; give it one clause.
- [ ] T2 Factors that determine a person's requirement (the exam list, any three or four with explanation): age, gender, body size or weight, occupation, level of physical activity, climate, pregnancy, lactation, health or illness (including recovery from injury or surgery), growth spurts, sedentary lifestyle, BMR (2023O, 2019O, 2016O, 2013O, 2009O, 2007O, 2005O, 2006H).
- [ ] T3 Pregnancy: extra energy for the growth of the baby and placenta and for the mother's added tissue, especially in the later months. Lactation: extra energy to make breast milk. (No kcal amounts.)
- [ ] T4 Climate: in cold weather the body burns more energy to keep warm (shivering is muscular work); the requirement is higher in a cold climate than in a warm one.
- [ ] T5 Health: fever, illness, burns, recovery from injury or surgery raise the requirement; long bed rest lowers the activity part. An underactive thyroid lowers BMR.
- [ ] T6 Comparison practice: an exam may describe a person (teenager who plays sport, builder, office worker, pregnant woman, older retired person) and ask the student to explain why their requirement is high or low. Model reasoning: name the factor, say whether it raises or lowers the requirement, give the reason.
- [ ] T7 Diagram: none required. A simple three-part chart of total daily energy (BMR, activity, digestion of food) is optional.

## 3. Traps

- BMR is not the same as total energy requirement. BMR is the minimum at rest; the requirement adds activity and digestion.
- Activity changes the total requirement, not BMR. A tip: "physical activity" is a factor in the requirement but not a factor in BMR.
- Do not say "men need more energy because they are men". The reason is more lean muscle and usually a larger body.
- "Metabolism" and "BMR" are not defined identically in schemes (2009O lists metabolism as a factor); say "metabolic rate, controlled by the thyroid hormones" and keep to BMR as the defined term.
- The difference between energy requirement and energy balance: this subsection explains what the body needs; whether intake matches it, positive and negative balance, belongs to "Energy balance".
- Energy values per gram (17, 16, 37, 29 kJ) belong to "Energy value of the nutrients"; do not repeat them beyond a pointer-free one-line mention that the nutrients supply energy.
- The RDA for energy, EAR or recommended kcal figures belong to "Dietary guidelines" if used at all (and are left out).
- Do not use: any daily kJ or kcal requirement figures for age groups, sexes, pregnancy or lactation, because they are changing, group-specific figures that no marking scheme states.
- Do not use: any percentage for BMR's share of daily energy (such as "60 to 75%"), because it is a changing estimate varying by source and is not in any scheme; say "the largest single part".
- Thermic effect of food is accepted as a correct fact but is not asked: give it one clause only, never a separate block, and never as an answer to "factors affecting BMR".

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain BMR and give two factors affecting it (Higher Level Section A almost every year: 2014, 2021, 2023, 2026; 6 marks). Full marks: the minimum energy at complete rest, for the working of internal organs, circulation, breathing and temperature, plus two factors with a reason each (for example age and gender; the schemes accept weight, health, hormones, pregnancy or lactation, occupation).
- List or outline three or four factors that influence the energy requirements of adults or teenagers (Ordinary Level Section A and longer parts: 2005, 2007, 2009, 2013, 2016, 2019, 2023, 2026). Expected answer content: three or four different factors, each with a short explanation of the direction of the effect (for example growth spurts raise it; a sedentary lifestyle lowers it; a cold climate raises it).
- A 12 to 20 mark "discuss factors" part (2013O, 2019O): four factors with two or three linked statements each, often with the person in the question (for example a teenager who plays sport, a manual worker, a person with a high energy requirement).
- Three functions of energy (2010H): basal metabolism, growth, physical activity.
- Explain BMR and the conditions it is measured in (2019O, 2009O).
- Questions that lead on from the requirement: planning a menu for a person with a high energy requirement (2013O, 2019O). The menu itself belongs to the meal-planning material, but the answer begins by linking the person's requirement to the foods chosen.
- Exam-tip material: always include a reason ("because...") with every factor; do not simply list; use "minimum" and "at complete rest" in the BMR definition; name the three conditions for measuring BMR.
