# Package: hom-lipids-s5 — Functions and energy value

- **loId:** `hom-lipids-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-lipids` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.4
- **Syllabus scope:** biological functions of lipids in the diet; contribution to the total energy value of the average diet.
  Also examined: health effects of a diet high in saturated fat (2024H); cholesterol (2007H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to Higher and Ordinary Level; no [HL] items (the HL "outline of the utilisation of triglycerides" belongs to the digestion and metabolism chapter). LDL, HDL and the effects of fatty-acid types on cholesterol are in "Fatty acids"; the fat-soluble vitamins in detail are in the vitamins chapter; obesity and heart disease as diet-related conditions are in the diet and health chapters, so here give each condition as a short link. Cooking uses of fat are in "Fats and oils as a food commodity".

## 1. Must cover

Biological functions of lipids (one block; the single most examined item, 2005H, 2004O, 2022O, 2023H, 2016O, 2021O)
- [ ] F1 Concentrated source of energy (heat and energy): fat gives more energy per gram than any other energy nutrient except alcohol, which is outside the nutrient list (see energy values below).
- [ ] F2 Excess energy from any food is stored as adipose (fat) tissue, which is a reserve of energy for when intake is low.
- [ ] F3 Insulation: adipose tissue under the skin forms an insulating layer that reduces heat loss and helps to keep the body temperature constant.
- [ ] F4 Protection: a layer of fat around delicate organs such as the kidneys and heart cushions them against knocks and holds them in place.
- [ ] F5 Carrier of the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K: lipid in the diet is needed for their absorption; foods rich in lipid (dairy fat, oily fish, egg yolk) supply them.
- [ ] F6 Source of essential fatty acids (see "Fatty acids"; one phrase).
- [ ] F7 Satiety: fat slows the emptying of the stomach, so it delays hunger and meals containing fat are more satisfying.
- [ ] F8 Structural: phospholipids and cholesterol form part of cell membranes ("form part of the cell wall/membrane" credited by 2005H, 2022O schemes).
- [ ] F9 Flavour and texture: fat adds flavour and a smooth mouth-feel to food (used in answers on cooking and food choice; not a biological function).
- [ ] F10 Digestion link (assumed, one line): triglycerides are digested to fatty acids and glycerol by lipase; excess is stored as adipose tissue (2026O fill-in: lipase; adipose).

Cholesterol (one block; 2007H)
- [ ] C1 Cholesterol is a fat-like substance (a sterol), found only in animal foods: egg yolk, offal (liver, kidney), meat, butter, cream, cheese, prawns and other shellfish. Plant foods contain none.
- [ ] C2 The liver makes cholesterol itself, in fact most of what the body has; so blood cholesterol is influenced more by the amount of saturated fat eaten than by the cholesterol in food.
- [ ] C3 Uses: part of cell membranes; bile salts that emulsify fat in digestion; steroid hormones such as oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone and cortisol; vitamin D is made in the skin from a cholesterol derivative.
- [ ] C4 Too much cholesterol in the blood (especially LDL) builds up on artery walls, narrows them and increases the risk of coronary heart disease (CHD) and stroke. A main constituent of most gallstones.
- [ ] C5 Dietary advice to lower blood cholesterol: reduce saturated and trans fat, eat more unsaturated fat, oily fish, oats, pulses, fruit and vegetables (fibre), keep to a healthy weight, be active, do not smoke; functional spreads with plant stanols or sterols (see "Fats and oils as a food commodity").

Health effects of a diet high in saturated fat (one block; 2024H, 2013O, 2015O)
- [ ] H1 Obesity: saturated-fat foods are energy-dense, and surplus energy is stored as adipose tissue.
- [ ] H2 Coronary heart disease and high blood pressure: raised LDL narrows arteries (atherosclerosis); scheme credits hypertension and stroke.
- [ ] H3 Type 2 diabetes: excess body fat reduces the effectiveness of insulin (insulin resistance).
- [ ] H4 Gallstones.
- [ ] H5 Also named in the 2024H scheme: varicose veins and infertility (hormonal or sperm effects). Keep to a clause; the first four carry the marks.
- [ ] H6 Lifestyle changes recommended at risk of CHD (2015O): low-fat, low-cholesterol, low-salt diet; lose weight; no smoking; limit alcohol; exercise; manage stress.
- [ ] H7 Healthy-eating message: replace saturated fats with unsaturated (mono- and polyunsaturated) fats, use fats and oils in small amounts (the fats, spreads and oils shelf of the Department of Health Food Pyramid), choose lean meat and low-fat dairy, grill, steam or bake instead of frying. Saturated fat should supply less than 10% of daily energy (HSE/Department of Health guidelines).

Energy value of lipids and contribution to the average diet (one block)
- [ ] E1 Energy values per gram (stable figures): fat 37 kJ or 9 kcal; protein 17 kJ or 4 kcal; carbohydrate 16 kJ or 3.75 kcal; alcohol 29 kJ or 7 kcal. Fat gives more than twice the energy per gram of carbohydrate or protein, so fatty foods are energy-dense (2005H scheme: one gram of fat provides 9 kcal or 37 kJ).
- [ ] E2 Calculation: energy from fat = grams of fat × 37 kJ (or × 9 kcal). Worked example with invented numbers, labelled as sample data: 20 g fat gives 740 kJ (180 kcal). Percentage of energy from fat in a meal or food label; used in data questions comparing spreads or meals.
- [ ] E3 Contribution to the average diet: a large part of the energy in the average Irish diet comes from fat, much of it hidden in processed and convenience foods, meat products, dairy, cakes, pastries, biscuits, chocolate, fried foods and takeaways; much of that is saturated. (Do not quote a national percentage.)
- [ ] E4 Dietary guidance on the contribution (use the principle only): reduce total fat and especially saturated and trans fat, replacing them with unsaturated fat and complex carbohydrate; saturated fat under 10% of energy. Over-consumption of fat leads to obesity, because fat is energy-dense and stores readily.
- [ ] E5 (assumed, 2025H data question) Reading a chart of energy sources in the adult diet: comment on each food group's share of energy, link to healthy-eating guidelines.

## 3. Traps

- Fat provides 9 kcal (37 kJ) per gram, not 20 and not 4. Carbohydrate is 3.75 kcal in the NOTES_STANDARD (some books say 4); use 3.75 kcal/16 kJ as the standard does.
- Lipids are the "concentrated" energy source, but carbohydrates are the body's main source of energy; do not say fat is the main source.
- Cholesterol and saturated fat are different. Dietary cholesterol is found in animal foods only; saturated fat in the diet raises blood LDL more than dietary cholesterol does. A "cholesterol-free" plant oil can still be high in saturated fat (coconut oil).
- LDL versus HDL is in "Fatty acids". Cholesterol as a substance is taught here.
- Essential fatty acids and the fat-soluble vitamins are listed here as functions only (one phrase each); do not teach the vitamins' own functions.
- Digestion and absorption of fat (bile, micelles, lipase detail) belong to the digestion chapter; one line here only.
- Do not use: "Irish healthy eating guidelines from the Department of Health advise replacing saturated fats with unsaturated fats rather than simply eating less fat", because "rather than simply eating less fat" is unconfirmed; the guidelines advise both small amounts of fats, spreads and oils and a choice of mono- or polyunsaturated types.
- Do not use: a national or recommended percentage of energy from total fat (for example "35%"), because it is unconfirmed in a search; only the saturated-fat figure of under 10% of energy is checked.
- Do not use: "the liver makes about 70% (or any figure) of the body's cholesterol", because no figure is checked; "most" is fine.
- Do not use: any recommended daily gram or kcal amounts of fat for a named group, because RDAs are not stated in the schemes for lipids and values change.

## 4. What exams ask

- Two (or three) biological functions of lipids: short answers worth 2 to 3 marks each (2005H, 2023H, 2022O, 2004O). Best list to learn: energy; adipose insulation and energy reserve; protects organs; carries vitamins A, D, E, K; supplies essential fatty acids; delays hunger; part of cell membranes. Name one dietary source with it (2022O).
- Long Ordinary Level "give an account of lipids" (2005O 32 marks, 2016O 28 marks, 2021O 28 marks): functions in the body about 8 marks, three or four functions with a reason each; energy value 4 marks (37 kJ or 9 kcal per gram).
- Nutritional significance of fats and oils (2024H, 20 marks): energy, insulation, organ protection, vitamins A, D, E, K, fat content of the different fats and oils; little protein, carbohydrate or minerals.
- Health effects of a diet high in saturated fat (2024H, 15 marks): obesity, CHD and high blood pressure, Type 2 diabetes, gallstones, each with its mechanism (adipose tissue; LDL narrowing arteries; insulin less effective).
- Cholesterol: define, source (animal foods only), uses, health risk, and how to lower it.
- True/false on energy value, saturated fat, cholesterol and CHD (2022O, 2013O).
- Data questions: working out the energy from fat in a food, comparing spreads or meals on saturated fat and energy; 2025H chart of the sources of energy in adults' diets.
- Practical advice on reducing fat intake (three ways, 2016O; four selection factors, 2021O): lean meat, trimming fat, low-fat dairy, grill/steam/poach/microwave, avoiding fried and processed foods, reading labels for saturates, choosing unsaturated spreads and oils, avoiding hydrogenated fats.
