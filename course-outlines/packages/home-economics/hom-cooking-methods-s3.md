# Package: hom-cooking-methods-s3 — Choice of cooking method

- **loId:** `hom-cooking-methods-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-cooking-methods` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.4
- **Syllabus scope:** choosing and applying suitable cooking methods to conserve nutrients and improve palatability; moist, dry and fat-based methods. Also examined: poaching and pot roasting (2007H); grilling (2023O); steaming for an older person (2025H); methods listed in 2013O, 2017O, 2019O, 2022O. No [HL] marks in this entry; mark nothing.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. The usual exam forms: name or classify methods, define one method, give foods or dishes for a method, true/false statements, guidelines for grilling, evaluate a method for a person (nutrition, ease, palatability).

## 1. Must cover

Classifying and choosing (one block)
- [ ] K1 Cooking methods are grouped as moist (water, stock or steam as the medium), dry (hot air or radiant heat, with little or no liquid or fat) and fat-based (frying, in fat or oil). Roasting, baking, grilling and barbecuing are dry (roasting uses a little fat in the tin); frying has its own group in the schemes.
- [ ] K2 Factors in choosing a method: the food (tender or tough, delicate or firm); nutrients to conserve; palatability (flavour, colour, texture); health needs (low fat, low salt, soft food); the person (older person, child); time, cost and equipment available; the occasion.
- [ ] K3 Conserving nutrients: methods that use little water and short times (steaming, stir-frying, microwaving, grilling) conserve water-soluble vitamins; boiling loses them to the water unless the liquid is used; deep-fat frying adds fat and energy; draining fat in grilling lowers fat.

Moist methods (one block)
- [ ] M1 Boiling: food cooked in water at boiling point (100 °C), bubbles breaking vigorously; quick; for vegetables, pasta, rice, eggs, potatoes; water-soluble vitamins are lost to the water.
- [ ] M2 Simmering: liquid kept just below boiling with small bubbles; for stocks, soups, stews and stewed fruit; gentler than boiling.
- [ ] M3 Stewing: slow, gentle cooking in a small amount of liquid in a covered pan or casserole (hob or oven); the liquid is served with the food so nutrients are kept; suits tough cuts and vegetables (e.g. Irish stew); heat transfer by conduction and convection.
- [ ] M4 Steaming: cooking in steam from boiling water without the food touching the water (steamer, a plate over a pan, a covered bowl in a pan); no fat added, nutrients and shape kept; for fish, chicken, vegetables, rice, puddings.
- [ ] M5 Poaching: cooking gently in liquid kept slightly below simmering (the scheme says about 85 °C), on the hob or in the oven; suits delicate foods: eggs, fish, fruit.
- [ ] M6 Braising: tough meat or vegetables browned in fat then cooked slowly with a little liquid, covered, in the oven or on the hob.
- [ ] M7 Pressure cooking: food cooked in a sealed pan in which steam builds pressure, raising the temperature above 100 °C and cutting the time; used for stocks, stews, tough meat, pulses.

Dry methods (one block)
- [ ] D1 Roasting: cooking in the oven in a roasting tin, with a little fat, uncovered; for meat, poultry, potatoes and vegetables; the surface browns, the inside cooks by conduction and convection.
- [ ] D2 Baking: cooking by the dry heat of an oven, by convection (and radiation); for bread, cakes, pastry, fish, pasta and potato dishes.
- [ ] D3 Grilling: cooking by radiant heat from an element or flame above or below the food; heat seals the surface; for tender cuts, steaks, chops, sausages, rashers, fish, tomatoes, mushrooms, peppers, kebabs; fat drips away.
- [ ] D4 Barbecuing: cooking on a grid over burning charcoal or gas by radiant heat, giving a smoky flavour.

Grilling guidelines (one block)
- [ ] G1 Preheat the grill to the required temperature (this seals the surface); oil the grid or the food so it doesn't stick; use thin pieces; turn with tongs, not a fork; don't salt beforehand; use a suitable time to avoid overcooking. (2023O: three guidelines.)

Fat-based methods (one block)
- [ ] F1 Shallow frying: food cooked in hot fat in a pan, partly covered by fat and turned; for eggs, fish, chops, pancakes.
- [ ] F2 Deep-fat frying: food fully immersed in hot fat in a deep pan or fryer; for chips and fish in batter; the food absorbs fat so it is higher in fat and energy.
- [ ] F3 Stir-frying: small, thin pieces cooked quickly in a wok with a little oil, stirred constantly; keeps colour, crispness and vitamin C.
- [ ] F4 Dry frying: cooking food in a non-stick pan with no added fat, relying on fat in the food (bacon, sausages).
- [ ] F5 Pot roasting: cooking food in a little fat in a covered pan on the hob (the 2007H definition); keeps the food moist in its own steam.

Dishes and examples (one block)
- [ ] X1 Examples the schemes accept: baking: apple crumble, sponge, soda bread, fish pie, lasagne, pizza, muffins; grilling: kebabs, chicken satay, burgers, fish, rashers, sausages, tomatoes, mushrooms, bruschetta; steaming: fish, chicken, rice, Christmas pudding, fruit, vegetables; roasting: meat, potatoes, vegetables; frying: dry, shallow, deep, stir-fry; moist: boiling, stewing, steaming, poaching, braising, pressure cooking.
- [ ] X2 True/false points examined: grilling is a fast method (not slow); deep frying is cooking food immersed in hot fat; poaching suits delicate foods such as eggs and fish.

Steaming for an older person (one block)
- [ ] S1 Evaluating steaming for an older person: easy (little attention or equipment, a whole meal can cook in a tiered steamer, suits small portions); nutrition (adds no fat, keeps water-soluble vitamins); palatability (soft, moist food that is easy to chew and swallow, keeps colour); drawbacks: can lack flavour and browning, and overcooking spoils texture and colour; herbs, lemon juice and a sauce add flavour.

Microwave cooking (one block, short)
- [ ] W1 (assumed from the appliance chapter) Microwave ovens cook and reheat quickly with little added fat and water, so vitamins are kept; they do not brown food unless a grill or combination mode is used; containers must be microwave-safe and metal must not be used. The working principle is taught in the appliances chapter.

## 3. Traps

- Pot roasting means a covered pan on the hob with a little fat, in the scheme's definition; don't describe it as cooking in an oven or as roasting uncovered.
- Poaching is below simmering; simmering is below boiling; don't mix up the three.
- Roasting is listed as dry in the schemes even though a little fat is used; don't put it under fat-based methods.
- Grilling is fast, with radiant heat, and suits tender or thin pieces; don't call it a slow method (a past true/false).
- Heat-transfer theory belongs to the previous subsection; here name the type in a clause only.
- Nutrient loss in detail belongs to the first subsection; use one sentence here.
- The meat chapter covers cuts of meat and joints; the fish chapter covers fish cookery; don't list cuts here.
- Do not use: "steaming retains the natural flavour of the food" as an unqualified advantage, because the 2025 scheme counts lack of flavour as a disadvantage; write that steamed food keeps its colour and moisture but can taste bland.
- Do not use: a pressure temperature or pressure in psi/kPa for pressure cooking, because it varies with the cooker; say only that it rises above 100 °C.
- Do not use: "stews take hours" as a fact, because times vary; say long, slow cooking.

## 4. What exams ask

- Classify methods as moist, dry and frying with two examples each (2013O).
- Define a method in one line (poaching, pot roasting, deep frying); true/false on grilling, deep frying, poaching.
- Give dishes or foods for grilling, baking, steaming and roasting (2017O, 2022O).
- Three guidelines for grilling.
- Evaluate steaming for an older person, referring to ease, nutritive value and palatability, with a conclusion.
- Choose a method to conserve nutrients or lower fat and say why.
