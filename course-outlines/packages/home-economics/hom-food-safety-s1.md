## 1. Must cover

Mould: what it is and where it lives
- [ ] M1 [HL] Moulds are multicellular, filamentous fungi. They are not plants (no chlorophyll) and not bacteria.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Moulds are saprophytes: they feed on dead organic matter (bread, cheese, jam, fruit, decaying plant material), digesting it outside the body and absorbing the products.
- [ ] M3 [HL] Habitat and sources: spores are everywhere, carried in the air and on dust, soil, damp surfaces, packaging, utensils and already-mouldy food, and they settle on food. A mouldy item spreads spores to food stored beside it.

Structure of a mould (with diagram)
- [ ] M4 [HL] Hypha: a thread-like filament that grows over and into the food and absorbs nutrients.
- [ ] M5 [HL] Mycelium: the tangled mass of hyphae that forms the visible fluffy or powdery growth.
- [ ] M6 [HL] Sporangiophore: an upright hypha that grows up from the mycelium. Sporangium: the round case at its tip that holds spores and bursts when ripe.
- [ ] M7 [HL] Conidia: in some moulds (Penicillium, Aspergillus) the hypha tip makes chains of spores (conidia) instead of a round sporangium.
- [ ] M8 [HL] Diagram: a mould on a food surface labelled hypha, mycelium, sporangiophore, sporangium (the 2012 diagram asked for sporangium, hypha/sporangiophore and mycelium).

Conditions for mould growth (six conditions; exams ask for four or five)
- [ ] C1 [HL] Food: dead organic matter, e.g. bread, cheese.
- [ ] C2 [HL] Warmth: most moulds are mesophiles; Penicillium and Aspergillus favour 20 to 25 °C (scheme). Freezing at -18 °C inactivates mould growth but does not kill it (scheme); cold slows growth.
- [ ] C3 [HL] Moisture: needed for growth, so frozen and very dry foods are unsuitable.
- [ ] C4 [HL] Oxygen: moulds are aerobic, so they grow on surfaces (jam) or through open-textured foods (bread).
- [ ] C5 [HL] pH: slightly acidic conditions suit them (scheme: about pH 4 to 6); strong acids inhibit growth.
- [ ] C6 [HL] Time: moulds need time to multiply (scheme: 24 to 48 hours for visible growth).

One named type of mould (exams ask for one: name, characteristics, conditions, reproduction)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Penicillium: saprophytic; green-blue, powdery; favours 20 to 25 °C; asexual reproduction only, by conidia; grows on bread, jam, cheese, fruit; destroyed by heat above 75 °C (2022 scheme).
- [ ] T2 [HL] Rhizopus (black bread mould): saprophytic; fluffy white mycelium; black pin-head sporangia; reproduces sexually and asexually.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Mucor: saprophytic; white hyphae; grey sporangia; reproduces sexually and asexually. Aspergillus: saprophytic; black or green; 20 to 25 °C; asexual only, by conidia.

Reproduction of moulds
- [ ] R1 [HL] Asexual: once the mycelium is established, a hypha grows upwards; its head forms a sporangium (round) or conidia (chains of spores); when ripe it bursts, spores travel in the air, and a spore that lands in suitable conditions germinates into new growth.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Sexual (Rhizopus, Mucor): two hyphae grow side by side and fuse; the wall between them breaks down; a zygospore forms; it is thick-walled and protects the spores until conditions suit; then it germinates and releases spores.

Yeast
- [ ] Y1 [HL] Yeasts are single-celled fungi, oval or round, larger than bacteria. They are eukaryotes (have a nucleus).
- [ ] Y2 [HL] Habitat and sources: skins of fruit (especially grapes), soil, air, plant surfaces and sugary foods.
- [ ] Y3 [HL] Growth conditions: sugar as food, moisture, warmth and time; yeast is facultative (respires with oxygen, ferments without it). In fermentation, sugar becomes carbon dioxide and alcohol.
- [ ] Y4 [HL] Reproduction: asexual, by budding; a bud grows from the parent cell, enlarges and breaks away.
- [ ] Y5 [HL] Structure and diagram: cell wall, cell membrane, nucleus, vacuole, granular cytoplasm (food reserves). The 2016 diagram asked for vacuole, nucleus and granular cytoplasm (food reserve).
- [ ] Y6 [HL] Yeast spoils sugary foods (juices, jam, syrups) by fermenting them; its useful roles are in a later subsection (cross-reference in one phrase only).

Bacterial cell (diagram)
- [ ] B1 [HL] Bacteria are prokaryotes: single cells with no true nucleus; the DNA lies free in the cytoplasm as a nucleoid.
- [ ] B2 [HL] Diagram labels: cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, flagella (not all bacteria have them), nucleoid. The 2007 diagram asked for ribosomes, flagella and cytoplasm.
- [ ] B3 [HL] Bacteria reproduce asexually by binary fission (one cell divides in two); under ideal conditions some divide about every 20 minutes.
- [ ] B4 (assumed) Fungi versus bacteria in one comparison: fungi are eukaryotic with a nucleus; bacteria are prokaryotic without one.

## 3. Traps

- Moulds and yeasts are both fungi; bacteria are not fungi. Do not describe yeast as a bacterium.
- Moulds make spores; yeast reproduces by budding. Do not give yeast spores or hyphae.
- Penicillium reproduces asexually only (scheme); Rhizopus and Mucor use both. Do not give Penicillium a zygospore.
- Freezing inactivates mould growth but does not kill it; heat above 75 °C destroys Penicillium. Do not write "freezing kills mould".
- An exam account of one mould needs name, description, conditions and reproduction. Do not mix features: green-blue powdery is Penicillium; black pin-head sporangia is Rhizopus.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: the food poisoning bacteria and their symptoms (food poisoning bacteria); psychrophile, mesophile, thermophile and the aerobic/anaerobic/facultative terms (food poisoning bacteria; use "mesophile" and "aerobic" here in one clause each); uses of yeast and mould in food (uses of micro-organisms); preservation methods (preservation chapter).
- Do not use: "moulds always produce toxins", because only some produce mycotoxins; say "some" and name no toxin.
- Do not use: any optimum or killing temperature for yeast, because no checked figure exists.
- Do not use: "yeast is anaerobic", because yeast is facultative (respires with oxygen, ferments without).

## 4. What exams ask

- Four (2015H) or five (2006H) conditions for mould growth: name each condition and give the reason (food, warmth, moisture, oxygen, pH, time). Four marks each in a long question; one or two marks each in a short one.
- One type of mould in detail (2022H, 2015H): name, saprophytic, colour and texture, temperature, foods it grows on, aerobic surface growth, and reproduction. Reproduction carries the most marks: describe sporangium or conidia release and germination, and the zygospore if the mould reproduces sexually.
- Label diagrams: parts of a mould (sporangium, hypha, mycelium), a yeast cell (vacuole, nucleus, granular cytoplasm), a bacterial cell (ribosomes, flagella, cytoplasm). Short questions, about 2 marks per label.
- Short differentiation questions: fungi versus bacteria; budding versus spore release.
