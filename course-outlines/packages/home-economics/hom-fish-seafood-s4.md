# Package: hom-fish-seafood-s4 — Fish processing and fish products

- **loId:** `hom-fish-seafood-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-fish-seafood` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.2
- **Syllabus scope:** methods of processing to extend shelf life (freezing, canning, smoking, salting); processed fish products. No [HL] marks in this subsection.
- **Also examined:** hot and cold smoking (2025H, 2019H); one process in detail: name, underlying principle, effects (2023H); one method with an advantage and a disadvantage (2022O); three processed fish products and why they are popular (2006O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Examiners ask for one process "in detail": name, principle, effects. The notes must give every process in that form. The general theory of preservation (micro-organisms, enzymes, water activity) is in the preservation chapter; here it is applied to fish.

## 1. Must cover

Why fish is processed (one block)
- [ ] W1 Fish spoils very quickly, so it is processed to extend shelf life: to kill micro-organisms or stop them multiplying, to slow or stop enzyme action, to remove or tie up the water they need, and to keep out oxygen. Processing also adds variety and convenience.
- [ ] W2 The processes the syllabus names for fish: freezing, canning, smoking, salting. Vacuum packing is also accepted by the scheme (2023H): removing air prevents oxidative rancidity and slows the growth of micro-organisms that need oxygen.

Freezing in detail (name, principle, effects)
- [ ] F1 Name and method: freezing, in industry at about −30 °C (fast freezing, often on the fishing vessel or soon after landing), stored at −18 °C or below.
- [ ] F2 Principle: low temperature stops micro-organisms multiplying and inactivates enzymes; the water in the flesh is held as ice so is unavailable to micro-organisms.
- [ ] F3 Fast freezing forms small ice crystals that damage the flesh less than slow freezing.
- [ ] F4 Effects: colour and flavour largely unchanged; nutrients well retained; texture may change; some B vitamins and extractives are lost (drip on thawing); poor wrapping causes freezer burn; air in the packaging lets oily fish go rancid, so wrapping or vacuum packing is used.
- [ ] F5 Advantages: simple, safe, little nutrient loss, bulk buying, out-of-season. Disadvantages: packaging and freezer running cost; fish must be thawed.

Canning (name, principle, effects)
- [ ] K1 Method: fish is cleaned, cooked, packed in cans with brine, oil or tomato sauce, sealed and heated to sterilise (commercial sterilisation) under pressure.
- [ ] K2 Principle: heat destroys micro-organisms and spores and inactivates enzymes; the airtight seal stops re-contamination.
- [ ] K3 Effects: keeps for a long time without refrigeration; flesh becomes soft; some heat-sensitive vitamins (B group) are partly lost; the soft bones of sardines and salmon are eaten and supply calcium; flavour changes from the cooking and the packing medium.
- [ ] K4 Examples: tuna, sardines, salmon, mackerel.

Salting and brining (name, principle, effects)
- [ ] L1 Method: dry salting (salt rubbed on or layered with the fish) or brining (soaked in a strong salt solution).
- [ ] L2 Principle: salt draws water out of the flesh and out of micro-organisms by osmosis, so the water is not available for them to grow; salt also slows bacterial growth and enzyme action.
- [ ] L3 Effects: firmer, drier flesh with a strong salty flavour; high sodium content (salted fish should be soaked before cooking and eaten in moderation: high salt intake is linked to high blood pressure); some B vitamins and soluble nutrients are lost with the water.
- [ ] L4 Examples: salted cod, salted herring.

Smoking (name, principle, effects; hot and cold compared)
- [ ] M1 Preparation (same for both): fish is filleted or split and trimmed, brined or dry-salted, rinsed and dried.
- [ ] M2 Smoke comes from smouldering wood, wood chips, sawdust or peat. Principle: the smoke compounds (the scheme names creosote and formaldehyde; phenols are also involved) coat the fish and act as preservatives and antioxidants; the salting and drying beforehand add to the preservation. Smoke also gives colour and flavour.
- [ ] M3 Cold smoking: the fish is exposed to smoke at a temperature not exceeding about 27 °C, so the flesh is not cooked by the process; the scheme says it needs further cooking. Cold-smoked salmon is the well-known product eaten as bought; kippers (herring) and smoked cod are normally cooked.
- [ ] M4 Hot smoking: the temperature is raised gradually to about 80 °C, so the fish is cooked during smoking and needs no further cooking. Examples: hot-smoked trout and mackerel.
- [ ] M5 Effects: flavour, colour and a glossy surface; longer shelf life than fresh but shorter than canned or frozen; salt content raised.
- [ ] M6 Examples: smoked salmon, trout, cod, mackerel.

Processed fish products (one block)
- [ ] P1 Frozen: fish fingers, fish cakes, fillets, breaded or battered portions, fish in sauce, ready fish meals.
- [ ] P2 Canned: tuna, sardines, salmon (in brine, oil or tomato sauce).
- [ ] P3 Smoked: trout, salmon, cod, mackerel.
- [ ] P4 Why they are popular (reasons examiners expect): ready to cook, no waste, save time and fuel, quick and easy, good value, convenient, versatile, long shelf life, available out of season; many have extra carbohydrate from breadcrumbs, batter or sauce.
- [ ] P5 Fish, crustaceans and molluscs are allergens that must be highlighted on labels (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011); safety of processing is controlled under HACCP-based official controls (food safety chapter).
- [ ] P6 (assumed) Osmosis, micro-organisms, enzyme action and HACCP are taught elsewhere; use the terms without redefining.
- [ ] P7 Diagram: none required.

## 3. Traps

- Hot versus cold smoking: the difference is the temperature and whether the fish is cooked. State both temperatures.
- The scheme says cold smoked fish "must still be cooked". Cold-smoked salmon is eaten uncooked in practice, so write: not cooked by the smoking, and most cold-smoked fish is cooked before eating (smoked salmon is the exception).
- Fast versus slow freezing: small crystals mean less damage. Freezing doesn't kill micro-organisms.
- Canning sterilises; salting works by osmosis; don't mix their principles.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: home freezing rules and storage (selection, spoilage and storage); cooking methods; general preservation principles, irradiation and fermentation (preservation chapter); the FSAI's role and HACCP (food safety chapter).
- Do not use: "the FSAI sets HACCP controls in processing plants" as written, because the FSAI is the central authority and coordinates enforcement through official agencies; processing plants run their own HACCP systems and official agencies inspect them. Say "the FSAI coordinates enforcement; the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority inspects seafood processing".
- Do not use: storage times for canned, frozen, salted or smoked fish, because they vary by product and source; say "a long time" for canned fish.
- Do not use: "formaldehyde" or "creosote" as health benefits or as safe additives, because the scheme names them only as the compounds that coat the fish; don't advise eating more smoked fish.
- Do not use: drying, pickling or fish paste (surimi) as required processes, because the syllabus names freezing, canning, smoking and salting; add them only as one-line extras if at all.

## 4. What exams ask

- Differentiate or outline cold and hot smoking (6 marks, 2019H, 2025H): preparation (fillet, brine or dry-salt, rinse, dry), smoke source, about 27 °C and further cooking needed versus about 80 °C and cooked.
- A detailed account of one process (18 marks, 2023H): name, underlying principle and effects. Freezing is the model answer (micro-organisms, water as ice, enzymes; colour and flavour kept, texture and vitamins B changed, freezer burn, vacuum packing); salting, smoking, canning and vacuum packing are accepted.
- Name one process and give an advantage and a disadvantage (2022O).
- Name three processed fish products and say why they are popular (2006O, 14 marks): frozen, canned and smoked examples; ready to cook, no waste, good value, available all year.
