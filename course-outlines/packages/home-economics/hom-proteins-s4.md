# Package: hom-proteins-s4 — Sources

- **loId:** `hom-proteins-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-proteins` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.2
- **Syllabus scope:** animal and plant protein sources; distribution in food of albumin, casein, gelatine and gluten, and [HL] myosin, actin and collagen. Also examined: naming a food source of named proteins (2016H, 2011H, 2005H); dietary sources as a heading in long answers (2023O, 2018O, 2013O, 2007O, 2010O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Albumin, casein, gelatine and gluten are common-level; myosin, actin and collagen are [HL]. The question style is "name one food source of each protein", so the notes must pair each protein with its food clearly. Which class each protein belongs to is in the classification subsection; what the protein does in cooking is in the properties subsection.

## 1. Must cover

Animal and plant sources
- [ ] A1 Animal protein sources: meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, yoghurt.
- [ ] A2 Plant protein sources: soya beans and soya products, pulses (peas, beans, lentils), cereals (wheat, rice, maize, oats), nuts and seeds; potatoes and vegetables contain only small amounts.
- [ ] A3 Cereals supply protein as well as carbohydrate; wheat, bread and pasta are everyday plant sources. Soya is the plant source that is high biological value (cross-reference by title only).
- [ ] A4 Name a source of each type on request (Ordinary: one animal, one plant; schemes accept e.g. meat, fish, eggs, milk and cheese for animal and soya beans, nuts, pulses, cereals for plant).

Albumin
- [ ] B1 Albumin is the main protein of egg white (ovalbumin); it is also found in meat, and lactalbumin is found in the whey of milk.
- [ ] B2 Albumin is soluble in water and coagulates (sets) on heating; this explains how egg white sets.

Casein
- [ ] C1 Casein (older term caseinogen) is the main protein of milk, and so is found in cheese and yoghurt.
- [ ] C2 Casein is coagulated by the enzyme rennin (in cheese-making) and by acid (souring milk), forming the curd.

Gelatine
- [ ] G1 Gelatine is formed when collagen from bones, skin and connective tissue is heated in moisture; it is not present in raw meat.
- [ ] G2 Gelatine sets to a gel on cooling; foods: jelly, aspic, mousses, marshmallows and some desserts and sweets; it comes from animal sources (so is not vegetarian).

Gluten
- [ ] W1 Gluten is the protein of wheat flour; it forms when the wheat proteins gliadin and glutenin are mixed with water.
- [ ] W2 Gluten is elastic and stretchy; kneading develops it, and it traps the carbon dioxide made by yeast in bread dough, so the dough rises. Foods: bread, pasta, cakes, pastry, flour products.
- [ ] W3 (assumed) Coeliac disease and gluten-free diets are in the diet and health subsection (cross-reference by title only).

Myosin, actin and collagen [HL]
- [ ] M1 [HL] Myosin and actin are the muscle proteins; found in the lean flesh of meat and fish; they contract to move muscle.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Collagen is the protein of connective tissue; found in meat, fish and bones; it changes to gelatine on long moist heating (the reason cheap cuts tenderise on slow cooking).
- [ ] M3 [HL] Elastin (meat) and globulin (egg) are also acceptable answers when asked for proteins in meat or egg (scheme lists).

Summary table
- [ ] T1 Table: protein, main food source, one line on what it does. Albumin: egg white, meat; casein: milk, cheese; gelatine: set desserts, from collagen; gluten: wheat flour, bread; myosin, actin: meat and fish muscle; collagen: connective tissue of meat and fish.

## 3. Traps

- Egg white protein is albumin; egg yolk is not "albumin". Students write albumin for the whole egg: say egg white.
- Casein is the protein of milk; whey proteins (lactalbumin) are the rest. Casein, not albumin, is what rennin coagulates.
- Gluten is formed when gliadin and glutenin meet water; flour itself contains gliadin and glutenin, not formed gluten.
- Gelatine and collagen: collagen is in meat; gelatine is made from it by moist heat. Do not name gelatine as a protein found in raw meat, bone or fish flesh.
- Oats, rice and maize do not contain gluten in the sense of wheat gluten; this is for the coeliac subsection: do not state which cereals are gluten-free here.
- Classification into fibrous, globular, simple and conjugated belongs to the classification subsection; coagulation, gel formation and foam formation to the properties subsection; high and low biological value to the biological value subsection.
- Do not use: gelatine as a protein present in raw meat (the 2006H scheme lists "gelatin" among proteins in meat), because gelatine forms only when collagen is heated in moisture; in an exam give collagen, myosin, actin or elastin instead.

## 4. What exams ask

- "Name one food source of each of the proteins listed" (Higher 6 marks, 2016H, 2005H): casein (cheese, milk), actin (meat, fish), albumin (eggs), gluten (wheat, flour), myosin (meat, fish), collagen (meat, fish). One correct food per protein.
- "Name the main type of protein in fish, eggs, wheat" (2011H): fish myosin/collagen/actin; eggs albumin; wheat gluten/gliadin/glutenin.
- Ordinary: "albumin is a protein present in the white of an egg" (2023O) and "albumin is the protein found in egg white" (2012O, true).
- Account of protein, dietary sources (Ordinary, 12 marks, 3–4 sources at 3–4 marks): name three or four foods from each of animal and plant; marks go for variety, e.g. meat, fish, eggs, milk/cheese; soya, pulses, cereals, nuts.
- "Name two proteins present in meat" (2006H): collagen, elastin, myosin, albumin, actin.
- Exam tips to build: "Pair every named protein with one food"; "Egg white = albumin, milk = casein, wheat = gluten"; "Gelatine is made from collagen by heating in moisture."
