# Package: hom-food-additives-s4 — Classes of food additives

- **loId:** `hom-food-additives-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-additives` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.6
- **Syllabus scope:** classification, examples, origin and functions of food additives, to include colourings, flavourings, sweeteners, preservatives, nutritional supplements and **[HL]** physical conditioning agents. Settings: examining food labels to find the additives used and suggest reasons. Also examined: physical conditioning agents (2025H, 2023H, 2010H, 2006H); artificial sweeteners (2026H, 2021H, 2011H); antioxidants, flavourings and emulsifiers (2018H, 2012H, 2011H). Only physical conditioning agents are Higher Level; antioxidants are in the Ordinary Level scheme (2005O lists them as an additive class). Emulsifiers and stabilisers are a physical conditioning agent: mark them HL.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. "Origin" means natural (plant, animal, mineral) or artificial/synthetic. EU control and E numbers as a system are in the next subsection; E numbers appear here only as part of examples.

## 1. Must cover

What an additive is (one block)
- [ ] A1 A food additive is a substance, natural or artificial, added to food during manufacture to improve colour, taste, texture, appearance, keeping qualities or nutritive value, or to help processing (the Ordinary Level 2012 definition).
- [ ] A2 Classes (the classes the schemes name): colourings; flavourings (including flavour enhancers); sweeteners; preservatives; antioxidants; nutritional supplements; and [HL] physical conditioning agents.
- [ ] A3 Origin: each class has natural examples (from plants, animals or minerals) and artificial examples (made by chemical synthesis). Natural does not mean safer; both must be approved.
- [ ] A4 Setting: read a label, find the additive (by name or E number), name its class and suggest why it was added.
- [ ] A5 Disadvantages (Ordinary 2012 asks for two): some people react (allergy, intolerance, hyperactivity warning on certain colours); additives may mask poor-quality food or encourage processed diets; sulphur dioxide destroys thiamine (vitamin B1); polyols can cause diarrhoea if eaten in excess.

Colourings (one block)
- [ ] B1 Function: replace colour lost in processing, make food more attractive, give uniform colour from batch to batch.
- [ ] B2 Natural: caramel (E150), chlorophyll (E140), carotene (E160a), cochineal (E120), turmeric/curcumin (E100). Artificial: tartrazine (E102, yellow).
- [ ] B3 Uses: sweets, soft drinks, desserts, baked goods, cheese products. Tartrazine and some other azo colours must carry a warning that they may affect activity and attention in children.

Flavourings and flavour enhancers (one block)
- [ ] F1 Function: add flavour to bland food, replace flavour lost in processing, strengthen existing flavour.
- [ ] F2 Natural: sugar, salt, herbs and spices (the scheme's list); uses: jam, tinned beans, cereals, meat products, sauces, stock cubes, cheese and butter. Artificial: esters and aldehydes made by synthesis; the 2018 scheme gives ethyl acetate (rum flavour), amyl acetate (pear flavour), benzaldehyde (cherry flavour) and maltol; uses: baked goods and cakes.
- [ ] F3 Flavour enhancers (E600–E699): improve an existing flavour and make it seem stronger; monosodium glutamate (E621) in soups, sauces, stock cubes, prepared meals. Flavourings in the narrow sense carry no E number; flavour enhancers do.

Sweeteners (one block)
- [ ] W1 Two classes (2011H): intense (high-intensity, non-nutritive) sweeteners and bulk sweeteners (polyols).
- [ ] W2 Intense sweeteners: many times sweeter than sugar, so tiny amounts, negligible energy; saccharin (E954), aspartame (E951), acesulfame K (E950), sucralose (E955). They lack the bulk, browning, preservation and texture of sugar, so a bulking agent may be added.
- [ ] W3 Bulk sweeteners (polyols): sorbitol (E420), mannitol (E421), xylitol; less sweet, used in larger amounts, give bulk and texture; also act as humectants; excess has a laxative effect.
- [ ] W4 Uses: low-energy and diet soft drinks, tabletop sweeteners, yoghurts, desserts, sugar-free sweets and chewing gum, slimming and diabetic products. Aspartame products carry a warning for people with phenylketonuria (a source of phenylalanine).
- [ ] W5 Nutritive sweeteners (sugar, honey, glucose syrup, fructose) are sweeteners in the Ordinary Level scheme and supply energy. Sweeteners are not permitted in food for infants and young children.

Preservatives (one block)
- [ ] R1 Function: inhibit the growth of bacteria, yeasts and moulds, delaying spoilage and extending shelf life.
- [ ] R2 Natural: salt, sugar, vinegar, smoke. Chemical: sulphur dioxide (E220), potassium sorbate (E202); used in soft drinks, jams, baked goods, dried fruit, wine. Sulphur dioxide must be declared as an allergen.

Antioxidants (one block)
- [ ] O1 Function: react with oxygen so that fats and oils do not go rancid (oxidative rancidity); extend shelf life and reduce waste.
- [ ] O2 Natural: vitamin C, ascorbic acid (E300); vitamin E, tocopherols (E306). Artificial: BHA (E320), BHT (E321).
- [ ] O3 Uses: fats and cooking oils, crisps, biscuits, stock cubes, cheese spread, chewing gum, tinned fish, fruit drinks, jams.

Nutritional supplements (one block)
- [ ] N1 Vitamins, minerals and other nutrients added to raise or restore the nutrient content (fortification: see the first subsection of this chapter). Their purpose is nutritional, not technological.

Physical conditioning agents [HL] (one block, heading ends " (Higher Level only)")
- [ ] X1 Definition: additives that change or maintain the physical properties of food: texture, consistency, moisture, stability, appearance.
- [ ] X2 Emulsifiers: hold two immiscible liquids, e.g. oil and water, together; the molecule has a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and a hydrophobic (oil-loving) tail; lecithin (E322), mono- and diglycerides (E471); mayonnaise, ice cream, sauces, margarine, chocolate. Diagram cue: an emulsifier at the boundary of an oil droplet and water.
- [ ] X3 Stabilisers (and thickeners): prevent an emulsion separating; alginates, guar gum, carrageenan, pectin; ice cream, sauces, desserts.
- [ ] X4 Humectants: prevent loss of moisture so food does not dry and harden; sorbitol, mannitol; marzipan, cakes, sweets.
- [ ] X5 Anti-caking agents: stop dried or powdered food forming lumps; polyphosphates (also increase water retention); cake mixes, dried milk.
- [ ] X6 Setting agents (pectin in jam) and firming agents (keep fruit and vegetables firm in processing).
- [ ] X7 Others the schemes accept: anti-foaming agents, anti-spattering agents, buffers (control pH), release agents, packaging gases (e.g. nitrogen, which stops oxidation).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: lecithin (E322) as an example of the E400–E499 range, because E322 is in the E300 range; the ranges are for the next subsection and are approximate.
- Do not use: vitamin A as an example of an antioxidant additive (the 2011 scheme accepts it), because the authorised antioxidant additives are vitamin C and E; carotenes are listed as colours.
- Do not use: "Splenda" or other brand names (Canderel, NutraSweet, Hermesetas), and the 2011 scheme's listing of Splenda as a bulk sweetener, because Splenda is a sucralose product (an intense sweetener) and brands change.
- Do not use: claims that aspartame or any named sweetener causes cancer or other disease, because the risk assessments are updated and the notes are not the place to adjudicate them; say only that approved sweeteners are safety-tested.
- Do not use: "sulphur dioxide destroys vitamin B" without qualification; say thiamine (vitamin B1).
- Do not use: figures for how many times sweeter each sweetener is, because the ratios are approximate and not asked.
- Salt, sugar, vinegar and smoke are accepted as natural preservatives and sugar, salt, herbs and spices as natural flavourings (scheme); don't contradict that, but also don't present them as EU-listed additives.
- Flavourings have no E number; flavour enhancers do. Don't write "E number for all additives".
- Antioxidant (stops fat going rancid) is not a preservative against micro-organisms. Emulsifier (joins) is not a stabiliser (keeps joined). Humectant keeps moisture in; an anti-caking agent stops lumps.
- Nutritional supplements here means nutrients added to food, not food-supplement tablets.
- The legal limits, E-number ranges and conditions of use belong to the legal-control subsection; labelling of additives belongs to the packaging and labelling subsection; fortification examples belong to the processed-foods subsection; rancidity chemistry belongs to the lipids chapter.
- Marks: only the physical conditioning agents are HL. Antioxidants, flavourings, sweeteners, preservatives, colourings and nutritional supplements are for all candidates.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name classes of additive with one example each (2005O, 2008H, 2010O, 2022O). Typical correct matches: turmeric colouring, herbs and spices flavouring, vitamin E antioxidant, sugar or salt or sulphur dioxide preservatives, aspartame or sorbitol sweeteners.
- Define a food additive and give two disadvantages (2012O).
- Write an informative account of flavourings and antioxidants (2018H, 20 marks): classes, two examples each, functions (two at 2 marks), and two uses. Flavourings: natural vs artificial, enhancers. Antioxidants: natural vs artificial, prevents oxidative rancidity, longer shelf life, less waste.
- Antioxidants table: example, function, example of use (2011H).
- Informative account of artificial sweeteners (2026H, 18 marks): intense vs bulk, examples, uses, and the limits (no bulk, polyol laxative effect, still count total energy).
- Classify sweeteners and give an example of each (2011H); state one function each of flavour enhancer, emulsifier and antioxidant (2005H).
- Name and give the function of two physical conditioning agents (2025H, 2023H, 2010H, 2006H): humectants (prevent drying), anti-caking agents or polyphosphates (stop lumping, retain water), emulsifier (hold oil and water together), stabiliser (prevent separation), setting agent (pectin in jam), firming agent; one function in one clause is enough for 6 marks. A 12-mark version asks for the role of one in more depth, e.g. the emulsifier head and tail.
- Explain the role of emulsifiers, with the hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tail (2012H).
