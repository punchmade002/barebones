# Package: hom-personal-finance-s4 — Saving

- **loId:** `hom-personal-finance-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-personal-finance` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.3
- **Syllabus scope (outline):** methods of saving.
- **Also examined (outline):** choosing a savings scheme — security, interest, access, tax (2023H, 2015H, 2010H). Past papers also ask the reasons for saving and named schemes (2024O, 2019O, 2018H, 2016O, 2012O).
- **Higher Level only:** nothing marked [HL]. Do not mark anything HL.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. Interest rates, DIRT rate, minimums and product terms change: leave them out. The only stable figure allowed is the EU-wide deposit guarantee limit, which a marking scheme states.

## 1. Must cover

Why save
- [ ] R1 Saving means setting aside part of income for future use instead of spending it. It requires planning (budget; "pay yourself first").
- [ ] R2 Reasons: security and less financial worry; an emergency fund (job loss, repairs, illness); to pay for planned large items or events (holiday, car, education, wedding, deposit for a home) without credit; to avoid loans and interest; to earn interest so money grows; for retirement; to set a good example for children; to build independence and a good financial reputation.
- [ ] R3 Advantages for teenagers of saving regularly (2024O): financial independence, a habit, security, future goals, avoiding credit.
- [ ] R4 Short-term saving (emergency or planned spend within a year) needs easy access; long-term saving (retirement, house) can accept restricted access for higher interest.

Methods of saving: where to put money
- [ ] S1 Banks and building societies: current account (not for saving), demand deposit (instant access, low interest, good for an emergency fund), notice account (withdrawal needs notice, usually better interest), term (fixed-term) deposit (money locked for an agreed period at a set rate; early access loses interest or is refused), regular saver (fixed monthly deposits, higher interest for regular saving).
- [ ] S2 Credit unions: member-owned, non-profit co-operatives that serve a local or work-based community (a common bond). Members buy shares/save regularly, often with payroll deduction; a dividend is paid at the end of the year if the credit union can afford it; saving builds a record for a low-cost loan; savings protected (see S5).
- [ ] S3 State Savings (run by the National Treasury Management Agency and sold through An Post, the Post Office): products include fixed-term savings certificates and savings bonds, instalment savings (regular monthly saving over a set period) and Prize Bonds (a chance to win prizes rather than interest). Returns on fixed-term products and instalment savings are not subject to DIRT or income tax; backed by the State; no loss of capital.
- [ ] S4 Other ways (one block): shares and investment funds, pensions (PRSA, occupational pension, AVCs), property. Higher return but the value can fall; not suited to an emergency fund; equity-linked products risk loss of capital.
- [ ] S5 Savings security: the Deposit Guarantee Scheme protects deposits in banks, building societies and credit unions authorised in Ireland up to a limit per person per institution (100,000 euro). State Savings are backed by the State. Higher interest usually means higher risk or less access.

Choosing a savings scheme (security, interest, access, tax)
- [ ] C1 Security: how safe the money is (guarantee scheme, State backing, stability of the institution, risk of loss with investments).
- [ ] C2 Interest: the rate paid, whether fixed or variable, and how often it is paid; the annual equivalent rate (AER) lets different schemes be compared because it includes the effect of compounding; compounding means interest earns interest.
- [ ] C3 Access: how quickly and at what cost money can be withdrawn (instant, notice, fixed term; penalties; branch or online; opening hours; location). Short-term needs need good access; long-term saving can lock money in for higher interest.
- [ ] C4 Tax: DIRT (Deposit Interest Retention Tax) is deducted from interest on most bank, building society and credit union deposits before the saver receives it; State Savings fixed-term products are exempt (S3). Net return matters, not only the headline rate.
- [ ] C5 Other factors: minimum or regular deposit, fees, convenience, whether the scheme suits the family's goal and income (a low-income family needs small deposits and easy access).
- [ ] C6 Matching scheme to need: emergency fund (demand deposit or credit union share account); holiday within a year (demand or notice account); long-term lump sum (term deposit or State Savings fixed term); regular small amounts (credit union or regular saver or instalment savings).
- [ ] C7 Worked recommendation pattern (2015H and 2016O): name the institution, name the scheme type, say how interest is paid, say how easy access is, say what tax applies, give a reason it suits the family.

## 3. Traps

- Credit union "shares" earn a dividend, not interest, and the dividend is not guaranteed.
- Higher interest generally means less access or more risk. Do not say a scheme is "best" without naming the criterion.
- Saving is not the same as investing: savings keep capital safe; investments can lose it.
- DIRT is tax on interest; do not say all savings are taxed, and do not say State Savings fixed-term products have DIRT.
- Investment funds and shares in companies are not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme; credit union savings (shares) are.
- Do not confuse saving with insurance (Insurance subsection) or with pensions as a PRSI benefit (Household income subsection); budgeting for savings is in the budgeting subsection; credit and credit union loans are in Methods of payment and credit.
- Do not use: "Post Office deposit account" or "An Post deposit book" as current products (2016 marking scheme), because they are no longer offered in that form; use State Savings.
- Do not use: National Solidarity Bonds as a product, because their current availability is unconfirmed (do not give a tax reason).
- Do not use: details of when State Savings pay interest, early-encashment rules or a maturity bonus for instalment savings, because they were not confirmed and they change.
- Do not use: any interest rate, AER value, DIRT percentage, minimum deposit or term bonus, because these change.
- Do not use: any fixed term length for State Savings products, because terms vary by issue and the 10-year term belonged to the Solidarity Bond.
- Do not use: "government guarantee" in the 2008-2010 sense of the bank guarantee (2010 marking scheme), because that scheme ended; use the Deposit Guarantee Scheme.
- Do not use: "Savings Protection Scheme" or sums such as 85,000 for credit unions, because the current limit is 100,000 euro.
- Do not use: named high-street bank savings products or provider names, because products change.

## 4. What exams ask

- Reasons for saving (4-6 marks, 18-mark parts): security, emergencies, avoiding credit, planned purchases, earning interest, example to children. Teen saving (2024O).
- Name saving schemes: bank deposit account, credit union, An Post/State Savings certificate, bond, instalment saving (2018H, 2019O, 2012O). Name a scheme suited to a low-income family or a particular family.
- Importance of interest rate, access, security and tax when choosing a scheme (2023H, 2010H): each explained with a link to the saver's need.
- Recommend a savings scheme for a family (2015H, 14 marks): institution, scheme, interest, access, tax; for 2016O one account and one reason (access, local branches).
- Explain DIRT and compare tax treatment; explain compounding or AER only at light depth.
