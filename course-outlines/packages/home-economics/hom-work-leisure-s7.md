## 1. Must cover

Defining leisure
- [ ] D1 Leisure is the residual time left after essential activities (paid or unpaid work, travel to work, school and study, sleep, meals and personal care, household and caring tasks) have been completed; time a person can choose to spend as they wish, for ease, relaxation and enjoyment.
- [ ] D2 A leisure pursuit (activity) is what a person chooses to do in leisure time: sport, music, reading, hobbies, crafts, gardening, socialising, drama, games, cinema, travel.
- [ ] D3 Leisure is not the same as free time that is used for chores. An activity can be work to one person and leisure to another (gardening, cooking) depending on whether it is chosen and enjoyed.
- [ ] D4 Leisure can be active (sport, walking, dance) or passive (reading, TV, listening to music) and can be done alone, with friends or with family.

Function and value of leisure in today's society
- [ ] V1 Relaxation and stress relief from the pressures of work, study and family; recovery from fatigue.
- [ ] V2 Physical wellbeing: fitness, weight control, a healthy heart, strong bones and muscles; helps prevent illness. Importance rises as many jobs are sedentary.
- [ ] V3 Skill development: learning new skills and talents (swimming, music, craft); challenges and a sense of achievement.
- [ ] V4 Social interaction: meeting people, friendships, community involvement; reduces isolation.
- [ ] V5 Family bonding: shared leisure builds relationships, communication and shared memories; parents who use free time constructively are role models for children.
- [ ] V6 Work-life balance: leisure offsets the demands of working life; helps people stay productive and reduces burnout. Also gratifies the senses and gives enjoyment, and contributes to personal development.
- [ ] V7 Value to society: healthier population, tourism and sport industries, community spirit, reduced demands on health services.

Influences on leisure patterns
- [ ] N1 Social influences: socio-economic group (affects what a person can afford and what is available); area and local facilities (clubs, transport); trends and fashions (e.g. skateboarding), peer groups and the media; greater access to foreign travel; technology and social media; education.
- [ ] N2 Cultural influences: traditions of a country or region (hurling, Gaelic football, camogie, Irish music, Irish dancing in Ireland); religious and community traditions; family traditions handed down (children take up parents' pastimes); ethnic and cultural background of a family.
- [ ] N3 Occupation: income (disposable income decides what is affordable); time (long or irregular hours leave little leisure); type of work (a desk worker may choose an active leisure; a manual worker a quiet one); work can lead to leisure (colleagues' clubs); part-time work and career breaks give more time.
- [ ] N4 Cost: membership and joining fees, equipment, clothing, travel; people with little money choose free or low-cost activities (walking, library, park). A recession (as in the 2014 and 2008 papers) reduces spending on costly leisure such as foreign holidays and encourages free or home-based activity.
- [ ] N5 Age and life-cycle stage: young people have fewer commitments; couples with young children have the least time and disposable income; families with school-age children organise leisure around school sport, clubs and outings; teenagers prefer peers; retired couples have more time and (often) money but may have physical limits; activities suitable and safe for the age.
- [ ] N6 Gender: some activities are male- or female-dominated; traditionally the time available to women was less because of household and caring work; gender roles are changing and many sports and activities are now open to everyone, though some differences remain.
- [ ] N7 Health and ability, and available facilities, also matter (one line).

Role of individual and family leisure in development
- [ ] L1 Individual leisure: chosen and enjoyed by one person alone or with friends, e.g. a hobby, running, a club; develops personal interests, independence and identity.
- [ ] L2 Family leisure: members of the family take part together: outings, walks, cycling, picnics, board games, cooking, holidays, sport; builds bonds, communication and shared memories; needs activities suitable for all ages and a cost the family can manage.
- [ ] L3 Physical development: fitness, muscle tone, coordination, energy levels, weight control, bone strength (weight-bearing activities help reduce osteoporosis risk), learning skills such as swimming.
- [ ] L4 Social development: teamwork, co-operation, communication, making friends, leadership, learning to win and lose, belonging to a club or community.
- [ ] L5 Emotional development: self-esteem and confidence, stress relief and relaxation, a sense of achievement and enjoyment, belonging, coping with success and failure.
- [ ] L6 Intellectual development (examiners sometimes include it with physical, social and emotional): concentration, problem solving and planning (chess, cards, puzzles), creativity (art, drama), learning.

Settings and practical work
- [ ] P1 Keep a time-use record for a day; divide into essential activities and leisure; work out leisure as a share of the day.
- [ ] P2 List leisure pursuits with costs (membership, equipment, travel) and benefits; case studies of families at different stages of the life cycle (young couple, family with young children, teenagers, retired couple).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Leisure pursuits... volunteering for pleasure" as an example of a leisure pursuit, because volunteering is classed as work in this chapter (voluntary work); mixing the two loses the definition marks.
- Do not use any figure for how many hours of leisure people have, how much Irish households spend on leisure, or fees, because they change.
- Do not use flamenco or other foreign examples as the main cultural example; use Irish ones (flamenco in Spain is accurate but unnecessary).
- Leisure is time, not an activity; define it as the time left after essential activities. Do not define it as "doing nothing".
- Do not confuse leisure with recreation or sport; sport is one form of leisure.
- Do not repeat the facilities evaluation (value for money, cost per visit, comparison of two facilities) here; it is the next subsection. Costs of pursuits can be mentioned as an influence.
- Voluntary work, unpaid work and paid work are in earlier subsections; refer to them only as "essential activities".
- Do not treat age groups as fixed; the life-cycle stage (children, retirement) matters more than age alone.
- Do not name specific commercial brands, gyms or companies.
- Social development, emotional development and physical development should be given separate headed points in an answer, as the question usually names them (2015H, 2016O, 2024O).

## 4. What exams ask

- Define leisure (2008O, 2016O; 6 marks): residual time after essential activities; chosen freely; for ease, relaxation and enjoyment.
- Discuss the function and value of leisure in today's society (2005O, 2007H): relaxation, physical wellbeing, skills, challenge, social interaction, family bonding, role modelling, work-life balance.
- Importance of leisure for all family members, and the effect of the recession on leisure (2014O): physical, social, emotional and intellectual wellbeing; free or low-cost choices during a recession.
- How age, occupation, culture and cost influence family leisure (2016O, 2024O), social and cultural influences (2007H), gender and age (2024O), three factors influencing family leisure (2005O, 2026O): a short developed point per factor with an example.
- The role of leisure in personal development, physical, social and emotional (2008O, 2014O, 2015H, 2024O, 2026O): fitness and skills; teamwork and friends; confidence, stress relief and belonging.
- Benefits of leisure for family members, evaluated (2024O); three benefits (2026O).
