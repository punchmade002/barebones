# Package: hom-education-s5 — Contemporary initiatives to improve access to education

- **loId:** `hom-education-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-education` · **syllabusRef:** 6.2
- **Syllabus scope:** "Contemporary initiatives to improve access to education [6.2] **[HL]**. Also examined: named initiatives (2022H, 2015H, 2014H, 2011H, 2008H, 2006H, 2004H)." The whole subsection is Higher Level only (Appendix C lists contemporary access initiatives at 6.2). Every block heading ends " (Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Questions ask students to "name and give details of" one or two initiatives (10 to 14 marks) or to "evaluate" initiatives for a group. A good answer names the scheme, who it is for, what it provides, and gives a short evaluation. This is the place for the detail of each scheme; the previous subsections name them in a clause. Initiatives come and change names: use only those below and say what they do, not amounts or numbers. Several schemes named in older marking schemes no longer exist under that name (see Traps).

## 1. Must cover

Routes into third-level education (Higher Level only) (one block)
- [ ] T1 [HL] HEAR (Higher Education Access Route): a CAO admissions route that gives reduced-points entry to places for Leaving Certificate school leavers from socio-economically disadvantaged backgrounds who meet set indicators (such as family income, social welfare or medical card, parental occupation and area). Eligibility does not guarantee a place; the points reduction varies by course and year. Successful students receive post-entry supports in college (dependent on the institution). Run by the participating colleges together (named in 2011H as the example, 10 marks).
- [ ] T2 [HL] DARE (Disability Access Route to Education): a CAO route for school leavers whose disability (such as dyslexia, autism, a sensory or physical disability, a mental health condition or a learning difficulty) has affected their second-level education; reduced points and college supports (assistive technology, exam accommodations, study skills). Apply through the CAO with supporting documents.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Other routes and supports: mature student entry; Post Leaving Certificate (PLC) courses at QQI Levels 5 and 6, which can lead to higher education (Higher Education Links Scheme); Level 7 courses in institutes of technology and technological universities; apprenticeships; the Free Fees Initiative; SUSI grants (means-tested, towards the cost of an approved course); distance learning such as the Open University; scholarships; Springboard+.
- [ ] T4 [HL] Springboard+ (previously Springboard): State-funded part-time or full-time higher education courses (Levels 6 to 9) that are free for unemployed people and returners to the workforce and part-funded for others, to improve employability; managed by the Higher Education Authority.

Retention and inclusion in second-level schools (Higher Level only) (one block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] DEIS (Delivering Equality of Opportunity in Schools, an action plan launched in 2005): identifies primary and second-level schools in disadvantaged communities and gives them extra support: an improved teacher ratio in some schools, the school meals programme, enhanced book grants, literacy and numeracy supports, the Home School Community Liaison scheme, the School Completion Programme, homework clubs, after-school supports and mentoring. Aim: reduce early school leaving and improve attendance, attainment and progression (2018H, 2025 OL).
- [ ] R2 [HL] The School Completion Programme (SCP): a targeted programme of local projects run through Tusla Education Support Service that supports children and young people at risk of leaving school early; offers homework clubs, mentoring, after-school and holiday activities, and helps with transfer from primary to second level. Part of DEIS, with schools in disadvantaged areas.
- [ ] R3 [HL] The Home School Community Liaison (HSCL) scheme: a school-based scheme, also run through Tusla Education Support Service, where a coordinator works with parents and the community to build partnership between home, school and community; helps parents support children's learning (courses for parents, home visits) and helps identify students needing support.
- [ ] R4 [HL] The Junior Certificate School Programme (JCSP; now often called the Junior Cycle School Programme), introduced in 1996 for students at risk of early school leaving: it makes Junior Cycle more accessible with short, achievable learning targets, literacy and numeracy support, and a profile or statements recognising achievement.
- [ ] R5 [HL] Leaving Certificate Applied (LCA): two-year modular programme, with continuous assessment, practical, oral and written examinations, certificate (Pass/Merit/Distinction), for students who prefer a practical or work-related approach; leads to PLC courses (15 OL: one initiative that retains young people in school, 10 marks). Leaving Certificate Vocational Programme (LCVP): vocational link modules (Preparation for the World of Work and Enterprise Education) with a portfolio and written exam; counts for CAO points. Transition Year gives an extra year for maturity and career exploration.
- [ ] R6 [HL] Educational Welfare Service (Tusla): enforces school attendance, follows up non-attendance and supports families, under the Education (Welfare) Act 2000.
- [ ] R7 [HL] Financial and practical supports: Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance (Department of Social Protection), book rental schemes and grants, the School Meals Programme, school transport (including special transport), free exam fees for medical-card holders, laptops and ICT for pupils with special needs.
- [ ] R8 [HL] Inclusion supports: learning support and special education teachers, SNAs, language support (English as an additional language), language assistants in Gaeltacht schools, visiting teachers, reasonable accommodations in exams (RACE), special classes, assistive technology.

Second-chance and adult access initiatives (Higher Level only) (one block)
- [ ] A1 [HL] Youthreach: an integrated education, training and work experience programme for early school leavers aged 15 to 20 without qualifications, run by Education and Training Boards (formerly VECs) in Youthreach centres, including community training workshops; up to two years; participants get a weekly training allowance and a nationally recognised QQI award (2004H, 2022H).
- [ ] A3 [HL] VTOS (Vocational Training Opportunities Scheme): full-time education and training courses (up to two years) for unemployed adults aged 21 and over, run through ETBs; personal development, business and computer skills; payment is made in place of the welfare payment (2004H).
- [ ] A4 [HL] Back to Education Allowance (BTEA): allows people getting certain social welfare payments to take an approved full-time second-level or third-level course and keep an allowance (Department of Social Protection) (2011H, 2022H). It has an annual Cost of Education Allowance add-on for participants with dependent children (introduced in Budget 2017; named without an amount).
- [ ] A5 [HL] Back to Education Initiative (BTEI): part-time further education courses for adults (including those with low qualifications), run by ETBs.
- [ ] A6 [HL] Community education and adult literacy: local, community-based courses run by ETBs; literacy and numeracy tuition (NALA, the National Adult Literacy Agency). Aontas, the Irish national association of adult education, promotes adult learning through advice, research and advocacy (2014H).
- [ ] A7 [HL] SOLAS (Further Education and Training Authority, set up 2013) funds and coordinates further education and training through ETBs, including apprenticeships.

How to answer (Higher Level only) (one block)
- [ ] H1 [HL] "Name and give details of one initiative" (10 to 14 marks): name (3 to 5 marks), then two or three points of detail: target group, what is provided, who runs it, benefit. Do not give three schemes for a one-scheme question.
- [ ] H2 [HL] "Evaluate": say how the scheme helps, then a limit (it does not reach everyone, depends on funding or on a student's disadvantage being identified).
- [ ] H3 [HL] Match scheme to group: pre-school (Early Start, ECCE, AIM, in the provision subsection); disadvantaged schools (DEIS, SCP, HSCL); at risk of leaving (JCSP, SCP, LCA); early school leavers (Youthreach, VTOS, BTEA); third-level access (HEAR, DARE, SUSI, free fees); adults (BTEA, BTEI, Springboard+, community education, Aontas).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "National Behaviour Support Service (NBSS)" as a current scheme, because its current status is unconfirmed after a search; leave it out and name Student Support Teams only if needed.
- Do not use: "Junior Cycle Support Programme" for JCSP, because that name is wrong (JCSP is the Junior Certificate School Programme, also called the Junior Cycle School Programme).
- Do not use: the National Adult Learning Council (NALC), because it was named in a 2014 marking scheme but its current status is unconfirmed; use Aontas, NALA and SOLAS.
- Do not use: FÁS, FETAC, HETAC, VECs or the National Educational Welfare Board as current, because they were replaced or are not confirmed (SOLAS and ETBs, QQI, Tusla Educational Welfare Service); older marking schemes name them.
- Do not use: "Springboard offers free part-time courses to job seekers" as the full description; the current scheme is Springboard+, covers returners as well and part-funds courses for others.
- Do not use: Traveller training centres as a separate current scheme, because a past marking scheme names them but their current status is unconfirmed; Traveller education is covered by general supports and Youthreach.
- Do not use: any training allowance, grant, allowance or fee amount, the number of DEIS schools, SCP projects, Springboard+ places or the number of points a HEAR or DARE student gets, because they change.
- Do not use: "Post Leaving Certificate courses and Level 7 courses provide an alternative route" as one vague line; say PLC courses (QQI Levels 5 and 6, with the Higher Education Links Scheme) and Level 7 courses are separate routes.
- Confusable: DEIS (a programme for schools in disadvantaged areas, not for individuals) vs HEAR (a third-level admissions route for individual students) vs SUSI (a grant); Youthreach (young early leavers, 15 to 20) vs VTOS (adults aged 21 and over) vs BTEA (welfare recipients on approved courses); HSCL (home, school, community link) vs SCP (activities to keep students in school); LCA vs LCVP.
- Describing the whole education system, school types, or the causes of disadvantage and early leaving belong to the provision and equality subsections. Child care, child benefit and the poverty and unemployment responses belong to their chapters; do not describe a general social welfare payment.
- Do not make up a named community initiative, college access programme or local scheme beyond those above.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name and give details of one contemporary initiative that improved access to third level (2011 HL, 10 marks): HEAR (disadvantaged students, reduced points, supports), DARE, BTEA, free fees and grants, Open University, apprenticeships, PLC courses.
- Name and give details of one national initiative improving access to education (2008 HL, 11 marks): Early Start (3 to 4 year olds in disadvantaged areas), DEIS, and others.
- Two initiatives that improved access to second-chance education (2004 HL, 14 marks): VTOS and Youthreach, each with target group, what is provided and the allowance.
- One contemporary initiative improving equality of opportunity (2014 HL, 10 marks): Aontas, adult learning bodies, and others.
- Two contemporary initiatives improving accessibility of education (2022 HL and earlier HL papers): LCA, LCVP, learning support teachers, PLC, VTOS, Youthreach, SNAs, homework clubs, BTEA, visiting teacher service, laptops, transport.
- Three supports that improve accessibility of second-level education (a past HL paper): financial (back to school allowance, BTEA), curriculum (JCSP, LCVP, LCA), examination supports (RACE, level differentiation).
- Initiatives for early school leavers (HL, evaluate two): DEIS, SCP, LCA, JCSP, Youthreach, VTOS.
