# Package: hom-textiles-fibres-s2 — Safety in household textiles

- **loId:** `hom-textiles-fibres-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-textiles-fibres` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.6 (core)
- **Syllabus scope:** safety considerations in selecting household textiles; the identification and effects of one fire-retardant finish; [HL] the fire safety requirements for domestic upholstered furniture, which from July 2026 are those of the EU General Product Safety Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/988). ## 1. Must cover

Safety considerations when selecting household textiles (one block; a "name two" list)
- [ ] S1 Fabric structure: loosely spun yarns and a raised (pile or nap) surface ignite very easily; smooth, tightly woven fabrics are safer.
- [ ] S2 Fibre content: blends of man-made and natural fibres burn more quickly than either alone (scheme point); cotton and other cellulosic fibres burn readily; many synthetics melt and drip.
- [ ] S3 Filling: foam and other fillings in cushions and upholstery burn fiercely and give off toxic fumes; check what the filling is and whether it is fire resistant.
- [ ] S4 Flame-retardant treatment: a finish applied to the fabric, or a fibre that is flame resistant in itself, lowers the risk of ignition.
- [ ] S5 Labels: read labels for fire-resistance information, e.g. a label saying the materials resist ignition by cigarette and match; read care labels so the finish is not damaged.
- [ ] S6 Higher-risk items: children's nightwear, upholstered furniture, curtains near heat, protective workwear; keep textiles away from heaters and cookers (one line).

Flame-retardant finish: Proban (one block; the syllabus asks for one finish, its identification and effects; Pyrovatex is a second)
- [ ] R1 Name: Proban (also Pyrovatex). A flame-retardant finish is a chemical treatment that reduces how easily a fabric ignites and burns.
- [ ] R2 Identification: Proban is used on cotton, linen and other cellulosic fabrics; phosphorus and nitrogen compounds are fixed in the fibre structure (not a surface coat); its presence is shown on the label or by the manufacturer, not by looking or touching.
- [ ] R3 Effect 1: reduces the flammability of the fabric (lowers the risk of ignition).
- [ ] R4 Effect 2: makes the fabric self-extinguish when taken away from a flame; an insulating char forms instead of the fabric burning on.
- [ ] R5 Drawbacks (scheme answers): may cause an allergic reaction in some people; the fabric may need special care in cleaning.
- [ ] R6 Uses: children's nightwear, protective workwear, upholstered furniture, military clothing (scheme list), curtains.
- [ ] R7 Follow care instructions so the finish is not damaged by washing or cleaning.

Fabric for upholstered furniture (one block)
- [ ] U1 Durable and hard wearing: resists abrasion and rubbing from constant use.
- [ ] U2 Resilient: springs back after being sat on, so cushions keep their shape (wool is resilient; cotton loses its shape).
- [ ] U3 Comfortable: pleasant to touch, not harsh against skin.
- [ ] U4 Stain resistant (spills wipe off), fade resistant, easy to clean.
- [ ] U5 Fire resistant: the fabric and the filling should resist ignition, shown on a label.
- [ ] U6 What a label such as "materials meet requirements for resistance to cigarette and match" tells the consumer: the covering has been tested and resists catching fire from a lit cigarette or a match.

Fire safety requirements for domestic upholstered furniture (one block, [HL] only)
- [ ] H1 [HL] The Irish fire safety order for domestic furniture that the syllabus names was revoked in July 2026 (S.I. No. 357 of 2026). Domestic upholstered furniture now falls under the EU General Product Safety Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/988.
- [ ] H2 [HL] The GPSR is an EU regulation, so it applies directly in Ireland (applicable since 13 December 2024). Its core duty: only safe products may be placed on the market; producers must assess risks, and sellers, including online sellers, must not supply unsafe goods.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Practical effect: furniture that meets EU product-safety requirements may be sold to consumers here; the separate Irish fire label is no longer a legal requirement, though consumers can still look for fire-resistance information from the supplier.
- [ ] H4 [HL] The CCPC (named in the outline for consumer protection) is the Irish body for consumer information and complaints (ccpc.ie).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the name, year or label rules of the old Irish furniture fire order, because it is revoked (outline Appendix A); say only that it was replaced.
- Do not use: "combustion-modified high-resilience (CMHR) foam", because it is UK terminology not confirmed for Irish rules.
- Do not use: "Proban is applied to viscose", because Proban is for cotton and other cellulosics and viscose use is unconfirmed; say cotton, linen and cellulosic fabrics.
- Do not use: "foam engulfs a room in flames", because it is dramatic and unconfirmed; say foam burns fiercely and gives off toxic fumes.
- Do not use: a claim that the CCPC enforces furniture fire rules, because this was not confirmed.
- Do not use: a claim that Proban or the label is compulsory, because it is not.
- Proban does not make fabric fireproof; it is flame retardant (reduces burning, self-extinguishes).
- A finish applied to a fabric is not the same as an inherently flame-resistant fibre.
- Fibre properties and the burning test belong to the fibre-profile subsection; care and washing belong to the clothing chapter.
- The [HL] block heading must end "(Higher Level only)"; the rest is core.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name one flame-retardant finish (Proban or Pyrovatex), usually 2 marks, and give two effects (reduces ignition risk, self-extinguishes, or the drawbacks allergy and special cleaning), or two uses (nightwear, workwear, upholstery, military clothing).
- Complete statements on flame-retardant finishes (reduce flammability, self-extinguish, Proban).
- Two safety considerations when choosing household textiles (loose yarns, pile surface, blends, fillings).
- Two desirable properties of upholstery fabric (durable, comfortable, resilient, stain resistant, hard wearing).
- Interpret a fire-resistance label on furniture (resists cigarette and match ignition).
- [HL] State what governs the fire safety of upholstered furniture now (the EU GPSR) and what it requires.
