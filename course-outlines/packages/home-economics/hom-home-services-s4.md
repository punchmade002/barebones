## 1. Must cover

Source and public treatment
- [ ] T1 Public water supply: water is taken from rivers, lakes or underground sources, treated at a treatment plant and piped to homes. Today the public water service is run by Uisce Éireann (formerly Irish Water, renamed from 2023).
- [ ] T2 Screening: coarse and fine screens remove floating debris such as leaves and twigs.
- [ ] T3 Coagulation and sedimentation: chemicals are added which make fine particles clump together; the clumps sink and are removed.
- [ ] T4 Filtration: water passes through filter beds of sand and gravel to remove remaining particles.
- [ ] T5 Chlorination (disinfection): chlorine is added to kill bacteria and other micro-organisms; a small amount stays in the water to keep it safe in the pipes.
- [ ] T6 Fluoridation: fluoride is added to many public supplies to help protect teeth from decay; it is carried out under the Health (Fluoridation of Water Supplies) Act 1960.
- [ ] T7 Testing: samples are tested regularly for quality and purity; treated water is stored in a covered reservoir (high ground gives pressure) before distribution through mains pipes.
- [ ] T8 Rural supply (urban or rural, 2021H): rural homes may use the public mains, a group water scheme, or a private well or spring; private supplies need to be protected from contamination, filtered and treated, and tested.

Cold water supply in the house
- [ ] C1 Mains pipe (the public main under the road) brings water to the area; a service pipe connects it to the house; a stopcock (stop valve) allows the supply to be turned off; the rising main carries water to the attic.
- [ ] C2 Stopcock: a valve on the service pipe (outside the house or under the kitchen sink) to turn off the water in a burst, a repair or when the house is empty.
- [ ] C3 Kitchen cold tap: usually fed directly from the mains, so drinking water is fresh from the main and not from the storage tank.
- [ ] C4 Cold water storage tank (cistern) in the attic: stores water for the bathroom taps, WC cisterns and the hot water cylinder; placed high so gravity gives pressure; covered and insulated; water in it is not for drinking.
- [ ] C5 Ball valve (ball cock, float valve) on the tank: a float on an arm closes the inlet when the water reaches the set level, so the tank is not over-filled; the same valve is in the WC cistern.
- [ ] C6 Overflow pipe (warning pipe): leaves the tank and goes through the wall to outside; carries water away if the ball valve fails, and warns that something is wrong; stops flooding of the attic and ceilings.
- [ ] C7 Distribution pipes lead from the tank to bathroom taps, WC cistern and the hot water cylinder (the cylinder is fed from the tank and is heated by the boiler or immersion).
- [ ] C8 Operation in sequence: water enters through the service pipe and stopcock; rises through the rising main; fills the tank until the ball valve shuts; flows down by gravity when a tap or cistern is opened; the ball valve refills the tank.
- [ ] C9 Diagram: a labelled cold water system: mains pipe, service pipe, stopcock, rising main, tank with ball valve and overflow, distribution pipes, taps, WC, hot water cylinder, kitchen tap.

Preventing freezing
- [ ] F1 Water expands when it freezes (at 0 °C), which can split pipes and tanks; the damage shows when the thaw comes.
- [ ] F2 Precautions: lag pipes and cover or insulate the storage tank, especially in the attic and on outside walls; keep the heating on low when away; keep attic hatch closed, open cupboard doors so warm air reaches pipes; repair draughts near pipes; let a cold tap drip in severe frost; know where the stopcock is.
- [ ] F3 If a pipe freezes: turn off at the stopcock, open the nearest tap, thaw gently from the tap end with a hot water bottle or warm cloth, never a naked flame or blowtorch; check for leaks after thawing.

Water conservation
- [ ] W1 Why: water is a finite resource; treating and pumping it uses energy; saving it cuts costs and emissions.
- [ ] W2 In the bathroom: shower rather than bath, short showers, water-saving or aerated showerheads, turn off taps while brushing teeth and shaving, dual-flush toilet cisterns, a cistern displacement device.
- [ ] W3 In the kitchen and laundry: wash vegetables and dishes in a basin, not under a running tap; full loads in washing machines and dishwashers, or economy programmes; do not overfill the kettle.
- [ ] W4 Fix leaks and dripping taps quickly; a dripping tap wastes a large amount; check the overflow pipe for dripping.
- [ ] W5 Garden and reuse: a rainwater butt for watering plants, rainwater harvesting for flushing toilets, grey-water reuse; sensor or aerator taps.
- [ ] W6 Link to energy: heating less hot water saves both water and energy (timing the immersion, solar heating, insulating the cylinder).

## 3. Traps

- Mains pipe is the public pipe; service pipe brings water into the house; rising main goes up to the tank. Do not use them as if they were one pipe.
- The ball valve controls the water level; the overflow pipe is the backup if it fails; the stopcock isolates the supply. Do not mix their functions.
- Drinking water comes from the kitchen cold tap on the mains, not the attic tank.
- Hot water systems (cylinder, immersion, wet central heating) belong to the heating subsection.
- Insulating the attic floor makes the attic colder, so the tank and pipes in it need their own lagging (see the freezing items and the insulation subsection).
- Water conservation: say why the action saves water. "Turn off taps" alone does not earn marks.
- Do not use: "insulate the attic floor so that heat from the house keeps the tank and pipes above freezing", because it is wrong: insulating the attic floor keeps heat in the rooms and the attic gets colder, so the tank and pipes must be lagged.
- Do not use: "domestic water charges", "water allowance" or any charge for water (the 2015 question stem mentions a new system of charges), because domestic water charges were abolished in 2017 and any excess-use charge is a changing policy; give no charges or thresholds.
- Do not use: "mixer taps deliver the right temperature without running hot and cold water separately" as a conservation method, because the claim is doubtful; use aerator or sensor taps and short showers.
- Do not use: "storage tank capacity of 230 litres" (2015 scheme), because tank sizes vary.
- Do not use: "heat bulb in the attic" (2024 scheme) as freezing precaution, because it is a fire risk and not standard advice.
- Do not use: "lime softens hard water" as a treatment stage (2010 scheme), because it is not confirmed as a standard stage in Irish treatment.
- Do not use: "Irish Water" as the current name; say Uisce Éireann (formerly Irish Water).

## 4. What exams ask

- Functions of the parts of the cold water system (2004O, 2010H, 2012O, 2015O, 2018O, 2023O, 2024O): stopcock (turns off supply for repairs or bursts), storage tank or cistern (stores water, high for pressure), ball valve (controls level), overflow pipe (carries excess outside), mains pipe, service pipe. Usually three or four functions at 4-6 marks each.
- Describe the cold water system with a diagram (2004O, 2015H, 2018O, 2021H): mains, service pipe, stopcock, kitchen tap, rising main, tank, distribution to taps, toilet and cylinder; storage in the attic for pressure; the tank water is not for drinking; urban or rural supply.
- Public water treatment (2010H, 2018O, 2024O): four or more stages, each with its purpose: screening, sedimentation, filtration, chlorination, fluoridation, testing, reservoir.
- Preventing freezing (2004O, 2024O): two or more precautions: lagging pipes, insulating the tank, heating on low, circulating warm air, a dripping tap.
- Conserving water (2015O, 2018O, 2021H, 2025H): three or four methods with reasons: shower for bath, dual flush, taps off, leaks repaired, full loads, rainwater harvest; at Higher Level link to energy efficiency and sustainability (time the immersion, insulate cylinder, grey water).
