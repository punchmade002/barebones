# Package: hom-digestion-metabolism-s3 — Digestion and absorption of lipids

- **loId:** `hom-digestion-metabolism-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-digestion-metabolism` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.4
- **Syllabus scope:** hydrolysis, digestion sequence and absorption mechanism; **[HL]** outline of the utilisation of triglycerides. Also examined: the digestion table for lipids (2014H, 2005H); lipase, fatty acids and glycerol fill-in (2009O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. This subsection takes lipids only. The structure of fats and oils (saturated and unsaturated, sources, cholesterol, essential fatty acids) belongs to the fats and oils chapter; the triglyceride is assumed. Protein and carbohydrate digestion are separate subsections. Ordinary and Higher Level share everything except the utilisation of triglycerides, which is Higher Level only.
- **Exam style:** a Section A table or fill-in: for a named gland or organ, give secretion, enzyme and product. Marks go for lipase and "fatty acids and glycerol". Bile appears as an Ordinary Level true/false item (it is made in the liver).

## 1. Must cover

Hydrolysis (assumed and core)
- [ ] H1 (assumed) Digestion is hydrolysis by enzymes (specific biological catalysts working at about 37 °C).
- [ ] H2 A triglyceride is one glycerol molecule joined to three fatty acids by ester bonds. Lipase hydrolyses these bonds, giving fatty acids and glycerol; intermediate products are monoglycerides and diglycerides.
- [ ] H3 Fat does not mix with water, so large fat globules offer little surface for lipase. This is why fat must be emulsified first.
- [ ] H4 Diagram: a triglyceride, with one glycerol backbone, three fatty acid chains and the ester bonds labelled.

The digestion sequence
- [ ] T1 Table to learn: organ, secretion, enzyme, substrate, product.
  - Pancreas (acts in the duodenum): pancreatic juice; lipase; fats (triglycerides); fatty acids and glycerol.
  - Small intestine: intestinal juice; lipase; fats (triglycerides); fatty acids and glycerol.
  - Liver and gall bladder: bile; no enzyme; emulsifies fat into droplets.
- [ ] T2 Mouth: little or no lipid digestion. Stomach: gastric lipase hydrolyses only a small proportion of triglycerides. Fat slows the emptying of the stomach, so a fatty meal stays there longer and keeps one feeling full.
- [ ] T3 Bile is made by the liver, stored and concentrated in the gall bladder, and released through the bile duct into the duodenum. It contains bile salts, bile pigments (such as bilirubin) and no enzymes.
- [ ] T4 Functions of bile: (a) bile salts emulsify fat, breaking large globules into tiny droplets, which increases the surface area for lipase; (b) bile is alkaline, so it neutralises the acid chyme from the stomach and gives the pancreatic and intestinal enzymes a suitable pH; (c) it carries waste products such as bilirubin out of the body.
- [ ] T5 Emulsification is a physical change, not hydrolysis: no bonds are broken. Define an emulsion as tiny droplets of one liquid dispersed in another.
- [ ] T6 Lipase from pancreatic juice and intestinal juice hydrolyses the emulsified triglycerides to fatty acids and glycerol (some monoglycerides). Most fat is digested in the small intestine.
- [ ] T7 Diagram: liver, gall bladder, bile duct, pancreas, pancreatic duct and duodenum, with the flow of bile and pancreatic juice.

Absorption and route
- [ ] A1 Absorption is through the villi of the small intestine; each villus has a capillary network and a lacteal (a lymph capillary).
- [ ] A2 Short-chain fatty acids and glycerol are small and water-soluble, so they pass into the capillaries and the hepatic portal vein to the liver.
- [ ] A3 Long-chain fatty acids and monoglycerides enter the cells of the villus, where they are rebuilt into triglycerides and packaged with protein (and other lipids) as chylomicrons. These are too big for the capillaries, so they enter the lacteals.
- [ ] A4 Lymph carries the chylomicrons along the lymphatic system to the thoracic duct, which empties into the blood near the heart. The fat therefore reaches the general circulation without first passing through the liver; the liver later removes what is left of the chylomicrons from the blood.
- [ ] A5 Route: mouth and stomach (little), duodenum (bile and lipase), villi (absorption), lacteals and lymph (long-chain fat) or capillaries and hepatic portal vein (short-chain products), liver, general circulation.
- [ ] A6 Diagram: villus cross-section showing the capillary network, lacteal and the two routes.

Utilisation of triglycerides [HL]
- [ ] U1 [HL] Fat is carried in the blood as lipoproteins (lipids packed with protein). Chylomicrons carry dietary fat; very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL) carry fat made in the liver.
- [ ] U2 [HL] At the body tissues the enzyme lipoprotein lipase hydrolyses the triglycerides to fatty acids and glycerol, which cells take up.
- [ ] U3 [HL] Fatty acids are oxidised in respiration, especially in muscle, to release energy (fat is the most concentrated energy source: 37 kJ or 9 kcal per gram). Glycerol is converted into glucose in the liver or used in respiration.
- [ ] U4 [HL] Surplus fatty acids are rebuilt into triglycerides and stored in adipose (fat) tissue under the skin and around organs, which gives insulation, protection of organs and an energy reserve.
- [ ] U5 [HL] The liver also converts surplus carbohydrate (and protein) into fat.

## 3. Traps

- Bile is **not an enzyme** and does not chemically digest fat; it emulsifies. Lipase digests. Students write "bile digests fat" or "bile is an enzyme".
- Bile is **made in the liver** and **stored in the gall bladder** (not made there). It is not made in the stomach or pancreas.
- Emulsification is physical, hydrolysis is chemical. Do not use the word "emulsify" for what lipase does.
- Lipase works in the duodenum and small intestine. Pancreatic lipase is the main one; the schemes accept intestinal juice lipase too, so give both rows.
- The lacteal carries fat, the capillary carries short-chain products; do not say all fat goes by the lacteal or all by the portal vein.
- The mouth has no lipid digestion for exam purposes. Do not teach lingual lipase.
- Triglyceride structure (saturated and unsaturated fatty acids, cholesterol, health effects) belongs to the fats chapter. Hydrolytic rancidity (lipase in food) belongs to the food-spoilage content.
- Do not use: "Liver: receives the absorbed fats and processes them before they are distributed around the body" from the old notes, because long-chain fat travels as chylomicrons in lymph and enters the blood at the thoracic duct, bypassing the liver at first; only the short-chain products go by the hepatic portal vein first.
- Do not say fats are absorbed as whole triglycerides; they are absorbed as fatty acids and glycerol (or monoglycerides) and rebuilt inside the cell.

## 4. What exams ask

- The Section A table (2014H, 2005H): for the small intestine, give intestinal juice, lipase, fatty acids and glycerol; for the pancreas, pancreatic juice, lipase, fatty acids and glycerol. The "change" or "product" column is always fatty acids and glycerol.
- A fill-in at Ordinary Level (2009O): the enzyme lipase converts lipids to fatty acids and glycerol.
- Ordinary Level 2023 true/false: bile is not produced in the stomach (it is produced in the liver).
- Higher Level long answers: trace fat through the digestive system. Expected content: T2 (little digestion before the small intestine), T3 and T4 (bile and emulsification), T6, A1 to A5. Marks for each secretion with its job.
- Higher Level utilisation: lipoproteins, lipase at the tissues, oxidation for energy, storage in adipose tissue, glycerol to glucose.
- Tips to make: three functions of bile (emulsify, neutralise acid chyme, remove waste); bile has no enzyme; emulsification increases surface area; fat leaves the villi by the lacteal, the other products by the portal vein; always give both fatty acids and glycerol.
