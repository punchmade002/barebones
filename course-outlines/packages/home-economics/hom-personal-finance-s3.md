# Package: hom-personal-finance-s3 — Methods of payment and credit

- **loId:** `hom-personal-finance-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-personal-finance` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.3
- **Syllabus scope (outline):** methods of payment (cash or credit) for household goods and services, with criteria for selection and availability; a brief outline of the protection given to the consumer by the Consumer Credit Act 1995 (as amended), including hire-purchase and other credit agreements. Settings: investigating forms of credit from advertisements, brochures and credit card material; comparing cash prices with the cost of credit.
- **Also examined (outline):** Consumer Credit Act 1995 (2026O, 2025H, 2023H, 2010H, 2004H).
- **Higher Level only:** nothing marked [HL]. Do not mark anything HL.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. The Act is the Consumer Credit Act 1995 (as amended). Housing finance (mortgages) is in the Housing subject. Interest rates, fees and limits are changing figures: leave them out.

## 1. Must cover

Methods of payment
- [ ] M1 Cash: notes and coins; immediate, no cost, easy to see what has been spent; unsafe in large amounts, no record, not accepted for online or distance purchases.
- [ ] M2 Cheque: a written order to a bank to pay a sum from the account to a named person; needs a current account; slower, can be refused if funds are short. Less used now but still examined.
- [ ] M3 Debit card: payment taken straight from the bank account (so spending stays within the balance); contactless or chip and PIN; can be used online and for cashback and ATM withdrawals. Older name: Laser card (no longer issued; marking schemes still use the term).
- [ ] M4 Credit card: buy now, pay later up to a credit limit; an interest-free period if the balance is cleared by the due date; minimum monthly repayment; interest on unpaid balance; card issuer's protection for some purchases (assumed, one clause only).
- [ ] M5 Direct debit: the account holder authorises a company to take varying amounts from the account on a due date (utility bills, insurance); and standing order: the account holder instructs the bank to pay a fixed amount to a named payee at regular intervals (rent, savings, loan repayments). Difference: amount fixed (standing order) or variable (direct debit); who controls the payment.
- [ ] M6 Online or mobile banking and payment apps: transfers and bill payments from a phone or computer; convenient, record kept, needs internet and security care.
- [ ] M7 Prepaid methods, gift vouchers and store credit: one line only.
- [ ] M8 Criteria for choosing a method: cost (fees, interest); security (risk of loss or fraud, record of payment); convenience and speed; availability (accepted by the seller, and whether the buyer holds the card or account); control over spending and budgeting; size of purchase; and whether the household needs to spread the cost.

Types of credit
- [ ] C1 Credit means obtaining goods, services or money now and paying later, usually with interest or charges.
- [ ] C2 Credit card (see M4), charge card (full balance repaid each month, no interest), store card (credit from one retailer, usually costly).
- [ ] C3 Overdraft: the bank lets the current account go below zero up to an agreed limit; interest on the amount overdrawn; short-term and flexible but can be costly.
- [ ] C4 Term (personal) loan from a bank, credit union or lender: a fixed sum, repaid in fixed instalments over a set period at an agreed interest rate; suits one-off large purchases such as a car; credit unions usually lend to members and often have lower rates (assumed, one clause).
- [ ] C5 Hire purchase (HP): the buyer pays a deposit and then instalments over an agreed period; uses the goods straight away; the finance company owns the goods until the last payment is made.
- [ ] C6 Other forms named in marking schemes: budget account (spreads bills), credit sale (assumed), moneylender (licensed lender, usually very expensive), mortgage (house purchase; see the Housing subject).
- [ ] C7 Advantages of credit: buy and use goods now, afford large or emergency purchases, spread cost, take advantage of sales, no need to carry cash, a good record helps later borrowing. Disadvantages: interest makes goods cost more than the cash price, encourages impulse buying and overspending, many agreements can lead to unmanageable debt, missed repayments can lead to penalties, repossession or a poor credit record.
- [ ] C8 Factors to consider before taking credit (2025O): whether the purchase is needed, the total cost, interest rate and APR, repayment amount and length, fees and penalties for missed payments, ownership and what happens if repayments are missed, and whether it fits the budget.
- [ ] C9 Cost of credit and comparing with cash: cash price (price paid in full at the time); total amount repayable (deposit plus all instalments); cost of credit (total repayable minus cash price); APR (annual percentage rate) is the yearly cost of the credit as a percentage, which allows different offers to be compared. Worked comparison: the household adds all payments, subtracts the cash price, and judges whether using the goods now is worth the extra.

Hire purchase detail
- [ ] H1 How HP works (C5); the buyer is the "hirer" until the final payment, then becomes the owner.
- [ ] H2 Advantages: use now, fixed repayments, no large lump sum; disadvantages: dearer than cash, buyer does not own the goods until the end, missed payments can lead to repossession, goods may lose value faster than the debt falls.
- [ ] H3 Protection: once one-third or more of the total hire-purchase price has been paid (counting deposit, instalments and the value of a trade-in), the owner cannot repossess the goods without a court order. Before that the owner must follow a procedure (a default notice and time to put things right). The buyer may end the agreement.

Consumer Credit Act 1995 (as amended): brief outline
- [ ] A1 The Act protects consumers who borrow or buy on credit; it covers credit agreements including hire purchase, and regulates lenders, moneylenders and credit advertising; the Central Bank of Ireland regulates the sector (named once).
- [ ] A2 A credit agreement must be in writing and signed, and gives the APR and the terms of repayment; the consumer must get a copy.
- [ ] A3 The consumer has a short cooling-off period (10 days after receiving a copy) to withdraw from a credit agreement in writing.
- [ ] A4 Credit advertisements must be accurate and show the APR.
- [ ] A5 Hire purchase: the one-third rule and the court order (H3). Rights over faulty goods bought on credit belong to the Consumer Rights and Protection subjects.
- [ ] A6 Where to get help: MABS and the Central Bank's consumer hub, the CCPC, and the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman for complaints about a bank or lender (names only, detail belongs to Consumer Rights and Protection).

## 3. Traps

- Hire purchase is different from buying on credit with a credit card: ownership stays with the finance company until the last instalment.
- Debit card takes money immediately; credit card borrows. Charge card has no interest because it is repaid in full.
- Direct debit amounts can change; standing order is fixed.
- Cost of credit excludes the cash price. Do not say the cost of credit is the total repayable.
- Do not say the Act bans credit or caps interest.
- Mortgages and housing loans are in the Housing subject; saving up before buying belongs to the Saving subsection; consumer rights on faulty goods, the CCPC and the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman belong to the Consumer Rights and Protection subjects; budgeting belongs to the Household expenditure and budgeting subsection.
- Do not use: the claim that the Consumer Credit Act 1995 replaced the Hire Purchase Acts 1946 and 1960 outright, because their exact repeal status is unconfirmed.
- Do not use: "Laser card" as a current product (2010 marking scheme), because it was withdrawn in 2014; use debit card.
- Do not use: the list "APR, total cash price, cost of credit, number and amount of instalments and date of each instalment" as what every credit advertisement must show, because it is unconfirmed; say only that an advertisement must be accurate and show the APR.
- Do not use: any interest rate, APR figure, credit limit, fee, overdraft charge or amount, because these change.
- Do not use: the claim that cheques are being phased out or have an end date, because no end date is confirmed; say only that they are used less.
- Do not use: section numbers of the Act, the termination-right section, and the exact default-notice period, because they are not needed and may be amended.
- Do not use: pawnbroker as a named type of credit, because it was named only in a marking scheme and the governing Act is unconfirmed.
- Do not use: anything about the new EU consumer credit directive or "buy now pay later" regulation, because the Irish transposition detail is not yet confirmed, although Directive (EU) 2023/2225 applies from 20 November 2026.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name types of credit for household goods or for a car: credit card, term loan, overdraft, hire purchase, store card, credit union loan (2015O, 2018O, 2022O, 2023O).
- State an advantage and a disadvantage of credit, or of credit for utility bills, holidays or a car (6 marks and 10 marks, many years; 2021H 16-mark evaluation of credit for a family holiday). Advantages: immediate use, spread cost, emergencies, sales, safer than cash. Disadvantages: interest, overspending, impulse buying, debt, repossession, poor credit rating. Answers should link each point to the household.
- Factors to consider when buying on credit (2025O): need, cost, interest, fees, repayment length, penalties, ownership.
- Explain two methods of payment: credit card, debit (Laser) card, direct debit, standing order (2010O): what each does and one feature.
- Explain hire purchase with an advantage and a disadvantage, including the one-third and court order rule (2009O).
- Outline the protection of the Consumer Credit Act 1995 (2026O, 2025H, 2023H, 2010H, 2004H): written agreement, APR, copy, cooling-off, advertising, hire-purchase protection; the Act's name must be right.
- Suggest a method of payment suitable for a given purchase (private health insurance: direct debit, card; a car: loan or HP).
- Compare cash price with cost of credit: needs the cash price, total repayable and APR idea.
