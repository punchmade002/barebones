# Package: hom-food-additives-s2 — Packaging and labelling

- **loId:** `hom-food-additives-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-additives` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.5
- **Syllabus scope:** packaging materials and their suitability for purpose and environmental impact; food labelling as a source of consumer information. Also examined: packaging materials (2026O, 2022H, 2010H); modified atmosphere and biodegradable packaging (2017H, 2004H); labelling (2026H, 2024O, 2016H, 2005H). Nothing in this subsection is Higher Level only; mark nothing HL (the legal side of food labelling law is HL in the syllabus [1.3.7] but is taught in the food-safety chapter's law subsection).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Labelling is taught here as consumer information and as an outline of what the current EU rules require. The nutrient and additive content of labels is cross-referenced only.

## 1. Must cover

Packaging materials and their uses (one block)
- [ ] K1 Metals (aluminium, tin/tinplate): strong, airtight, heat-processable, recyclable; used for canned fruit and fish, takeaway containers, aerosol whipped cream. Cans are lacquered inside so the metal does not react with the food.
- [ ] K2 Glass (jars, bottles): hygienic, transparent, impervious, reusable and recyclable, resealable; used for beverages, mayonnaise, sauces, preserves, oil. Heavy and breakable.
- [ ] K3 Plastics (polythene, PET and others): light, flexible, moisture-proof, can be heat-sealed and transparent; used for polythene bags, soft-drink bottles, yoghurt pots, dairy spreads, dried pasta. Mostly non-biodegradable.
- [ ] K4 Polystyrene: lightweight, insulating, shock-absorbing, easily moulded and stackable; used for takeaway containers and egg trays.
- [ ] K5 Paper and cardboard: light, cheap, printable, renewable, biodegradable, recyclable; used for cereal boxes, egg cartons, paper bags for bread, cakes, flour and sugar. Not moisture-proof unless coated.
- [ ] K6 Waxed carton: waxed or coated board is waterproof, cheap, printable, lightweight and heat-sealable; recyclable/biodegradable according to the scheme.

Suitability for purpose (one block)
- [ ] S1 The qualities examiners list: strong and durable; protects from damage and contamination; airtight or moisture- and vapour-proof; hygienic and safe (does not taint or react with the food, odourless); easy to open and reseal; lightweight, stackable, easy to store; heat resistant where food is heated in the pack; printable; attractive and economical.
- [ ] S2 Functions: protection, containment (portion size), information, tamper evidence, convenience.

Environmental impact (one block)
- [ ] E1 Metal and plastic: not biodegradable and made from non-renewable resources, but recyclable. Glass: reusable and recyclable. Paper and cardboard: renewable, biodegradable, recyclable. Polystyrene: bulky, not readily biodegradable.
- [ ] E2 Reduce impact by using less packaging, reusing containers and recycling. A statement "all packaging is biodegradable and recyclable" is false.
- [ ] E3 Irish context (2024O names it): the Re-turn deposit return scheme for plastic drinks bottles and aluminium and steel cans, which supports recycling by refunding a deposit (15c for containers under 500 ml, 25c for 500 ml and over).

Modified atmosphere and biodegradable packaging (one block)
- [ ] M1 Modified atmosphere packaging (MAP): air removed and replaced with a controlled mixture of carbon dioxide, nitrogen and oxygen, and the pack heat-sealed; slows the growth of bacteria, yeasts and moulds, so shelf life increases. Used for fresh meat, fish, fruit and vegetables, cheese and baked goods; also keeps colour.
- [ ] M2 Biodegradable packaging: breaks down into the raw materials of nature through the action of micro-organisms. Examples from the scheme: paper bags (bread, cakes, sugar, flour) and cardboard (cereals, takeaway foods, eggs).
- [ ] M3 Diagram cue: a labelled sealed pack, with the gas mixture in the tray and the heat-sealed film lid.

Legal labelling requirements (one block)
- [ ] L1 Current law: Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 on the provision of food information to consumers, given effect in Ireland by the European Union (Provision of Food Information to Consumers) Regulations 2014 (S.I. No. 556 of 2014). The official agencies working under FSAI service contracts enforce it; the FSAI coordinates.
- [ ] L2 Mandatory particulars (outline): name of the food; list of ingredients in descending order by weight; allergens emphasised in the ingredients list; net quantity; date of minimum durability (best before) or use-by date; special storage conditions and instructions for use; name and address of the food business operator; country of origin or place of provenance where required; nutrition declaration.
- [ ] L3 Additives are named by class name followed by the specific name or E number (e.g. preservative: E202).
- [ ] L4 Date marks: use-by on highly perishable food (safety; do not eat after); best-before on other food (quality; may still be safe after).
- [ ] L5 A lot or batch code lets a product be traced and recalled.

Nutrition declaration, claims and labelling as consumer information (one block)
- [ ] N1 The nutrition declaration: energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, salt; per 100 g or 100 ml, and often per portion as well.
- [ ] N2 Nutrition and health claims (such as "low fat", "high in fibre") must be accurate and not misleading under EU rules; "low fat" doesn't mean low sugar, salt or energy.
- [ ] N3 How labels help consumers (the Ordinary Level 2025 question asks for three benefits): compare products; choose for special diets (low salt, coeliac, allergy); safety and waste reduction (dates, storage, reheating); buy Irish (origin); traceability and complaints (manufacturer details, lot code); marks for quality assurance, organic and fair trade guide ethical choice.
- [ ] N4 Information on packaging as a role of packaging (2024O): ingredients, weight, nutrition, allergens, product identity, cooking instructions, branding.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "the Food Safety Authority of Ireland enforces the labelling regulations" as stated in the old notes, because the FSAI coordinates and the official agencies under its service contracts (HSE Environmental Health Service, Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, local authorities and others) carry out the enforcement.
- Do not use: "lot or batch code" as a Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 requirement, because lot marking comes from separate EU rules; say only that labels carry a lot code for traceability (it is a scheme answer).
- Do not use: the Labelling Regulations of 1982 and 1991 or the Consumer Information Act 1978, because they are superseded, even if a marking scheme names them.
- Do not use: any figure for font size, percentage thresholds for "low fat" or other claims, or nutrient reference values, because they are changing or unnecessary.
- "Paper packaging is moisture proof" is false (2022O). Paper is only moisture-resistant when waxed or coated.
- Use-by is a safety date; best-before is a quality date. Don't swap them. Don't say food is "unsafe" the day after a best-before date.
- Biodegradable (breaks down naturally) is different from recyclable (reprocessed); a material can be one without the other. Glass is recyclable but not biodegradable; paper is both.
- MAP is a packaging technique that keeps food longer; it is not an additive or a preservative added to the food.
- Front-of-pack traffic-light labelling is voluntary in Ireland; don't state it as a legal requirement.
- Neighbours: the additive classes and E-number system belong to the classes and legal-control subsections (only the labelling rule for additives is given here); the details of general food law and the Acts on food hygiene belong to the food-safety chapter; the Consumer Protection Act 2007 and misleading descriptions belong to the consumer-rights chapter; contaminants that migrate from packaging into food belong to the contaminants subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name three packaging materials with one use each (Ordinary 2006, 6 marks); name two desirable qualities of packaging (Ordinary 2015); state a different benefit of polystyrene, a glass bottle and a waxed carton (Higher 2022); three advantages of paper or cardboard (Ordinary 2026: light, cheap, printable, renewable, recyclable, biodegradable).
- True/false statements on packaging (2022O): it protects food from damage and contamination (true); paper is moisture proof (false); all packaging is biodegradable and recyclable (false).
- Explain modified atmosphere packaging and biodegradable packaging with a use each (Higher 2004, 2017; 6 marks): the gas mixture and the slowing of micro-organisms; breaks down into the raw materials of nature, with paper bags and cardboard as examples.
- Evaluate the role of packaging for suitability for purpose, environmental impact and information (Ordinary 2024, 15 marks): a good answer gives three developed paragraphs, uses a material-by-material comparison, names recycling and Re-turn, and lists label contents.
- Three benefits of labelling to a consumer (Ordinary 2025, 6 marks) and four items of consumer information with the importance of each (Ordinary 2006).
- Analyse how labelling helps in choosing convenience foods (Higher 2026, 16 marks): nutrition declaration compared per 100 g; ingredient order; allergens; additives by name or E number; dates; storage; origin; quality marks; plus the point that the full label must be read, because claims do not tell the whole story.
- Labelling questions in 2016H and 2005H ask for the contents of a label as part of a food profile (name, ingredients, nutrition, date, storage, manufacturer).
