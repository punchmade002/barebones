# Package: hom-food-preservation-s5 — Chemical preservation

- **loId:** `hom-food-preservation-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-food-preservation` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.9
- **Syllabus scope:** sugar, salt, vinegar, smoke and permitted chemical preservatives; chutneys and pickles. Also examined: chutney making (2026O, 2011O). No Higher Level only items marked. Ordinary and Higher Level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The syllabus groups the "natural" preservatives (sugar, salt, vinegar, smoke, and alcohol in the 2004 scheme) with permitted additives, and adds chutneys and pickles as the applied home methods. Smoking is examined on its own at Higher Level in 2019 and 2025 (cold versus hot smoking of fish). Permitted preservatives are regulated under the EU additives regulation; the notes should name it and the E-number idea without a list of limits. Additives in general (colours, emulsifiers, antioxidants) belong to the food additives chapter; here only preservatives are covered.

## 1. Must cover

Principle (one block)
- [ ] P1 Chemical preservatives work by one or more of: drawing water out of micro-organisms and food by osmosis (sugar, salt); lowering the pH (vinegar, other acids); poisoning or inhibiting organisms or their enzymes (sulphur dioxide, benzoates, sorbates, nitrite, smoke chemicals); acting as antioxidants or inhibiting mould.
- [ ] P2 Natural (traditional) preservatives: sugar, salt, vinegar, smoking and alcohol (2004O scheme). Permitted additives: sorbic acid, benzoic acid, sulphur dioxide and sulphites, nitrites and nitrates.
- [ ] P3 Many chemical methods are combined with heat (jam, chutney) or with drying (smoked and salted fish).

Sugar (one block; examined 2009O, 2010H, 2021H, 2024H)
- [ ] S1 A strong sugar solution draws water out of micro-organisms by osmosis, so they cannot multiply (jam, marmalade, syrups, crystallised and candied fruit, condensed milk).
- [ ] S2 The concentration must be high enough; low-sugar jam can ferment or grow mould (yeasts and moulds tolerate sugar better than bacteria), so sealing is also needed.
- [ ] S3 Sugar's other roles in this setting: gel formation with pectin, sweetness, colour. A health point: preserves with a lot of added sugar; the Department of Health healthy eating guidelines advise limiting foods and drinks high in sugar.

Salt (one block)
- [ ] L1 Salt draws water out of food and micro-organisms by osmosis and makes it unavailable to organisms and enzymes. Dry salting: the food is packed in salt (salt cod, bacon, salted herring); brining: the food is soaked in a strong salt solution (olives, ham, cucumbers, pickled meat).
- [ ] L2 Curing: salt with sodium nitrite or nitrate (and sometimes sugar) preserves meat (bacon, ham, corned beef); nitrite inhibits Clostridium botulinum and keeps the pink colour.
- [ ] L3 Disadvantage: high sodium (salt) intake is linked to high blood pressure; the Department of Health healthy eating guidelines advise reducing salt; salted and cured foods are high in sodium. Salted foods need soaking or rinsing to remove salt before use.

Vinegar (acetic acid) (one block)
- [ ] V1 Vinegar is a dilute solution of acetic acid. It lowers the pH of the food so that most spoilage and food poisoning bacteria cannot grow. It also gives flavour.
- [ ] V2 Used in pickles (onions, beetroot, red cabbage, cucumber, eggs), chutneys, relishes and sauces; sometimes with sugar and spices; the vinegar must be strong enough that the food's own water does not dilute it.
- [ ] V3 Spiced vinegar (cold or hot) pours over packed vegetables in sterilised jars; malt vinegar is the usual home choice; use non-metallic lids because the acid corrodes metal.

Chutneys and pickles (one block; examined 2026O, 2011O, 2018O, 2015O)
- [ ] C1 Chutney: fruit and/or vegetables (apples, tomatoes, onions, marrow, plums) chopped and boiled slowly with vinegar, sugar and spices until thick; the cooking destroys micro-organisms and enzymes; the vinegar and sugar inhibit growth afterwards; hot jars, sealed, then matured (it improves in flavour after weeks) in a cool dark place.
- [ ] C2 Chutney is a preservation method: the 2015O and 2018O schemes treat it as true that it prevents spoilage, and the 2011O scheme names sugar and vinegar as the preservative in chutney.
- [ ] C3 Pickles: raw (or lightly cooked or brined) vegetables, eggs or fruit packed whole or in pieces in spiced vinegar (and sometimes brine first). Chutney is boiled to a thick mixture; pickle keeps its pieces whole in a liquid.
- [ ] C4 Chutney method in outline for a practical: sterilise jars; prepare and chop the fruit and vegetables; cook slowly with vinegar, sugar and spices until the mixture is thick and no liquid vinegar shows when a spoon is drawn across; pot into hot jars; seal with vinegar-proof lids; label; store cool and dark; refrigerate once opened.
- [ ] C5 Spoilage causes: too little vinegar or sugar; not cooked long enough; jars or lids not sterile or a faulty seal; metal lid corroded by the vinegar.

Smoking (one block; examined 2019H, 2025H)
- [ ] M1 Smoking exposes food (fish, meat, cheese) to smoke from smouldering wood (oak, beech, hickory) or sawdust; smoke deposits chemicals (phenols, formaldehyde, organic acids) that slow microbial growth and oxidation, and the surface dries, giving flavour and colour. Smoking is almost always combined with salting (brining or dry salting) first.
- [ ] M2 Cold smoking: food is smoked at a low temperature not exceeding about 27 °C; the food is not cooked, so (as the 2019H scheme says) it requires further cooking. Smoked salmon is an example of a cold-smoked fish.
- [ ] M3 Hot smoking: the temperature is raised gradually to about 80 °C so that the food is cooked as it is smoked and is ready to eat (smoked mackerel).
- [ ] M4 Steps (2025H): fillet and trim; brine or dry-salt; rinse and dry; smoke at the chosen temperature; cool.
- [ ] M5 Smoked foods have limited shelf life unless chilled; high in salt; some smoke compounds (tars) are a health concern if eaten in large amounts.

Permitted chemical preservatives (one block)
- [ ] A1 Regulated in the EU by Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 on food additives: only listed substances may be used, in listed foods, up to maximum levels. They are given E numbers (E200 to E299 are the preservatives) and are tested for safety by the European Food Safety Authority.
- [ ] A2 Examples: sorbic acid (E200) and sorbates (E202 potassium sorbate) against moulds and yeasts in cheese, baked goods, soft drinks; benzoic acid (E210) and sodium benzoate (E211) in soft drinks, pickles, fruit products; sulphur dioxide (E220) and sulphites in dried fruit, wine, fruit juice, preventing browning and inhibiting organisms; sodium nitrite (E250) and sodium nitrate (E251) in cured meats.
- [ ] A3 Labelling: under Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 a preservative in the ingredients list must be given with its function ("preservative") and its name or E number. Sulphur dioxide and sulphites above the stated level are one of the 14 allergens that must be emphasised in the ingredients list; some people (asthmatics) react to sulphites.
- [ ] A4 Evaluation: benefits (longer shelf life, safety, less waste, lower cost); concerns (sensitivity or allergic reactions in some people, consumer preference for fewer additives, high sodium in salt-based preservation).
- [ ] A5 Other preservation by alcohol (2004O scheme): fruit preserved in brandy or rum; keep as one line.

## 3. Traps

- Salt and sugar preserve by osmosis; vinegar by acidity; they are different principles. Do not say "vinegar draws out water".
- Chutney and jam differ: chutney uses vinegar, sugar and spices and needs no pectin; jam relies on pectin, acid and sugar to set. Jam making belongs to heat processing.
- Cold smoking does not cook the food; hot smoking does. Cold smoked fish must be treated as not cooked (the 2019 and 2025 schemes), though ready-to-eat cold-smoked salmon is sold under its own safety controls; do not advise eating raw smoked fish at home.
- Chemical preservation is not the same as "additives" generally; do not drift into colours, emulsifiers, stabilisers or antioxidants (food additives chapter, with physical conditioning agents at Higher Level).
- Permitted preservatives are controlled by the EU additives regulation; the "E number" shows an approved additive, not that the additive is natural or unsafe.
- Fermentation also produces acid and alcohol that preserve, but it is Higher Level and has its own subsection; vinegar made by fermentation is described there. Irradiation is also sometimes listed as "chemical" in an old scheme (2004O); in this chapter it is a separate subsection.
- Sugar and salt are health issues too: say what the guidelines advise (limit sugar and salt) without giving amounts.
- Labelling law in full belongs to the food legislation subsection of the food safety chapter; give only the one-line link here.
- Do not use: "Preservatives are used at concentrations that stop micro-organisms from growing; a low concentration may encourage their growth instead", because the general statement is unconfirmed; keep the sugar point only (low-sugar jam can ferment).
- Do not use: "Permitted preservatives carry E numbers and must be named in the ingredients list... under Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011" in that loose form; the label gives the function (preservative) followed by the name or the E number.
- Do not use: cold smoking "below about 30 °C"; the schemes give 27 °C.

## 4. What exams ask

- Name or match preservation methods (2026O, 2021O, 2018O, 2015O, 2011O, 2004O, 2004O natural preservatives): chutney is fruit/vegetables boiled with sugar, vinegar and spices; sugar and vinegar are the preservatives in chutney; sugar is the preservative in jam; two natural preservatives (sugar, vinegar, salt, smoking, alcohol).
- Chutney making (2026O, 2011O): ingredients and function of each (fruit/vegetable, vinegar as acid, sugar, spices for flavour), heat destroys organisms, sealing hot.
- Cold smoking and hot smoking of fish (2019H and 2025H, 6 marks): how each is carried out, with the temperature (27 °C and 80 °C) and whether further cooking is needed.
- Higher: the role of sugar (preservation by osmosis; also gel formation and fermentation, 2010H, 2021H) and salt as preservatives within a longer "set out details" answer for a method of preservation; fish preservation (2023H: salting, brining, smoking, canning).
- Possible evaluation: advantages and disadvantages of preservatives in food (shelf life, safety versus sensitivity, high salt and sugar).
- Tip content: for each substance give how it works (osmosis, pH, poison) and one food example.
