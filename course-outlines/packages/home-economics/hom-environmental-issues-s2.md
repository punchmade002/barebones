## 1. Must cover

The waste hierarchy and sustainable waste management
- [ ] H1 Sustainable waste management means reducing waste first, then reusing, recycling and recovering value, and disposing of only what is left. The ranking from most to least preferred: prevention, minimisation, reuse, recycling, recovery (including energy recovery), disposal. Marking schemes phrase it as reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose.
- [ ] H2 Prevention and minimisation come first: buying only what is needed and avoiding excess packaging stops waste being created.
- [ ] H3 Reuse: using an item again for its original or another purpose (refilling a container, reusing a jar, repairing, donating, second-hand).
- [ ] H4 Recycling: processing used materials into new products (paper, glass, metals, some plastics, textiles). It saves raw materials and usually energy, and keeps waste out of landfill.
- [ ] H5 Recovery: burning residual waste to generate heat or electricity (energy from waste); also gas collected from landfill used for energy (2013O scheme).
- [ ] H6 Disposal, mainly landfill, is the least preferred option: it uses land, and food and other organic waste decomposes without oxygen and releases methane, a potent greenhouse gas.
- [ ] H7 The waste hierarchy is applied in Irish law through the Waste Management Act 1996 (as amended), which sets up controls on waste collection and movement and gives local authorities and the EPA roles in promoting waste reduction, recycling and reuse (2004O scheme).

Composting
- [ ] K1 Biodegradable material (food scraps, tea leaves, garden waste) is broken down by micro-organisms into a nutrient-rich soil conditioner.
- [ ] K2 Benefits: keeps biodegradable waste out of landfill so less methane is produced; returns nutrients and organic matter to soil; reduces the need for peat-based compost; is cheap for households.
- [ ] K3 In Irish households food and garden waste is collected in the brown bin where a collection is provided; the three bins are green (dry recyclables), brown (food and garden waste) and black (general waste).

Household actions to reduce waste
- [ ] A1 Plan meals and write a shopping list so food is not thrown away.
- [ ] A2 Buy concentrated products (concentrated detergents need less packaging per wash), buy in bulk where it cuts packaging per unit, use refill stations and refillable containers.
- [ ] A3 Use reusable bags, bottles and containers instead of single-use ones; reuse jars, tubs and shopping bags.
- [ ] A4 Separate waste at home into recyclables, food and garden waste, and general waste so each stream can be treated.
- [ ] A5 Choose products in recyclable, recycled or biodegradable packaging, and products with the EU Ecolabel; repair rather than replace.
- [ ] A6 Take paint and other household chemicals to a civic amenity site, not the bin.

Symbols on packaging (each one: what it tells the consumer)
- [ ] Y1 Recycling symbol (three arrows forming a loop): the packaging is made of material that can be recycled (2019O and 2009O schemes also say "reduce, reuse, recycle"). It does not guarantee that a local collection accepts it. A percentage inside the arrows shows recycled content. A number inside a triangle on plastic is a plastic-type code: 1 is PET (drinks bottles) and 2 is HDPE (milk bottles, detergent containers).
- [ ] Y2 Green Dot: the producer of the packaging has contributed financially to the recovery and recycling of packaging waste. In Ireland it is shown by members of the Repak scheme. It does not mean the pack is recyclable or recycled.
- [ ] Y3 EU Ecolabel (a flower symbol): an audited, voluntary label showing the product or service causes minimum damage to the environment across its life, considering the energy and materials used in making it, pollution produced and how far it can be reused or recycled (2022H, 2010H schemes).

Initiatives to reduce the effect of excess packaging (2007H)
- [ ] I1 The levy on single-use plastic shopping bags, charged at the point of sale since 2002, discouraged use and increased use of reusable bags.
- [ ] I2 The landfill levy, a charge per tonne on waste sent to landfill, encourages recycling and recovery.
- [ ] I3 Packaging producers must meet national recovery and recycling targets under Irish packaging regulations, and most meet them by funding the Repak scheme (the Green Dot).
- [ ] I4 Local authority recycling schemes: kerbside collections and bring banks or civic amenity centres for glass, cans, paper and plastics.
- [ ] I5 The Re-turn deposit return scheme, which began on 1 February 2024: a refundable deposit is paid on plastic drinks bottles and aluminium and steel cans, which are returned in shops or to reverse vending machines. Glass and dairy containers are not included.
- [ ] I6 Consumer choices (refuse over-packaged goods, choose loose produce, bring own containers) and manufacturer action (less material per product, lighter packaging).

Packaging materials and their waste (2007H, 2026O)
- [ ] P1 Flexible films (thin plastic films): low cost, a good barrier against moisture and gases, can be heat-sealed, fit closely to the food, light, can be printed on, suitable for frozen food (2007H scheme: two advantages). Drawback: many multi-layer films are hard to recycle.
- [ ] P2 Glass can be reused or recycled repeatedly without loss of quality but is heavy to transport. Steel and aluminium cans are highly recyclable; aluminium can be recycled repeatedly without loss of quality. Paper and card are recyclable but their fibres shorten each time. Paper and card advantages for 2026O: renewable raw material, recyclable, biodegradable, light, printable (give three).
- [ ] P3 Biodegradable packaging breaks down naturally through the action of micro-organisms (2017H, 2004H ask for an explanation with an example, such as paper or card, or compostable starch-based packaging).

Disposal of electrical appliances (WEEE) (2026O, 2025H, 2021O, 2014H, 2008O)
- [ ] W1 WEEE is waste electrical and electronic equipment. It must not go in the general household bin because it contains hazardous substances (for example lead, mercury, cadmium and flame retardants) and valuable recoverable materials. The crossed-out wheeled bin symbol means separate collection.
- [ ] W2 Disposal routes: first repair, sell or donate a working appliance; the retailer must take back an old appliance free of charge when a new equivalent one is bought (including on home delivery); a civic amenity site or recycling centre accepts it; larger electrical shops must accept very small electrical items without a purchase; batteries can be returned to shops without buying anything.
- [ ] W3 Old fridges and freezers contain refrigerant gases and insulating foam which must be removed by an authorised treatment facility; make the appliance safe before disposal (the 2008O scheme says to remove dangerous parts).
- [ ] W4 Take batteries out of appliances and dispose of them separately; erase personal data from smart appliances (2026O scheme).
- [ ] W5 The legal basis is the WEEE Directive as applied in Ireland by the European Union (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment) Regulations 2014; schemes accept "WEEE Directive" or "WEEE" without a year.

## 3. Traps

- The Green Dot is not a recycling symbol: it says the producer paid towards recovery, not that the pack can be recycled. The Ecolabel is not the Green Dot. Do not mix the recycling loop, the Green Dot and the Ecolabel in one answer.
- Recycling and composting differ: recycling reprocesses materials such as glass, metal and paper into new products; composting rots organic matter into soil conditioner.
- Reuse is not recycling: reuse keeps the item whole; recycling breaks it down.
- Energy recovery is not recycling.
- Recycling symbol: the Irish exam answer is "contains recyclable material"; do not say the item is certainly accepted in the household recycling bin.
- Older fridges contain CFC or HCFC refrigerant; modern ones use other refrigerants. Say "refrigerant and foam" without naming the gas.
- Boundaries: what packaging does for food and the stages of food processing belong to the food industry chapter; pollution effects of landfill gas and burning to the pollution subsection; energy labels and star ratings to the appliances chapter; food waste at home (planning meals, storing food) is covered in the household-practices subsection, so give only a one-line link here.
- Do not use: the landfill levy or plastic bag levy amount (the 2007H scheme gives a landfill levy figure), because these amounts have changed.
- Do not use: "remove the doors from old fridges and freezers" as a required rule, because no source confirms it; use "make the appliance safe, and let an authorised facility remove refrigerant and foam".
- Do not use: "return unwanted medicines to a collection point" as a stated household scheme, because it is unconfirmed here; say "do not put paint, oil, chemicals or batteries in the bin" only.
- Do not use: "pay the WEEE charge" (2024H scheme), because the current treatment of any visible charge on appliances is unconfirmed.

## 4. What exams ask

- How consumers contribute to sustainable waste management (2019H, 2014H; two points): reduce, reuse, recycle, compost, buy in bulk, buy recyclable products or concentrated detergents, recover energy.
- What the Green Dot tells the consumer (2022H, 2010H): the producer has paid towards recovery and recycling of packaging; in Ireland, Repak members. What the Ecolabel tells them: product causes minimum damage to the environment, judged over its life. What the recycling symbol tells them (2019O, 2009O): material is recyclable; reduce, reuse, recycle.
- One initiative to reduce the effect of packaging (2007H, 2014O): plastic bag levy, landfill levy, local authority recycling, Reduce Reuse Recycle, Green Dot, WEEE, Ecolabel, deposit return. Tip: name it, then give two details for the marks.
- Two advantages of flexible films (2007H): cost, barrier, heat sealing, shape, light weight, printing, frozen food.
- How to dispose of electrical appliances (2026O, 2021O, 2008O, 2014H, 2025H): retailer take-back, civic amenity or recycling centre, WEEE, repair or donate first, special care with fridges, batteries out. Tip: three separate routes earn three separate marks.
- Ways to reduce waste when shopping for food (2007O, 2016O): refuse excess packaging, loose produce, reusable bags, recyclable or biodegradable packaging, refill and bulk buying.
