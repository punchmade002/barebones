# Package: hom-dairy-eggs-s2 — Milk processing

- **loId:** `hom-dairy-eggs-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-dairy-eggs` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.2, 1.3.5

## 1. Must cover

Why milk is processed (one block)
- [ ] W1 Raw milk is a perfect medium for bacteria (water, lactose, protein, neutral pH), spoils within days and can carry pathogens (for example tuberculosis bacteria, Salmonella, Listeria, E. coli). Heat treatment destroys pathogens and most spoilage bacteria, making milk safe and extending its shelf life. (Assumed: bacteria grow in warm, moist conditions; refrigeration slows but does not kill them.)
- [ ] W2 Every heat treatment loses some heat-sensitive vitamins (B1 thiamine, B12, folate and vitamin C), so the method balances safety, shelf life and nutrient loss: the more severe the heat, the greater the loss and the more changed the flavour.
- [ ] W3 Milk is the syllabus's example of a food processed to extend shelf life, so these subsections must be answerable as "stages of production, packaging and labelling" (see W16-W19).

Pasteurisation (one block, can be asked as the one process "in full")
- [ ] P1 Temperature and time: 72 to 75 °C for 15 to 25 seconds (scheme figures; the 2024O scheme gives 72 °C for 15 to 25 seconds); then cooled rapidly to below 10 °C and packaged. Heating in a heat exchanger (plates) as a continuous flow. 
- [ ] P2 Effect on safety: destroys pathogenic bacteria and most spoilage bacteria (not all, and not spores), so the milk keeps for only a few days and must be refrigerated; it is not sterile.
- [ ] P3 Effect on nutritive value and flavour: small loss of thiamine (B1) and vitamin C; taste, colour and other nutrients little changed.
- [ ] P4 Shelf life: short (days) in the fridge compared with UHT; once opened, use within a few days.
- [ ] P5 Pasteurisation is also needed for milk used for cream, yoghurt and cheese.

Homogenisation (one block)
- [ ] H1 What it is: milk is forced at high pressure through a very small opening, which breaks the fat globules into much smaller ones spread evenly through the milk.
- [ ] H2 Effects: the cream does not rise to form a layer on top (no cream line), the milk looks whiter and tastes creamier and smoother, and the smaller fat globules are more easily digested.
- [ ] H3 Order: normally carried out before the heat treatment (pasteurisation or UHT); it is not a heat treatment and does not make milk safe. The 2021H scheme includes it as the first step of the pasteurisation answer.

Sterilisation (one block)
- [ ] S1 How: milk is homogenised, filled into bottles, sealed and then heated in the sealed bottle to about 110 to 120 °C for about 20 to 30 minutes (approximate; the marking schemes give no figure), then cooled.
- [ ] S2 Effect on safety: destroys all bacteria and their spores, so the milk is sterile and keeps for several months unopened without refrigeration; once opened it must be refrigerated.
- [ ] S3 Effect on nutrients and flavour: more vitamin loss than pasteurisation (thiamine, vitamin C and some B vitamins); creamy colour and a cooked, caramelised taste.

Ultra-heat treatment, UHT (one block)
- [ ] U1 How: milk is heated to about 132 °C for 1 to 3 seconds (steam injection or heat exchanger), cooled rapidly and packed aseptically (into sterile packaging in sterile conditions) in sterile airtight cartons. (2026H, 2017H schemes.)
- [ ] U2 Effect: destroys all bacteria (commercially sterile milk); unopened it keeps for several months without refrigeration; once opened, refrigerate and use within a few days.
- [ ] U3 Nutritive effect: some loss of heat-sensitive B vitamins and vitamin C; a slight cooked flavour. Loss is less than in in-bottle sterilisation because the heating time is so short.
- [ ] U4 Advantages: long unopened shelf life, no refrigeration needed in the shop or the cupboard, convenient for travel, holidays and emergencies. Disadvantage: flavour change.

Other processed milks (one block)
- [ ] O1 Evaporated milk: about half the water is removed by heating; canned and sterilised; unsweetened; keeps for months; used in cooking and drinks.
- [ ] O2 Condensed milk: water removed and sugar added; the high sugar concentration helps preserve it; thick and sweet; used in sweet dishes and confectionery.
- [ ] O3 Dried milk powder: all water removed by spray drying (milk sprayed as droplets into hot air) or roller drying (a film of milk dried on heated rollers); keeps for months; reconstituted with water; used in baking, infant formula and where fresh milk is not available.
- [ ] O4 These count as processes to extend shelf life; if one is chosen for an answer, give the method, effect and labelling as for pasteurisation.

Stages of production, packaging and labelling (one block; the "processed food profile")
- [ ] L1 Production stages, in order: collection from farms in refrigerated tankers; testing for quality and bacterial content on arrival; cooling and storage; separation (if cream is wanted) and standardising the fat content to whole, semi-skimmed or skimmed; fortification if needed; homogenisation; heat treatment (pasteurisation, sterilisation or UHT); cooling; filling and packaging in hygienic conditions; labelling, storage (chilled for pasteurised milk) and distribution.
- [ ] L2 Packaging: glass bottles, plastic bottles, waxed or plastic-coated cardboard cartons, aseptic cartons (UHT), metal cans (evaporated and condensed milk). The package protects the milk from contamination and light (light destroys riboflavin and vitamin A), holds a stated quantity, is hygienic and convenient, and is increasingly recyclable.
- [ ] L3 Label: name of the food and type of milk (whole, semi-skimmed, skimmed, fortified), brand and name and address of producer, quantity, nutrition information (energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, salt), date mark, storage instructions, and allergen information (milk). Pasteurised milk carries a use-by date; UHT milk carries a best-before date.
- [ ] L4 Legal basis for labels: Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 on food information to consumers, given effect in Ireland by the European Union (Provision of Food Information to Consumers) Regulations 2014.
- [ ] L5 Diagram cue: a flow chart of milk from farm to shop (collection, testing, separation and standardising, homogenisation, heat treatment, packaging, labelling, distribution).
- [ ] L6 Table cue: a table of pasteurisation, sterilisation and UHT with temperature, time and effect columns (the 2026H, 2017H, 2008H form of question).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: percentages of water removed in evaporated milk or of sugar in condensed milk, because they are not in the schemes and vary by product.
- Do not use: "UHT milk is only pasteurised more strongly" or "UHT destroys only some bacteria"; UHT is commercially sterile. Pasteurisation is the one that leaves some bacteria and all spores.
- Do not confuse sterilisation (in the sealed bottle, long, high temperature, cooked flavour) with UHT (flash heat in a continuous flow, then aseptic packing, slight flavour change).
- Homogenisation is not a way of making milk safe; it changes texture and appearance. It is not heat treatment, and it does not separate the fat.
- "Aseptic" means packed into sterile packaging in a sterile environment; do not use it for pasteurised milk.
- Do not name a brand of milk or a dairy company.
- Milk composition and milk types belong to the milk-nutrition subsection; cream separation by centrifuge for cream production belongs to the cream, butter and yoghurt subsection (here only as a stage of standardising).
- General bacteria, enzymes and spoilage, and other preservation methods (freezing, drying, canning) belong to the preservation chapter.
- Do not use the Food Hygiene Regulations 1950-1989 or the Labelling Regulations of 1982 and 1991, even where an old scheme mentions them; the current instruments are as in L4.

## 4. What exams ask

- A table or short answer for UHT, pasteurisation or sterilisation: the temperature (for UHT 132 °C), the time (1 to 3 seconds), and an effect (all bacteria destroyed and long unopened shelf life, or slight flavour change and small vitamin loss). Higher Level repeatedly asks UHT.
- "Describe one process to extend the shelf life of milk" (18 or 12 marks: name, how carried out, packaging and labelling or effect on nutritive value). Expected: pasteurisation with homogenisation first, 72 to 75 °C for 15 to 25 s, cool to below 10 °C, packaging in a named container, and label contents (type, brand, quantity, nutrition, date, storage); losses of B1 and C. Other accepted processes: sterilisation, UHT, evaporation, condensing, drying, with the correct method.
- Function questions: what homogenisation does (even fat, creamier, easier to digest) and what sterilisation does (kills all bacteria, keeps for months).
- "Name two heat treatments for milk" (2013O): pasteurisation, sterilisation, UHT, plus evaporated, condensed and dried as concentrated types.
- Labelling: what must appear on milk (type, brand, quantity, nutrition, date, storage), and that the law is the EU food information regulation.
- Food-waste questions: check the date, store at 4 °C, return milk to the fridge promptly, keep covered.
