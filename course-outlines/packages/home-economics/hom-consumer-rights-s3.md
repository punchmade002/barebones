# Package: hom-consumer-rights-s3 — Consumer responsibility: information and rights

- **loId:** `hom-consumer-rights-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-consumer-rights` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2.2
- **Syllabus scope:** the responsibility of the consumer in informed decision-making with regard to consumer information and rights. Also examined: consumer responsibilities (2023H, 2021H, 2006H); the marking schemes also ask consumer rights (2024O, 2015O, 2013O, 2006O) and the parties to a consumer contract (2007O). No [HL] items in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Ordinary and Higher Level. This subsection is about what the consumer must do and what the consumer is entitled to in general terms. The legal detail (Acts, remedies) is in the consumer protection legislation subsection, the bodies in the voluntary and statutory bodies subsection, and the steps of a complaint in the complaints subsection. The environmental responsibilities of consumers are in the environmental issues chapter; one bullet here only.

## 1. Must cover

Who is who in a consumer transaction (one block)
- [ ] P1 (assumed) The consumer is an individual who buys goods or services for personal use, not for business.
- [ ] P2 (assumed) The retailer or trader sells in the course of business and is the consumer's first point of contact for a fault; the retailer is responsible for faulty goods even if the manufacturer made the fault.
- [ ] P3 The manufacturer makes the product and gives any manufacturer's guarantee; a guarantee is a contract between the manufacturer and the consumer, offered in addition to (not instead of) statutory rights. The supplier or distributor moves goods from manufacturer to retailer.

Sources of consumer information (one block)
- [ ] I1 Labels, instructions and care labels: contents, use, care and hazard warnings.
- [ ] I2 Unit pricing (price per kilogram or litre) lets pack sizes be compared.
- [ ] I3 Advertising and sales material: useful but to be checked against the label and independent information.
- [ ] I4 Independent testing and magazines: the Consumers' Association of Ireland publishes comparative findings in Consumer Choice.
- [ ] I5 Citizens Information (free, confidential information on consumer rights) and the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (guidance on rights and unfair trading).
- [ ] I6 Terms and conditions, returns policy and guarantee wording, read before buying.
- [ ] I7 Marks and symbols as information: quality marks (Bord Bia Quality Mark), the CE mark (the manufacturer declares the product meets EU safety requirements), the energy rating label on appliances, safety and hazard symbols.
- [ ] I8 Reviews, word of mouth and online comparison: useful but may be biased; check more than one source.

Responsibilities of the informed consumer (one block; the list examiners ask for)
- [ ] R1 Know your rights and the laws that protect consumers.
- [ ] R2 Be informed about goods and services before buying; compare value (price against quality, durability and running cost), not price alone.
- [ ] R3 Read and understand labels, terms, guarantee conditions and cancellation terms.
- [ ] R4 Follow the manufacturer's instructions and care labels; a fault caused by misuse is unlikely to be the trader's responsibility.
- [ ] R5 Keep receipts and proof of purchase, which are needed for a claim.
- [ ] R6 Report faults promptly, stop using a faulty or unsafe product, and complain where appropriate.
- [ ] R7 Give accurate, truthful information when complaining, including a clear description of the problem and the redress wanted.
- [ ] R8 Plan purchases, set a budget, and resist pressure selling and impulse buying.
- [ ] R9 Use resources responsibly and consider the environment (one bullet: reduce waste, reuse and recycle packaging).
- [ ] R10 Check the total cost, including interest and charges, before agreeing to credit or easy-pay.

Caveat emptor (one block)
- [ ] C1 Caveat emptor means "let the buyer beware": the duty was on the buyer to inspect goods before buying.
- [ ] C2 Modern consumer law limits caveat emptor by giving rights that apply automatically, but inspecting goods and asking questions before purchase is still the best protection.
- [ ] C3 Questioning vague claims ("the best", "new") that lack evidence.

Consumer rights in general terms (one block)
- [ ] G1 Right to safety: goods and services must not endanger health or life.
- [ ] G2 Right to honest and truthful information about goods and services.
- [ ] G3 Right to choice: a range of goods and services is available.
- [ ] G4 Right to value for money: quality that matches the price paid; goods of satisfactory quality.
- [ ] G5 Right to redress: repair, replacement or refund when goods or services are faulty.
- [ ] G6 Right to services completed to a high standard, by a qualified person, using good materials (the 2024 scheme words it this way).
- [ ] G7 No right to a refund simply for a change of mind in a shop purchase (past scenario questions turn on this); a retailer may offer an exchange or credit note as goodwill.
- [ ] G8 Linking rights and responsibilities: each right is only as useful as the consumer's knowledge and action (a right to information works only if labels are read).

## 3. Traps

- Consumer rights are given in different lists in different papers. Teach the schemes' list (G1-G6) as "consumer rights"; do not present one fixed number of rights.
- A guarantee adds to statutory rights; it never replaces them. The retailer, not the manufacturer, is the first point of contact for statutory rights.
- Responsibilities are the consumer's duties (be informed, read labels, keep receipts, complain, follow instructions, behave responsibly). Rights are what the consumer is entitled to. Examiners separate them; do not blur them.
- The legal remedies, the Acts and the sale-goods rules belong to the consumer protection legislation subsection; the body descriptions to the bodies subsection; the step-by-step complaint to the complaints subsection.
- Do not use: the "five basic consumer rights" as an official or fixed list, because the schemes name between five and seven and the Kennedy-style lists differ; present G1-G6 only.
- Do not use: Office of the Director of Consumer Affairs, Office of Consumer Affairs, National Consumer Agency or Comhairle as current bodies (2006, 2007 and 2014 schemes), because they have been replaced; the current bodies are the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission and the Citizens Information Board.
- Do not use: "delay always weakens a claim" as a legal rule, because the law fixes a short-term right to reject for goods rather than a general deadline; say "report faults promptly, as delay can make a problem harder to prove".

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline two or three consumer responsibilities (6 marks): the scheme accepts be informed and know rights and laws, read labels, follow instructions, keep receipts, complain where appropriate, judge quality against price, use resources responsibly, give accurate information when complaining.
- Describe how consumer responsibilities help wise choices (10 marks): link each responsibility to its result, for example reading labels avoids unsuitable or unsafe purchases, keeping receipts makes a refund possible.
- Name and explain two to four consumer rights (6 to 16 marks): name the right and explain it in one sentence.
- Scenario questions: whether a shopper is entitled to a refund (faulty camera, yes with proof; disliked colour, no statutory right) and which responsibilities the shopper should have shown.
- Name a reliable source of consumer advice: Citizens Information or the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission.
- Complete a sentence on guarantees: a contract between the manufacturer and the consumer.

## Linking responsibilities to results (for "describe how responsibilities assist wise choices")

- Being informed and comparing sources: the buyer finds out whether a product suits the need and the budget before paying.
- Reading labels and terms: avoids unsuitable, unsafe or mis-sold items, for example an allergen or a short guarantee.
- Comparing value, not price alone: a cheaper item that wears out quickly costs more over time.
- Keeping receipts: makes a repair, replacement or refund possible.
- Following instructions: prevents damage that would leave the buyer without a remedy.
- Complaining and giving accurate details: gets the problem put right and helps other consumers.
- Planning and budgeting: reduces impulse buying and debt.
