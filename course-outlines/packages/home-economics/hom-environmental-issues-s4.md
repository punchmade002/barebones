## 1. Must cover

Environmental impact of household activity
- [ ] I1 Everyday household activities use energy, water and materials and produce emissions and waste. The impact depends on how much energy an activity uses and which fuel supplies it.
- [ ] I2 Space heating and hot water make up the largest share of household energy use.
- [ ] I3 Much household electricity comes from burning fossil fuels at power stations, which releases carbon dioxide; using less electricity cuts emissions. Water must be pumped, treated and often heated, so saving water saves energy.
- [ ] I4 Packaging, food waste and discarded appliances add to waste landfilled or recovered.

Heating the home (group-study topic: effects and recommendations)
- [ ] H1 Heat escapes through the roof, walls, floor, windows and gaps. Roof or attic insulation is one of the most effective measures in an existing house.
- [ ] H2 Cavity wall insulation fills the gap between the two leaves of a masonry wall.
- [ ] H3 Draught-proofing seals gaps around doors, windows, letterboxes and floorboards (keep controlled ventilation where fuel is burned).
- [ ] H4 Double or triple glazing reduces heat loss through windows; thick curtains at night add insulation.
- [ ] H5 A lagging jacket on the hot water cylinder and lagged pipes cut heat loss during storage and transport.
- [ ] H6 Controls: a room thermostat and programmer run the heating only when needed; lowering the setting slightly reduces fuel use; thermostatic radiator valves control individual rooms; timers on the immersion heater.
- [ ] H7 Reflective foil behind radiators on outside walls sends heat back into the room.
- [ ] H8 An annual boiler service keeps combustion efficient and safe.
- [ ] H9 Renewable heat, such as heat pumps, solar thermal panels and stoves burning wood from managed forests, reduces reliance on fossil fuels.
- [ ] H10 Emissions from burning fuels at home (carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, smoke) and what they do to the environment (2018H, 2023H); reduction: smokeless fuel, efficient boiler, controls, renewables.

Washing clothes (group-study topic)
- [ ] L1 Heating water uses most of the energy in a wash, so lower temperatures save energy; modern detergents work well at 30 to 40 °C.
- [ ] L2 Run the machine with a full load, since a part load uses nearly as much energy and water.
- [ ] L3 Use the economy or eco programme, which saves energy or water per load.
- [ ] L4 A high spin speed removes more water so drying is quicker; line drying instead of a tumble dryer saves a large amount of energy; a dryer is among the most energy-hungry appliances.
- [ ] L5 Use the correct detergent dose; too much leaves residue and sends more product to drains.
- [ ] L6 Clothes: wash only when needed, repair and reuse clothes (the clothing chapter takes fast fashion).

Food consumption and shopping (group-study topic; 2016O, 2024O, 2022O)
- [ ] F1 Food waste in landfill decomposes without oxygen and releases methane, a greenhouse gas; wasted food also wastes the land, water, fertiliser and energy used to produce, transport and package it.
- [ ] F2 Plan meals, shop with a list, check dates and stock, use leftovers, freeze surplus promptly, and buy only what will be eaten (2024O scheme).
- [ ] F3 Store food correctly: fridge at 5 °C or below, freezer at −18 °C or below, which slows spoilage and cuts waste.
- [ ] F4 Plant foods (vegetables, pulses, cereals) generally need less land, water and energy than meat and dairy, so more plant-based meals lower the impact of the diet.
- [ ] F5 Buy seasonal and local food, which saves energy for transport and heated or refrigerated growing and storage.
- [ ] F6 Shopping choices (2016O scheme): choose loose produce and products without packaging, buy in bulk, use reusable bags, buy refills and products in recycled or reusable packaging, choose products with the Ecolabel, and cut the number of shopping trips.
- [ ] F7 Compost food and garden waste or use the brown bin.

Choosing appliances: the energy label (2025H, 2014H)
- [ ] E1 The EU energy label rates appliances from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient).
- [ ] E2 The label also gives annual or per-cycle energy use and the noise level; for washing machines and dishwashers it also shows water use.
- [ ] E3 Choose a high rating and the right size for the household: an oversized appliance wastes energy.
- [ ] E4 Other criteria: repairable, durable, little packaging, eco and low-temperature programmes.

Using appliances efficiently (2025H, 2024H, 2014H)
- [ ] U1 Switch appliances off at the socket instead of standby, which draws electricity continuously.
- [ ] U2 Use LED bulbs, which use far less power than incandescent bulbs and last much longer.
- [ ] U3 Use the oven only when needed and do not preheat longer than necessary; use a microwave, slow cooker or air fryer for small amounts; boil only the water needed in a kettle; use lids on pans.
- [ ] U4 Keep fridge filters and condenser coils clean and the door seals sound; do not set the fridge colder than needed; keep the door closed and do not overpack a fridge (2025H scheme).
- [ ] U5 Use full loads and eco or cold cycles in washing machines and dishwashers.
- [ ] U6 Keep appliances working by servicing and repairing them, or sell or donate them, rather than replacing them; dispose of old ones through the retailer take-back or a recycling centre (detail in the waste subsection).

Water use in the home (2025H)
- [ ] V1 Showers use less hot water than baths; short showers save energy and water.
- [ ] V2 Fix dripping taps and leaks; turn taps off when brushing teeth; use aerator or sensor taps and efficient showerheads.
- [ ] V3 Dual-flush toilets save water on every flush.
- [ ] V4 Harvest rainwater in a butt for the garden; reuse grey water; insulate the cylinder and pipes and time the immersion to save water-heating energy; solar thermal heating can supply hot water.

## 3. Traps

- A full washing load is efficient; a half load is not "half the energy".
- A hotter wash is not usually needed for cleaning: higher temperature mainly costs energy.
- Standby is not "off": the appliance still uses electricity until switched off at the socket.
- Energy labels: the rescaled A to G scale replaced the old A+++ to D scale in 2021. Older papers and schemes mention A+++; "A-rated" in the schemes (2024H) means the most efficient on the label. Do not compare old and new ratings.
- Boundaries: fridge star ratings, appliance working principles and the details of the energy label belong to the appliances chapter; types of heating system, fuels, electricity, wiring and BER belong to the home services and housing chapters; recycling, WEEE and packaging detail to the waste subsection; pollution effects of emissions to the pollution subsection; renewable energy sources to the resources subsection. Give each a one-line link here only.
- Fridges and freezers: the 2025H scheme says to keep doors closed and not overpack the fridge. Keep to that.
- Do not use: CFL (energy-saving) bulbs as the recommended bulb (2009O, 2013O, 2004O schemes name them), because many CFLs are being phased out under EU hazardous-substance rules and LED is the standard choice; if mentioned, say "LED (older schemes say CFL)".
- Do not use: the old A+++ energy rating scale as a current rating, because it was replaced by A to G in 2021.
- Do not use: any figure for energy used by appliances, heat lost through each part of a house, savings per degree, or fuel and electricity prices, because they change.

## 4. What exams ask

- How consumers can be environmentally responsible when choosing, using and disposing of appliances (2025H, 2014H, 2024H): choose a high energy-label rating and the right size, repairable, low packaging; use full, eco, cold cycles, switch off standby, keep fridge doors shut; dispose through retailer take-back, a recycling centre or WEEE; take care with fridges. Tip: organise by choose, use, dispose, with at least one point under each.
- Strategies to save energy in the home (2025O, 2024H, 2023O, 2016O, 2012H): insulation, controls, efficient appliances, LED lighting, standby, repairing taps, solar. Tip: name the inefficient use, then the strategy and why it works.
- Household water management (2025H): shorter showers, repair leaks, dual-flush toilets, rainwater harvesting, solar water heating, lagging, timed immersion.
- Environmentally aware food shopping and reducing food waste (2016O, 2024O, 2022O, 2007O): loose produce, reusable bags, bulk, Ecolabel, list and meal plan, check dates, freeze leftovers.
- Effects of burning fossil fuels in the home (2023H, 2018H): name an emission, its effect and a strategy to cut it.
- Group-study recommendations: expect an applied question asking for strategies for heating, washing clothes or food use.
