# Package: hom-social-change-s2 — Working hours, leisure time and conditions of work

- **loId:** `hom-social-change-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-social-change` · **syllabusRef:** 6.1
- **Syllabus scope:** item 2, working hours, leisure time and conditions of work [6.1]: reduction in working hours and increase in leisure time; improved pay and conditions of work. Chapter-wide also examined: 2026O, 2023O, 2015O (reduction in working hours; improved conditions of work), 2015H, 2023H, 2025H (flexibility in working hours). Settings: comparing family life a hundred years ago with today; interviewing an older relative. No [HL] items in this subsection (the outline marks "changes in patterns of work" [HL] in the Work and Leisure chapter, not here).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Common to both levels. The question is always "what did this change do to family life": shorter hours and more leisure, better pay and conditions, and the effect of each on time together, income, health and relationships. Employment law is named only as the cause of better conditions; the Acts are given at the depth below.

## 1. Must cover

Reduction in working hours (one block)
- [ ] W1 The standard working week has shortened over the past century, overtime has become less common for many workers, and the law now sets limits. Say "shortened", never a number of hours.
- [ ] W2 Law: the Organisation of Working Time Act 1997 sets an average maximum working week of 48 hours, a minimum daily rest period, rest breaks during the working day, a weekly rest period, paid annual leave and public holiday entitlements. An employee cannot lawfully be required to work beyond these limits.
- [ ] W3 Flexible arrangements: part-time work, job-sharing, flexitime, term-time working, shift work, compressed hours, remote and hybrid working. Part-time and flexible hours let a parent work around school hours; shift work and long opening hours in retail and services mean some family members work evenings and weekends, so families must plan time together. A right to request flexible and remote working exists under the Work Life Balance and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 2023.
- [ ] W4 Effects on family life: more time for shared meals, homework, household tasks, outings and care of children and older relatives; better work-life balance and less stress; stronger relationships. Negative: shift work, long commuting and irregular hours cut time together; both parents working full-time leaves less time for meals and supervision; technology means work follows people home (emails out of hours).

Increase in leisure time (one block)
- [ ] W5 Why leisure time increased: shorter hours; paid annual leave (family holidays became regular); weekends as free time; labour-saving appliances (washing machine, dishwasher, microwave) cutting housework; convenience foods; rising incomes to spend on leisure.
- [ ] W6 How families use it: home entertainment (television, radio, computers, internet, streaming, gaming) often indoors; clubs and community sport (GAA, soccer, swimming, dance); cinema, eating out, holidays, travel; more spending on leisure as incomes rose.
- [ ] W7 Effects on family life: shared activities strengthen relationships and communication; children's physical, social and emotional development; relaxation reduces stress. Negative: screen-based leisure can separate family members; leisure costs money, so some families cannot afford it (assumed: the definition, function and value of leisure and the factors influencing choice belong to the Work and Leisure chapter).

Improved pay and conditions of work (one block)
- [ ] W8 Pay: real pay has risen with productivity and living standards, giving more disposable income to save, buy a home, and pay for education, holidays and leisure. The National Minimum Wage Act 2000 sets a legal minimum hourly rate, protecting low-paid workers and their families. Wages must be paid in line with the Payment of Wages Act 1991 (assumed: no amounts).
- [ ] W9 Leave and family-friendly rights: statutory paid annual leave; maternity leave and maternity benefit (Maternity Protection Acts 1994 and 2004); paternity leave (Paternity Leave and Benefit Act 2016); parental leave (unpaid, Parental Leave Act 1998) and Parent's Leave and Benefit (Parent's Leave and Benefit Act 2019); statutory sick leave (Sick Leave Act 2022: 10 days a year from 2026, after stages of 3 days in 2023, 5 in 2024 and 7 in 2025). Give no week counts, and no day counts except the sick leave days above.
- [ ] W10 Security and safety: written terms of employment; protection against unfair dismissal (Unfair Dismissals Acts); a safe workplace under the Safety, Health and Welfare at Work Act 2005, with the Health and Safety Authority as the enforcing body; grievance and disciplinary procedures; redundancy payments; complaints to the Workplace Relations Commission.
- [ ] W11 Representation: trade unions negotiate pay and conditions through collective agreements on behalf of members.
- [ ] W12 Occupational pension schemes provide retirement income, reducing dependence on adult children (assumed: occupational pension versus State pension is explained in the Personal Finance and Budgeting chapter). Automatic enrolment: the My Future Fund scheme started on 1 January 2026 and enrols employees aged 23 to 60 earning over EUR 20,000; the employee, the employer and the State all contribute (no rates).
- [ ] W13 Other benefits: canteens, health insurance, bonuses, training and career breaks (generic; say "such as").
- [ ] W14 Effects on family life: higher incomes allow bigger or better housing, savings, household goods, education and holidays; financial security reduces stress and arguments; time off for children and illness lets parents care without losing all income; healthier, safer work lowers the risk of injury that would cut family income. Negative: better pay and the cost of housing lead many families to need two incomes (link to women's work).

Settings (one short block)

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any figure for the length of the working week, the minimum wage, sick pay, maternity, parental or paternity benefit amounts or the number of weeks of any leave except the two-week paternity leave, because these are changing figures or unconfirmed.
- Do not use: maternity leave of 26 weeks and Parent's Leave of 9 weeks per parent, because they could not be confirmed by search and must be checked before use.
- Do not use: the Protection of Young Persons (Employment) Act 1996 details here. It is in the Work and Leisure chapter. One clause naming it as a law protecting young workers is fine.
- Do not use: a flat "maximum working week" with no qualifier, because the Act sets an average limit with exceptions for some occupations; say "average maximum".
- Shift work and part-time work are not themselves "better conditions"; they change the pattern of hours. Be clear which effect is positive and which negative.
- Leisure here means the increase in free time and what it did to family life. Defining leisure, its function and value, the factors that influence the choice of leisure activities and evaluating leisure facilities belong to the Work and Leisure chapter. Do not write a leisure essay.
- Technology, patterns of work (decline of primary and secondary industry, growth of services) and work-life balance as dual-earner role conflict belong to the Work and Leisure chapter; mention only as a cause or effect.
- Higher household incomes can mean longer hours, not shorter. Do not say hours fell for everyone: many families now have both parents working, so total family working hours may be higher than a century ago.
- Improved conditions are largely the result of legislation and trade unions, not employers' generosity. Name the Act or union for each improvement.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss the impact on family life of a reduction in working hours, and of improved conditions of work (Ordinary Level 20 marks in 2015, three-part social change questions in 2023 and 2026). Expected: time together, shared meals and childcare, leisure, lower stress; higher income, security, health and safety; each effect linked to the family.
- Discuss how flexibility in working hours affects work-life balance, with examples (Higher Level): part-time, job-sharing, flexitime, term-time, remote working; benefits for parents and carers; problems such as lower pay, fewer promotions, blurred boundary between work and home.
- Describe the improvements in conditions of work over time: pay, paid leave, sick pay, maternity and parental leave, health and safety, pensions, trade union representation.
- Social and economic change questions list reduction in working hours as one of three or four changes; each needs a clear family effect, not just a description.
- Short questions: two effects of more leisure time on a family; two benefits of shorter working hours; name two rights an employee has under law.
