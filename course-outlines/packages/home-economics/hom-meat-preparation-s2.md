# Package: hom-meat-preparation-s2 — Selection and storage

- **loId:** `hom-meat-preparation-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-meat-preparation` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.2
- **Syllabus scope:** buying meat and meat products; effects of storage and safe storage. Some items are [HL] (labelling regulation, FSAI and food-law regulations, official controls, FSAI service-contract agencies); the rest are for all students.
- **Also examined:** Bord Bia Quality Assurance Scheme and other measures to keep meat safe (2025H, 2023H); factors affecting consumer decisions when buying meat (2010H, 2005O); guidelines for storing meat (2017O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Legislation is given as in Appendix A of the outline (June 2027 instruments only). Checked against FSAI and safefood pages and Bord Bia's published scheme documents.

## 1. Must cover

Buying meat and meat products (one block)
- [ ] B1 Judging fresh meat: fresh red meat is a bright, even colour, firm and moist (not wet or slimy), with a fresh smell and little drip in the pack; grey, brown or green patches, stickiness or a sour smell mean spoilage. Fresh poultry skin is creamy, moist and unbroken with no strong smell. Fat should look white to creamy and firm, not yellow and soft.
- [ ] B2 Lean cuts look with little visible fat; marbling in beef gives flavour and juiciness. Cut shape and size suit the cooking method (see "Preparation and cooking").
- [ ] B3 Where to buy: butcher (cut to order, advice), supermarket (prepacked, wide choice, labelled), farm shop, market; check the premises look clean, meat is kept chilled, raw and cooked meats are separate.
- [ ] B4 Prepacked meat label (the regulation is [HL]: Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 as given effect by the European Union (Provision of Food Information to Consumers) Regulations 2014): name of food, ingredients in descending order of weight with allergens emphasised, net quantity, date mark, storage instructions, name and address of the business, origin where required, nutrition declaration.
- [ ] B5 Date marks: use-by on highly perishable foods like raw meat is a safety date and must not be passed; best-before (on e.g. frozen or canned meat) is a quality date.
- [ ] B6 Origin and traceability: beef labels show an animal reference code and origin (country of birth, rearing and slaughter, or one country) and the slaughterhouse; origin labelling is also required for fresh, chilled and frozen pigmeat, sheep, goat and poultry. Labels allow meat to be traced back to the farm.
- [ ] B7 Frozen meat: buy solid, undamaged packs without ice crystals, frost or thaw juice; do not buy above the load line of the freezer cabinet.
- [ ] B8 Shopping practice: buy meat last, bring it home in an insulated bag, and refrigerate or freeze promptly.
- [ ] B9 Consumer protection: faulty or unfit meat can be returned; the Consumer Rights Act 2022 gives rights for goods (satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described). The Consumer Protection Act 2007 (enforced by the CCPC) prohibits misleading claims; the FSAI Act 1998 and food law prohibit unsafe food.

Factors affecting consumer decisions (one block, exam list)
- [ ] F1 Price and income: cheaper cuts, mince, offal, chicken pieces; special offers; cost per portion; bulk-buying and freezing.
- [ ] F2 Convenience, time and skills: ready-prepared, marinated, processed; household cooking skills and equipment (cooker, microwave, freezer space).
- [ ] F3 Health: low fat, low salt, lean cuts, skinless poultry; iron for those at risk of anaemia; limiting processed meat on advice.
- [ ] F4 Family size, age and likes: portion sizes, children's preference, older people's tender meat; religious and cultural rules; vegetarians in the household.
- [ ] F5 Quality, appearance and freshness; traceability and labelling; origin (Irish meat, Bord Bia Quality Mark).
- [ ] F6 Ethics and environment: animal welfare, organic, free-range, sustainability, food miles, packaging.
- [ ] F7 Advertising, recipes, seasonality and in-store promotions.

Bord Bia Quality Assurance Scheme (one block)
- [ ] Q1 Bord Bia is the Irish Food Board, the State agency that promotes Irish food and drink and runs quality assurance schemes; its Quality Mark shows the product comes from an assured source.
- [ ] Q2 The farm-level scheme (beef and lamb; separate schemes exist for pigmeat and poultry) is audited independently and sets standards for animal welfare and care, animal feed, herd management, veterinary medicine records, livestock identification and traceability, pest control, hygiene and the environment. Meat processors are separately assured (a meat processor scheme).
- [ ] Q3 Assured and non-assured product is kept apart in processing so the claim can be traced; non-compliances must be corrected.
- [ ] Q4 Benefits: consumers get confidence in quality, safety and welfare; producers get market access and sustainability credentials; Bord Bia advises industry and government and promotes Irish meat abroad.

Other measures that keep meat safe (one block)
- [ ] M1 Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine: animal health, disease testing, veterinary controls on farms, residue (drug and banned-substance) testing, EU import rules, abattoir approval and supervision.
- [ ] M2 Veterinary inspection of animals before slaughter (ante-mortem) and of carcasses and organs after slaughter (post-mortem) in approved abattoirs; unfit meat is condemned. [HL] Official veterinary staff, the HSE and local authorities act under service contracts with the FSAI.
- [ ] M3 FSAI [HL for the FSAI Act, Regulation 178/2002, Regulation 2017/625 and the 2020 Regulations; Regulation 852/2004 and the 2006 Regulations are not HL]: the independent State body set up under the Food Safety Authority of Ireland Act 1998 to see that food is safe; it coordinates enforcement, advises and publishes guidance. Food law: Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 (unsafe food banned, traceability one step back and one step forward); Regulation (EC) No 852/2004, given effect by the European Communities (Hygiene of Foodstuffs) Regulations 2006 (hygiene and HACCP-based procedures for food businesses); Regulation (EU) 2017/625 as given effect by the European Union (Official Controls in Relation to Food Legislation) Regulations 2020 (official inspections).
- [ ] M4 Records: farmers record medicines and feed; animals are identified (ear tags and cattle passports); meat is traceable from farm to pack.
- [ ] M5 Bans: hormonal growth promoters are banned in EU farm animals; medicines have withdrawal periods before slaughter.

Safe storage (one block, guidelines)
- [ ] T1 Refrigerator at 5 °C or below (FSAI gives 0-5 °C); the danger zone for bacterial growth is 5-63 °C.
- [ ] T2 Raw meat: wrapped or in a covered container on the bottom shelf, away from cooked and ready-to-eat food, so juices cannot drip; use before the use-by date.
- [ ] T3 Separate chopping boards and knives for raw meat, wash hands and surfaces (cross-contamination).
- [ ] T4 Freezing: freeze fresh meat before its use-by date, wrapped in moisture-proof packaging, in portions; freezer at -18 °C or below; label and date; rotate stock.
- [ ] T5 Thawing: in the fridge, on a tray or in a container on the bottom shelf; once thawed, cook within the time on the pack; cook and do not refreeze raw thawed meat (cooked dishes made from it can be frozen).
- [ ] T6 Cooked meat: cool quickly (within about 1-2 hours, in shallow containers), refrigerate, reheat until steaming hot (75 °C core), reheat once only.
- [ ] T7 Minced meat, sausages and offal spoil faster than joints because they have a larger surface area for bacteria; poultry often carries Salmonella and Campylobacter on the surface.
- [ ] T8 Vacuum-packed meat keeps longer unopened (refrigerated); treat as fresh once opened.

Effects of storage on meat (one block)
- [ ] E1 Refrigeration slows but does not stop bacterial growth and enzyme action, so spoilage still occurs; use-by dates apply.
- [ ] E2 Freezing inhibits enzymes and stops microbial growth, but does not kill all micro-organisms (they regrow on thawing).
- [ ] E3 Slow freezing forms large ice crystals that damage cells, giving drip loss on thawing (loss of extractives and soluble nutrients, mainly B vitamins) and a softer texture; fast (blast) freezing gives small crystals and less damage.
- [ ] E4 Freezer burn: dry grey-white patches where air reaches the surface; fat can go rancid.
- [ ] E5 Spoilage signs: slime, sour smell, colour change. Myoglobin oxidises to brown metmyoglobin on exposure to air (harmless early, but a quality sign).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "use fresh raw meat within 2 to 3 days of purchase", because no authority figure was confirmed. Say "before the use-by date on the pack, stored at 5 °C or below", and "if it has no date, cook or freeze on the day".
- Do not use: "freezing stops enzyme activity", because enzymes are slowed, not fully stopped. Say "inhibits enzyme activity and microbial growth".
- Do not use: "growth promoters" as something farmers may use. Hormonal growth promoters are banned in the EU; the marking scheme's wording refers to record-keeping of medicines and antibiotics.
- Do not use: audit intervals, minimum rearing days or other Bord Bia scheme figures, because they are changing standards.
- The Food Safety Authority is not the inspector: it coordinates; HSE, Department of Agriculture, local authorities and others inspect under service contracts.
- Use-by is safety, best-before is quality; confusing them loses easy marks.
- Refrigeration does not kill bacteria; freezing does not kill all bacteria.
- Do not name the Sale of Goods and Supply of Services Act 1980, the Consumer Information Act 1978 or the Food Hygiene Regulations; they are superseded (Appendix A).
- Belongs to neighbouring subsections: tenderising, cooking methods and cooking effects to the preparation and cooking subsection; curing, canning, vacuum and modified-atmosphere packing as shelf-life processes to the processing subsection (here only one line). Detailed bacteria (Salmonella, Campylobacter, Listeria) are in the food poisoning chapter; name them only as hazards.

## 4. What exams ask

- Bord Bia QA scheme (2025H, 12 marks; three well-developed points): what it is (quality assurance and sustainability mark), what farms must do (welfare, regulated feed, herd management, medicine and traceability records, pest control), independent inspections of farms and processing, advice to government and industry.
- Measures that keep meat safe in the Irish food industry (2023H, 12 marks): Department of Agriculture controls; farmer records of medicines; feed and abattoir controls; disease testing; identification and traceability; Bord Bia assurance. Name current bodies only.
- Factors affecting consumer decisions when buying meat (2010H, 20 marks; 2005O): at least four or five factors, each explained with meat examples (finance and cheaper cuts, time and skill, health, organic and welfare, traceability, labels, family tastes).
- Guidelines for storing meat (2017O): fridge temperature and bottom shelf, covered, separate from cooked foods, use-by, freezing and thawing rules, cool cooked meat quickly.
- Buying meat: signs of freshness, labels and date marks; why use-by matters.
- Tip candidates: always pair a storage rule with its reason (for example bottom shelf, so raw juices cannot drip on food); name the current Act and body, not the old ones; mention traceability as the link between label, farm and recall.
