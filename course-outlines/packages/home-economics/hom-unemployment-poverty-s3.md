# Package: hom-unemployment-poverty-s3 — Effects of unemployment

- **loId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-unemployment-poverty` · **syllabusRef:** 6.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "Effects of unemployment" [6.5]: on the individual, the family unit and society. Common to Ordinary and Higher Level; no [HL] marks.
- **Also examined:** the effects question is the most regularly asked item in this chapter: 2026H (20 marks, individuals and society), 2023O (24 marks: individual, family, society), 2022H (12 marks, society), 2018O (24 marks, the young adult, the family, society), 2017H (20 marks, individuals, families and society; five effects at 4 marks with one reference to each level), 2010O (20 marks), 2006O (24 marks, six effects at 4 marks with two for each level). Ordinary and Higher both require all three levels.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Built from the 2006O-2026H marking schemes. The current notes are accurate and match the schemes; the only changes are to wording that states a possible effect as certain.

## 1. Must cover

Effects on the individual (one block)
- [ ] I1 Financial insecurity: the fall in income forces cuts in spending and a lower standard of living; the person may rely on savings, credit or welfare payments, which are lower than a wage.
- [ ] I2 Debt and arrears: unpaid bills and loans; mortgage or rent arrears; in serious cases repossession of the home or homelessness (the schemes list "mortgage arrears, repossession, homelessness").
- [ ] I3 Loss of status and self-esteem: work gives identity, purpose and social standing; its loss can leave the person feeling worthless, a failure or guilty.
- [ ] I4 Stress, anxiety and depression; feelings of hopelessness, especially in long-term unemployment. Ill health can follow.
- [ ] I5 Social isolation and boredom: loss of daily contact with colleagues and of routine; less money for leisure and social life.
- [ ] I6 Some people turn to alcohol, drugs or gambling to cope, which adds to problems (the schemes list "addiction").
- [ ] I7 Loss of skills over time and falling confidence make getting a job harder (links to residual and long-term unemployment, one line).
- [ ] I8 For a young adult (2018O): delayed independence, remaining at the parental home, delay in starting a family or buying a home, and the risk of emigrating.

Effects on the family unit (one block)
- [ ] F1 Reduced income lowers the family's standard of living: difficulty paying for food, heating, clothes and household bills; reliance on welfare payments.
- [ ] F2 Debt and housing risk: arrears on rent, mortgage or utilities; risk of losing the home.
- [ ] F3 Strain on relationships: money worries, changed roles and the stress of one or both adults being at home can cause arguments and in some cases separation or family breakdown.
- [ ] F4 Children: may have to give up activities (sport, music, school trips), may lack books, technology or a quiet place to study, may be teased or feel different from classmates, and may leave school early. Unemployed parents give fewer role models of working life.
- [ ] F5 Intergenerational unemployment: children raised in a household with no one in work may come to expect unemployment and carry the disadvantage into their own adult lives (the 2022H scheme: "weak employment role models, intergenerational unemployment").
- [ ] F6 Family members may emigrate for work, which breaks up the family's support network (2017H asked about movement in search of work).

Effects on society (one block)
- [ ] C1 Cost to the State: higher spending on social welfare payments and related supports (medical cards, fuel and housing supports), and less income from income tax and PRSI, so less money for public services; taxes may have to rise.
- [ ] C2 Loss of production and skills: the economy's output falls and unused skills are wasted.
- [ ] C3 Crime, antisocial behaviour and vandalism are associated with areas of high long-term unemployment (the schemes list crime, drug misuse and vandalism). Word this as "can lead to", not as a rule.
- [ ] C4 Emigration of working-age and skilled people reduces the population, the skills base and future tax income.
- [ ] C5 Black spots and local decline: falling local spending closes shops and services, the area acquires a bad reputation that deters investment, and people move away (2022H scheme: "bad area reputation/black spots and migration; population loss harms firms and services").
- [ ] C6 Social exclusion and inequality: the gap widens between those in work and those without; greater demand on health services (stress, depression, addiction).
- [ ] C7 The black economy: some people take undeclared work, which loses the State tax and PRSI (a minor point, listed in some schemes).

- [ ] C8 Pressure on health and community services: more people use mental health, addiction and counselling services, and local authorities, voluntary bodies and community groups take on more work. Name the effect, then say who bears the cost.
- [ ] C9 Effects on those still in work: job insecurity, fear of redundancy and, where an area empties, less local demand for their products and services. One line; examiners rarely ask for it.

Linking effects to levels (one short block)
- [ ] L1 Effects chain from one level to the next: lost income (individual) leads to arrears and strain (family), and, multiplied across a town, to closed shops and falling tax income (society). Showing one chain in a Higher Level answer earns the analysis mark that a plain list does not.
- [ ] L2 The same effect can be placed at more than one level, but give it once, at the level the question asks about; for example, depression is an individual effect, while the cost of treating it falls on society.

Structure of an answer (one short block)
- [ ] A1 Answers earn marks by covering each level asked for: the 2006O and 2017H schemes require at least one effect for the individual, the family and society, then further effects. Each point needs a name and a short explanation.

## 3. Traps

- The three levels are distinct: debt and loss of confidence are individual effects; arguments and children missing out are family effects; cost to the State, crime and emigration are society effects. Mixing them loses the mark that requires one reference per level.
- Do not repeat the causes (automation, recession) here. Rural depopulation is a cause in the causes subsection and an effect here only in the local-decline form.
- The statutory and voluntary responses (jobseeker payments in detail, SOLAS, SVP) belong to the responses subsections; this subsection mentions only that the State pays welfare, as a cost.
- The effects of poverty (poor diet, poor housing, poor health) belong to the poverty effects subsection even where they overlap with long-term unemployment; keep to the loss of work, income and status here.
- Do not use: "boredom and loss of routine can lead to poorer physical health" as stated in the current notes, because it is not in the schemes and is unconfirmed as a general claim; say that stress and depression can affect health and that loss of routine can lead to boredom.
- Do not use: figures from past-paper stems or named studies (the 327,000 peak and the percentage falls quoted in 2017H), because they belong to those papers.
- Do not use: "crime is caused by unemployment" or "the unemployed are criminals", because the evidence is an association with areas of high unemployment and the notes must not state a stereotype as a fact.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss or outline the effects of unemployment on the individual, the family and society (24 marks Ordinary, 20 marks Higher): six points at 4 marks or five points at 4 marks, with at least one reference for each of the three levels. The same answer works for the "young adult" version by tilting the individual effects toward young people.
- Describe the effects of unemployment on society alone (12 marks, 2022H): local decline, crime and antisocial behaviour, cost to the State, emigration, intergenerational unemployment.
- Discuss effects on individuals and society (20 marks, 2026H): the same content in two groups.
- Tips: name the level before each effect; give a reason why it follows (lost income, so debt); aim for one more effect than the minimum; do not describe the causes.
