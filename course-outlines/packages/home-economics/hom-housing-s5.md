# Package: hom-housing-s5 — Location, planning and professional services

- **loId:** `hom-housing-s5` · **chapterId:** `hom-housing` · **syllabusRef:** 4.2 (Elective 1, Home Design and Management)
- **Syllabus scope:** factors that influence the choice of location and house style; the procedure for obtaining planning permission from the local authority under the Planning and Development Act 2024, with appeals to An Coimisiún Pleanála; professional services — architects, engineers, surveyors, solicitors, builders, books of house plans. No [HL] marks in this subsection.
- **Settings:** a case study of a person or family with particular needs, used for the location, house design, interior and services tasks that follow.
- **Also examined:** outline and full planning permission (2012O, 2016H, 2021H, 2024O); why planning permission is needed (2012O, 2015O); professional services (2004O, 2009H, 2015O, 2019H, 2022H, 2024O, 2026H).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Style factors in more detail are in "Housing styles"; building standards, certificates and grants are in "House design and building standards".

## 1. Must cover

Choosing a location (one block)
- [ ] L1 Urban or rural setting: urban sites give services, transport and jobs but cost more and offer less space; rural sites give space, quiet and a lower land price but may need a private water supply and wastewater treatment system, and longer travel.
- [ ] L2 Closeness to work, schools, childcare, shops, health services, public transport, family and friends, and the daily time and cost of travel.
- [ ] L3 Site conditions: soil and ground stability, slope and drainage, flood risk (checked by a site survey), shelter from wind, and risks such as pollution, pylons, factories or landfill nearby.
- [ ] L4 Aspect and orientation: how much daylight and solar heat the house will receive; privacy and views.
- [ ] L5 Services available: electricity, water, sewerage, gas, broadband, refuse collection, and the cost of connecting them.
- [ ] L6 Budget and value: the price of the site and servicing costs; the prospects for the value of the property (future developments in the area).
- [ ] L7 Planning rules for the area (the development plan) that may limit where and what may be built, and the needs of the household (the case-study family).

Choosing a house style for the site (one block)
- [ ] H1 Family size, stage and lifestyle set the number of bedrooms, living space and storage; bungalow against two-storey; traditional against modern, set against the budget.
- [ ] H2 Special needs (for example a wheelchair user) may need a single-storey house, step-free access and wide doors.
- [ ] H3 Future changes: scope to extend or convert the attic, add a garage, within planning rules.
- [ ] H4 Fit with surroundings and neighbouring houses, personal taste.
- [ ] H5 (assumed) Housing styles in Ireland today are in "Housing styles"; one phrase only.

Why planning control exists (one block)
- [ ] R1 The law requires planning permission for most building work unless the work is exempt under the planning regulations (for example some small extensions).
- [ ] R2 Reasons (the marking scheme's list): developments must fit the local development plan; houses are not built overlooking neighbours; factories are kept out of residential areas; scenic areas, tourist attractions and old buildings are protected; new houses should conform in style with the area; property values are protected; services and infrastructure are planned.

Outline and full permission (one block)
- [ ] O1 Outline permission: asks whether the planning authority agrees in principle to a development before detailed plans are drawn; it needs only a site location map and basic details of the proposal, not detailed house plans. It is not permission to build.
- [ ] O2 Full permission: requires full detail: drawings, the site layout map, house plans, elevations and finishes, the materials to be used, water supply and drainage, the fee, and copies of the public notices (newspaper and site notice).
- [ ] O3 A person who is granted outline permission must still get full permission (detailed approval) before building.

Procedure for full planning permission (one block, sequence)
- [ ] P1 Optional first step: a pre-planning meeting with the planning authority, to discuss the proposal before applying; prepare drawings with an architect.
- [ ] P2 Publish a notice of the application in a local newspaper and erect a site notice on the site.
- [ ] P3 Lodge the application with the local authority: application form, fee, site location map, house plans and drawings, materials, and a copy of the notices; the authority acknowledges it.
- [ ] P4 The application goes on the planning register, open for public inspection; members of the public may make a written submission or objection.
- [ ] P5 Officials inspect the site and examine the plans against the development plan; the authority may ask for further information.
- [ ] P6 The authority grants permission (often with conditions) or refuses it, and must give reasons for a refusal.
- [ ] P7 Appeal: an applicant refused, or a person who objected, may appeal to An Coimisiún Pleanála, which replaced An Bord Pleanála in June 2025. If nobody appeals, permission is confirmed.
- [ ] P8 Diagram (optional, one cue): a flowchart of the sequence from pre-planning to the decision and the appeal route.
- [ ] P9 The Act: the procedure is set in the Planning and Development Act 2024 (the notes name the Act and An Coimisiún Pleanála; do not give the timing).

Professional services (one block per service or a table)
- [ ] S1 Architect: advises on site and design, prepares plans and drawings from the family's brief and budget, prepares and submits the planning application, advises on builders and materials, and may supervise construction.
- [ ] S2 Engineer (structural or site engineer): advises on the structure and foundations, supervises structural work, and solves building problems.
- [ ] S3 Surveyor: surveys the site or building, checks boundaries and defects, identifies problems and how to deal with them before they affect the building; a quantity surveyor estimates and controls costs; may draw up a "snag list" of faults before final payment.
- [ ] S4 Solicitor: the legal side, including contracts, checking title deeds, rights of way (with the land registry), registering ownership, legal advice on planning and building regulations and loan paperwork.
- [ ] S5 Builder: agrees the price and completion date in a contract, prepares the site, manages subcontractors (electrician, plumber, plasterer, tiler), follows the drawings and puts right faults.
- [ ] S6 Others: interior designer, landscape architect, electrician, plumber and BER assessor; the benefits and drawbacks of using an interior designer (Ordinary Level 2026): expertise and access to suppliers against cost.
- [ ] S7 Book of house plans: a catalogue of ready-drawn designs, cheaper than commissioning an architect; the plans are not tailored to the site or the family, and must still be submitted for planning permission and often adapted by an architect or engineer.

Assumed knowledge
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A local authority is the county or city council; a "development plan" sets how land in the area may be used.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any time limit for planning (decision "after two months", "a month later", "12 weeks", "within two weeks of the notice", "five years", a three-year life for outline permission, the length of time a site notice stays up, the four-week appeal window, the five-week submission period), because these appear in 2005 to 2012 marking schemes and the periods are set by regulation and are being revised under the 2024 Act; the notes give the order of the steps, not the time.
- Do not use: the fee for a submission or for an application, because fees change.
- Do not use: "An Bord Pleanála" as the appeals body, because it was replaced by An Coimisiún Pleanála in June 2025, although the 2012, 2019 and 2024 Ordinary Level marking schemes name the old name.
- Outline permission is not the same as permission to build, and outline is not "a short form of full permission"; the difference is the level of detail required and what it settles.
- A planning application is not made to the Building Control Authority: planning permission (about whether and what to build) is separate from building control (whether it is built to the Building Regulations; see "House design and building standards").
- An architect designs; an engineer advises on structure and supervises; a surveyor inspects and reports; a builder constructs; a solicitor deals with law. Do not give the architect the engineer's structural work, or the surveyor the builder's role.
- A book of house plans is not a substitute for planning permission.
- Belongs to a neighbouring subsection: style factors (housing styles); design factors, energy efficiency, building standards, certificates and warranties (house design and building standards); interior design (the interior chapter); central heating and wiring (the services chapter).

## 4. What exams ask

- Procedure for full planning permission (asked almost every year, 10 to 15 marks, Ordinary and Higher Level; 2004, 2005, 2011, 2016, 2019, 2021, 2024): a numbered sequence with the newspaper and site notices, lodging with the local authority with plans and fee, the planning register and public inspection, site inspection, the decision with reasons for refusal, and the appeal route. In 2019 and 2024 the scheme also credited a pre-planning meeting.
- Difference between outline and full permission, then the full procedure (Ordinary Level 2012, 24 marks): four marks each for what outline and full need, then four developed steps.
- Why planning permission is necessary (Ordinary Level 2012, 2015): fits the development plan, keeps factories away from houses, protects scenery and old buildings, protects neighbours.
- Professional services (2004O, 2009H, 2015O, 2019H, 2022H, 2024O, 2026H): name three, describe the role of each in specific terms; architect, engineer, solicitor, surveyor and builder are the standard five; 2022 asked for services for a home-office conversion and 2024 named architect, engineer and solicitor.
- Factors affecting location and style for a case-study family (the notes should let a student apply aspect, services, proximity, budget and the family's needs).
- Marks follow the specific role: "draws plans, applies for permission, may supervise" beats "helps with the house".
