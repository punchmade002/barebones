## 1. Must cover

What a refrigeration appliance does (one block, lead)
- [ ] A1 A refrigerator keeps food cold (0 to 5 °C, at or below 5 °C) to slow the growth of micro-organisms and enzyme activity and so slow spoilage; a freezer stops growth by holding food at -18 °C or colder. A refrigerator removes heat from inside the cabinet and gives it out into the room; it does not "make cold". (Food storage and the danger zone are in the food-safety and preservation chapters; use the 5 to 63 °C danger zone only as a one-line link.)
- [ ] A2 Types (2017O, 2013O): under-counter (standard) fridge, larder fridge (all fridge), fridge-freezer (two compartments, one or two compressors), upright freezer, chest freezer, American-style (side-by-side doors, often with ice and water dispenser), integrated (built into the kitchen units), retro style. A student picks one to study.

Parts and their functions (one block)
- [ ] P1 Compressor: a pump driven by an electric motor; it draws in low-pressure refrigerant gas from the evaporator and compresses it into a hot, high-pressure gas. It is usually at the back or base.
- [ ] P2 Condenser: a coil of tubing (black grille at the back or in the walls) where the hot gas gives out heat to the surrounding air and changes into a liquid.
- [ ] P3 Expansion device (capillary tube or expansion valve): a narrow tube that lowers the pressure of the liquid refrigerant before it enters the evaporator.
- [ ] P4 Evaporator: coil of tubing inside the cabinet (often in the freezer compartment or behind the back panel) where the liquid refrigerant evaporates (changes to gas), absorbing heat from the cabinet and food, so the cabinet cools.
- [ ] P5 Refrigerant: the fluid that circulates in the sealed system; it evaporates at a low temperature, drawing heat from inside the fridge, and releases that heat when it condenses.
- [ ] P6 Thermostat: senses the cabinet temperature; switches the compressor motor off when the set temperature is reached and on again when the cabinet warms, keeping it at 5 °C or below; the dial is turned to a colder or warmer setting.
- [ ] P7 Other parts: insulated cabinet, door seal (gasket) that keeps cold air in, interior light that comes on when the door opens, shelves and drawers.
- [ ] P8 Diagram: the closed loop of compressor, condenser, expansion device, evaporator with arrows for refrigerant flow and labels for gas or liquid and hot or cold at each part.

The working principle in sequence (one block)
- [ ] W1 The motor drives the compressor, which forces the gaseous refrigerant into the condenser under high pressure.
- [ ] W2 In the condenser the gas loses heat to the room and condenses to a liquid.
- [ ] W3 The liquid passes through the capillary tube or expansion valve into the evaporator, where the pressure is low.
- [ ] W4 In the evaporator the liquid boils (evaporates), absorbing heat from inside the cabinet; the cabinet and food are cooled.
- [ ] W5 The gas returns to the compressor and the cycle repeats until the thermostat switches the motor off.
- [ ] W6 Modern refrigerators use a hydrocarbon refrigerant (isobutane) that does not damage the ozone layer; older appliances used CFC refrigerants, now banned.

Guidelines for use and care (one block)
- [ ] G1 Siting: level surface, away from the cooker, radiators, boiler and direct sunlight, with space behind and above for air to flow around the condenser.
- [ ] G2 Temperature: set the thermostat so the cabinet is at 5 °C or below (0 to 5 °C); keep a thermometer if necessary; the freezer compartment at -18 °C.
- [ ] G3 Loading: cool hot food before storing; cover or wrap food; do not overload, so air can circulate; put raw meat and fish on the bottom shelf in covered containers so juices cannot drip onto other food; keep cooked and raw foods separate; do not store opened tins in the tin.
- [ ] G4 Use: open the door as little and as briefly as possible; check the door seal; store food by date and use older food first (first in, first out); defrost food in the fridge.
- [ ] G5 Defrosting and cleaning: defrost the freezer regularly if it is not frost-free (switch off, unplug, collect water, never use a knife or sharp object to remove ice); clean the inside with warm water and bicarbonate of soda; wipe the door seal; clean the condenser grille dust from the back occasionally.
- [ ] G6 Power cut or holiday: keep doors shut; leave the door open and switch off if left empty for a long time to prevent mould.
- [ ] G7 Energy efficiency (2007H, 2013O, 2017O): a high A to G class; correct size; keep away from heat; door closed; cool food before storing; defrost; clean the condenser; do not set colder than needed; check the seal; modern features that save energy.

Modern features (one block)
- [ ] M1 Frost-free (automatic defrost): a fan circulates cold air and a heater periodically melts the frost, so no manual defrost.
- [ ] M2 Digital temperature display and electronic controls; door-open alarm; fast-freeze or quick-chill button; holiday mode.
- [ ] M3 Zones and drawers: humidity-controlled crisper drawers; a chill or meat and fish compartment; adjustable or gated shelves; wine or bottle racks.
- [ ] M4 Water and ice dispenser (usually American style); antibacterial interior coating; LED lighting; inverter (variable-speed) compressor that runs at the speed needed, saving energy and noise; smart connection (monitor and control from a phone).
- [ ] M5 Energy label and star rating (cross-reference by name): the A to G class and kWh per year; the star rating applies to the freezer compartment.

Underlying working principles (Higher Level only; one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] H1 [HL] Heat flows naturally from hot to cold; a refrigerator moves heat from the cold cabinet to the warm room against that flow, which takes work from the compressor and so a continuous supply of electrical energy.
- [ ] H2 [HL] The boiling point of a liquid depends on pressure: lowering the pressure lowers the boiling point, so the refrigerant boils at a low temperature in the evaporator; raising the pressure raises the condensing temperature so the hot gas can release heat to the room air.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Latent heat of vaporisation: a liquid absorbs a large amount of heat as it evaporates without a rise in its own temperature, which is how the evaporator takes heat from the cabinet; the same heat is released when the gas condenses in the condenser.
- [ ] H4 [HL] Compression raises both the pressure and the temperature of the gas, which is why the condenser is warm.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Freon 12" or "liquid ammonia" as the refrigerant in a modern domestic refrigerator (the 2018H scheme names Freon 12, liquid ammonia and isobutane), because CFC-12 has been phased out as it damages the ozone layer, and ammonia is not confirmed as a household compression-fridge refrigerant; teach isobutane as the modern refrigerant.
- Do not use: the compressor "makes the cold" or "the fridge produces cold air"; heat is moved out, not cold made.
- Do not use: the four-star temperature range "-18 to -25 °C up to one year" as a storage band for the whole freezer without saying it is the marking scheme's figure; keep to the star rating as in the selecting-appliances subsection.
- Do not use: figures for the energy use, price or noise of a named fridge, because these change.
- Do not use: the refrigerant pressures or temperatures at each stage as numbers, because none are checked; say "high" and "low" only.
- Condenser versus evaporator: the condenser gives out heat (outside, warm) and changes gas to liquid; the evaporator takes in heat (inside, cold) and changes liquid to gas. These are the most commonly swapped in answers.
- Compressor versus condenser: the compressor squeezes the gas; the condenser cools it. Do not describe the compressor as cooling.
- The thermostat switches the motor, not the evaporator; it does not control the refrigerant directly.
- Do not put the star rating as a measure of fridge (fresh-food) temperature; it applies to the freezer compartment only. The star rating and energy label are taught in the selecting-appliances subsection; use one line here.
- Food storage rules (use-by dates, bacterial growth, danger zone) belong to food safety; use only the fridge-use guidelines here.
- The working-principle physics (pressure, boiling point, latent heat) is [HL]; at Ordinary Level give the cycle in plain words without the physics.

## 4. What exams ask

- Functions of the parts, 6 marks, Higher almost every year (2005H, 2010H, 2013H, 2015H, 2021H, 2024H): two or three of compressor, condenser, evaporator, refrigerant and thermostat. One short sentence each: the compressor compresses the gas and drives it round; the condenser cools gas to liquid; the evaporator turns liquid to gas, absorbing heat from the cabinet; the refrigerant carries the heat and evaporates at a low temperature; the thermostat keeps the cabinet at 5 °C or below by switching the motor.
- Full study (2018H, 2025H, 2007H, 2013O, 2017O): type of refrigerator; working principle in sequence; guidelines for use (three or four with reasons); modern features (three); energy efficiency rating (A to G scale; how to save energy). 22-38 marks.
- Selecting a refrigerator for a family (2017O, 20 marks): four factors: initial and running cost, size and capacity, energy efficiency, space, brand, ease of cleaning, safety.
- Star rating (2007H, 12 marks): the four ratings with temperature and storage time.
- Environmental responsibility with fridges (2025H): efficient class, correct size, door shut, no overloading, responsible disposal of the old appliance through take-back.
- Tips to build: name the appliance; say gas or liquid and hot or cold for each part; add the thermostat; give each guideline with its reason; give the energy label scale direction.
