# Package: hom-energy-nutrition-s6 — Water

- **loId:** `hom-energy-nutrition-s6` · **chapterId:** `hom-energy-nutrition` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.7
- **Syllabus scope:** general properties of water; biological importance. Also examined: biological functions of water (2019H, 2015H, 2012O, 2005H, 2009O, 2019O true/false); water in pregnancy and sport (2026O, 2023O).
  No item in this subsection is Higher Level only. Water is placed here provisionally in the outline; it is a nutrient-chapter topic examined in Section A almost every few years as a 6-mark list.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Water is not an energy nutrient, and this subsection follows the chapter's discussion of energy. Minerals such as calcium and fluoride are in "Minerals" chapter; hydrolysis in digestion is in the digestion chapter; pregnancy and sports diets belong to the meal-planning chapter. Here each is covered only for the role of water.

## 1. Must cover

General properties (one block)
- [ ] P1 Pure water is colourless, odourless and tasteless (2005H scheme).
- [ ] P2 Boils at 100 °C and freezes at 0 °C at normal atmospheric pressure (2005H).
- [ ] P3 A good solvent: many minerals, sugars, vitamins (water-soluble) and gases dissolve in it; this lets it carry nutrients and wastes in the body and is why water-soluble vitamins and minerals can be lost in cooking water.
- [ ] P4 Absorbs and retains heat well: it takes a lot of heat to warm it and it cools slowly, so it keeps body temperature steady.
- [ ] P5 Neutral: pH 7 for pure water.
- [ ] P6 Ice is less dense than water, so it floats; water expands when it freezes. Steam: water converts to steam at over 100 °C (2005H also lists this).
- [ ] P7 Hard and soft water: hard water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium salts; soft water has little. Hard water is a source of calcium (2019H calcium sources include hard or bottled water).
- [ ] P8 Water has no energy value (assumed; see the energy chapter) and contains no protein, fat or vitamins of its own; it is not a source of energy.

Biological functions of water (one block; the commonest exam list)
- [ ] B1 Water is a component of all body cells and of every body fluid (2019O true/false: True): blood plasma, lymph, tissue fluid, saliva, digestive juices, mucus, urine, sweat.
- [ ] B2 Transport: carries dissolved nutrients, oxygen (in blood), carbon dioxide, hormones and enzymes around the body and into cells, in blood plasma and tissue fluid.
- [ ] B3 Removal of waste: the kidneys filter blood, and water carries urea and other waste out in urine; water also lost in faeces, sweat and breath.
- [ ] B4 Regulation of body temperature: sweat evaporates from the skin and cools the body; water in blood and tissues spreads heat; body temperature stays near 37 °C (2019O true/false: True).
- [ ] B5 Digestion: water takes part in hydrolysis, the splitting of large food molecules into smaller ones with the addition of water, in the digestion of carbohydrate, protein and fat (2019O true/false: "water prevents digestion and absorption" is False; it aids it). Saliva and digestive juices, mostly water, soften food and help swallowing.
- [ ] B6 Prevents constipation: keeps faeces soft, especially with a high-fibre diet.
- [ ] B7 Lubricates joints and membranes; cushions organs (for example the fluid around the brain and spinal cord; one clause).
- [ ] B8 Quenches thirst and prevents dehydration (2012O, 2009O).
- [ ] B9 Minerals in water: provides calcium and fluoride (2015H scheme names calcium and fluoride); fluoride helps protect teeth against decay. Public water supplies in Ireland are fluoridated under the Health (Fluoridation of Water Supplies) Act 1960.
- [ ] B10 Water is the medium for chemical reactions in the cells (metabolism).

Dehydration and thirst (one block)
- [ ] D1 Water is lost every day in urine, faeces, sweat and breath and must be replaced by drinks and food (fruit, vegetables, soups). Water is the main drink advised in the healthy eating guidelines.
- [ ] D2 Thirst is the signal that the body's water content has fallen.
- [ ] D3 Dehydration: water losses exceed intake. Signs: thirst, dark and small amounts of urine, headache, tiredness, dizziness, dry mouth. Severe dehydration: confusion, a fall in blood pressure and collapse, needing urgent medical help. Causes: heat, exercise, fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, not drinking enough.
- [ ] D4 Factors that raise water needs: hot weather, exercise, fever, vomiting and diarrhoea, pregnancy, breastfeeding, high-fibre or high-protein diets.
- [ ] D5 Sugary drinks add empty kilocalories; plain water gives hydration without energy; choose water.

Water in pregnancy (one block; 2026O, 2023O)
- [ ] M1 A pregnant woman's blood volume rises; water carries nutrients and oxygen to the foetus across the placenta and waste away.
- [ ] M2 Amniotic fluid (mostly water) cushions and protects the baby and keeps its temperature steady.
- [ ] M3 Prevents constipation, which is common in pregnancy.
- [ ] M4 Breastfeeding needs extra water because breast milk is mostly water.

Water in sport (one block; 2026O, 2023O)
- [ ] S1 Exercise raises heat production; sweating cools the body and loses water and salts (sodium, potassium).
- [ ] S2 Dehydration reduces stamina, strength, concentration and coordination, and raises the risk of cramps, heat exhaustion and heat stroke.
- [ ] S3 Drink before, during and after exercise, in small regular amounts rather than a large amount at once; water is enough for most activity.
- [ ] S4 For prolonged or very intense exercise, isotonic sports drinks containing sodium and sugar can replace lost salts and energy; they are not needed for ordinary activity and add empty kilocalories if used unnecessarily.
- [ ] S5 Diagram: none required. A simple water balance diagram (intake: drinks, food, metabolism; output: urine, sweat, breath, faeces) is optional.

## 3. Traps

- Water is not a source of energy and should not be listed with the energy nutrients, but it is essential and a nutrient in the broad sense.
- Hydrolysis uses water; do not confuse with hydration or dehydration. Hydrolysis is the chemical splitting of molecules; dehydration is loss of body water.
- Do not say water prevents digestion (2019O false statement); water helps digestion.
- Thirst quenching is a function but a weak mark point; give transport, temperature, waste and digestion first.
- Sweat cools the body by evaporation, not because water is cold.
- Do not describe water as "supplying minerals" in general: the schemes name calcium and fluoride; hard water has more calcium.
- Calcium and fluoride as nutrients, and hydrolysis enzymes, belong to the mineral and digestion chapters.
- Pregnancy menu planning and sports-diet planning belong to the meal-planning material; here only water's role.
- Do not use: any recommended daily volume of water (for example "eight glasses" or litres a day), because the figures vary by age, sex, activity and source and no scheme states them.
- Do not use: the percentage of body weight that is water (for example "about 60 to 70 per cent"), because it is a changing estimate that varies with age and body composition and is not in the schemes.
- Do not use: the claim that sports drinks are needed for ordinary exercise, because they are only for prolonged or intense activity; water is the recommended drink.

## 4. What exams ask

- State or list two or three biological functions of water (6 marks in Section A: 2005H, 2009O, 2012O, 2015H, 2019H; frequent). Full marks: three clear functions with a brief explanation each, for example transport of nutrients, oxygen, hormones and enzymes; regulation of temperature through sweat; removal of waste in urine; hydrolysis and digestion; prevention of constipation; part of blood plasma and body fluids.
- State a property of water (2005H, 2 marks): boiling and freezing points, solvent, retains heat, neutral, colourless, odourless, tasteless.
- True or false statements (2019O): water is in all body cells (True); regulates temperature through perspiration (True); prevents digestion and absorption (False).
- Importance of water in the daily diet (2009O, 10 marks): thirst, temperature, waste removal, dehydration, transport and digestion, in about five linked statements.
- Water in pregnancy and sport (2026O, 2023O): transport of nutrients and oxygen to the foetus, amniotic fluid, constipation, extra fluid for breastfeeding; replacing sweat losses, avoiding dehydration, drinking before, during and after exercise.
- Exam-tip material: always add a short reason with each function; name the waste product urea; link dehydration to loss of concentration and to heat exhaustion in sport.
