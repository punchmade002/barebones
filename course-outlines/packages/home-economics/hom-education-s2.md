# Package: hom-education-s2 — Factors that influence educational achievement

- **loId:** `hom-education-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-education` · **syllabusRef:** 6.2
- **Syllabus scope:** "Factors that influence educational achievement [6.2]." No "Also examined" items listed in the outline. No Higher Level only material: common to both levels (past papers ask it at both levels and at HL in 2004, 2011, 2018).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. "Educational achievement" means how well a student does in school or college: results, progression, staying on. This subsection lists the influences on it. Why the system gives unequal results for some groups (gender, socio-economic status, disadvantage, early school leaving) belongs to the equality subsection; the initiatives and programmes (DEIS, JCSP, HEAR and so on) belong to the provision and access subsections. Where a past marking scheme lists a programme as a "factor", give it as a support that improves achievement in one clause only.

## 1. Must cover

Individual factors (one block)
- [ ] A1 Intellectual ability (intelligence): affects the pace of learning and the level a student can reach. Ability alone does not decide results: work and support matter too. Learning difficulties (dyslexia, for example) can lower achievement if unsupported.
- [ ] A2 Attitude to education and motivation: students who value learning and set goals tend to achieve more; a student who sees school as pointless will often disengage.
- [ ] A3 Work ethic: commitment to homework, revision, attendance and class participation, directly affects results.
- [ ] A4 Health: illness, tiredness, poor sleep or poor nutrition reduce concentration and attendance. Mental health and stress matter too.
- [ ] A5 Subject and course choice: choosing subjects and a level that match interests and ability improves engagement and results; at third level, choosing the wrong course can lead to poor results or dropping out.
- [ ] A6 Part-time work during term time (second and third level) cuts study time and leaves students tired.

Family and home factors (one block)
- [ ] F1 Parental attitude: parents who value education, take an interest in homework and school, and have high expectations tend to raise achievement.
- [ ] F2 Parents' own education: affects the expectations they set, their understanding of the school system and how much help they can give with schoolwork and choices.
- [ ] F3 Family size: in larger families, parents' time, attention and money for each child may be more limited; older children may have caring duties.
- [ ] F4 Family finances and socio-economic background: pay for books, uniform, a computer, extra tuition, trips and activities, and decide if a student must work part-time or leave early. (The wider equality issue is in the next subsection but one; here give it as a factor in one or two points.)
- [ ] F5 Home environment: a quiet place to study, books, a computer and internet access, a routine for homework, and the absence of overcrowding, all help; their absence makes study difficult.
- [ ] F6 Family circumstances: separation, bereavement, illness, addiction, conflict or unemployment at home cause stress, lower concentration and attendance; a cycle of poverty and low expectations can pass down through generations.

Peers, school and community factors (one block)
- [ ] P1 Peer group: positive peers encourage effort and study; negative peer pressure encourages truancy, disruption and early leaving. Bullying damages confidence and attendance.
- [ ] P2 School attended and school environment: the school's ethos, discipline, atmosphere and expectations of its pupils; teacher expectations and teacher quality; the range of subjects offered; single-sex or mixed; a supportive, safe environment helps.
- [ ] P3 School resources: class size, access to technology and equipment, laboratories, libraries, guidance counsellor; supports such as learning support teachers, special needs assistants (SNAs) and exam accommodations help pupils with special needs (named only here; detail in the provision subsection).
- [ ] P4 Locality (setting): a school in a disadvantaged area may have more early school leaving, fewer after-school facilities and less access to resources, and students may live far from school or lack transport; good local amenities, libraries and community supports help.
- [ ] P5 Attendance: poor attendance leads to gaps in learning and lower attainment, and is linked with early leaving.
- [ ] P6 Programmes and supports (named in marking schemes as factors): Transition Year, LCA, LCVP, JCSP, work experience, access routes to third level and exam accommodations can raise achievement for students who might otherwise drop out. One clause each, no description.

Third-level (one block)
- [ ] T1 At third level: correct choice of course; family situation and cost (cost of fees, accommodation, travel); working part-time; home and college environment; positive or negative peers; locality and distance from home; parental attitude; college supports; ability and work ethic (2015 OL).

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: any percentage, retention or early-leaving figure, or the numbers in a past paper's chart, because they change each year.
- Do not turn this into a list of types of school or of education programmes; a factor is an influence on results, not a description of a scheme. Keep any programme to a clause.
- Do not write "intelligence" as a fixed or the only cause; marking schemes accept it as one factor among several.
- Keep factors under their headings so a student can give "four factors" with one reason each: factor, then how it raises or lowers achievement (a factor with no effect stated earns no marks).
- Gender, socio-economic status as a system-wide inequality, disadvantaged groups and early school leaving as a topic belong to "Equality of opportunity in education"; give them here only as "family finances", "locality" and "attendance".
- Do not use the word "streaming" as a settled fact; schools differ. Not needed here.
- Do not refer to the Junior Certificate as the current qualification (it is Junior Cycle now); say Junior Cycle.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss or analyse the factors that influence educational achievement (2004 HL, 2011 HL 24 marks, 2018 HL, 2021 OL "give an account of four factors"). Expected: three or four factors each with a reason: ability, attitude and work ethic, family influence (parental attitude and education, finances, size), home environment (quiet space, books, internet), peers (positive or negative), school and locality (resources, class size, technology, teacher expectations), and supports for special needs.
- Third-level version (2015 OL, four factors): course choice, cost and part-time work, home and college environment, peers, parental attitude.
- Reasons students drop out of school early (2015 OL, using a chart): family problems, health, cycle of poverty, financial problems, parental attitude, negative peer pressure, bullying, ability, family size, home environment, locality. Answers draw on this subsection's factors; the topic itself is in the equality subsection.
- Short questions: name two effects of poor health or bullying on achievement; name three home conditions that help study.
