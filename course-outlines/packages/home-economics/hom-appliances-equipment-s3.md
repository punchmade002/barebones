## 1. Must cover

What a motor appliance does (one block, lead)
- [ ] A1 Appliances with a motor turn electrical energy into movement (kinetic energy): food processor, blender, hand mixer, vacuum cleaner, washing machine, dishwasher pump, extractor fan, hairdryer fan. Appliances with a heating element turn electricity into heat instead (kettle, toaster, iron; see the heating-element subsection). Some have both (washing machine, hairdryer, dishwasher).
- [ ] A2 (2016O) Classification: food processor = motor; kettle = element; toaster = element.

Design of a food processor (one block)
- [ ] D1 Parts: a motor housed in a heavy, stable base with non-slip feet; a drive shaft (spindle) that carries the turning movement to the attachment; a bowl (clear, with a measuring scale); a lid with a feed tube and a pusher; interchangeable attachments (chopping or S-blade, slicing and grating discs, dough blade, whisk).
- [ ] D2 Safety design: a safety interlock stops the motor unless the bowl and lid are correctly locked; overload cut-out protects the motor from overheating; the pusher keeps fingers out of the feed tube; non-slip feet; the blade comes to a stop quickly when switched off.
- [ ] D3 Controls: on/off, speed settings, a pulse button for short bursts.
- [ ] D4 Materials and cleaning: heat-resistant, impact-resistant plastic bowl or glass; stainless steel blades; removable dishwasher-safe parts; smooth, sealed base wiped with a damp cloth.
- [ ] D5 Diagram: a food processor in section: base with motor and drive shaft, bowl, blade, lid with feed tube and pusher, interlock.
- [ ] D6 Selection factors specific to a motor appliance (2009O): capacity, wattage and motor power, range of attachments actually needed, noise, cord length and storage, ease of assembling and cleaning, safety features, guarantee and service. General factors (price, brand, energy label) are in the selecting-appliances subsection.

Guidelines for use and care (one block)
- [ ] G1 Before use: read the manufacturer's instructions; assemble correctly; check the plug, flex and bowl are undamaged; place on a dry, level surface away from the worktop edge and from water.
- [ ] G2 Use: fit the correct attachment; lock bowl and lid before switching on; do not overfill (stay below the maximum mark); cut food into even pieces; use the pusher, never fingers or utensils; use pulse for control; use the right speed; do not run longer than the instructions allow because the motor can overheat (pause to cool).
- [ ] G3 Safety: switch off and unplug before changing attachments, opening the lid, scraping the bowl or cleaning; wait until the blade has stopped; keep hands, hair and loose clothing away from moving parts; handle blades by the hub; keep out of reach of young children.
- [ ] G4 Cleaning and storage: wash the bowl, lid and attachments in warm soapy water or the dishwasher if allowed; do not immerse the motor base, wipe it with a damp cloth; dry blades well; store blades safely; wind or store the flex neatly.
- [ ] G5 Electrical care: dry hands before touching the plug; never overload a socket; have damage repaired by a qualified person; use the correct fuse in the plug (3 A for low-power appliances, 13 A for high-power ones; the manufacturer states which).
- [ ] G6 Other motor appliances in a line each: hand mixer (start on low speed, switch off before lifting beaters out); blender (add liquid first, do not run empty, do not blend very hot liquid with the lid sealed); vacuum cleaner (empty the dust container or change the bag before it is full, clear blockages with the machine off); washing machine (do not overload, use the recommended detergent amount, suitable programme).

Energy efficiency (one block, 2024H)
- [ ] E1 Motor appliances use little energy compared with heating appliances because they run for short periods: use the right size, avoid running longer than needed, process several jobs in one go (dry foods first), switch off at the socket, and choose an efficient, correctly sized model.
- [ ] E2 (assumed) Cost to run = power in kW x hours x unit price; a short-use appliance costs very little. Do not give a unit price.
- [ ] E3 Appliances with a motor that run for long periods (washing machine, dishwasher, fridge) are where the energy label matters; use eco programmes and full loads.

Underlying working principles (Higher Level only; one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] H1 [HL] A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a force (the motor effect). The force is greatest when the wire is at right angles to the field; its direction is found with Fleming's left-hand rule (thumb = force or motion, first finger = field, second finger = current).
- [ ] H2 [HL] Simple DC motor: a coil between the poles of a permanent magnet or electromagnet; the forces on the two sides of the coil are in opposite directions, so the coil turns. The split-ring commutator and carbon brushes reverse the current every half turn so the coil keeps turning the same way.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Energy conversion: electrical energy in, kinetic energy out; some is wasted as heat (resistance of the coil, friction in bearings). The shaft's rotation is passed to the blade, drum or fan by a drive shaft, gears or a drive belt in some designs.
- [ ] H4 [HL] Universal motor: the common motor in small kitchen appliances and vacuum cleaners; it runs on AC or DC, spins at high speed and is lightweight; its speed can be changed by altering the voltage supplied (variable-speed controller). Its carbon brushes wear and need replacing.
- [ ] H5 [HL] Induction motor: runs on AC, no brushes or commutator, quiet, long-lived; used where steady, quieter running is wanted (some older washing machines, pumps, fridge compressors).
- [ ] H6 [HL] Diagram: a simple motor with magnet poles, coil, commutator, brushes, battery or supply, with arrows for current and force.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "a belt turns the attachments" as a general fact about food processors (2024H scheme), because many models drive the blade directly from the motor shaft; belts and gears appear in some designs only.
- Do not use: "induction motors drive the drum in washing machines" as a general statement, because modern machines commonly use universal or inverter-driven brushless motors; say only that a washing machine has a motor that turns the drum.
- Do not use: a named motor type for a specific commercial model.
- Do not use: figures for the speed (rpm) or power of motors, because these vary by model.
- Do not confuse the motor appliances with element appliances: the kettle and toaster have heating elements, not motors, and belong to the heating-element subsection.
- The refrigerator's compressor is driven by a motor but belongs to the refrigeration subsection; mention it only in one phrase.
- Do not teach the working principle at Ordinary Level beyond "electrical energy is changed to movement"; the motor effect, commutator and motor types are Higher Level only and must sit in their own block marked "(Higher Level only)".
- Fleming's left-hand rule is for motors; do not give the right-hand rule (generators) as part of this subsection.
- A transformer belongs to the microwave subsection; a motor and a transformer are not the same.
- General selection criteria (price, brand, energy label, guarantee) belong to the selecting-appliances subsection; here only the factors that are particular to a motor appliance.

## 4. What exams ask

- 2024H (Higher, study of one appliance with a motor): working principle (electrical energy to kinetic energy; a motor turns the blade or drum), guidelines for use (correct attachment, locked lid, do not overfill, unplug before cleaning), energy efficiency (right-sized, avoid overuse, efficient model). Higher answers add a short working-principle explanation with named parts.
- 2009O (Ordinary, 30 marks): type of appliance (food processor, with a description); selection factors; design (heavy base, locking bowl, controls, materials); guidelines for use. Give a concrete appliance and parts, not generalities.
- 2016O: tick motor or element for kettle, food processor and toaster.
- Expect marks per guideline at about 3-4 marks: state the guideline and the reason (for example "never use fingers in the feed tube because the blade can cut").
- Tips to build: name an appliance first; link each guideline to a hazard; add the unplugging-before-cleaning rule; for Higher, link the motor to energy conversion.
