# Package: hom-meal-planning-s2 — Dietary requirements through the life cycle

## 1. Must cover

Factors affecting requirements (one block)
- [ ] R1 Age: needs change with each life stage (growth in childhood and adolescence, maintenance in adults, falling energy needs in older age).
- [ ] R2 Sex: men generally need more energy (more muscle, larger body); girls and women need more iron once menstruation begins.
- [ ] R3 Health status: illness, allergies, diabetes, digestive disorders change the foods allowed or how nutrients are absorbed (details of conditions belong to other subsections).
- [ ] R4 Pregnancy and breastfeeding raise the need for some nutrients.
- [ ] R5 Activity: energy and fluid needs rise with activity; a sedentary adult needs less energy.
- [ ] R6 Energy balance: energy in food should match energy used; surplus is stored as fat.
- [ ] R7 A balanced diet: contains all the nutrients in the correct proportions for the individual's needs and good health (2008O definition).

Babies and pre-school children (one block)
- [ ] B1 Breast milk supplies all a baby's needs for about the first six months; weaning starts at about six months, when the iron stored at birth is running low; first foods include puréed meat and iron-fortified cereal.
- [ ] B2 Breastfed babies (and babies taking little formula) need a vitamin D supplement from birth to 12 months, because breast milk contains little vitamin D.
- [ ] B3 Salt kept low and sugar limited for babies and young children; no added salt or sugar to baby foods.
- [ ] B4 Pre-school guidelines (2025O, 2022O): regular balanced meals in small, manageable portions and nutritious snacks between; attractive presentation; discourage fussy eating by offering new flavours one at a time without forcing; involve children in preparation; limit sugary food and drink to protect teeth; include fruit and vegetables, protein for growth, calcium and vitamin D for bones and teeth, iron for blood; follow the food pyramid.

Children (one block)
- [ ] C1 All nutrients in balanced proportion, portions suited to age and activity, from the food pyramid (2021O); protein for growth, carbohydrate and fat for energy, calcium for bones and teeth, iron for blood, fibre, water; nutritious snacks; less sugar and salt.
- [ ] C2 Ways to include vegetables for children: stir-fries, cut shapes, grated or blended into sauces, smoothies, soups, vegetables in lunchboxes (2021O).

Adolescents (one block)
- [ ] T1 Growth spurt (about 12 to 19 years) raises the need for all nutrients.
- [ ] T2 Protein from animal and plant sources for growth and repair; calcium (with vitamin D) to build peak bone mass and reduce later osteoporosis; iron (especially girls, who start menstruating) with vitamin C to help absorption, to avoid anaemia; high-energy starchy foods (pasta, potatoes, rice, cereals) for active teenagers; fruit and vegetables for vitamin C and fibre.
- [ ] T3 Limit high-fat, salty and sugary snacks and fizzy drinks; avoid skipping meals, especially breakfast; three varied meals daily (2022H, 2012O).
- [ ] T4 Factors affecting adolescent requirements (2012O): age, activity level, gender, health status, pregnancy, faddy eating.
- [ ] T5 Factors making a healthy diet difficult for teenagers (2022H): likes and dislikes, early exposure to fatty, salty and sugary foods, convenience and eating on the go, availability, cost, advertising, peers.

Adults (one block)
- [ ] D1 Match energy to activity to keep a healthy weight; a balanced diet with all the nutrients in proportion (2006O sedentary worker: reduce fat and sugar, eat fibre-rich foods, calcium, iron and vitamins, limit salt).
- [ ] D2 Starchy foods, fruit and vegetables for fibre; protein at main meals; unsaturated fats replace saturated; limit sugar and salt; women of child-bearing age need iron and folate.

Older people (one block)
- [ ] O1 Energy needs fall (less muscle, less activity) so smaller but nutrient-dense portions.
- [ ] O2 Protein to keep muscle and repair tissue; calcium and vitamin D for bones, because calcium absorption falls with age (2007O); vitamin B12 absorption can fall.
- [ ] O3 Fibre and fluid to prevent constipation; reduce salt (blood pressure) and saturated fat; iron and vitamin C.
- [ ] O4 Practical needs: soft, moist, easily chewed food for people with poor teeth or dentures; appetising food for reduced appetite; small frequent meals; fortified foods; limited budget and mobility affect shopping and cooking (2025H).
- [ ] O5 Diet-related conditions common in older people (2007O): osteoporosis, diabetes, coronary heart disease, high blood pressure, high cholesterol.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding (one block; examined most often)
- [ ] P1 Protein (HBV: lean meat, fish, fully cooked eggs, pasteurised dairy) for growth of fetus, placenta and maternal tissue.
- [ ] P2 Folic acid (folate) before conception and in early pregnancy to prevent neural-tube defects; foods: green vegetables, fortified cereals, plus a supplement as advised.
- [ ] P3 Iron for increased blood volume and the baby's stores, with vitamin C at the same meal; calcium and vitamin D for the baby's bones and teeth and to protect the mother's bones; fibre and fluids to prevent constipation; essential fats (omega-3 from oily fish) for the baby's brain and nervous system; iodine and other vitamins (Higher answers list vitamins A, C, D, K, folate).
- [ ] P4 Energy: a modest increase, mainly in the later stage; "not eating for two"; balance with activity and use the food pyramid; low-GI starchy foods.
- [ ] P5 Foods to avoid or limit: alcohol; liver or pâté and unpasteurised milk and soft mould-ripened cheese (listeria risk); raw or undercooked meat, fish and eggs (listeria, salmonella); limit caffeine and salt. Accommodate taste changes and morning sickness (dry toast before rising, small frequent meals).
- [ ] P6 Water: needed for blood plasma and fetal tissues, to carry nutrients and remove waste, and to prevent dehydration and constipation (2026O part).
- [ ] P7 Breastfeeding women need extra energy, protein, calcium and fluid to make milk.
- [ ] P8 A one-day menu for a pregnant woman: breakfast, lunch and dinner meeting the points above (2026O, 2021O): for example fruit juice, porridge made with pasteurised milk and berries, wholemeal toast; vegetable soup, wholemeal bread, yoghurt, fruit; well-cooked lean chicken or fish with vegetables and potatoes. Menu guidance must use pasteurised dairy and fully cooked foods.

Convalescents (one block)
- [ ] V1 Small, appetising, easily digested meals because appetite is reduced; light seasoning; avoid fried, greasy and very spicy food.
- [ ] V2 Protein for tissue repair (lean meat, fish, eggs, dairy); iron with vitamin C for blood; fibre-rich fruit and vegetables and fluids; regular balanced meals (2022O).

Active people (one block)
- [ ] X1 More energy, mainly complex carbohydrate (wholegrain bread, pasta, rice, potatoes); extra fluid before, during and after exercise; protein for muscle repair; snacks such as fruit, yoghurt or wholegrain sandwiches.

Irregular working hours (one block; 2021H, 2016H)
- [ ] W1 Strategies: plan ahead with a list; batch cook and freeze portions; reheatable dishes (soups, casseroles); a slow cooker or timer; packed meals with fruit, wholegrain bread and yoghurt; keep staples and some frozen or prepared ingredients; avoid skipping meals, fast food and vending machines; balanced portions following guidelines.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "needs per kilogram of body weight are highest in infancy and adolescence", because per-kilogram needs are highest in infancy; adolescents have the highest total needs for several nutrients, not per kilogram.
- Do not use: peanuts as a food a pregnant woman must avoid (in the 2008 scheme), because it is superseded advice; keep to alcohol, listeria and salmonella risks.
- Do not use: any RDA, micrograms, portion counts or caffeine limits (not stated in the schemes in a form to teach, and they change).
- Do not use: "eating for two"; the point is the opposite, a modest rise mainly late in pregnancy.
- Folic acid (the supplement form) and folate (the food form) are the same nutrient for this purpose; use "folic acid/folate".
- Do not confuse convalescents (recovery from illness: small, light, protein-rich) with active people (extra energy and fluid).
- Do not write a menu that contains the foods a pregnant woman should avoid.
- Belongs elsewhere: vitamin and mineral chemistry, sources and properties (nutrient chapters); diseases such as osteoporosis, anaemia, CHD (diet-related problems); vegetarian and vegan planning (modified diets); costing and shopping lists (meal management).
