## 1. Must cover

What pollution is and its types
- [ ] T1 Pollution is the release of substances or energy into the environment at a rate that harms living things or damages natural processes.
- [ ] T2 Air pollution: gases and particles in the atmosphere, mainly from burning fossil fuels, industry, traffic and domestic fires.
- [ ] T3 Water pollution: harmful substances entering rivers, lakes, groundwater or the sea, from sewage, farming, industry and households.
- [ ] T4 Noise pollution: unwanted or excessive sound.
- [ ] T5 Land and litter pollution: waste discarded on land or in water, which harms wildlife and blocks drains (one bullet; the three types examined are air, water and noise).

Causes of air pollution
- [ ] A1 Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, peat, gas) for electricity, transport and heating releases carbon dioxide and sulphur dioxide (coal, oil and peat especially).
- [ ] A2 Nitrogen oxides come from vehicle engines and any high-temperature combustion.
- [ ] A3 Carbon monoxide comes from incomplete burning, for example faulty boilers, stoves and vehicle exhausts.
- [ ] A4 Smoke and particulates come from burning solid fuels such as coal, wood and turf in open fires and stoves, and from industry. Smoke and fog together form smog (named in the 2018H scheme).
- [ ] A5 CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) from old fridge refrigerants and foams and older aerosols destroy ozone (see Traps: these are phased out).
- [ ] A6 Methane (a greenhouse gas) is released from landfill and from farm animals; the 2023H scheme lists it as an emission.
- [ ] A7 Overuse of chemical sprays and cleaning products, and burning rubbish, add pollutants (2016H scheme).

Effects of air pollution
- [ ] AE1 Global warming and climate change: carbon dioxide, methane and other greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere, raising temperatures and sea levels and changing weather and crops (2023H, 2018H schemes).
- [ ] AE2 Acid rain: sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides dissolve in rainwater to form acids, which lower the pH of soil and water, harm plants and fish, dissolve limestone and corrode metal and stone buildings.
- [ ] AE3 Ozone depletion: CFCs break down ozone in the stratosphere, so more harmful ultraviolet radiation reaches the earth.
- [ ] AE4 Health: particulates and smoke cause respiratory illness and asthma; carbon monoxide is a poison that stops the blood carrying oxygen (it has no smell, which is why detectors are used).
- [ ] AE5 Smog and smoke reduce air quality and visibility.

Causes and effects of water pollution
- [ ] W1 Causes: untreated or poorly treated sewage; fertiliser and slurry washed off farmland; industrial effluent, chemicals and pesticides; oil and fuel leaks running into drains; household chemicals, paint and oil poured down drains; phosphates, historically in laundry and dishwasher detergents (see Traps).
- [ ] W2 Eutrophication: nitrates and phosphates feed rapid growth of algae (an algal bloom); the bloom blocks light so submerged plants die; when the algae die, bacteria decompose them and use up dissolved oxygen; fish and other life die.
- [ ] W3 Toxic substances build up along the food chain, harming fish and birds, and chemical contamination of drinking water sources threatens health (2004O scheme also lists loss of aquatic life and water unfit to drink).

Noise pollution
- [ ] NO1 Sources: road and air traffic, construction, industry, lawnmowers, music systems, televisions and alarms.
- [ ] NO2 Noise is measured in decibels (dB); the scale is logarithmic, so an increase of 10 dB is about a tenfold increase in sound intensity.
- [ ] NO3 Effects: stress, headaches and tension, disturbed sleep, reduced concentration, and permanent hearing loss with long exposure.

How consumers can reduce pollution (2019O, 2023O, 2013O, 2016H, 2023H, 2004O)
- [ ] R1 Air: walk, cycle, use public transport or share journeys; choose an electric, hybrid or low-emission vehicle and keep it serviced with tyres inflated; use smokeless fuel or renewable heat (heat pumps, solar); use energy-efficient appliances; dispose of old fridges and freezers properly so CFCs are not released; do not burn rubbish.
- [ ] R2 Water: do not pour paint, oil or chemicals down drains; use fertiliser and chemicals sparingly; compost garden waste; keep boilers and oil tanks maintained so they do not leak.
- [ ] R3 Noise: keep music and television volumes down, avoid noisy work at unsocial hours, maintain equipment, and use double glazing and sound insulation.
- [ ] R4 General: reduce, reuse and recycle; buy only what is needed; choose biodegradable or second-hand products; avoid single-use packaging (2023O scheme).

Government and public initiatives against pollution (2014O, 2004O)
- [ ] G1 The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), set up under the Environmental Protection Agency Act 1992, licenses large industrial and waste activities, monitors air and water quality and enforces environmental law.
- [ ] G2 The Waste Management Act 1996 (as amended) controls waste collection, movement and disposal and gives local authorities and the EPA roles in promoting reduction, recycling and reuse (2004O scheme names this Act).
- [ ] G3 Solid fuel regulations: the Air Pollution Act 1987 (Solid Fuels) Regulations 2022 ban the sale of smoky (bituminous) coal across the State from 31 October 2022 and restrict the sale of turf at retail and of wet wood, to cut smoke and particulates from home heating. Local authorities enforce them.
- [ ] G4 Levies and taxes: the landfill levy on waste sent to landfill, the plastic bag levy (since 2002) and a carbon tax on fossil fuels (introduced in 2010) make polluting choices dearer.
- [ ] G5 Other schemes named in the 2014O scheme as clean-green initiatives: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle; Green Dot (Repak); WEEE take-back; EU Ecolabel; and, since 2024, the Re-turn deposit return scheme. Detail is in the waste subsection; give a name and one line.
- [ ] G6 Support for renewable energy and home energy upgrades (grants through the Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland, SEAI) reduces the fossil fuel burned.

## 3. Traps

- Global warming and ozone depletion are different problems: greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane) cause warming; CFCs thin the ozone layer. Acid rain is a third problem (sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides).
- Carbon dioxide does not cause acid rain, and CFCs do not cause global warming in a first answer; keep each cause with its own effect when asked for "cause and effect".
- Acid rain does not damage the ozone layer.
- Carbon monoxide is a poison from incomplete burning, not a main cause of global warming.
- CFCs: they have been banned in the EU for decades. Old fridges can still hold them, which is why fridge disposal matters, but a student cannot now "choose CFC-free aerosols" as an everyday decision. Keep CFC as a cause in exam answers and describe it in the past tense for aerosols.
- Phosphates: EU rules now limit phosphorus in household detergents, so detergents are no longer the main source. Schemes (2019O, 2013O, 2004O) still credit "phosphate-free detergent" and "washing powders" as an answer, so mention it once, as historical, beside fertiliser, slurry and sewage as the main sources.
- Eutrophication is caused by nitrates and phosphates (nutrients), not by toxic chemicals.
- Boundaries: renewable resources and fossil-fuel depletion belong to the resources subsection; recycling, packaging, the Green Dot and WEEE detail belong to the waste subsection; heating, appliance and shopping habits belong to the household-practices subsection; electrical safety of fuel appliances is in the appliances and home services chapters.
- Do not use: "restrictions on the sale of smoky coal in urban areas", because the ban on sale now applies nationwide (S.I. 529 of 2022).
- Do not use: "particulates and carbon monoxide cause asthma and respiratory illness", because carbon monoxide poisons by blocking oxygen carriage in the blood; give it separately.
- Do not use: a pH of 4.1 for acid rain (2018H scheme) or any pH, decibel level or tonnage figure, because they are changing or unsourced figures; the 10 dB = ten times rule is fine.
- Do not use: "avoid products containing CFCs" as a current consumer choice, because CFC aerosols and appliances have been phased out for decades (see above).

## 4. What exams ask

- Name types of pollution and give an example, cause or effect of each (2026O, 2017O, 2011O, 2014O). Expected: water, air, noise; for example slurry or sewage, carbon dioxide or smoke, traffic. Tip: give one cause and one effect per type; do not mix them.
- Air pollution: a cause, its effect and how it can be reduced (2016H); two consumer measures (2023H); emissions from burning fuels in the home and their effects (2018H, 2023H, 2004H: carbon dioxide and global warming; sulphur dioxide and acid rain; smoke, smog; ozone). Tip: name the gas, its effect and one reduction measure.
- Three ways to reduce pollution (2019O, 2023O, 2013O): public transport, electric cars, smokeless fuel or renewables, efficient appliances, reuse and recycle, careful use of chemicals and detergents.
- Types, causes and effects of two types of pollution (2004O, 20 marks; two causes and two effects each).
- One government initiative against pollution (2014O, 2004O): name it for three marks and give two points, for example the Waste Management Act 1996 or the solid fuel regulations.
