# Package: hom-home-services-s7 — Ventilation

- **loId:** `hom-home-services-s7` · **chapterId:** `hom-home-services` · **syllabusRef:** 4.5.5 (Elective 1)
- **Syllabus scope (outline):** "Ventilation [4.5.5]: underlying principles and their application; natural and artificial methods." Settings: evaluating ventilation in the home economics room or a room at home. Also examined: 2025H, 2024O, 2022H, 2017H, 2012H, 2004H; condensation (2015H). No [HL] marks.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. Ventilation is asked almost every year in four parts: why it is needed (importance), the principle, a natural method, an artificial method (nearly always the extractor fan or cooker hood for the kitchen), plus the effects of poor ventilation and condensation.

## 1. Must cover

Why ventilate
- [ ] V1 Ventilation is the continuous replacement of stale air with fresh air, without causing a draught or lowering the temperature much.
- [ ] V2 Importance (list for 3-4 marks each): provides fresh air (oxygen) for breathing; removes stale air, carbon dioxide and impurities; removes smells and cooking fumes; controls humidity; controls temperature; reduces condensation, mould and damp; supplies air for combustion in open fires, stoves and gas or oil appliances; helps prevent infection spread and respiratory problems such as asthma.
- [ ] V3 Why modern houses need more care: insulated, airtight construction, double glazing, tight doors and windows, and sealed fireplaces cut natural air flow, so artificial methods are often needed; building regulations (Part F, ventilation) require adequate ventilation in new homes.

Effects of poor ventilation
- [ ] E1 Stale, stuffy air with lingering smells; high humidity and condensation on cold surfaces (windows, corners, outside walls).
- [ ] E2 Damp, mould and mildew, peeling paint and wallpaper, rotting wood, rusting metal, musty smells: damage to the structure; wet insulation insulates less.
- [ ] E3 Health: worse asthma, bronchitis and allergies; headaches and tiredness from stale air with more carbon dioxide and less oxygen.
- [ ] E4 Combustion appliances starved of air burn incompletely, which can give carbon monoxide.
- [ ] E5 Kitchens and bathrooms: steam, heat, grease and smells collect; overheating; slippery floors.

Principles
- [ ] PR1 Convection: warm stale air is lighter and rises to high outlets; cooler fresh air enters through low openings to replace it; this circulation exchanges air.
- [ ] PR2 Suction (pressure difference): a fan lowers the air pressure inside the room so stale air is drawn out; fresh air enters through gaps and vents to replace it.
- [ ] PR3 Wind and cross-ventilation: wind moving across the outside of a building creates pressure differences, so air enters on the windward side and leaves on the opposite side; windows on opposite sides give the greatest air change.
- [ ] PR4 Appliances that burn fuel need an air supply and a flue (chimney) to carry waste gases up and out by convection.

Natural methods
- [ ] N1 Windows and doors: the simplest method; window open at top and bottom gives convection; opposite windows give cross-ventilation.
- [ ] N2 Air bricks and wall vents (and older types such as Coopers discs): openings in outside walls; high and low outlets give exchange; must never be blocked.
- [ ] N3 Trickle vents: small adjustable slots in window frames giving a slow continuous airflow.
- [ ] N4 Chimneys, fireplaces and flues: warm gases rise and draw air through the room.
- [ ] N5 Limits: natural ventilation depends on the weather, wind and season, so the air change is not constant and can cause draughts and heat loss.
- [ ] N6 Diagram: a room with a high vent letting warm stale air out and a low air brick letting fresh air in.

Artificial methods
- [ ] A1 Extractor fan: placed high on an outside wall, in a ceiling or a window (kitchen, bathroom, toilet); an electric motor turns the blades; the shutters open when it is switched on and close when it is off; the fan creates suction and draws out stale air, steam and smells; fresh air flows in through gaps, under doors or vents. Controlled by switch, pull cord, timer or humidity sensor.
- [ ] A2 Cooker hood: a motor and fan above the cooker draw air through grease filters. Ducted: the air is carried outside through a duct. Ductless (recirculating): filters trap grease and fumes and the cleaned air is returned to the room (does not remove moisture or heat). Filters need cleaning or replacing.
- [ ] A3 Mechanical ventilation system: ducts in ceiling or floor, fans supply fresh air and extract stale air; heat-recovery versions warm the incoming air using heat from the outgoing air; suits airtight houses. Air conditioning also controls humidity and temperature.
- [ ] A4 Diagram: extractor fan in an outside wall with shutters, and a ducted cooker hood with its duct to the outside.
- [ ] A5 Recommendation for a kitchen: an extractor fan or ducted cooker hood plus a window or trickle vent; give the principle of suction and the replacement of air.

Condensation
- [ ] C1 Warm air holds more water vapour than cold air; when warm, moist air meets a cold surface it cools and the vapour condenses to liquid water.
- [ ] C2 Sources of moisture: cooking, kettles, bathing and showers, drying clothes indoors, breathing, unflued gas or paraffin heaters.
- [ ] C3 Effects: damp, mould, peeling paint and wallpaper, rust, rotting wood, musty smells, damp insulation which loses its effect, respiratory problems.
- [ ] C4 Prevention: ventilate (extractor fans, trickle vents, opening windows); lids on pots; dry clothes outside or in a vented room; keep the heating steady; insulation and double glazing keep surfaces warm; wipe condensation; keep furniture away from walls.

Practical context
- [ ] X1 Evaluate a room: smells, stuffiness, condensation on windows, mould in corners; check vents, air bricks and fans are present, working and not blocked; recommend an extractor fan or hood, trickle vents, keeping kitchen door closed while cooking, and moving drying indoors to a ventilated space.

## 3. Traps

- Ventilation is not the same as a draught: ventilation is controlled; a draught is uncontrolled cold air that wastes heat.
- Poor ventilation does not itself make carbon monoxide; a lack of air makes combustion incomplete, which does. Carbon monoxide's chemistry is in the emissions subsection.
- Condensation is not caused by "damp walls"; it is water vapour condensing on a cold surface. The prevention is a warm surface, less moisture, and ventilation.
- Insulation and ventilation work together: a well-insulated airtight house needs ventilation. The insulation subsection covers the methods.
- A ductless cooker hood recirculates and does not remove steam; a ducted hood or extractor fan does.
- State the principle for the method named; naming a fan alone does not earn the principle marks.
- Heating principles belong to the heating subsection; the kitchen layout and appliances to other chapters.
- Do not use: "air conditioning" as a standard method for an Irish kitchen (2011 scheme lists it as an alternative), because it is unusual in Irish homes; mention as a one-clause alternative only.
- Do not use: "Coopers disc" as a description of how a vent works, because the schemes only name it and the design is not confirmed here; use "wall vent".
- Do not use: carbon dioxide or carbon monoxide "build-up" in a kitchen as the main effect of poor ventilation, because the main marked effects are condensation, damp, smells and stale air; give gases as a secondary effect.

## 4. What exams ask

- Importance of adequate ventilation (2004H, 2007O, 2012H, 2017H, 2024O, 2024H): three points at 3-5 marks: fresh air, removes stale air and smells, controls humidity and temperature, reduces condensation, aids combustion.
- Effects of poor or inadequate ventilation (2022H, 2025H): stuffy air, condensation, mould, damp, smells, gas build-up, asthma, overheating and slippery floors in a kitchen.
- Underlying principle of ventilation (2025H): convection or thermal expansion, or suction for a fan.
- Natural method and artificial method with principle (2004H, 2007O, 2017H, 2024O, 2012H, 2022H): name (3-4 marks) and explain the principle (extractor fan, ducted or ductless cooker hood, mechanical system; natural: windows, air bricks, vents, flues).
- Why artificial methods are needed in modern homes (2011O): airtight and insulated construction, double glazing, tight doors; to control humidity, remove smells, prevent condensation and mould.
- Condensation: effects and prevention (2015H): three points: mould on paint and wallpaper, rusting, rotting wood, damp insulation, musty smells, respiratory problems; reduce by ventilation, extractor fans, heating, insulation and lids on pots.
- Practical: evaluate the ventilation of a room and recommend improvements.
