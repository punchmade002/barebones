# Package: hom-family-caring-s3 — Older people in the family

- **loId:** `hom-family-caring-s3` · **chapterId:** `hom-family-caring` · **syllabusRef:** 3.1.6
- **Syllabus scope:** the importance of independence for the older person; generation conflict and how to deal with it. Settings: investigating accommodation options for older family members, with advantages and disadvantages; role-play and case studies on generation conflict. Also examined: supports for older people (2024H, 2013O); roles of grandparents (2025H, 2018O, 2014H; also 2026O, 2006O). No [HL] marks in this subsection.
- **Prepared by:** Sonnet-class model, 2026-11-23. Common to Ordinary and Higher Level. Adolescent-parent conflict and communication are in the previous subsection; changes in family structure and a longer life expectancy as a social trend belong to the family-structure chapters.

## 1. Must cover

Importance of independence (one block)
- [ ] I1 Independence means an older person keeps control over daily decisions and activities. Reasons it matters: dignity and self-esteem; physical health (activity keeps strength, mobility and balance); mental health (choice and purpose reduce depression and loneliness); an independent person needs less care, so the load on family is lighter; staying in their own home keeps routines, friends and community ties.
- [ ] I2 Independence is not the same as being alone: it means choosing, with support where needed (assumed distinction, one line).

Ways older people keep their independence (one block; Also examined 2026O, 2018O)
- [ ] W1 Adapting the home: handrails, ramps, non-slip flooring, good lighting, level-access shower, stairlift or downstairs bedroom. Local-authority housing adaptation grants help with the cost (no amounts).
- [ ] W2 Housing choices: sheltered housing (self-contained units with a warden or manager and shared facilities); single-storey home or bungalow; granny flat; downsizing (lower upkeep and running costs).
- [ ] W3 Social contact: active retirement groups, community and voluntary groups, visits from neighbours and relatives, which prevent isolation.
- [ ] W4 Personal alarm or alert system to summon help in an emergency while living alone.
- [ ] W5 Help with meals and tasks: Meals on Wheels and similar community meal services, home help (see S1).

Supports for older people (one block; Also examined 2024H, 2013O, 2006O)
- [ ] S1 Home support: the HSE's Home Support Service (formerly "home help") gives help with personal care and everyday tasks for people living at home; public health nurses visit at home and advise on care; home care packages exist for higher needs.
- [ ] S2 Financial: the State pension (contributory, or non-contributory and means-tested), and other social welfare supports; no amounts or ages.
- [ ] S3 Health and travel: medical card (free GP visits and subsidised prescribed medicines, with a small charge per item, for those who qualify; granted on income (means test) or on medical need or hardship) and the Free Travel scheme (Travel Pass) for older people.
- [ ] S4 Voluntary and community: Meals on Wheels groups, Age Action, ALONE, active retirement groups, and Active Retirement Ireland; family, neighbours and friends.
- [ ] S5 How each support maintains independence: say what it does and the independence it protects (the exam asks for the link, e.g. a pension gives financial independence, travel pass keeps social life, home support allows staying at home).

Accommodation options, advantages and disadvantages (one block; Settings)
- [ ] A1 Own home (with adaptations and supports): familiar, independent, community ties; but upkeep, isolation, adaptation costs.
- [ ] A2 Living with a family member: companionship and close support; but loss of privacy, possible conflict, growing dependence, strain on the carer.
- [ ] A3 Granny flat or extension: privacy with family nearby; but building cost and shared-space tensions.
- [ ] A4 Sheltered housing: self-contained, security, neighbours, a warden; but communal rules, limited choice of location.
- [ ] A5 Residential or nursing home care: round-the-clock nursing and personal care for high needs; but loss of home and independence, and cost; the Nursing Homes Support Scheme ("Fair Deal", run by the HSE) helps pay for long-term care, with the resident contributing according to income and assets.
- [ ] A6 The choice depends on health, mobility, finances, family support and above all the older person's own wishes.

Generation conflict (one block; Also examined 2018O)
- [ ] C1 Definition: generation conflict is disagreement between members of different generations because their attitudes, values and experiences differ.
- [ ] C2 Causes: parents and adolescents over curfews, friends, technology; adults and their parents over care, living arrangements, spending, the older person's role in the household; older people judging the young by their own youth; changing values (marriage, religion, lifestyle); loss of independence by the older person leading to decisions being made for them.
- [ ] C3 Dealing with it: good communication, listening and avoiding confrontation; clear expectations and boundaries; patience and understanding that values change; respect for experience on both sides; compromise and negotiation (visiting times, care arrangements); acting early; family meetings. Role-play (Settings): a case study of an older person who resists moving into a granny flat.

Roles and responsibilities of grandparents (one block; Also examined 2025H, 2026O, 2018O, 2014H, 2006O)
- [ ] G1 Roles: childcare (school collection, minding while parents work, reducing costs); emotional support; passing on values, traditions, culture and family history; practical help (homework, meals, maintenance); financial help (e.g. a house deposit); advice and role-model.
- [ ] G2 Responsibilities: respecting parents' rules and not undermining their decisions; honest communication; the family's duty in return to provide transport, care, company and money support when grandparents need it.
- [ ] G3 Living with grandparents: advantages (time with grandchildren, relaxed relationships since they do not discipline, practical help) and disadvantages (loss of privacy, differing child-rearing views, extra caring burden as they age) (2006O).

Investigation: accommodation options for an older relative (Settings) as a method in one bullet.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "Community meal services provide meals at reduced rates", because Meals on Wheels is run by local voluntary groups who charge their own fee or none, so "reduced" is not confirmed; say meals delivered to the home.
- Do not use: "granny flat ... the need for planning permission" as a general disadvantage, because a granny flat for a family member is often exempt from planning permission under the planning regulations; if mentioned, say planning rules should be checked and cost is the disadvantage.
- Do not use: "Home help ... provided through the HSE" as the only name; the current service is the Home Support Service. Say "home support (home help)" so either exam word is covered.
- Independence is the key theme: every support or accommodation point should say what it does for independence.
- Generation conflict is between generations (adolescent-parent, adult-grandparent); do not use conflict between partners (role conflict, previous subsection).
- Adolescent-parent conflict strategies are taught in the previous subsection; here give generation-conflict ones with older people.
- Wills and succession (the 2006 paper asked about making a will) belong to the succession/family law material, not here.
- Pensions, welfare and income: explain what they do; no amounts.
- Do not teach the HSE's full services list or ageing statistics.

## 4. What exams ask

- Describe three ways older people can maintain independence (2026O, 2018O): adapting the home, sheltered housing or granny flat, active retirement groups, home support, meals, personal alarm; each point developed in a sentence or two.
- Discuss roles and responsibilities of older people/grandparents in the family (2026O, 2025H, 2018O, 2014H): childcare, financial help, emotional support, passing on values, advice; with some responsibilities.
- Supports available to older people and how they help independence (2024H, 2013O): choose two, name and say how each helps.
- Explain generation conflict and outline two ways to deal with it (2018O): definition, causes, communication, boundaries, patience, compromise.
- Advantages and disadvantages of grandparents living with a family or of accommodation options (2006O).
- Tips: pair each support with its effect on independence; give both an advantage and a disadvantage when asked to discuss accommodation.
