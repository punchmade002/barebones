# Package: hom-lipids-s2 — Triglycerides and the classification of lipids

- **loId:** `hom-lipids-s2` · **chapterId:** `hom-lipids` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.4
- **Syllabus scope:** **[HL]** chemical composition and molecular structure of a triglyceride; classification of lipids according to source and proportion of saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids.
  Also examined (from past papers): elements in lipids (2004–2023O true/false, 2008O), lipids are fatty acids plus glycerol (2017O), digestion of lipids to fatty acids and glycerol (2009O, 2005H, 2026O).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The fatty acids themselves are in the previous subsection ("Fatty acids"); food sources in detail are in "Sources"; digestion and absorption of triglycerides is in the digestion chapter. Higher and Ordinary Level except the [HL] items.

## 1. Must cover

What lipids are (one block)
- [ ] L1 (assumed) Lipids are the fats and oils of the diet. Fats are solid at room temperature, oils are liquid. Most dietary lipid is in the form of triglycerides.
- [ ] L2 Elements: lipids (fats and oils) are made of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen only; they contain no nitrogen (so, unlike protein, they are not a source of nitrogen). Asked as a list (2008O, 3 marks) and as true/false (2023O, 2017O, 2014O).
- [ ] L3 Lipids are made up of fatty acids and glycerol (2017O true/false).

Triglyceride: composition and structure [HL] (two blocks)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Composition (2018H, 9 marks with elements): a triglyceride has one molecule of glycerol and three molecules of fatty acid; elements C, H and O.
- [ ] T2 [HL] Glycerol is an alcohol with three hydroxyl (–OH) groups, one on each of its three carbon atoms (formula C₃H₈O₃).
- [ ] T3 [HL] Each fatty acid joins to one –OH group of glycerol by an ester bond (–COO–), formed by condensation, in which a molecule of water is removed. Three fatty acids, so three molecules of water are released. The result is "tri-glyceride" (tri = three).
- [ ] T4 [HL] The three fatty acids may be the same or different, and may be saturated, mono- or polyunsaturated; this mixture decides whether the fat is solid or liquid.
- [ ] T5 [HL] Diagram: glycerol as a three-carbon backbone with three fatty acid chains attached by ester links; label glycerol, fatty acid chains, ester bonds, and show 3H₂O released. (Diagram cue from NOTES_STANDARD: triglyceride.)
- [ ] T6 Reverse: in digestion the enzyme lipase hydrolyses triglycerides to fatty acids and glycerol (asked as a fill-in at Ordinary Level 2009O, 2026O; the pancreas secretes pancreatic juice containing lipase, 2005H). State in one line only; detail belongs elsewhere.
- [ ] T7 (assumed) Other lipids exist (phospholipids such as lecithin, and sterols such as cholesterol), but the syllabus takes triglycerides; one line naming them is enough.

Classification by source (one block)
- [ ] CS1 Animal lipids (examples: butter, cream, cheese, milk fat, meat fat, lard, suet, dripping, egg yolk).
- [ ] CS2 Vegetable or plant lipids (olive oil, sunflower oil, corn oil, soya oil, rapeseed oil, nuts, avocado, soya beans, cereals, margarine and spreads made from plant oils).
- [ ] CS3 Marine lipids (oily fish such as salmon, mackerel, herring, sardines, trout; fish-liver oil; fish oils; 2013O matches sardines to marine, 2018O salmon, 2008O accepts tuna).
- [ ] CS4 Three classes, asked as "classification" in 2021O, 2016O and 2008O. Matching items name a food and ask animal, plant or marine.

Classification by fatty acid content (one block)
- [ ] CF1 A lipid is classed as saturated, monounsaturated or polyunsaturated according to the type of fatty acid in the largest proportion. Every fat and oil is a mixture of all three types.
- [ ] CF2 Typically saturated: butter, lard, suet, dripping, fat on meat, cheese, coconut oil, palm oil. Typically monounsaturated: olive oil, rapeseed oil, avocado, many nuts. Typically polyunsaturated: sunflower oil, corn oil, soya oil, oily fish oils.
- [ ] CF3 Fatty-acid type explains the state: mostly saturated gives a fat solid at room temperature; mostly unsaturated gives an oil.
- [ ] CF4 Exam model answer for "classification": two lines, source (animal, plant, marine) and fatty-acid type (saturated, monounsaturated, polyunsaturated).

## 3. Traps

- Saturated is not the same as animal and unsaturated is not the same as plant: the 2006O scheme loosely pairs "saturated (animal)" and "unsaturated (vegetable)", but coconut and palm oils are plant sources high in saturated fat, and oily fish oil (marine) is polyunsaturated. Teach the two classifications as separate.
- Every fat is a mixture: butter has monounsaturated fat too; olive oil also has saturated fat. "Classed as" means predominant type.
- "Lipids contain no nitrogen" is the examined answer for fats and oils. The triglyceride has C, H and O only.
- Glycerol is not a fatty acid. The triglyceride has one glycerol, not three.
- Condensation removes water; hydrolysis (in digestion and hydrolytic rancidity) adds water. Hydrolytic rancidity is in "Properties", digestion elsewhere.
- Fatty acid classes and cis/trans are in the previous subsection; sources in depth, including HL distribution of fatty acid types, are in "Sources".
- Do not use: "Lipids are composed of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms only" as a universal rule for every lipid, because phospholipids contain phosphorus and nitrogen. Write "fats and oils (triglycerides) contain only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen".
- Do not use: a named fraction or percentage of saturated, mono- or polyunsaturated fat for any food, because the proportions vary with the product and source and none is checked here.

## 4. What exams ask

- Elements in lipids: carbon, hydrogen, oxygen (3 marks, 2008O) and true/false "lipids contain nitrogen" (false) appear nearly every second year at Ordinary Level.
- Triglyceride structure at Higher Level (2018H, 9 marks): composition (elements, 3 marks) and structure (3 points at 2 marks): three fatty acids, one glycerol, glycerol an alcohol with three –OH groups, each fatty acid joined by an ester link with loss of water. Draw and label if a diagram is asked.
- Classification (2021O, 2016O, 28-32 mark account question headed classification, functions, dietary sources): animal, plant, marine; saturated, monounsaturated, polyunsaturated; one or two examples each.
- Matching and listing questions: sort foods (avocado, sardines, cheese; salmon, olive oil, butter) into animal, plant or marine; list three sources under saturated and unsaturated headings.
- Digestion fill-in (2009O, 2026O): lipase converts lipids to fatty acids and glycerol. 2005H asks gland, secretion, enzyme and change: pancreas, pancreatic juice, lipase, fatty acids and glycerol.
- Expect these items to be combined with functions, sources and properties in the long Ordinary Level "give an account of lipids" question (28-32 marks); classification earns about 7-8 marks of it.
