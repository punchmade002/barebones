# Package: hom-appliances-equipment-s4 — An appliance with a heating element

- **loId:** `hom-appliances-equipment-s4` · **chapterId:** `hom-appliances-equipment` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1.5
- **Syllabus scope:** "an appliance with a heating element: guidelines for use, and [HL] underlying working principles." Also examined: 2022H (construction, working principle, guidelines for use), 2004H (working principle, guidelines for using the appliance, energy efficiency), 2022O (study of a small electrical kitchen appliance: type, guidelines for use, care and cleaning), 2015O (small appliance), 2016O (element or motor). Higher Level only: the underlying working principles.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-11-23. The marking schemes use the electric kettle as the example; the package also covers the toaster and iron in shorter form. The kettle is the one to teach in depth. The Ordinary Level "working principle" is that the element turns electrical energy into heat; the resistance explanation and the thermostat mechanism are the [HL] material.

## 1. Must cover

What a heating-element appliance does (one block, lead)
- [ ] A1 A heating element is a coil or strip of high-resistance wire that gets hot when current flows through it; electrical energy is converted into heat energy. Examples: kettle, toaster, iron, electric grill, immersion heater, hairdryer (element plus fan motor), oven.
- [ ] A2 (2016O) The kettle and the toaster have an element, not a motor.
- [ ] A3 Heat reaches the food or water by conduction (through metal in contact), convection (currents in water or air) and radiation (infrared from a toaster or grill element).

Construction of an electric kettle (one block, 2022H)
- [ ] K1 Body of plastic, stainless steel or glass (cool-touch or insulated); lid, spout with filter or limescale filter; insulated handle; water-level indicator with minimum and maximum marks.
- [ ] K2 Heating element, usually in the base (concealed or flat plate, easier to clean than an exposed coil); thermostat or steam switch that cuts the power when the water boils; automatic switch-off and boil-dry protection; on/off switch with light.
- [ ] K3 Cordless (cord-free) kettle: the kettle sits on a base unit that carries the flex, which reduces the risk of a trailing flex; the 360-degree base lets it be placed either way.
- [ ] K4 Plug with a fuse (13 A for a kettle) and an earth wire, unless the appliance is double insulated.
- [ ] K5 Diagram: a labelled kettle in section showing element, thermostat or steam switch, spout, level scale, handle, base and flex.
- [ ] K6 Toaster (short): nichrome elements on either side of the slots, lever with a spring and timer or bimetallic release, crumb tray, browning control. Iron (short): sole plate heated by the element, thermostat dial, water tank and steam vents (steam iron), heel rest, cord guard.

Guidelines for use and care (one block)
- [ ] G1 Kettle use: read the instructions; fill between the minimum and maximum marks (the element must be covered, and overfilling can spurt boiling water); close the lid; switch off and unplug to fill if the kettle is not cordless; do not run it empty; boil only the amount needed.
- [ ] G2 Kettle safety: use dry hands; never immerse the kettle base or plug in water; keep the flex away from hot surfaces, out of reach of children and not trailing over a worktop edge; do not overload the socket; do not use with damaged flex or plug; keep away from the edge of the worktop; handle the hot kettle by the handle; beware of steam from the spout.
- [ ] G3 Kettle care and cleaning: unplug and let it cool; wipe the outside with a damp cloth; descale regularly (a descaling solution or a vinegar or citric acid solution, rinsed well); wash the filter; do not use abrasives on the element.
- [ ] G4 Toaster guidelines: never put a knife or metal into the slots; unplug before removing stuck bread; empty the crumb tray regularly; do not use under a cupboard; keep away from curtains.
- [ ] G5 Iron guidelines: choose the temperature for the fabric (lowest for delicate fabrics and synthetics, ironing the least heat-resistant garments first); stand on the heel rest; never leave switched on and unattended; unplug before filling the tank; keep the flex clear of the sole plate; store only when cool.
- [ ] G6 Electrical safety features: a fuse in the plug, an earth wire or double insulation, a thermostat, a thermal cut-out, an RCD in the fuse board (cuts the supply quickly if current leaks to earth).

Energy efficiency (one block, 2004H)
- [ ] E1 Boil only the water needed; descale to keep the element efficient (scale insulates the element, so the kettle takes longer and uses more energy); keep the lid closed; choose a correctly sized kettle with a concealed element and automatic switch-off; do not leave the toaster or iron on.
- [ ] E2 (assumed) Energy in kWh = power in kW x time in hours; a 2 kW kettle used for 0.1 hour uses 0.2 kWh. Heating appliances have high power, so they use more energy than motor appliances when on.
- [ ] E3 A kettle is more efficient than boiling water in a saucepan on a hob because less heat escapes; a microwave can also heat small amounts of water but is not interchangeable with a kettle (cross-reference by name only).

Underlying working principles (Higher Level only; one block, heading ends "(Higher Level only)")
- [ ] H1 [HL] The heating effect of a current: when current flows through a resistance, electrical energy is converted to heat; the power converted is P = I squared times R (also P = V times I).
- [ ] H2 [HL] The element is made of nichrome (a nickel-chromium alloy) because it has high resistance, a high melting point and does not oxidise quickly when hot. Resistance rises with the length of wire and falls as it gets thicker.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Thermostat: a bimetallic strip made of two metals that expand by different amounts. When it heats, it bends and breaks the circuit at the set temperature; when it cools, it straightens and remakes the circuit. In a kettle, steam from the boiling water can trigger a bimetallic strip to switch the kettle off.
- [ ] H4 [HL] The thermostat is connected in series with the element so that opening it cuts the current to the element.
- [ ] H5 [HL] Diagram: a circuit with the supply, bimetallic thermostat in series, and the element in the water, with arrows for the direction of heat transfer.

## 3. Traps

- Do not use: the wattage of a "typical" kettle as a fact, because power varies by model; use the 2 kW only as a labelled example in a calculation.
- Do not use: a figure for how much energy descaling saves, because none is checked.
- Do not use: the claim that a toaster uses a bimetallic strip in every model, because some use a timer or electronic control; say "a timer or bimetallic release".
- Do not mix the heating element with a motor: a kettle and a toaster have no motor. A hairdryer has both (element and fan motor).
- The working principle of the thermostat and the resistance explanation are [HL] and must be in a separate block marked "(Higher Level only)"; at Ordinary Level say only that the element changes electricity into heat and the thermostat switches the kettle off at the right temperature.
- A refrigerator's thermostat and a kettle's thermostat do different jobs (one switches a motor, the other an element); do not blur them. The refrigeration subsection has the refrigerator thermostat.
- The microwave is not a heating-element appliance: it has no element; do not describe it here.
- Cooking hobs, cooker rings and ovens are heated by elements, but this subsection studies one small appliance; do not write a cooker study.
- General selection criteria and the energy label belong to the selecting-appliances subsection; electrical wiring of a plug and ring circuit belong to the Home Design elective.

## 4. What exams ask

- 2022H (Higher, study of an appliance with a heating element): construction (named parts of a kettle), working principle (current meets resistance in the element and produces heat; conduction and convection heat the water; the thermostat switches off at boiling point), guidelines for use. Name the appliance first, then the parts.
- 2004H (Higher, 30 marks): working principle (electrical energy converted to heat in the element: two points), three guidelines (about 3 marks each), energy efficiency (one point).
- 2022O (Ordinary): a small electrical kitchen appliance (kettle): type, guidelines for use and care and cleaning; four or so guidelines with reasons.
- 2016O: tick element or motor.
- Tips to build: name the appliance and the parts; state each guideline with its reason (dry hands because of electric shock); add descaling and unplugging; for Higher, name nichrome and the bimetallic strip.
