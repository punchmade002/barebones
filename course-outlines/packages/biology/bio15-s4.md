# Package: bio15-s4 — Second-stage process

- **loId:** `bio15-s4` · **chapterId:** `bio15` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2.4
- **Syllabus (Higher Level only):** "second-stage process: pyruvate is broken down to produce
  AcetylCoA and one molecule of carbon dioxide. AcetylCoA enters a series called citric acid
  cycle resulting in the production of CO2 and H2O. Electrons from the cycle transferred
  through an electron transport chain, energy from this transfer is used in the production of
  ATP molecules which can be used for other biochemical reactions within the cells." Outcome

## 1. Must cover

Pyruvate to acetyl CoA (one block, process; the link step)
- [ ] P1 When oxygen is present, each pyruvate (C₃) made in glycolysis moves from the cytoplasm into the matrix of a mitochondrion.
- [ ] P2 In the matrix, pyruvate is broken down: one carbon atom is removed as one molecule of carbon dioxide, and hydrogen is removed and picked up by NAD⁺, forming NADH.
- [ ] P3 The remaining two-carbon (acetyl) group is joined to coenzyme A, a carrier molecule, forming acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl CoA, C₂). Per pyruvate: pyruvate + coenzyme A + NAD⁺ → acetyl CoA + CO₂ + NADH. Since each glucose gives two pyruvate, this happens twice per glucose (2 CO₂, 2 NADH).

The citric acid cycle (one block, process; numbered stages)
- [ ] K1 The citric acid cycle (also called the Krebs cycle) is a cycle of enzyme-controlled reactions in the matrix of the mitochondrion.
- [ ] K2 1. Acetyl CoA passes its two-carbon acetyl group into the cycle, where it joins a four-carbon compound to form citric acid (C₆), which gives the cycle its name. Coenzyme A is released to collect another acetyl group.
- [ ] K3 2. In a series of steps, citric acid is broken down back to the four-carbon compound, which is regenerated to accept the next acetyl group; that is why it is a cycle.
- [ ] K4 3. During each turn, two carbon atoms are removed as two molecules of CO₂, so the carbon of the acetyl group leaves as CO₂.
- [ ] K5 4. Hydrogen (protons and electrons) is removed at several steps and loaded onto NAD⁺, forming NADH; a small amount of ATP (one per turn) is also made directly.
- [ ] K6 The cycle turns twice for each glucose (once per acetyl CoA). With the link step, all six carbon atoms of glucose have now left as six molecules of CO₂, matching the 6CO₂ in the respiration equation (glycolysis releases none).
- [ ] K7 Products of the cycle, as the specification puts it, "CO₂ and H₂O": the CO₂ is released directly; the hydrogen removed is carried by NADH to the electron transport chain, where it ends up in water (E4). The cycle doesn't need oxygen directly, but stops without it, because NADH can't be unloaded and NAD⁺ runs out.

The electron transport chain (one block, process; numbered stages)
- [ ] E1 The electron transport chain is a series of electron carriers (protein complexes and mobile carriers) in the inner mitochondrial membrane, especially on the folds called cristae.
- [ ] E2 1. NADH from glycolysis, the link step and the citric acid cycle delivers its high-energy electrons (and protons) to the start of the chain. NAD⁺ is released to collect more.
- [ ] E3 2. The electrons are passed from carrier to carrier, each at a lower energy level. The energy released at each transfer is used to make ATP from ADP and Pi (the mechanism, via a proton gradient and ATP synthase, is in "The mitochondrion's internal structure and ATP synthase"). Most of the ATP from aerobic respiration is made here.
- [ ] E4 3. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor: at the end of the chain, low-energy electrons combine with oxygen and protons (H⁺) to form water: 4e⁻ + 4H⁺ + O₂ → 2H₂O. This is the only step of respiration that uses oxygen directly, and it is where the 6H₂O of the equation comes from.
- [ ] E5 4. If oxygen is absent, electrons can't leave the end of the chain, the whole chain stops, NADH can't be unloaded, and the citric acid cycle and link step stop too; the cell can only carry out glycolysis and fermentation (see "First-stage process"). Cyanide is a poison because it blocks the last complex of the chain, with the same effect.
- [ ] E6 5. The ATP made is exported from the mitochondrion to the cytoplasm and used for other biochemical reactions in the cell, such as synthesis of proteins, active transport and muscle contraction (uses in "ATP").
- [ ] E7 Energy yield: far more ATP per glucose is made in the second stage than the net 2 ATP of glycolysis; aerobic respiration releases many times more energy per glucose than fermentation. Don't give a total.

Modelling the two-stage process (one block; the model the outcome asks for)
- [ ] M1 Stage 1, glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate; ATP made directly (net 2); NAD⁺ loaded to NADH.
- [ ] M2 Stage 2 (mitochondrion): link step and citric acid cycle in the matrix: pyruvate → acetyl CoA → CO₂; NAD⁺ loaded to NADH; a little ATP made directly. Electron transport chain on the inner membrane (cristae): NADH unloaded to NAD⁺; electrons pass to oxygen, forming water; most ATP made (ADP + Pi → ATP).
- [ ] M3 Transfer molecules: NAD⁺/NADH carries electrons and hydrogen from the breakdown of glucose (glycolysis, link step, cycle) to the electron transport chain, and returns empty; coenzyme A carries the acetyl group into the cycle and returns; ATP carries energy from the mitochondrion (and cytoplasm) to the cell's activities, returning as ADP + Pi to be reloaded. All are recycled, so only small amounts are needed.
- [ ] M4 Tracing the atoms: the carbon of glucose leaves as CO₂ (link step and cycle); the hydrogen of glucose ends up in water, combined with oxygen breathed in (electron transport chain). The oxygen breathed in therefore ends up in water, not in CO₂.
- [ ] M5 Diagram: the two-stage model. Draw a cell with the cytoplasm and one mitochondrion (outer membrane, inner membrane with cristae, matrix). Cytoplasm: glucose → 2 pyruvate (net 2 ATP; NAD⁺ → NADH). Arrow of pyruvate into the matrix → acetyl CoA (+ CO₂) → into the citric acid cycle drawn as a circle (acetyl group + C₄ → citric acid C₆ → back to C₄), with CO₂ leaving and NAD⁺ → NADH. Arrows of NADH to the electron transport chain on the inner membrane; electrons along the chain to O₂ + H⁺ → H₂O; ATP made; NAD⁺ returning. Label every transfer molecule with what it carries.
- [ ] M6 Comparison with photosynthesis's two-stage model (one bullet, paired): in photosynthesis, NADPH and ATP are made in stage 1 (light energy) and used in stage 2 to build glucose; in respiration, NADH is made as glucose is broken down and used at the end of stage 2 to make ATP. The membrane comparison is in "The mitochondrion's internal structure and ATP synthase".

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading. Key terms (acetyl CoA, coenzyme A, citric acid cycle, Krebs cycle, matrix, electron transport chain, final electron acceptor) are Higher Level.
- Specification wording: it says the citric acid cycle results "in the production of CO₂ and H₂O". The cycle itself releases CO₂ and hydrogen (carried by NADH); the water is formed at the end of the electron transport chain. Teach K7, which keeps the specification's wording but says where the water forms; don't write that water is made in the cycle itself.
- Pyruvate (C₃) gives acetyl CoA (C₂) and one CO₂ per pyruvate; two of each per glucose. Students often say "acetyl CoA is C₃" or give one CO₂ per glucose.
- The link step and the cycle are in the matrix; the electron transport chain is on the inner membrane (cristae). Don't put the cycle "on the cristae".
- Oxygen is used only at the end of the chain; it is not used in the citric acid cycle, and it doesn't become CO₂ (M4).
- Electrons lose energy along the chain; ATP isn't "made from electrons".
- FAD and FADH₂ are not on the specification; don't mention them, even though textbooks do.
- The proton gradient, ATP synthase and the comparison with the thylakoid membrane belong to "The mitochondrion's internal structure and ATP synthase"; E3 names ATP synthase in one clause at most.
- Glycolysis and fermentation detail belong to "First-stage process"; M1 summarises in one bullet for the model.
- No other diagram cues beyond M5.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the 3-carbon molecule that enters the mitochondrion and the two carbon-containing molecules formed from it (acetyl CoA and CO₂).
- Name the cycle that acetyl CoA enters and where it takes place; name three of its products (CO₂, NADH, ATP).
- Outline the events of the electron transport chain; name the final electron acceptor.
- State what happens to the products of the cycle (CO₂ released; NADH to the chain; ATP used in the cell).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Describe what happens to pyruvate when it enters a mitochondrion, before the citric acid cycle." Model answer: P1–P3 (in matrix; loses one CO₂; hydrogen to NAD⁺; acetyl group joined to coenzyme A to form acetyl CoA).
2. 12 marks, Higher: "Outline the events of the second stage of aerobic respiration, from acetyl CoA to the formation of water." Model answer: K2–K5, K7, E2–E4. Must name oxygen as the final electron acceptor and the location of the cycle and the chain.
3. 9 marks, Higher: "Explain why the citric acid cycle stops if a cell is deprived of oxygen, even though the cycle doesn't use oxygen directly." Model answer: E4–E5, K7 (no final acceptor; chain stops; NADH not unloaded; no NAD⁺ for the cycle).
