## 1. Must cover

- [ ] U1 A neuron (nerve cell) is the basic unit of the nervous system; it carries electrochemical nerve impulses. Neurons are long and thin so a single cell can connect distant parts of the body.
- [ ] U2 (assumed) Three types: sensory neurons carry impulses from receptors to the CNS; motor neurons carry impulses from the CNS to effectors; interneurons (relay or connector neurons) carry impulses between neurons within the CNS.

Parts of a neuron and their functions (one bullet per part, relating structure to function)
- [ ] U3 Cell body: contains the nucleus and most of the cytoplasm and organelles; keeps the cell alive and makes the neurotransmitter and the proteins the neuron needs.
- [ ] U4 Dendrites: short, branched extensions that receive impulses from other neurons or receptors and carry them towards the cell body; branching lets one neuron receive signals from many others.
- [ ] U5 Axon: a long single fibre that carries impulses away from the cell body; its length lets impulses travel to distant effectors.
- [ ] U6 Myelin sheath: a fatty insulating layer around many axons; it speeds up the impulse, which jumps between the small gaps in the sheath (nodes of Ranvier).
- [ ] U7 Schwann cell: a cell that wraps around the axon in the PNS, its membrane forming the myelin sheath.
- [ ] U8 Neurotransmitter vesicles: tiny sacs in the swollen axon terminals (synaptic knobs) that store neurotransmitter, released to pass the impulse to the next cell. (Release itself is in "Neurotransmission"; one sentence here.)

Motor and sensory neurons compared
- [ ] U9 Motor neuron: cell body at one end, inside the CNS, with many dendrites; one long axon runs out to an effector, ending in axon terminals on a muscle or gland.
- [ ] U10 Sensory neuron: a long fibre (dendron) carries impulses from the receptor to the cell body, which sits to one side of the fibre in a ganglion outside the CNS; a shorter axon carries the impulse on into the CNS.
- [ ] U11 Relate structure to function: the motor neuron's many dendrites in the CNS receive input from many interneurons; its long axon reaches the effector. The sensory neuron's long dendron reaches from a receptor (e.g. in the skin of the foot) to the spinal cord; its cell body lies off the main pathway, so it doesn't slow the impulse.
- [ ] U12 Interneuron: short, with many branched connections, found only in the CNS; connects sensory to motor neurons and links many neurons together.
- [ ] U13 Diagram: a motor neuron and a sensory neuron, labelling cell body, nucleus, dendrites, dendron (sensory), axon, myelin sheath, Schwann cell, node of Ranvier, axon terminals, neurotransmitter vesicles, and an arrow for the direction of the impulse.

Myelin and disease
- [ ] U14 Multiple sclerosis (MS): the body's immune system damages the myelin sheath in the CNS, slowing or blocking impulses; symptoms include problems with movement, balance and vision.

## 3. Traps

- "Detailed knowledge of electrochemistry" is not required anywhere in this chapter: no resting potential, no millivolt figures, no sodium-potassium pump.
- Don't say Schwann cells make myelin in the CNS (other cells do; don't name them).
- Neurotransmitter release, receptors and the synaptic cleft belong to "Neurotransmission". Here U8 only.
- Dendrite (short, branched, on the cell body) and dendron (the long fibre of a sensory neuron) are different words; use each correctly.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Label a diagram of a neuron; give the function of the myelin sheath, dendrites or axon.
- Name the type of neuron shown; state the direction of the impulse.
- State the function of the Schwann cell or of the vesicles.
- Compare a sensory and a motor neuron.
