## 1. Must cover

What photosynthesis is (one block)
- [ ] P1 Photosynthesis is the process in which green plants, algae and some bacteria (e.g. cyanobacteria) use light energy, absorbed by chlorophyll, to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen as a by-product. These organisms are autotrophs (they make their own food; autotrophic nutrition is defined in "Characteristics of living things", use the term without redefining it).
- [ ] P2 Energy conversion: light energy is converted to chemical energy stored in the bonds of glucose. Almost all food chains, and so almost all the energy used by living things, start with this conversion; oxygen in the air also comes from photosynthesis.
- [ ] P3 Why it is anabolic: it builds a large molecule (glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆) from small inorganic molecules (CO₂ and H₂O), and it needs (takes in) energy, supplied by light. Respiration is the matching catabolic process (see "Respiration as a catabolic process"). Don't redefine anabolism and catabolism; they are in "Biomolecules and metabolism".
- [ ] P4 Word equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, with "light energy" and "chlorophyll" written above the arrow.
- [ ] P5 Balanced equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light energy, chlorophyll above the arrow). Check the balance: 6 C, 18 O and 12 H on each side. Six molecules of CO₂ supply the six carbon atoms of one glucose molecule.

Sources of the reactants and fate of the products (one block)
- [ ] S1 Carbon dioxide: in land plants it diffuses from the air into the leaf through the stomata (pores, mainly on the lower surface), through the air spaces and into the mesophyll cells; some comes from the plant's own respiring cells. Aquatic plants take in CO₂ dissolved in the water (partly as hydrogencarbonate ions).
- [ ] S2 Water: absorbed from the soil by root hair cells (by osmosis), carried up the stem to the leaves in the xylem. Only a tiny fraction of the water a plant takes up is used in photosynthesis; most is lost in transpiration. (Transport detail belongs to "Transport in Plants".)
- [ ] S3 Light: from the sun (or artificial light in greenhouses and labs). Chlorophyll, the green pigment in chloroplasts, absorbs mainly red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.
- [ ] S4 Oxygen: used by the plant's own cells in respiration, and the excess diffuses out of the leaf through the stomata into the air (or water). The oxygen released comes from the splitting of water, not from carbon dioxide; this was shown in the 1940s by experiments in which water labelled with a heavy isotope of oxygen (¹⁸O) gave labelled oxygen gas.
- [ ] S5 Glucose: used in the plant's own respiration to release energy (ATP); the rest is converted to other substances, as below.

Glucose to sucrose and complex carbohydrates (one block)
- [ ] G1 Sucrose: glucose is joined to fructose to form the disaccharide sucrose, the form in which carbohydrate is transported from the leaves in the phloem to growing tips, roots, fruits and storage organs. Sucrose suits transport because it is soluble and less reactive than glucose (it is not a reducing sugar), so it isn't used up on the way.
- [ ] G2 Starch: glucose is joined into the polysaccharide starch for storage, first as starch grains in the chloroplast stroma during the day, and long-term in storage organs and seeds (e.g. potato tubers, cereal grains). Starch suits storage because it is insoluble, so it doesn't affect osmosis, and it is compact.
- [ ] G3 Cellulose: glucose is joined into the polysaccharide cellulose, which forms plant cell walls; it is the most abundant organic compound on Earth. One sentence: plants also convert glucose to lipids (oils in seeds) and, with nitrates from the soil, to amino acids; that conversion is taught in "Biomolecules and metabolism", so give it in one phrase only.

Photosynthesis as a carbon sink (one block)
- [ ] C1 A carbon sink is anything that takes in (absorbs) more carbon from the atmosphere than it releases, storing it. Photosynthesis is the only major biological process that removes CO₂ from the air: the carbon is "fixed" into glucose and then into sucrose, starch, cellulose and the rest of the plant's biomass.
- [ ] C2 The carbon stays locked up as long as the biomass survives: in wood of trees for decades or centuries, in peat bogs for thousands of years (because waterlogged, acidic conditions slow decomposition), and in ocean sediments from algae and phytoplankton. Respiration, decomposition and burning return it to the air as CO₂ (see "Respiration as a catabolic process").
- [ ] C3 Sustainability link: forests, peatlands and the oceans' algae and phytoplankton are the main natural carbon sinks; cutting forests, draining or burning peat and burning fossil fuels (carbon fixed by photosynthesis millions of years ago) release stored carbon and add to the CO₂ that drives climate change. The carbon cycle and climate strategies belong to "Nutrient Cycling"; one or two bullets here.

Cellular location and the overall sequence (one block, process)
- [ ] L1 Location: photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts. In a leaf, most chloroplasts are in the palisade mesophyll cells just below the upper surface, where light is strongest; spongy mesophyll cells and guard cells also have them. (Chloroplast structure is in "Structures and organelles of animal and plant cells"; use thylakoid, granum and stroma without redefining them.)
- [ ] L2 Photosynthesis happens in two linked stages. Stage 1, the light-dependent stage (light stage), takes place on the thylakoid membranes (grana): 1. chlorophyll absorbs light energy; 2. the energy is used to split water molecules (photolysis) into oxygen, protons (H⁺) and electrons; 3. the oxygen is released; 4. the energy is trapped in ATP, and the protons and high-energy electrons are picked up by a carrier molecule (named in the Higher Level subsections).
- [ ] L3 Stage 2, the light-independent stage, takes place in the stroma: 1. carbon dioxide enters the stroma; 2. it is combined with the protons and electrons (hydrogen) from stage 1; 3. energy from the ATP made in stage 1 drives the reactions; 4. glucose is formed. The reactions are controlled by enzymes, so this stage is affected by temperature.
- [ ] L4 The link between the stages: stage 1 supplies the energy (ATP) and hydrogen that stage 2 needs; stage 2 returns the spent carriers (ADP and the empty hydrogen carrier) to stage 1. The light-independent stage doesn't use light directly, but it stops soon after dark because stage 1 stops supplying ATP and hydrogen. Older books call it the "dark stage"; that name is misleading, since it happens in the light.
- [ ] L5 Diagram: a flow diagram of a chloroplast in outline, with the light-dependent stage drawn on a thylakoid stack (inputs: light, H₂O; outputs: O₂ out of the cell; ATP and hydrogen passed to the stroma) and the light-independent stage drawn in the stroma (inputs: CO₂, ATP, hydrogen; output: glucose → sucrose, starch, cellulose; ADP and the empty carrier returned to the thylakoid).

## 3. Traps

- Plants respire all the time, day and night, as well as photosynthesising; don't say plants "photosynthesise instead of respiring" or "respire only at night".
- The oxygen comes from water, not from carbon dioxide (S4).
- Chlorophyll reflects green light; it doesn't absorb it.
- Don't call the light-independent stage the "dark stage" except to note the older name (L4); it doesn't need darkness.
- Don't name NADP⁺ or NADPH in this subsection's blocks or key terms: they are Higher Level. L2–L4 say "a carrier molecule" or "hydrogen carrier". Don't describe photons, reaction centres, pigment-to-pigment transfer, cyclic and non-cyclic pathways or the electron transport chain; these belong to "The light-dependent reactions", "The light-independent reactions" and "The chloroplast's internal structure".
- The respiration equation, its stages and carbon release belong to "Respiration as a catabolic process"; P3 and C2 refer to it in one phrase.
- Factors affecting the rate (light intensity, CO₂, temperature) and limiting factors belong to "Investigation: rate of photosynthesis"; L3 mentions temperature in one clause only.
- Leaf structure, stomata and the transport of water, CO₂ and sucrose belong to "Transport in Plants"; S1, S2 and G1 give one-line links only. Don't add a leaf diagram.
- Testing a leaf for starch (destarching, iodine) was Junior Cycle practical work and is not on this specification; don't add it.
- Chemosynthesis is mentioned in "Characteristics of living things"; don't repeat it.
- No other diagram cues beyond L5.
