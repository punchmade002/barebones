## 1. Must cover

Overview (one short block)
- [ ] O1 The male reproductive system produces sperm (the male gametes) and the hormone testosterone, and delivers sperm into the female's vagina during sexual intercourse. Sperm pass, in order: testis → epididymis → vas deferens → urethra (where fluids from the seminal vesicles and prostate are added) → out through the penis. A student should be able to give this pathway in order.
- [ ] O2 Semen is sperm suspended in seminal fluid. Seminal fluid (from the seminal vesicles and the prostate) gives the sperm a liquid to swim in, supplies an energy source, and is slightly alkaline overall, which neutralises the acidity of the vagina and of any urine left in the urethra; sperm are damaged by acid. Sperm make up only a small part of the volume of semen.

The testes and scrotum (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] T1 Scrotum: a sac of skin and muscle hanging outside the abdomen that holds the two testes. It keeps the testes about 2–3 °C below core body temperature (37 °C), which sperm production needs; this is why the testes are outside the body. Muscles in the scrotum pull the testes closer to the body when it is cold and relax to let them hang lower when it is warm.
- [ ] T2 Testes (singular testis): the two oval male gonads (primary sex organs). Each is packed with many long, tightly coiled seminiferous tubules, whose walls produce sperm by meiosis, continuously from puberty (detail in "Gametes and fertilisation"). The coiling packs a very long length of tubule, and so a large sperm-producing surface, into a small organ.
- [ ] T3 Between the seminiferous tubules are interstitial cells, which secrete testosterone into the blood; the testes are therefore also endocrine glands (see "Glands, locations and hormones"). One bullet.
- [ ] T4 (assumed) The testes form inside the abdomen of the foetus and normally descend into the scrotum before birth. One bullet; don't teach undescended testes beyond "a testis that has not descended cannot produce sperm normally", which is a known cause of male infertility (see "Fertility control and infertility").

The ducts (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] D1 Epididymis: a single, very long, tightly coiled tube lying against the back of each testis. Sperm from the seminiferous tubules pass into it, where they are stored and mature, gaining the ability to swim. Its coiling gives a long tube for storage in a small space.
- [ ] D2 Vas deferens (sperm duct; plural vasa deferentia): a muscular tube carrying sperm from the epididymis, up into the abdomen, around the bladder, to join the urethra. During ejaculation its smooth muscle contracts in waves (peristalsis) to push sperm along. It is the tube that is cut and tied in a vasectomy (see "Fertility control and infertility").
- [ ] D3 Urethra: the tube running from the bladder through the length of the penis to the outside. In males it carries both urine and semen, but never at the same time: during ejaculation a ring of muscle (sphincter) at the base of the bladder closes, so urine can't mix with semen. Contrast with the female, where the urethra carries only urine and opens separately from the vagina (see "The female reproductive system").

The glands (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] G1 Seminal vesicles: two glands behind the bladder whose ducts join the vasa deferentia. They secrete a thick, alkaline fluid rich in the sugar fructose, which the sperm's mitochondria respire to release energy for swimming. This fluid makes up most of the volume of semen.
- [ ] G2 Prostate gland: a single gland, about the size of a walnut, just below the bladder, surrounding the start of the urethra. It secretes a thin, milky fluid containing enzymes that keep the semen liquid after ejaculation, so the sperm can swim free. The secretions of the seminal vesicles and prostate together form seminal fluid.
- [ ] G3 (assumed) Because the prostate surrounds the urethra, an enlarged prostate, common in older men, can press on the urethra and make urination difficult. One bullet; prostate cancer is not required.

The penis (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] P1 Penis: the male organ that places semen in the vagina during intercourse, and through which urine and semen leave the body via the urethra. It contains spongy erectile tissue: during sexual arousal its blood vessels widen and the spaces fill with blood under pressure, so the penis becomes larger, firm and erect, which allows it to enter the vagina.
- [ ] P2 Glans: the rounded, sensitive tip of the penis, with many nerve endings; stimulation of these leads to ejaculation. The urethra opens at its tip.
- [ ] P3 Foreskin: a fold of loose skin that covers and protects the glans when the penis is not erect, and pulls back (retracts) during an erection. It is removed in circumcision. One bullet each for glans and foreskin.
- [ ] P4 Ejaculation: at the peak of sexual stimulation, waves of muscle contraction in the epididymis, vasa deferentia, glands and urethra expel semen from the penis. The semen from one ejaculation (a few millilitres) contains many millions of sperm. One bullet.

Diagram (one bullet in the most suitable block)
- [ ] DG1 Diagram: side view (longitudinal section) of the male reproductive system, with the bladder for orientation. Label: testis, scrotum, epididymis, vas deferens (sperm duct), seminal vesicle, prostate gland, urethra, penis, glans, foreskin, and the bladder. Draw the vas deferens running up from the epididymis and looping up and over the ureter behind the bladder to join the urethra just below the bladder, with the seminal vesicle behind the bladder and the prostate around the urethra below it.

Summary (one comparison block)
- [ ] S1 One bullet per part in the form "part – where it is – function – how its structure suits the function", for all ten syllabus parts, in the order of the sperm pathway (scrotum, testis, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, prostate, urethra, penis, glans, foreskin).

## 3. Traps

- Accuracy point: many older textbooks say "the prostate secretes an alkaline fluid". In fact the alkaline secretion that neutralises acid comes mainly from the seminal vesicles; prostatic fluid is slightly acidic and milky and contains enzymes. Say that seminal fluid as a whole is slightly alkaline (O2), and give each gland the role in G1 and G2. Don't say the prostate "produces sperm" or "stores sperm".
- Sperm are made in the testes (seminiferous tubules), stored and matured in the epididymis. Don't say sperm are made in the epididymis or stored in the seminal vesicles.
- The vas deferens joins the urethra, not the bladder. The ureters (kidney to bladder) are not part of this system; don't confuse ureter and urethra.
- The scrotum's function is temperature, not "protection".
- Cowper's (bulbourethral) glands and the internal detail of the testis (Sertoli cells, stages of sperm development) are not on the specification; don't teach them. Name the interstitial cells only for testosterone (T3).
- Meiosis and sperm structure belong to "Gametes and fertilisation"; testosterone's roles (puberty, sperm production, secondary sexual characteristics) belong to "Hormones in reproduction" and "Glands, locations and hormones". Name them here in one phrase each.
- Vasectomy and male infertility belong to "Fertility control and infertility"; one phrase each here.
- The "sperm duct" of older papers is the vas deferens; give both names once.
- Only one diagram cue (DG1). No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Draw and label a diagram of the male reproductive system (testis, sperm duct, prostate, epididymis, penis, scrotum, urethra, seminal vesicle).
- Give the function of a named part (prostate, epididymis, seminal vesicle, scrotum, vas deferens).
- Explain why the testes are outside the body.
- Name the fluid that carries sperm and state where it is made.
- Trace the route of sperm from where they are made to the outside.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "State where sperm are (i) produced and (ii) stored, and explain why the testes are held in the scrotum." Model answer: T2 (seminiferous tubules of the testes), D1 (epididymis), T1 (2–3 °C below body temperature, needed for sperm production).
2. 9 marks: "Name the two glands that add fluid to sperm and give one function of each fluid. Explain why semen must be slightly alkaline." Model answer: G1 (seminal vesicles, fructose for energy, most of the volume, alkaline), G2 (prostate, enzymes keeping semen liquid), O2 (neutralises vaginal acidity, which would damage sperm). Must not say the prostate makes sperm.
3. 12 marks: "Draw a labelled diagram of the male reproductive system and give the function of the epididymis, vas deferens, urethra and penis." Model answer: DG1 with all ten parts labelled; D1, D2 (peristalsis during ejaculation), D3 (urine and semen, not at the same time), P1 (erectile tissue fills with blood; places semen in the vagina).
