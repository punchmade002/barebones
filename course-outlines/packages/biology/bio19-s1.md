## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The two divisions
- [ ] N1 The nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates responses by carrying nerve impulses. It has two divisions: the central nervous system (CNS: brain and spinal cord) and the peripheral nervous system (PNS: all the nerves outside the CNS).
- [ ] N2 (assumed) A neuron is a nerve cell; a nerve is a bundle of many neuron fibres wrapped in connective tissue. A nerve impulse is an electrochemical signal travelling along a neuron. (Neuron structure itself is in "Neurons"; give only this one-line definition.)
- [ ] N3 The CNS receives information, processes it and sends out instructions; the PNS carries impulses between the CNS and the rest of the body.

The PNS: where fibres and cell bodies are
- [ ] P1 The PNS contains sensory neurons, which carry impulses from receptors to the CNS, and motor neurons, which carry impulses from the CNS to effectors (muscles and glands).
- [ ] P2 Nerve fibres of the PNS run through the body in nerves; 31 pairs of spinal nerves leave the spinal cord between the vertebrae.
- [ ] P3 Each spinal nerve joins the cord by two roots: the dorsal (back) root carries sensory fibres in; the ventral (front) root carries motor fibres out.
- [ ] P4 Location of cell bodies: sensory neuron cell bodies lie in the dorsal root ganglion, a swelling on the dorsal root just outside the spinal cord; motor neuron cell bodies lie in the grey matter of the CNS; interneurons lie entirely within the CNS.

The spinal cord
- [ ] S1 The spinal cord runs from the base of the brain down the back, inside the vertebral column, which protects it. (Vertebrae themselves are in The Musculoskeletal System; don't repeat.)
- [ ] S2 Structure in cross-section: a central canal containing cerebrospinal fluid; an H- or butterfly-shaped core of grey matter; white matter surrounding it.
- [ ] S3 Grey matter is made of cell bodies and interneurons (unmyelinated); white matter is made of myelinated axons, white because myelin is fatty. In the spinal cord grey matter is inside and white matter outside; in the brain the cerebral cortex is grey matter on the outside with white matter beneath.
- [ ] S4 Functions: relays impulses between the body and the brain; carries out spinal reflexes (detail in "The reflex action").
- [ ] S5 Diagram: transverse section of the spinal cord labelling central canal, grey matter, white matter, dorsal root, dorsal root ganglion, ventral root, spinal nerve.

Protection of the CNS (assumed)
- [ ] M1 (assumed) The brain is protected by the skull (cranium) and the spinal cord by the vertebrae.
- [ ] M2 (assumed) Three membranes, the meninges, surround the brain and spinal cord. Cerebrospinal fluid between the inner meninges, and in the brain's cavities and the central canal, cushions the CNS against shock and supplies nutrients. Meningitis is inflammation of the meninges, usually caused by bacterial or viral infection.

The brain: relate each part's structure to its function
- [ ] B1 Cerebrum: the largest part, divided into left and right cerebral hemispheres joined by the corpus callosum. Its outer layer, the cerebral cortex, is grey matter, deeply folded to increase its surface area and so the number of neurons. Functions: voluntary movement, interpreting the senses, memory, thought, language, intelligence and personality. The left hemisphere controls the right side of the body and vice versa.
- [ ] B2 Cerebellum: at the back of the brain below the cerebrum, also with a folded surface. Function: coordination of muscular movement, balance and posture.
- [ ] B3 Medulla oblongata: at the base of the brain where it joins the spinal cord. Function: controls involuntary actions, including breathing rate, heart rate and blood pressure, and reflexes such as swallowing, coughing and sneezing.
- [ ] B4 Hypothalamus: below the cerebrum, above the pituitary gland, to which it is connected. Function: controls homeostasis (body temperature, water balance, hunger and thirst) and links the nervous and hormonal systems by controlling the pituitary.
- [ ] B5 Diagram: vertical section of the brain labelling cerebrum, cerebral cortex, corpus callosum, cerebellum, medulla oblongata, hypothalamus, pituitary gland, spinal cord.

## 3. Traps

- The specification says "cranial nerves, sympathetic and parasympathetic systems are not required". Don't name or describe them. Don't divide the PNS into autonomic and somatic.
- The reflex arc belongs to `bio19-s2`; neuron structure (dendrites, axon, myelin, Schwann cell) to `bio19-s3`. Here mention neurons only as N2 and P1–P4 need.
- The pituitary's hormones belong to The Endocrine System. Name the pituitary here only as the gland the hypothalamus controls.
- Homeostatic feedback loops belong to Homeostasis. B4 is one sentence of function only.
- The brain regions B1–B4 aren't named in the specification. They are the "parts of the central nervous system" textbooks teach, so include them, but don't add any further regions (no thalamus, no lobes).
- Don't write "grey matter is unmyelinated neurons" for the brain cortex in a way that contradicts S3; keep it as cell bodies.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "Distinguish between the central nervous system and the peripheral nervous system, and state where the cell bodies of sensory neurons and motor neurons are found." Model answer: CNS vs PNS (P1, N1), dorsal root ganglion, grey matter of CNS.
2. 12 marks: "Relate the structure of each of the following to its function: (i) the cerebrum, (ii) the cerebellum, (iii) the medulla oblongata." Model answer covers exactly those three parts, one structural feature and its functions for each.
3. 9 marks: "Describe the structure of the spinal cord as seen in a transverse section and explain why white matter is white." Model answer: central canal, grey matter, white matter, roots, ganglion; myelin is fatty.
