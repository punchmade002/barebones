## 1. Must cover

Roles of carbohydrates (one block)
- [ ] C1 Respiratory and catabolic: glucose is the main respiratory substrate. It is broken down in respiration to release energy, which is trapped as ATP (one phrase; detail in "Respiration" and "Transfer Molecules"). The brain relies mainly on glucose for its energy.
- [ ] C2 Photosynthetic: glucose is the product of photosynthesis, the anabolic process in which plants use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water (detail in "Photosynthesis"). Almost all the carbohydrate in food chains therefore comes from photosynthesis.
- [ ] C3 Anabolic and storage: excess glucose is built up into storage polysaccharides: starch in plants, glycogen in the liver and muscles of animals. These are broken down again (catabolism) when glucose is needed.
- [ ] C4 Structural: cellulose forms plant cell walls; chitin forms fungal cell walls and insect exoskeletons; the pentose sugars ribose and deoxyribose are part of RNA and DNA nucleotides.
- [ ] C5 Transport: plants move sugar from leaves to other parts as sucrose in the phloem.
- [ ] C6 In the diet, cellulose cannot be digested by humans but acts as dietary fibre, adding bulk that helps peristalsis and prevents constipation (one bullet; diet detail in "The Digestive System").

Roles of lipids (one block)
- [ ] L1 Respiratory and catabolic: fats are respired to release energy, especially when glucose and glycogen run low. A gram of fat releases about twice as much energy as a gram of carbohydrate or protein, so lipid is a compact long-term energy store.
- [ ] L2 Storage (anabolic): excess food is built into triglycerides and stored as fat in adipose tissue under the skin and around organs in animals, and as oils in seeds (e.g. sunflower seeds) and fruits (e.g. olives) in plants.
- [ ] L3 Insulation: fat under the skin reduces heat loss (thick blubber in whales and seals). Fat also insulates nerve fibres: the myelin sheath is rich in lipid (one phrase; detail in "The Nervous System").
- [ ] L4 Protection: fat around organs such as the kidneys and heart cushions them against physical damage.
- [ ] L5 Structural: phospholipids form the bilayer of all cell membranes, including the membranes inside chloroplasts and mitochondria.
- [ ] L6 Photosynthetic: the light-absorbing pigments of photosynthesis are held in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast, which are built of phospholipids. Plants also convert some of the glucose made in photosynthesis into oils.
- [ ] L7 Waterproofing: waxes (lipids) form the waxy cuticle on leaves, reducing water loss, and waterproof feathers and fur.
- [ ] L8 Regulators: steroid hormones such as testosterone, oestrogen and progesterone are lipids. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are absorbed with dietary fat (detail in "Vitamins").

Roles of proteins (one block for general roles, one for the molecular-mechanism proteins)
- [ ] P1 Enzymes: almost all enzymes are proteins. They are biological catalysts that speed up the reactions of metabolism without being used up, e.g. amylase digests starch to maltose (mechanism in "Enzymes").
- [ ] P2 Hormones: some hormones are proteins, e.g. insulin, made by the pancreas, which lowers blood glucose (other hormones are lipids: see L8).
- [ ] P3 Immunity: antibodies are proteins made by lymphocytes (B cells). Each binds to a specific antigen on a pathogen, marking it for destruction (detail in "Immunity").
- [ ] P4 Transport of substances in the body: haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen.
- [ ] P5 Movement: actin and myosin are the contractile proteins of muscle (detail in "The Musculoskeletal System").
- [ ] P6 Energy: protein is respired only when carbohydrate and fat are in short supply. Excess amino acids cannot be stored; the liver removes their amino group (deamination), forming urea, which is excreted (one phrase; detail in "The Digestive System" and "The Urinary System").
- [ ] P7 Growth and repair: proteins are the main raw material for new cells and tissues, so children, pregnant women and people recovering from injury need more.

Proteins necessary for molecular mechanisms and membrane interactions (one block)
- [ ] M1 Membrane interactions: proteins embedded in the phospholipid bilayer control what enters and leaves cells and how cells communicate. Four kinds are named in the specification (M2–M5).
- [ ] M2 Channel proteins form water-filled pores through the membrane that let specific ions or molecules pass by diffusion, e.g. sodium and potassium ion channels in neurons, which allow nerve impulses.
- [ ] M3 Transporters (carrier proteins) bind a specific molecule and change shape to move it across the membrane. Some use energy from ATP to move substances against a concentration gradient (active transport), e.g. the sodium–potassium pump. Detail in "Transport Across Membranes".
- [ ] M4 Receptors are proteins on the cell surface (or inside the cell) with a shape that fits one signalling molecule, such as a hormone or neurotransmitter. When it binds, the cell responds, e.g. insulin receptors on liver and muscle cells. This is why a hormone affects only its target cells.
- [ ] M5 Structural proteins give strength and support: keratin in hair, nails and the outer skin; collagen in skin, tendons, ligaments, cartilage and bone. Proteins also form the cytoskeleton inside cells.
- [ ] M6 Diagram: a section of cell membrane (phospholipid bilayer) with a channel protein, a carrier protein and a receptor protein with its signalling molecule, each labelled.

Hormones as regulators (one block)
- [ ] H1 A hormone is a chemical messenger made by an endocrine gland and carried in the blood to target cells, where it regulates their activity. Hormones regulate growth, development, metabolism and reproduction.
- [ ] H2 Hormones are biomolecules: some are proteins (e.g. insulin) and some are steroid lipids (e.g. testosterone, oestrogen). One bullet; the glands and hormones are taught in "The Endocrine System".
- [ ] H3 Hormones work only on cells with the matching receptor proteins (link to M4). Plants also make chemical regulators (plant growth regulators), e.g. auxin. One bullet.

Nutritional sources (one block, three bullets per biomolecule at most)
- [ ] F1 Carbohydrate sources. Sugars: fruit, honey, jam, sweets, fizzy drinks; lactose in milk. Starch: bread, pasta, rice, potatoes, breakfast cereals. Fibre (cellulose): vegetables, fruit, wholegrain cereals and brown bread, beans.
- [ ] F2 Lipid sources. Fats (mostly saturated): butter, cream, cheese, lard, fatty meat. Oils (mostly unsaturated): olive and sunflower oils, nuts and seeds, avocados, oily fish such as salmon and mackerel.
- [ ] F3 Protein sources. Animal: meat, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, yoghurt. Plant: beans, peas, lentils, nuts, soya and tofu, cereals. Animal proteins contain all the essential amino acids; many single plant foods lack one or more, so vegetarian and vegan diets combine a range of plant proteins (e.g. beans with cereal).
- [ ] F4 Health link: diets high in saturated fat are linked to raised blood cholesterol and heart disease; diets high in free sugars are linked to tooth decay, obesity and type 2 diabetes. Sustainability link: producing plant protein generally needs less land and water and causes lower greenhouse gas emissions than producing meat. Two bullets; balanced diet detail belongs to "The Digestive System".

## 3. Traps

- Don't say all enzymes are proteins without "almost"; a few RNA catalysts exist. Keep "almost all" and don't elaborate.
- Don't say all hormones are proteins (steroid hormones are lipids).
- Lipids give about twice the energy of carbohydrate per gram; don't give "nine times" or exact kJ values.
- Structures belong to "Structures of biomolecules"; refer to "triglycerides" and "phospholipids" by name only.
- Membrane transport processes (diffusion, osmosis, active transport detail) belong to "Transport Across Membranes"; M2–M3 are one bullet each.
- Glands, named hormones and their effects belong to "The Endocrine System"; antibody detail to "Immunity"; ATP to "Transfer Molecules"; photosynthesis and respiration stages to their own chapters.
- Balanced diet, dietary fibre's health role and dietary choices belong to "The Digestive System" (Diet); F4 and C6 are brief links only.
- The diagram cue in M6 is the only one wanted. No other diagram cues.
