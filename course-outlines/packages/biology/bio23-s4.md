## 1. Must cover

What the cycle is (one block)
- [ ] C1 The menstrual cycle is the series of changes in the ovary and the uterus that happens about once a month in a woman from puberty to menopause, except during pregnancy. Its purpose is to release an egg and prepare the endometrium to receive an embryo.
- [ ] C2 It lasts about 28 days on average, but a cycle of about 21 to 35 days is normal, and length varies between women and from month to month. Day 1 is the first day of bleeding.
- [ ] C3 Two sets of changes run side by side: the ovarian cycle (a follicle develops, releases its egg and becomes the corpus luteum) and the uterine cycle (the endometrium is shed, rebuilt and thickened). They are coordinated by hormones.
- [ ] C4 The first period (menarche) occurs during puberty; the cycles end at the menopause (see "Hormones in reproduction").

The four phases (one block, phase by phase, with typical days for a 28-day cycle)
- [ ] P1 Menstruation (days 1–5, about): if no egg was fertilised in the previous cycle, the thickened outer layer of the endometrium breaks down and is shed with a small amount of blood through the cervix and vagina (a period). Shedding lasts about 3–7 days.
- [ ] P2 Follicular phase (day 1 to about day 13, overlapping menstruation): in the ovary, several follicles start to develop, and usually one grows into a large, mature, fluid-filled follicle containing the egg, called a Graafian follicle. In the uterus, after menstruation ends, the endometrium is repaired and grows thicker (from about day 6). Near the end of this phase the cervical mucus becomes thin, clear and slippery, letting sperm through.
- [ ] P3 Ovulation (about day 14): the Graafian follicle bursts at the surface of the ovary and releases its egg, which is swept into the fallopian tube. This is the most fertile time; the egg survives about 12–24 hours (see "Gametes and fertilisation").
- [ ] P4 Luteal phase (about days 15–28): the empty follicle turns into a yellow mass of cells, the corpus luteum ("yellow body"). The endometrium thickens further, becoming spongy, with more blood vessels and glands, ready for an embryo to implant. If no embryo implants, the corpus luteum breaks down after about 10–14 days and the endometrium is shed: the next menstruation (day 1) begins. If an embryo implants, the corpus luteum stays active (see "Hormones in reproduction") and there is no period.
- [ ] P5 Ovulation happens about 14 days before the next period starts. The luteal phase is fairly constant (about 14 days); differences in cycle length come mostly from the follicular phase. So in a 35-day cycle ovulation is about day 21, not day 14.
- [ ] P6 Summary, one bullet per phase: phase – days – what happens in the ovary – what happens in the endometrium.
- [ ] P7 Diagram: a timeline from day 0 to day 28 showing, above, the ovary (follicle growing, Graafian follicle, ovulation at day 14, corpus luteum forming and then breaking down) and, below, the thickness of the endometrium (shed on days 1–5, thickening to day 28); label the four phases along the timeline.

Hormone changes in each phase (Higher Level only; one block, phase by phase)
- [ ] H1 [HL] The four hormones and their sources: FSH and LH are made by the pituitary gland; oestrogen is made mainly by the developing follicle (and in smaller amounts by the corpus luteum); progesterone is made by the corpus luteum. One bullet.
- [ ] H2 [HL] Menstruation: oestrogen and progesterone are both low, because the corpus luteum of the last cycle has broken down. The fall in progesterone is what causes the endometrium to break down. Low progesterone no longer holds back the pituitary, so FSH begins to rise.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Follicular phase: FSH stimulates follicles to develop and one becomes the Graafian follicle; the growing follicle secretes oestrogen, so oestrogen rises steadily. Oestrogen repairs and thickens the endometrium and makes the cervical mucus thin. Rising oestrogen at first inhibits FSH, so FSH falls back and usually only one follicle completes development.
- [ ] H4 [HL] Ovulation: when oestrogen reaches a high peak (around day 12–13), it stimulates the pituitary to release a large surge of LH (with a smaller rise in FSH). The LH surge causes the Graafian follicle to burst and release its egg about a day later (ovulation, about day 14). Oestrogen falls just after ovulation.
- [ ] H5 [HL] Luteal phase: LH causes the empty Graafian follicle to develop into the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone (and some oestrogen), so progesterone rises to a peak around day 21. Progesterone maintains and further thickens the endometrium, making it ready for implantation, and makes the cervical mucus thick again. High progesterone (with oestrogen) inhibits FSH and LH, so no new follicle develops and no further egg is released while the corpus luteum is active. FSH and LH stay low.
- [ ] H6 [HL] End of the cycle: without pregnancy, low LH means the corpus luteum is no longer maintained; it breaks down after about 10–14 days, so progesterone and oestrogen fall. The endometrium can't be maintained and is shed (menstruation), and the fall removes the inhibition of FSH, which rises and starts a new cycle. If an embryo implants, a hormone from the embryo keeps the corpus luteum alive, so progesterone stays high and menstruation doesn't happen (detail in "Hormones in reproduction").
- [ ] H7 [HL] Summary table, one bullet per phase: phase – FSH – LH – oestrogen – progesterone – ovary structure (follicle growing / Graafian follicle bursts / corpus luteum active / corpus luteum breaks down). Menstruation: FSH rising, LH low, oestrogen low, progesterone low. Follicular: FSH high then falling, LH low, oestrogen rising, progesterone low. Ovulation: LH surge, small FSH peak, oestrogen at its peak then falling, progesterone low. Luteal: FSH and LH low, progesterone high (peak about day 21), oestrogen moderate (a second, smaller peak); both falling at the end.

Feedback between the ovary and the pituitary (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] H8 [HL] The cycle is controlled by feedback. Negative feedback: progesterone (and moderate oestrogen) inhibits the pituitary's release of FSH and LH; this keeps FSH and LH low in the luteal phase, and their fall lets the corpus luteum break down (see "Homeostasis as a stimulus-response model" for negative feedback). Positive feedback: high oestrogen just before ovulation causes more LH release, giving the LH surge. One bullet each.
- [ ] H9 [HL] Each hormone controls the next: FSH → follicle grows → oestrogen → LH surge → ovulation and corpus luteum → progesterone → FSH and LH inhibited → corpus luteum breaks down → progesterone falls → menstruation and FSH rises. Diagram: this sequence as a loop (a flow chart) linking the pituitary, the ovary and the endometrium, with "+" on the oestrogen-to-LH arrow and "−" on the progesterone-to-FSH/LH arrow.
- [ ] H10 [HL] Technology links, one bullet: home ovulation test kits detect the LH surge in urine, showing that ovulation is likely within about a day or two; the combined contraceptive pill gives steady oestrogen and progesterone, which by negative feedback keep FSH and LH low, so no follicle matures and no LH surge occurs (the pill is in "Hormonal manipulation" and "Fertility control and infertility").

The hormone graphs (Higher Level only; one skills block)
- [ ] H11 [HL] How to read the standard graphs of a 28-day cycle: FSH has a rise early in the cycle and a small peak at day 14; LH is low except for a sharp, tall peak just before day 14; oestrogen rises through the follicular phase to a peak just before the LH surge, drops after ovulation, then has a lower second rise in the luteal phase; progesterone is low until ovulation, rises to a peak around day 21, then falls to day 28. Exam answers explain each change by its cause (e.g. "progesterone rises after day 14 because the corpus luteum secretes it").
- [ ] H12 [HL] Diagram: two graphs on the same 28-day time axis, one for the pituitary hormones (FSH, LH) and one for the ovarian hormones (oestrogen, progesterone), lined up with the ovary events and the endometrium thickness from P7; label the LH surge, the oestrogen peak, the progesterone peak and ovulation.

## 3. Traps

- Mark every hormone-level item H1–H12 as Higher Level; don't put hormone-level points in the Ordinary blocks. The Ordinary blocks may say "hormones" in general (C3).
- The LH surge, not FSH, causes ovulation. Progesterone, not oestrogen, maintains the endometrium in the luteal phase; oestrogen repairs and thickens it in the follicular phase.
- The corpus luteum makes progesterone; the pituitary makes FSH and LH. Don't say the ovary makes FSH or the pituitary makes progesterone.
- A fall in progesterone causes menstruation; don't say "low oestrogen causes the endometrium to break down" alone.
- The Graafian follicle is a mature follicle in the ovary; the corpus luteum forms from the same follicle after ovulation. They are not separate organs.
- Ovulation isn't always on day 14 (P5).
- Name the embryo's hormone in H6 only as "a hormone from the embryo"; hCG is named and explained in "Hormones in reproduction".
- Don't teach GnRH or the hypothalamus's role in the cycle; the specification names the four hormones only.
- How the pill works belongs to "Hormonal manipulation" (H10 gives one phrase); natural methods of fertility awareness belong to "Fertility control and infertility".
- Menstrual disorders were on the previous syllabus and aren't on this specification.
- Diagram cues P7, H9 and H12 only. No other diagram cues.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "Name the four phases of the menstrual cycle and state what happens in the uterus during any two of them." Model answer: P1–P4, with endometrium shed (menstruation) and thickening (follicular, luteal).
2. 12 marks, Higher: "Describe the changes in the levels of FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone during a 28-day cycle, with reference to the Graafian follicle and the corpus luteum." Model answer: H2–H6 in order, including FSH stimulating the follicle, follicle making oestrogen, oestrogen peak causing the LH surge, LH causing ovulation and the corpus luteum, the corpus luteum making progesterone, and the fall of progesterone causing menstruation.
3. 9 marks, Higher: "Explain the roles of negative and positive feedback in controlling the menstrual cycle." Model answer: H8–H9 (progesterone inhibits FSH and LH; high oestrogen triggers the LH surge), with one consequence of each.
