## 1. Must cover

The axial skeleton (lead or one bullet)
- [ ] A1 The axial skeleton forms the central axis of the body: the skull, the vertebral column (vertebrae and discs), the ribs and the sternum. Its main jobs are protecting the brain, spinal cord, heart and lungs, and supporting the head and trunk; the appendicular skeleton is attached to it (see "The appendicular skeleton").

Skull (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] K1 The skull has two parts: the cranium, a dome of curved, flat bones that encloses and protects the brain, and the facial bones, which form the face, hold the teeth and form the eye sockets (orbits) and nasal cavity.
- [ ] K2 The bones of the cranium are joined by sutures: wavy, interlocking joints filled with fibrous tissue that allow no movement, so the cranium acts as one rigid, strong box. Its domed shape spreads the force of a blow.
- [ ] K3 The lower jaw (mandible) is the only bone of the skull that moves; it is joined to the cranium at a freely movable joint on each side, allowing chewing and speech.
- [ ] K4 The skull also protects the sense organs: the eyes sit in the bony orbits, and the inner ear is enclosed in bone. At the base of the cranium a large opening (the foramen magnum) lets the spinal cord pass down from the brain, and the skull rests on the first vertebra.
- [ ] K5 In a newborn baby the cranial bones are not yet fused: soft, membrane-covered gaps called fontanelles allow the skull to change shape slightly during birth and allow the brain to grow; they close in the first two years.
- [ ] K6 Diagram: side view of the skull, labelling cranium, sutures, orbit (eye socket), facial bones, upper jaw, lower jaw (mandible), teeth and foramen magnum (or "opening for spinal cord").

Vertebral column and vertebrae (one structure-and-function block for the column; one for a vertebra)
- [ ] V1 The vertebral column (backbone or spine) is a flexible column of 33 vertebrae stacked one on another, from the skull to the pelvis. It supports the head and trunk, protects the spinal cord, provides attachment for ribs and muscles, and joins the axial skeleton to the pelvic girdle.
- [ ] V2 Five regions, each suited to its job: 7 cervical vertebrae (neck): small and light, they support the head and allow it to nod and turn. 12 thoracic vertebrae (chest): each joins a pair of ribs. 5 lumbar vertebrae (lower back): the largest and strongest, they carry most of the weight of the upper body and allow bending. 5 sacral vertebrae, fused into one bone, the sacrum, which is joined to the pelvic girdle and passes the body's weight to the legs. About 4 coccygeal vertebrae, fused to form the coccyx (tailbone), which anchors some muscles.
- [ ] V3 The first two cervical vertebrae are specialised: the atlas (first) carries the skull and allows the head to nod; the axis (second) has a peg on which the atlas turns, allowing the head to rotate from side to side.
- [ ] V4 The column has an S-shaped curve when seen from the side (curving forward in the neck, back in the chest, forward in the lower back). The curves act like a spring, absorbing shock when walking and running, and keep the body's weight balanced over the legs.
- [ ] V5 Vertebrae get larger from the neck to the lower back because each one carries the weight of everything above it.
- [ ] V6 Structure of a vertebra and how each part suits its job: the centrum (body), a thick cylinder of bone at the front, bears the body's weight; the neural arch at the back surrounds the neural canal (vertebral canal), through which the spinal cord runs, so the cord is enclosed in bone; processes (projections), the spinous process at the back and a transverse process on each side, provide attachment for muscles and ligaments, acting as levers for the back muscles; smooth surfaces called facets form small joints with the vertebrae above and below, guiding and limiting movement. In thoracic vertebrae, extra facets form the joints with the ribs.
- [ ] V7 Between neighbouring vertebrae there are small gaps on each side through which the spinal nerves leave the spinal cord (spinal nerves are in "CNS and PNS").
- [ ] V8 Diagram: (a) side view of the vertebral column labelling the cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral and coccyx regions, the S-shaped curves and the discs between vertebrae; (b) top view of a single (lumbar or thoracic) vertebra labelling centrum, neural arch, neural canal with spinal cord, spinous process, transverse processes and facets.

Intervertebral discs (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] D1 Position: an intervertebral disc lies between the centra of each pair of neighbouring vertebrae, from below the axis down to the sacrum, joining them together. There are no discs within the fused sacrum and coccyx.
- [ ] D2 Structure: each disc is a pad of cartilage with a tough outer ring of fibrous cartilage (fibrocartilage) surrounding a soft, jelly-like centre.
- [ ] D3 Function, shock absorption: the discs compress under load and spring back, cushioning the vertebrae and the brain against the jolts of walking, running and jumping.
- [ ] D4 Function, preventing friction and wear: they stop the bones of neighbouring vertebrae rubbing against each other.
- [ ] D5 Function, flexibility: each disc allows a small amount of movement between two vertebrae, so the joints between vertebrae are slightly movable; added together along the column these small movements let the back bend forwards, backwards and sideways and twist.
- [ ] D6 Function, holding the column together: the discs (with ligaments) bind the vertebrae into one column while allowing it to bend.
- [ ] D7 The discs are squeezed slightly during a day of standing and sitting and recover while lying down, so a person is slightly taller in the morning than in the evening. One bullet.

Ribs and sternum (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] R1 There are 12 pairs of ribs: curved, flat bones that form the rib cage around the chest. At the back every rib is joined to a thoracic vertebra by small joints that let it move.
- [ ] R2 At the front, the first seven pairs (true ribs) are joined directly to the sternum by strips of flexible costal cartilage; pairs 8 to 10 (false ribs) are joined to the cartilage of the rib above; pairs 11 and 12 (floating ribs) are attached only at the back.
- [ ] R3 Function: the rib cage protects the heart and lungs (and partly the liver, stomach and kidneys). Because the ribs are curved and joined by flexible cartilage, the cage can absorb blows and can move: the intercostal muscles between the ribs raise and lower the rib cage, changing the volume of the chest in breathing (mechanism in "Gas exchange and the breathing mechanism").
- [ ] R4 The sternum (breastbone) is a long, flat bone in the centre of the front of the chest. It protects the heart and anchors the ribs (through the costal cartilages) and the clavicles (see "The appendicular skeleton"). Like other flat bones it contains red bone marrow.
- [ ] R5 Diagram: front view of the rib cage labelling the sternum, ribs, costal cartilage, true, false and floating ribs, thoracic vertebrae (behind), and the position of the clavicles.

## 3. Traps

- The neural canal (with the spinal cord) is at the back of the vertebra, behind the centrum; the centrum bears the weight.
- Discs lie between the centra (bodies), not between the spinous processes, and are cartilage, not bone or "fluid sacs".
- The sacrum and coccyx are fused vertebrae, so they have no discs and no movement within them.
- Floating ribs are still attached to vertebrae at the back; they are "floating" only at the front.
- The pelvic girdle and the clavicle and scapula are part of the appendicular skeleton, not the axial skeleton.
- Slipped (prolapsed) discs, scoliosis and other disorders are not on the specification. Don't teach them.
- Spinal-cord structure (grey and white matter, nerve roots) belongs to "CNS and PNS" and "The reflex action"; V7 names spinal nerves only.
- Joint classification and synovial-joint structure belong to "Muscles and joints"; here say only that sutures are immovable and the joints between vertebrae slightly movable.
- The diagram cues in K6, V8 and R5 are the only ones wanted. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the parts of the axial skeleton; label a diagram of the skeleton or of a vertebra.
- State the position and function(s) of the intervertebral discs.
- Name the regions of the vertebral column and relate the size of vertebrae to their position.
- Give the function of the skull, ribs and sternum; explain how the structure of the rib cage suits protection and breathing.
