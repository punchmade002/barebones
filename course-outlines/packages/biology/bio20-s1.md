# Package: bio20-s1 — Glands, locations and hormones

## 1. Must cover

The endocrine system (assumed)
- [ ] H1 (assumed) A hormone is a chemical messenger made by an endocrine gland, carried in the blood, that acts on specific target cells or organs. Only cells with receptors for that hormone respond.
- [ ] H2 (assumed) Endocrine glands are ductless: they secrete hormones directly into the blood. Exocrine glands secrete through ducts (e.g. salivary and sweat glands). The pancreas is both: an exocrine gland making digestive enzymes and an endocrine gland making insulin.
- [ ] H3 Diagram: outline of the human body showing the position of the pituitary, thyroid, parathyroids, adrenals, pancreas, ovaries and testes.

One block per gland: location, hormone, functions
- [ ] G1 Pituitary gland: at the base of the brain, below and attached to the hypothalamus. Called the "master gland" because several of its hormones control other glands. Ordinary and Higher hormones:
  - TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone): stimulates the thyroid to release thyroxine.
  - Oxytocin: causes the uterus to contract during labour and causes milk to be released from the breasts during breastfeeding. It is made in the hypothalamus and released from the pituitary.
- [ ] G2 (Higher Level only block) Pituitary hormones FSH, LH and ADH:
  - FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone): in females, stimulates a follicle in the ovary to develop an egg and to produce oestrogen; in males, stimulates sperm production in the testes.
  - LH (luteinising hormone): in females, a surge of LH causes ovulation and the empty follicle to become the corpus luteum; in males, stimulates the testes to produce testosterone.
  - ADH (antidiuretic hormone): makes the collecting ducts of the kidney more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood and a smaller volume of more concentrated urine is produced. It is made in the hypothalamus and released from the pituitary.
- [ ] G3 Thyroid gland: in the neck, in front of the trachea, just below the larynx. Thyroxine controls the metabolic rate (the rate of respiration in cells) and is needed for normal growth and development. Thyroxine contains iodine.
- [ ] G4 Parathyroid glands: four small glands on the back of the thyroid. Parathyroid hormone raises the calcium level of the blood when it falls, by releasing calcium from bone and increasing calcium absorption from the gut and reabsorption in the kidneys.
- [ ] G5 Adrenal glands: one on top of each kidney. Adrenaline prepares the body for "fight or flight" in stress or danger: it increases heart rate and breathing rate, raises blood glucose by causing the liver to convert glycogen to glucose, diverts blood to the muscles, and dilates the pupils.
- [ ] G6 Pancreas: in the abdomen, behind the stomach. Clusters of cells called the islets of Langerhans secrete insulin. Insulin lowers blood glucose: it makes cells take up glucose from the blood and makes the liver and muscles convert glucose to glycogen for storage.
- [ ] G7 Testes: in the scrotum, outside the abdomen. Testosterone causes sperm production to begin at puberty and the male secondary sexual characteristics (growth of facial and body hair, deepening of the voice, increased muscle growth).
- [ ] G8 Ovaries: in the lower abdomen, one on each side of the uterus. Oestrogen causes the female secondary sexual characteristics (breast development, widening of the hips) and repairs and thickens the endometrium after menstruation. Progesterone maintains the endometrium and maintains pregnancy.

## 3. Traps

- Don't teach glucagon: the specification names insulin only for the pancreas.
- Don't add hormones the syllabus list doesn't name (no prolactin, growth hormone, cortisol or melatonin here). Prolactin is in Human Reproduction.
- Thyroid and other gland disorders (goitre, diabetes types, Graves' disease) aren't on the specification. Leave them out; insulin treatment for diabetes belongs to "Hormonal manipulation".
- The changing levels of FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone in the menstrual cycle belong to Human Reproduction. Give only the functions above.
- ADH's role in osmoregulation is detailed in The Urinary System; G2 gives one function only.
- How hormones act in feedback loops belongs to Homeostasis.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define hormone; distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands.
- Name the gland that produces a named hormone, give its location, and give one function.
- Label glands on an outline diagram of the body.
- State the effect of adrenaline on the body.
