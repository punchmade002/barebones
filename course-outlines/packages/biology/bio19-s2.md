- **loId:** `bio19-s2` · **chapterId:** `bio19` · **syllabusRef:** 2.4.4
- **Syllabus:** "role, structure and mechanisms of the reflex action (cranial nerves,
  sympathetic and parasympathetic systems are not required)." Part of outcome 4: relate the
  structure of the parts of the CNS and PNS to their functions. Ordinary and Higher level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20.

## 1. Must cover

Stimulus and response (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) A stimulus is a change in the internal or external environment that can be detected; a receptor detects it; an effector (a muscle or gland) carries out the response.

What a reflex is
- [ ] R1 A reflex action is a rapid, automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus that happens without conscious thought.
- [ ] R2 Role: protects the body from harm (e.g. pulling a hand from a hot object, blinking when something approaches the eye), and maintains posture and normal body functions (e.g. coughing and sneezing clear the airways).
- [ ] R3 Reflexes are inborn (we don't learn them), and the same stimulus always produces the same response.

The reflex arc (structure)
- [ ] R4 The reflex arc is the pathway an impulse follows in a reflex action: receptor → sensory neuron → interneuron in the grey matter of the spinal cord → motor neuron → effector.
- [ ] R5 The sensory neuron enters the spinal cord through the dorsal root; its cell body is in the dorsal root ganglion. The motor neuron leaves through the ventral root.
- [ ] R6 Diagram: reflex arc drawn through a transverse section of the spinal cord, labelling receptor (in skin), sensory neuron, dorsal root ganglion, interneuron, grey matter, white matter, motor neuron, ventral root, effector (muscle), with arrows showing the direction of the impulse.

Mechanism: the withdrawal reflex, step by step
- [ ] R7 Worked sequence for touching a hot object: pain/heat receptors in the skin of the finger are stimulated → an impulse travels along a sensory neuron to the spinal cord → it passes across a synapse to an interneuron in the grey matter → across a synapse to a motor neuron → the motor neuron carries the impulse to the biceps (effector) → the biceps contracts and the hand is pulled away.
- [ ] R8 Why reflexes are fast: the impulse travels a short route through the spinal cord, crosses few synapses, and doesn't wait for a decision by the brain.
- [ ] R9 The brain is informed afterwards: interneurons also carry impulses up the spinal cord to the brain, so pain is felt just after the hand has moved. The brain can sometimes override a reflex (e.g. holding on to a hot plate to avoid dropping it).

Other reflexes
- [ ] R10 The knee-jerk (patellar) reflex: tapping the tendon below the kneecap stretches the thigh muscle; the sensory neuron synapses directly with the motor neuron in the spinal cord (no interneuron), so the leg kicks. It is used by doctors to test the nervous system.
- [ ] R11 Some reflexes are controlled by the brain rather than the spinal cord, e.g. blinking, coughing, sneezing and the pupil narrowing in bright light. Name them only; give no nerve pathways.

## 3. Traps

- No cranial nerves, sympathetic or parasympathetic systems (the specification excludes them).
- Don't say reflexes "bypass the brain completely"; R9 is the accurate version.
- Neuron structure belongs to "Neurons"; what happens at a synapse belongs to "Neurotransmission". Here say only that the impulse "passes across a synapse".
- Spinal cord structure is described in "CNS and PNS"; don't redescribe grey and white matter beyond naming them in the arc.
- Don't use the word "reflex" for learned automatic actions such as riding a bike.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define reflex action; give one example and its benefit.
- Draw or label a reflex arc; name the neuron types in order; say where the interneuron is.
- Explain why a reflex is faster than a voluntary action.
- Name the effector in a given reflex.
