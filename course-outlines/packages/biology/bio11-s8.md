# Package: bio11-s8 — Dihybrid crosses

- **loId:** `bio11-s8` · **chapterId:** `bio11` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.7
- **Syllabus (Higher Level only):** "monohybrid and dihybrid crosses." Outcome 7: "model
  inheritance to the second generation of two unlinked traits in crosses involving homozygous
  and heterozygous parents."
- **Level:** every block in this subsection ends its heading with " (Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-26. "To the second generation" means P → F1 → F2:
  a cross of two true-breeding parents, then a cross of the F1 offspring with each other, plus
  crosses of heterozygotes with homozygotes. The standard layout and Punnett square are taught
  in "Monohybrid crosses"; the Law of Independent Assortment, its chromosome basis and
  Mendel's dihybrid figures are in "Mendel's Laws". Use them without restating them.
  Incomplete dominance is explained in "Incomplete dominance".

## 1. Must cover

What a dihybrid cross is (one block)
- [ ] D1 A dihybrid cross follows the inheritance of two different traits (two genes) at the same time, e.g. seed shape and seed colour in peas. "Unlinked" means the two genes are on different chromosomes, so they assort independently and every combination of their alleles is equally likely in the gametes. (Genes on the same chromosome are in "Linkage".)
- [ ] D2 Key for the worked examples: R = round seed, r = wrinkled; Y = yellow seed, y = green. A genotype always has two letters for each gene (four letters in all), written with the two alleles of one gene together and the dominant first: RrYy, not RYry.

Working out the gametes (one Skills block)
- [ ] G1 Each gamete gets one allele of each gene, so every gamete has two letters, one for each gene (never two of the same gene: RY, not Rr).
- [ ] G2 Method: pair each allele of the first gene with each allele of the second gene. For RrYy: R with Y, R with y, r with Y, r with y, giving RY, Ry, rY, ry, in equal numbers (the Law of Independent Assortment).
- [ ] G3 The number of gamete types depends on how many genes are heterozygous: RRYY gives one type (RY); RRYy gives two (RY, Ry); Rryy gives two (Ry, ry); RrYy gives four (RY, Ry, rY, ry); rryy gives one (ry). Write each different gamete once.

Worked example 1, part 1: P → F1 (one block, every line of the standard layout)
- [ ] F1 True-breeding round yellow × true-breeding wrinkled green. Parents' phenotypes: round yellow × wrinkled green. Parents' genotypes: RRYY × rryy. Gametes: RY × ry. F1 genotype: all RrYy. F1 phenotype: all round yellow (both dominant traits).

Worked example 1, part 2: F1 × F1 → F2 (one block; the core cross of the subsection)
- [ ] F2 Parents (F1): round yellow × round yellow. Genotypes: RrYy × RrYy. Gametes: RY, Ry, rY, ry × RY, Ry, rY, ry. Punnett square: 4 × 4 = 16 boxes, each a 1/16 chance.
- [ ] F3 The Punnett square, written row by row (gametes RY, Ry, rY, ry across the top, the same down the side): row RY: RRYY, RRYy, RrYY, RrYy. Row Ry: RRYy, RRyy, RrYy, Rryy. Row rY: RrYY, RrYy, rrYY, rrYy. Row ry: RrYy, Rryy, rrYy, rryy.
- [ ] F4 F2 phenotypes: 9 round yellow (at least one R and at least one Y) : 3 round green (at least one R, yy) : 3 wrinkled yellow (rr, at least one Y) : 1 wrinkled green (rryy). The ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 is the signature of a dihybrid F2 with two unlinked genes, each with complete dominance. Mendel's F2 counts, close to this ratio, are in "Mendel's Laws".
- [ ] F5 F2 genotypes (nine kinds): 1 RRYY, 2 RRYy, 2 RrYY, 4 RrYy, 1 RRyy, 2 Rryy, 1 rrYY, 2 rrYy, 1 rryy (total 16). Exams rarely ask for all nine; give them as one bullet.
- [ ] F6 Two of the four F2 phenotypes (round green and wrinkled yellow) are new combinations, found in neither the P nor the F1 generation. They appear because the genes assort independently, and they are a source of variation in offspring.
- [ ] F7 Diagram: the full 4 × 4 Punnett square of F3, with gametes labelled on both sides, and the boxes coloured or marked by phenotype (9 round yellow, 3 round green, 3 wrinkled yellow, 1 wrinkled green).

Shortcut and check: multiplying probabilities (one bullet)
- [ ] P1 Because the genes are independent, the dihybrid result is the two monohybrid results multiplied: Rr × Rr gives 3/4 round and 1/4 wrinkled; Yy × Yy gives 3/4 yellow and 1/4 green; so round yellow = 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16, round green = 3/4 × 1/4 = 3/16, wrinkled yellow = 3/16, wrinkled green = 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16. Use this to check a Punnett square; show the full square in an exam answer when asked for a genetic cross.

Worked example 2: double heterozygote × double homozygous recessive (one block)
- [ ] B1 Round yellow (RrYy) × wrinkled green (rryy): the dihybrid test cross. Gametes: RY, Ry, rY, ry × ry. Offspring genotypes: RrYy, Rryy, rrYy, rryy (1 : 1 : 1 : 1). Phenotypes: 1 round yellow : 1 round green : 1 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green (25% each). A 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 result shows that the dominant-looking parent was heterozygous for both genes and that the genes assort independently. The same cross with linked genes is in "Linkage".
- [ ] B2 The same pattern with other traits: in peas with tall (T) dominant to dwarf (t) and yellow (Y) dominant to green (y), TtYy × ttyy gives 1 tall yellow (TtYy) : 1 tall green (Ttyy) : 1 dwarf yellow (ttYy) : 1 dwarf green (ttyy).

Worked example 3: double heterozygote × single heterozygote (one block)
- [ ] B3 RrYy × Rryy (round yellow × round green). Gametes: RY, Ry, rY, ry × Ry, ry. Punnett square 4 × 2 = 8 boxes; column Ry: RRYy, RRyy, RrYy, Rryy; column ry: RrYy, Rryy, rrYy, rryy. Phenotypes: 3 round yellow : 3 round green : 1 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green (3 : 3 : 1 : 1). Check: 3/4 round × 1/2 yellow = 3/8 round yellow.

Worked example 4: a dihybrid cross with incomplete dominance (one block)
- [ ] C1 Snapdragons: tall (T) is dominant to dwarf (t); red (R) and white (W) flower colour show incomplete dominance (RW = pink). Homozygous dwarf red × tall pink heterozygous for height: ttRR × TtRW. Gametes: tR × TR, TW, tR, tW. Offspring: TtRR (tall, red), TtRW (tall, pink), ttRR (dwarf, red), ttRW (dwarf, pink), in a 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 ratio, so 50% of the offspring have pink flowers and 50% are tall.
- [ ] C2 To the second generation: TTRR × ttWW gives an F1 of all TtRW (tall pink); F1 × F1 gives 3 tall red : 6 tall pink : 3 tall white : 1 dwarf red : 2 dwarf pink : 1 dwarf white (out of 16), found by multiplying 3/4 tall and 1/4 dwarf by 1/4 red, 1/2 pink and 1/4 white.

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading.
- Gametes have one letter per gene (RY), never both alleles of one gene (Rr) and never four letters.
- 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 needs both parents to be heterozygous for both genes and the genes to be unlinked; don't apply it to other crosses.
- Students often list the 16 boxes correctly but miscount phenotypes; R_Y_ means at least one dominant allele of each gene.
- The statement and chromosome explanation of independent assortment belong to "Mendel's Laws"; one clause in G2 only.
- Linked genes and the changed ratios they give belong to "Linkage". Don't discuss crossing over.
- Diagram cues: only F7. No other diagram cues; write the other squares as text, column by column or row by row as in F3 and B3.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Using a genetic cross, give the genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring of a dihybrid cross (e.g. a double heterozygote × a double homozygous recessive) and the ratio.
- Give the gametes produced by a given two-gene genotype.
- Give genotypes for described parents (e.g. "homozygous tall, red") and the offspring, including crosses that combine dominance with incomplete dominance, and give a percentage of offspring with a stated phenotype.
- Explain the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Give all the possible gametes of a pea plant with the genotype TtYy and explain why they occur in equal numbers." Model answer: G2 (TY, Ty, tY, ty), with the independent-assortment reason in one sentence.
2. 15 marks, Higher: "A true-breeding round, yellow-seeded pea plant is crossed with a wrinkled, green-seeded plant. The F1 plants are crossed with each other. Using genetic crosses, show the genotypes and phenotypes of the F1 and F2 and give the F2 phenotype ratio." Model answer: F1 then F2–F4, every line of the layout, the 16-box square, 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green.
3. 12 marks, Higher: "A pea plant with the genotype RrYy (round, yellow) is crossed with a plant with the genotype Rryy (round, green). The genes are not linked. Using a Punnett square, show the genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring and give the phenotype ratio." Model answer: B3: gametes RY, Ry, rY, ry × Ry, ry; the 8-box square; 3 round yellow : 3 round green : 1 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green, checked by P1 multiplication.
