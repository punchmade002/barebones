# Package: bio23-s3 — Gametes and fertilisation

- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-29. "Mitosis and meiosis" (The Cell Cycle) already

## 1. Must cover

Gametes and meiosis (one short block)
- [ ] M1 (assumed) A gamete is a haploid sex cell (n = 23 in humans). Sperm and eggs are made by meiosis, so each carries one chromosome of each pair, and each gamete is genetically different (see "Mitosis and meiosis"). At fertilisation two haploid gametes fuse to give a diploid zygote (2n = 46). One bullet; use the terms without redefining them.
- [ ] M2 Meiosis happens only in the gonads: in the seminiferous tubules of the testes (sperm) and in the ovaries (eggs). The cells that undergo meiosis are diploid; every other cell in the body divides by mitosis.

Sperm production (one block)
- [ ] SP1 Sperm production begins at puberty and then continues every day throughout a man's life, though it declines in old age. Many millions of sperm are made each day in the walls of the seminiferous tubules, from diploid cells that first multiply by mitosis and then divide by meiosis.
- [ ] SP2 Each cell that completes meiosis gives four sperm, all of equal size. Half the sperm carry an X chromosome and half a Y (see "Sex determination").
- [ ] SP3 The new sperm move into the epididymis, where they are stored and mature, becoming able to swim (see "The male reproductive system"). It takes about two to three months for a sperm to be produced and matured, so a change in lifestyle or health affects sperm only after some weeks. Keep the figure as "about two to three months".
- [ ] SP4 Production needs the lower temperature of the scrotum and the hormones FSH and testosterone (control in "Hormones in reproduction").

Egg production (one block)
- [ ] EG1 Egg production starts before birth: while a girl is a foetus, her ovaries form all the immature eggs she will ever have, each inside a follicle, and each begins meiosis but stops part-way. She is born with about one to two million; most degenerate, and only about 400–500 are ever released in her life. No new eggs are made after birth.
- [ ] EG2 From puberty, usually one follicle each month develops fully and releases its egg at ovulation (the monthly cycle is in "The menstrual cycle"). Meiosis continues around ovulation but is completed only if a sperm enters the egg.
- [ ] EG3 Division in egg production is unequal: almost all the cytoplasm goes to one cell, the egg, and the other products are tiny cells called polar bodies, which degenerate. So one cell undergoing meiosis gives one egg, not four. This gives the egg a large store of cytoplasm, food reserves and organelles for the early embryo. Don't name the stages of meiosis.
- [ ] EG4 Because the eggs are as old as the woman, they age with her: in older women more eggs have errors in chromosome separation, which is why the risk of conditions such as Down syndrome rises with the mother's age (see "Mutations") and why fertility falls with age (see "Fertility control and infertility").

Gamete structure (one structure-and-function block for each gamete, or one paired block)
- [ ] GS1 Sperm: a very small, mobile cell, about 0.05 mm long. Head: contains the haploid nucleus (23 chromosomes) and, at its tip, the acrosome, a cap containing enzymes that digest a path through the layers around the egg. Middle piece: packed with mitochondria that release energy (ATP) by aerobic respiration to power the tail. Tail: a flagellum that beats to swim. Very little cytoplasm, which keeps the sperm small and streamlined.
- [ ] GS2 Egg (ovum): one of the largest cells in the body, about 0.1 mm across, just visible to the naked eye; it can't move by itself. It contains a haploid nucleus (23 chromosomes, always including an X), a large volume of cytoplasm with food reserves and many mitochondria, and a cell membrane. Around it is a thick, clear jelly-like coat (the zona pellucida) and an outer layer of follicle cells.
- [ ] GS3 Comparison, as paired points: size (sperm tiny, about 0.05 mm long; egg far larger, about 0.1 mm across, with a far greater volume); movement (sperm swims with a tail; egg is moved by cilia); numbers (millions of sperm per ejaculation; usually one egg a month); cytoplasm and food (sperm almost none; egg a large store); sex chromosome (X or Y; always X); production (continuous from puberty, four per meiosis; begins before birth, one per meiosis).
- [ ] GS4 Diagram: a sperm cell labelled head, acrosome, nucleus, middle piece, mitochondria, tail; and an egg labelled nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, zona pellucida (jelly coat), follicle cells. Draw the egg much larger.

From intercourse to the egg (one block)
- [ ] J1 During intercourse semen is ejaculated into the top of the vagina, near the cervix. Sperm swim through the cervical mucus (easiest around ovulation, when it is thin), through the uterus and into the fallopian tubes, helped by contractions of the uterus. Only a few hundred of the millions of sperm reach the egg; most die in the acidic vagina, are blocked by mucus, or go into the tube with no egg.
- [ ] J2 Survival times: sperm can survive up to about five days in the female tract; an egg survives only about 12–24 hours after ovulation. So intercourse in the five days before ovulation or on the day of ovulation can lead to fertilisation (used in "Fertility control and infertility").

Fertilisation (one process block, numbered steps)
- [ ] F1 Fertilisation is the fusion of the haploid nucleus of a sperm with the haploid nucleus of an egg to form a diploid zygote. In humans it normally takes place in the upper part of a fallopian tube, within a day of ovulation.
- [ ] F2 Steps: 1. Sperm reach the egg and push between the follicle cells. 2. The acrosome of a sperm releases its enzymes, which digest a path through the zona pellucida (the acrosome reaction). 3. The membrane of one sperm fuses with the egg's membrane, and the sperm's nucleus enters the egg; the tail is left outside. 4. At once the egg's outer layer changes and the zona pellucida hardens, so no other sperm can enter: this ensures the zygote receives only one set of chromosomes from the father. 5. The egg completes its meiosis. 6. The sperm nucleus and egg nucleus fuse, giving a zygote with 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, one of each pair from each parent.
- [ ] F3 At fertilisation the sex of the child is decided by whether the sperm carries an X or a Y (see "Sex determination"), and the zygote receives its mitochondria from the egg only (see "Nuclear and non-nuclear inheritance"). One bullet; name the titles.
- [ ] F4 Outcomes of fertilisation: restores the diploid number; combines genetic material from two parents, so the offspring is genetically unique (with meiosis, a source of variation); and starts the development of the embryo. The zygote then begins to divide by mitosis as it moves down the tube (continued in "Pregnancy").
- [ ] F5 Twins (assumed, one bullet): identical twins come from one zygote that splits into two embryos early in development, so they have the same genes; non-identical (fraternal) twins come from two eggs released at the same time and fertilised by two different sperm, so they are no more alike genetically than other brothers and sisters.
- [ ] F6 Diagram: a sequence showing a sperm entering an egg (acrosome digesting the zona pellucida), the sperm nucleus inside the egg, and the two nuclei fusing to form the zygote; label sperm nucleus, egg nucleus, zona pellucida, zygote, n and 2n.

## 3. Traps

- Don't teach the stages of meiosis (meiosis I and II, prophase I and so on), or the names of the cell stages in sperm and egg formation (spermatogonia, spermatocytes, oogonia, oocytes): they aren't on the specification. Crossing over is named in "Mitosis and meiosis" only.
- Don't give a ratio of egg to sperm volume; GS3 says "far larger".
- Fertilisation is fusion of the nuclei, not just a sperm "entering" or "touching" the egg.
- Fertilisation happens in the fallopian tube, not in the uterus; implantation happens in the uterus (see "Pregnancy").
- A sperm's X or Y decides sex; the egg always carries an X. Don't say the mother decides the sex.
- The sperm's mitochondria power swimming but are not passed on (F3).
- Don't repeat the pathway of sperm through the male system (it's in "The male reproductive system"); J1 starts in the vagina.
- Hormonal control of sperm and egg production belongs to "Hormones in reproduction" and "The menstrual cycle".
- Diagram cues GS4 and F6 only. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the type of cell division that produces gametes; state the number of chromosomes in a human sperm or egg.
- Draw a sperm and show where the mitochondria are; explain why the middle piece has many mitochondria.
- State where fertilisation takes place; define fertilisation.
- Compare sperm and egg.
- Suggest a sperm-structure cause of male infertility (e.g. faulty tail, so poor swimming).
