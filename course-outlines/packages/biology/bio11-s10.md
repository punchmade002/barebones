# Package: bio11-s10 — Sex determination

- **loId:** `bio11-s10` · **chapterId:** `bio11` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.9
- **Syllabus:** Outcome 9: "model sex determination by X and Y chromosomes in humans."
  Ordinary and Higher level. No Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-26. "Model" means set out the X/Y cross in the
  standard layout (from "Monohybrid crosses") and explain the result. Genes carried on the X
  chromosome (colour blindness, haemophilia) belong to "Sex-linked traits", which is Higher
  Level; keep this subsection to sex itself so Ordinary Level students have it complete.

## 1. Must cover

Human chromosomes (one block)
- [ ] C1 (assumed) One-clause reminder only, as the numbers are taught in "Chromosome structure and genes": 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, 22 pairs of autosomes (the same in both sexes) and one pair of sex chromosomes, which decide the person's sex.
- [ ] C2 Females have two X chromosomes (XX); males have one X and one Y (XY). The X is a large chromosome carrying many genes (roughly 800–900 protein-coding genes); the Y is much smaller and carries few genes (well under 100), most of them concerned with male development and sperm production.
- [ ] C3 A karyotype is a photograph or diagram of a cell's chromosomes arranged in their pairs by size; it shows the sex chromosomes as XX or XY. Diagram: a simplified karyotype of 22 numbered autosome pairs plus the sex-chromosome pair, with a female (XX) and a male (XY) version of pair 23, the Y drawn clearly smaller than the X.

Gametes and the cross (one block, the standard layout)
- [ ] G1 Gametes are haploid, with 23 chromosomes: 22 autosomes and one sex chromosome. All of a woman's eggs carry an X (23,X). A man's sperm carry either an X (23,X) or a Y (23,Y), in equal numbers, because the X and Y separate into different cells in meiosis.
- [ ] G2 The cross. Parents' phenotypes: female × male. Parents' genotypes: XX × XY. Gametes: X × X, Y. Punnett square: XX, XX / XY, XY (or 2 × 1: XX, XY). Offspring genotypes: 1 XX : 1 XY. Offspring phenotypes: 1 female : 1 male (50% each).
- [ ] G3 Diagram: the Punnett square of G2, with the mother's egg (X) on one side and the father's sperm (X and Y) on the other, each box labelled with genotype and sex.
- [ ] G4 Conclusions: the sex of a child is decided at fertilisation by the sperm: an X-carrying sperm gives a girl (XX); a Y-carrying sperm gives a boy (XY). So the father's gamete decides the sex; the mother always gives an X. Every pregnancy has an independent 1 in 2 chance of a girl or a boy, whatever the sex of earlier children; across a large population the sex ratio is close to 1 : 1.
- [ ] G5 A boy receives his X from his mother and his Y from his father; a girl receives one X from each parent. (This is why X-linked traits pass from mother to son; see "Sex-linked traits".)

How the Y chromosome causes maleness (one block)
- [ ] Y1 The Y chromosome carries the SRY gene (sex-determining region of the Y). In an XY embryo, from about the sixth to seventh week of development, SRY switches on the development of the undifferentiated gonads into testes. The testes then make testosterone, which brings about the development of the male reproductive system.
- [ ] Y2 In an XX embryo, with no Y and so no SRY, the gonads develop into ovaries and the female reproductive system develops. So it is the presence of a Y (SRY), not the number of X chromosomes, that makes the embryo male.

## 3. Traps

- The father's sperm decides the sex, not the mother's egg.
- "Sex chromosomes" are not only in gametes: every body cell has the pair. Gametes have one sex chromosome; body cells have two.
- Previous children do not change the 50% chance for the next child.
- Don't teach other sex-determining systems (birds ZW, temperature in reptiles, haplodiploidy in bees): the outcome is for humans only.
- Don't teach sex-chromosome number disorders (Turner, Klinefelter) or intersex conditions; not on the specification.
- Genes on the X (colour blindness, haemophilia), carriers and pedigree charts belong to "Sex-linked traits" (Higher Level); G5 points to it in one clause.
- The production of sex hormones and the reproductive system belong to "Human Reproduction"; Y1 mentions testosterone only as the link.
- Meiosis belongs to "Mitosis and meiosis"; G1 uses it in one clause.
- Diagram cues: only C3 and G3. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the sex chromosomes of a male and a female.
- Using a genetic cross, show how sex is determined in humans and explain why there is an equal chance of a boy or a girl.
- Explain why the father determines the sex of the child.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "State the sex chromosomes found in a human male and female, and the sex chromosomes found in their gametes." Model answer: C2 (XY, XX) and G1 (sperm X or Y, eggs X).
2. 9 marks: "Using a genetic cross, show how sex is inherited in humans and explain why the chance of having a daughter is 50%." Model answer: G2 in the standard layout and G4 (half the sperm carry X, all eggs carry X, random fertilisation).
3. 6 marks: "A couple have three sons. Explain whether their next child is more likely to be a daughter." Model answer: G4: each fertilisation is independent; the chance stays 50%; the sex depends on which sperm fertilises the egg.
