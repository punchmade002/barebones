## 1. Must cover

The molecules and their roles (one structure-and-function block, one bullet per molecule)
- [ ] O1 [HL] DNA: holds the gene, the base sequence that codes for the protein. In transcription only one of the two strands of the gene is read (the template strand); the DNA is not used up or changed, and stays in the nucleus.
- [ ] O2 [HL] RNA polymerase: the enzyme that carries out transcription. It binds to the DNA at the start of a gene, unwinds and separates the two strands there, joins free RNA nucleotides into a strand complementary to the template, and moves along the gene until it reaches the end.
- [ ] O3 [HL] mRNA (messenger RNA): a single-stranded copy of one gene's code, a series of codons, which carries the code from the nucleus to a ribosome. It is short-lived and broken down after use.
- [ ] O4 [HL] tRNA (transfer RNA): small RNA molecules in the cytoplasm, each carrying one specific amino acid, which they deliver to the ribosome; each tRNA's anticodon pairs with a matching codon on the mRNA, so it is the molecule that "translates" codons into amino acids.
- [ ] O5 [HL] rRNA (ribosomal RNA): with proteins, makes up the ribosome. It holds the mRNA and tRNAs in position, and the rRNA of the large subunit catalyses the formation of peptide bonds between amino acids (it acts as an enzyme).
- [ ] O6 [HL] Ribosome: the site of translation, made of rRNA and protein in two subunits, a small subunit that binds the mRNA and a large subunit with room for two tRNAs at a time. It moves along the mRNA one codon at a time. Ribosomes are free in the cytoplasm (making proteins used in the cell) or attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum (making proteins for export or for membranes; see "Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus" in "Structures and organelles of animal and plant cells").

Transcription (one process block, numbered)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Transcription is the making of an mRNA copy of a gene. It takes place in the nucleus.
- [ ] T2 [HL] 1. RNA polymerase attaches to the DNA at the start of the gene, unwinds the double helix there and separates the two strands by breaking the hydrogen bonds.
- [ ] T3 [HL] 2. One strand, the template strand, is read. Free RNA nucleotides pair with its exposed bases by complementary base pairing: A on DNA pairs with U on RNA, T with A, C with G, and G with C.
- [ ] T4 [HL] 3. RNA polymerase joins the RNA nucleotides into a strand of mRNA as it moves along the gene. Behind it, the DNA strands re-join and rewind.
- [ ] T5 [HL] 4. At the end of the gene, RNA polymerase detaches and the finished mRNA is released. The mRNA leaves the nucleus through a nuclear pore and goes to a ribosome.
- [ ] T6 [HL] Diagram: a section of DNA unwound by RNA polymerase, with the template strand and the mRNA strand growing alongside it; label DNA, template strand, RNA polymerase, free RNA nucleotides, mRNA, base pair A–U, direction of movement.

The structure of tRNA (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] A tRNA is a single strand of about 80 RNA nucleotides that folds back on itself, held in shape by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases in some regions, giving a clover-leaf shape (in two dimensions; in three dimensions it folds further into an L shape).
- [ ] R2 [HL] At one end of the folded molecule is the amino-acid attachment site, where one specific amino acid is attached (by a specific enzyme, using energy from ATP).
- [ ] R3 [HL] At the opposite end is a loop with the anticodon: three unpaired bases that pair with a complementary codon on mRNA.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Relating structure to the codon nature of the code: because the code is read in codons of three bases, each tRNA has an anticodon of exactly three bases, so each tRNA matches one codon (three bases of mRNA) and brings one amino acid; the attachment of a specific amino acid to each tRNA with a specific anticodon is what links each codon to its amino acid. The ribosome holds two tRNAs side by side on neighbouring codons, so the code is read three bases at a time without gaps or overlaps.
- [ ] R5 [HL] Diagram: a clover-leaf tRNA, labelled: single RNA strand, regions of base pairing (hydrogen bonds), anticodon loop with three bases, amino-acid attachment site, amino acid.

Translation (one process block, numbered)
- [ ] L1 [HL] Translation is the making of a polypeptide from the code in mRNA. It takes place at ribosomes in the cytoplasm.
- [ ] L2 [HL] 1. Start: the small ribosomal subunit attaches to the mRNA and moves along it to the start codon (AUG). A tRNA with the anticodon UAC, carrying methionine, pairs with the start codon. The large subunit then joins.
- [ ] L3 [HL] 2. A second tRNA, whose anticodon is complementary to the next codon, arrives carrying its amino acid and pairs with that codon in the second position in the ribosome.
- [ ] L4 [HL] 3. A peptide bond forms between the two amino acids (a condensation reaction, releasing water), catalysed by the rRNA of the ribosome. The first tRNA's amino acid is now attached to the second amino acid.
- [ ] L5 [HL] 4. The ribosome moves along the mRNA by one codon (three bases). The first tRNA, now without its amino acid, is released into the cytoplasm, where it can pick up another of the same amino acid. A new tRNA pairs with the codon now in the empty position.
- [ ] L6 [HL] 5. Steps 2–4 repeat, the chain growing by one amino acid each time, in the order set by the codons.
- [ ] L7 [HL] 6. End: when a stop codon (UAA, UAG or UGA) is reached, no tRNA matches it; a protein release factor binds instead, the polypeptide is released, and the ribosome's subunits separate from the mRNA.
- [ ] L8 [HL] Many ribosomes can translate the same mRNA at once, one behind the other (a polysome), so many copies of the protein are made quickly. Translation needs energy, both to attach amino acids to their tRNAs (from ATP) and to move the ribosome along the mRNA.
- [ ] L9 [HL] Diagram: a ribosome (small and large subunits) on an mRNA strand, with two tRNAs paired to neighbouring codons, a growing polypeptide chain attached to one, a peptide bond forming, and a released tRNA leaving; label mRNA, codon, anticodon, tRNA, amino acid, peptide bond, ribosome (large and small subunits), polypeptide, direction of movement.

From polypeptide to functional protein (one block)
- [ ] F1 [HL] The sequence of amino acids (the primary structure) determines how the chain folds: bonds form between amino acids in different parts of the chain (e.g. hydrogen bonds, and in some proteins sulphur bridges), giving the protein its particular three-dimensional shape. Some proteins are made of more than one polypeptide (e.g. haemoglobin has four).
- [ ] F2 [HL] The shape determines the function, e.g. the active site of an enzyme or the binding site of an antibody or receptor (see "Genes, proteins and traits"). A change in the amino-acid sequence can change the shape and stop the protein working (see "Mutations").
- [ ] F3 [HL] Proteins made on the rough endoplasmic reticulum are folded and modified there and in the Golgi apparatus, then packaged into vesicles for export (e.g. digestive enzymes, insulin). One bullet; detail in "Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus".
- [ ] F4 [HL] In prokaryotes, which have no nucleus, transcription and translation both take place in the cytoplasm, and ribosomes can begin translating an mRNA while it is still being transcribed.

Replication and transcription compared (one comparison block, paired points)
- [ ] C1 [HL] Product: replication makes a complete copy of all the DNA (two DNA molecules); transcription makes an RNA copy of one gene (mRNA).
- [ ] C2 [HL] Strands used: replication uses both strands as templates; transcription uses only one strand of the gene.
- [ ] C3 [HL] Enzyme: DNA helicase and DNA polymerase vs RNA polymerase.
- [ ] C4 [HL] Nucleotides and pairing: DNA nucleotides with deoxyribose, A–T vs RNA nucleotides with ribose, A on DNA pairing with U.
- [ ] C5 [HL] When: replication once per cycle in S phase; transcription throughout the cell's life, of whichever genes are needed, many times.
- [ ] C6 [HL] Fate of the product: new DNA stays in the nucleus; mRNA leaves for the cytoplasm and is broken down after use.

Modelling transcription and translation (one skills block, with the worked example)
- [ ] M1 [HL] Method: 1. write the mRNA complementary to the template strand (A → U, T → A, C → G, G → C); 2. divide it into codons from the start codon; 3. write the tRNA anticodon complementary to each codon; 4. use the codon table given to find the amino acids; 5. stop at a stop codon.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Worked example: template DNA TAC AAA CCG ATT → mRNA AUG UUU GGC UAA → anticodons UAC, AAA, CCG (none for UAA) → polypeptide methionine–phenylalanine–glycine, then stop. Three codons for amino acids, so three amino acids and two peptide bonds.
- [ ] M3 [HL] Physical or role-play model: paper strips for DNA (in a "nucleus" area) are copied onto mRNA strips that are carried to a "ribosome" area; cards for tRNA have a three-base anticodon on one side and an amino acid on the other; students match anticodons to codons in order and link the amino acids with clips (peptide bonds). Limitations: it is slow, flat and ignores the enzymes, energy and folding.

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading.
- RNA polymerase is the polymerase in transcription; DNA polymerase is in replication ("Modelling DNA replication and information flow"). The syllabus says "polymerase": teach RNA polymerase here.
- Transcription is in the nucleus; translation is at ribosomes in the cytoplasm. mRNA, not DNA, goes to the ribosome. The ribosome reads codons; it does not "make" the code.
- Codons are on mRNA, anticodons on tRNA; tRNA brings amino acids, it doesn't make them. The anticodon matches the codon, not the DNA triplet (though it has the same sequence as the template strand with U for T).
- Peptide bonds join amino acids; hydrogen bonds join codon to anticodon.
- Don't teach introns and exons, splicing, the 5′ cap or poly-A tail, promoter and terminator sequences by name, the A, P and E sites, wobble pairing, or the names of aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases or initiation and elongation factors; they are not on the specification. Coding and non-coding DNA belong to "Chromosome structure and genes".
- The features of the code (triplet, degenerate, universal, non-overlapping) and the decoding method belong to "The genetic code"; M1 is the procedure applied to transcription and translation, and M2 uses a new sequence. Don't restate the features.
- The overview "DNA → mRNA → protein" and why mRNA is used belong to "Modelling DNA replication and information flow".
- Diagram cues: T6, R5 and L9 only. No other diagram cues.
