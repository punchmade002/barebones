# Package: bio14-s3 — The light-dependent reactions

- **loId:** `bio14-s3` · **chapterId:** `bio14` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2.4
- **Syllabus (Higher Level only):** "light dependent reactions (non-cyclic pathway) involves
  the absorption of a photon of light, passed from one pigment molecule to others in the
  chloroplast until it reaches a reaction centre in a chlorophyll molecule. Photolysis of water
  due to attraction from positively charged chlorophyll molecule results in production of
  oxygen and electron transfer results in the production of ATP and NADPH (from NADP+); light
  dependent reactions (cyclic pathway) – some electrons return to chlorophyll and transfer
  surplus energy to the formation of ATP in an alternative pathway." Outcome 4: "model the
  two-stage processes of photosynthesis and respiration; make particular reference to the role
  of transfer molecules." The second stage, and the model of the two stages together, are in
  "The light-independent reactions".

- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-26. Keep to the specification's level of detail: one
  reaction centre, one electron transport chain, no named photosystems. ATP is assumed from
  "ATP"; NADP⁺ as an electron carrier, oxidation and reduction, and the NADP⁺ + 2e⁻ + H⁺ →
  NADPH equation are assumed from "NAD⁺ and NADP⁺" and are used here without redefining them.
  The structure of the thylakoid membrane (where the pigments, chain and ATP synthase sit, and

## 1. Must cover

Capturing light: photons, pigments and the reaction centre (one block, process)
- [ ] C1 (assumed) Light travels as tiny packets of energy called photons. A pigment is a molecule that absorbs light of particular colours (wavelengths).
- [ ] C2 The thylakoid membranes hold several pigments: chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b and the yellow and orange carotenoids. Having several pigments widens the range of colours of light the plant can use.
- [ ] C3 The pigment molecules are grouped in clusters (light-harvesting or antenna complexes) of hundreds of molecules around a reaction centre. When a pigment molecule absorbs a photon, the energy is passed from one pigment molecule to the next until it reaches the reaction centre, a special chlorophyll molecule (chlorophyll a).
- [ ] C4 At the reaction centre the energy raises (excites) an electron of the chlorophyll to a high energy level. This high-energy (energised) electron leaves the chlorophyll molecule and is taken up by the first electron acceptor of an electron transport chain.
- [ ] C5 Having lost a negatively charged electron, the reaction-centre chlorophyll molecule is left positively charged. It must regain an electron before it can absorb energy and release an electron again.

Non-cyclic pathway (one block, process; numbered stages)
- [ ] N1 1. Light energy reaches the reaction centre and energised electrons leave the chlorophyll (C3–C4).
- [ ] N2 2. Electron transfer: the high-energy electrons are passed along an electron transport chain, a series of electron carriers in the thylakoid membrane. At each transfer they lose some energy, and this energy is used to make ATP from ADP and Pi.
- [ ] N3 3. At the end of the chain the electrons (now at lower energy) are passed to NADP⁺. Each NADP⁺ takes two electrons and one proton (H⁺), forming NADPH. NADPH carries the electrons and hydrogen to the light-independent reactions.
- [ ] N4 4. Photolysis: the positively charged chlorophyll molecule strongly attracts electrons, and it pulls them from water molecules. This splits the water (photolysis means "splitting by light", because the energy for it comes ultimately from light): 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂.
- [ ] N5 5. Products of photolysis: the electrons replace those lost by the chlorophyll, so it is neutral again and ready to absorb more energy; the protons (H⁺) are used in forming NADPH (and contribute to the proton gradient described in "The chloroplast's internal structure"); the oxygen is a by-product, used in respiration or released through the stomata.
- [ ] N6 Why "non-cyclic": the electrons travel in one direction only, from water through chlorophyll and the chain to NADP⁺; they don't return to the chlorophyll they came from. It produces ATP, NADPH and oxygen.

Cyclic pathway (one block, process)
- [ ] Y1 In the cyclic pathway some energised electrons leaving the reaction-centre chlorophyll are passed along carriers of the electron transport chain and then return to the same chlorophyll molecule instead of going to NADP⁺.
- [ ] Y2 As they pass along the carriers, they transfer their surplus energy to the formation of ATP from ADP and Pi. When they arrive back, the chlorophyll is neutral again, so no electrons are taken from water.
- [ ] Y3 Products: ATP only. No NADPH is made, water is not split and no oxygen is released.
- [ ] Y4 Why it is needed: the light-independent reactions use more ATP than NADPH (3 ATP for every 2 NADPH for each CO₂ molecule fixed), so the cyclic pathway tops up the supply of ATP. It also runs when NADP⁺ is in short supply (when most is already loaded as NADPH), so the energised electrons have somewhere to go.

Comparing the two pathways (one comparison block, paired points)
- [ ] K1 Route of electrons: non-cyclic, one way from water to NADP⁺; cyclic, from chlorophyll round the carriers and back to chlorophyll.
- [ ] K2 Products: non-cyclic, ATP, NADPH and O₂; cyclic, ATP only.
- [ ] K3 Water: split (photolysis) in non-cyclic; not used in cyclic.
- [ ] K4 Final electron acceptor: NADP⁺ in non-cyclic; the chlorophyll molecule itself in cyclic.
- [ ] K5 Both: begin with light energy reaching the reaction centre and an energised electron leaving chlorophyll; both pass electrons along carriers in the thylakoid membrane and make ATP; both take place on the thylakoid membranes.
- [ ] K6 Diagram: the two pathways on one energy diagram. Show a chlorophyll molecule at the bottom receiving light (a wavy arrow) from a cluster of pigment molecules; an arrow for the energised electron rising to an electron acceptor; then (non-cyclic) a downhill chain of carriers labelled "ATP made" ending at NADP⁺ + H⁺ → NADPH, with water entering below the chlorophyll as H₂O → 2H⁺ + ½O₂ + 2e⁻ and the electrons going to chlorophyll; and (cyclic) a branch from the chain looping back to the chlorophyll labelled "ATP made". Label photon, pigment molecules, reaction centre (chlorophyll), electron acceptor, electron transport chain, ATP, NADP⁺, NADPH, photolysis, O₂, H⁺.

Summary of the light-dependent reactions (one block)
- [ ] S1 Location: thylakoid membranes (grana) of the chloroplast, where the pigments and carriers are held (see "The chloroplast's internal structure").
- [ ] S2 Inputs: light energy, water, ADP + Pi and NADP⁺. Outputs: ATP and NADPH (passed to the stroma for the light-independent reactions) and oxygen (by-product).
- [ ] S3 Energy conversion: light energy becomes chemical energy, held in ATP and in the high-energy electrons of NADPH.
- [ ] S4 Transfer molecules: ATP carries energy and NADPH carries electrons and hydrogen (reducing power) to the light-independent reactions; ADP + Pi and NADP⁺ come back to be reloaded. The full two-stage model is drawn in "The light-independent reactions".

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading. All key terms (photon, reaction centre, photolysis, non-cyclic pathway, cyclic pathway, electron transport chain, NADPH) are Higher Level.
- Specification wording, followed deliberately: it describes non-cyclic flow with a single reaction centre, and says water is split "due to attraction from" the positively charged chlorophyll. Real chloroplasts use two photosystems in series (the "Z-scheme"), and the water is split by an enzyme complex driven by the positively charged chlorophyll. Teach the specification's version (N4) and don't add photosystems, the Z-scheme or the water-splitting enzyme.
- Photolysis is not "light hitting water and splitting it"; light is absorbed by the pigments, and the positively charged chlorophyll pulls electrons from water (N4).
- The oxygen comes from water, not CO₂ (taught in "Photosynthesis as an anabolic process"; don't repeat the isotope evidence).
- Older Irish textbooks and past papers call the cyclic pathway "pathway 1" and the non-cyclic pathway "pathway 2", and describe NADP⁺ first gaining two electrons to become NADP⁻ before attracting a proton. One bullet may note these older names, so students recognise them; teach N3 as the reaction.
- NADPH, not "NADPH₂" or "NADH". NAD⁺ belongs to respiration.
- In the cyclic pathway, water isn't split, so there is no oxygen and no NADPH; students often say cyclic "makes NADPH".
- Energy is lost by electrons along the chain, not by the ATP. Electrons are not "used up"; in non-cyclic flow they end on NADPH.
- How the energy from the chain is coupled to ATP synthesis (protons pumped into the thylakoid space, ATP synthase) belongs to "The chloroplast's internal structure" and, in full, to "The mitochondrion's internal structure and ATP synthase". N2 and Y2 say only that the energy is used to make ATP.
- What NADPH and ATP do next belongs to "The light-independent reactions".
- No other diagram cues beyond K6.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the particles released by chlorophyll when it absorbs light (energised electrons).
- Describe the non-cyclic pathway: route of the electrons, photolysis of water, production of ATP, NADPH and oxygen.
- Distinguish between the cyclic and non-cyclic pathways in terms of electron movement and products.
- State the products of the light-dependent stage that pass to the light-independent stage, and the role of each.
- State the source of the oxygen given off and of the electrons that replace those lost by chlorophyll.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Distinguish between the cyclic and non-cyclic pathways of the light-dependent reactions." Model answer: K1–K3 (electrons return to chlorophyll vs go to NADP⁺; ATP only vs ATP, NADPH and O₂; water split only in non-cyclic).
2. 12 marks, Higher: "Describe the events of the non-cyclic pathway, from the absorption of a photon to the production of NADPH and oxygen." Model answer: C3–C5, N2–N6. Must include pigment-to-pigment transfer to the reaction centre, the positive charge on chlorophyll and photolysis.
3. 9 marks, Higher: "Explain why a chloroplast needs the cyclic pathway as well as the non-cyclic pathway." Model answer: Y1–Y3 and Y4 (extra ATP for the light-independent reactions; electrons have an acceptor when NADP⁺ is scarce).
