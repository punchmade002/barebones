## 1. Must cover

What mechanical breakdown does (one block, three bullets)
- [ ] M1 Mechanical (physical) digestion breaks food into smaller pieces, or fat into smaller droplets, without changing the molecules: no chemical bonds are broken and no new substances are formed. Chemical digestion (by enzymes) breaks bonds; see "Chemical breakdown".
- [ ] M2 Why it matters: smaller pieces have a much larger total surface area, so enzymes can act on more of the food at once and digestion is faster. It also makes food soft and moist enough to swallow, and mixes food with digestive juices.
- [ ] M3 The four mechanisms: chewing by the teeth (mouth), peristalsis (whole gut), churning (stomach), and emulsification of fats by bile salts (duodenum).

The role of teeth (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] T1 Chewing (mastication) breaks food into small pieces and mixes it with saliva; the tongue rolls the moistened food into a bolus for swallowing. Mucus in saliva lubricates the bolus.
- [ ] T2 Incisors: at the front; chisel-shaped with sharp, flat edges, for biting and cutting food.
- [ ] T3 Canines: one on each side of the incisors in each jaw; pointed, for gripping and tearing food. Human canines are small compared with those of carnivores.
- [ ] T4 Premolars and molars: at the back; broad, with ridged surfaces (cusps), for crushing and grinding food. Premolars have two cusps; molars are larger with more cusps. The jaw moves sideways as well as up and down, so molars grind.
- [ ] T5 (assumed) Dental formula: the number of each type of tooth in one half of the upper jaw over one half of the lower jaw, in the order incisors, canines, premolars, molars. Adult human: 2.1.2.3 over 2.1.2.3, so 16 teeth on each side of the mouth (8 in each quarter) and 32 teeth in total. Child's milk (deciduous) teeth: 2.1.0.2 over 2.1.0.2, 20 teeth in total; they have no premolars. The third molars are the wisdom teeth, which appear last (often in late teens or later) or not at all.
- [ ] T6 (assumed) Structure of a tooth: the crown above the gum and the root fixed in the jawbone. Enamel, the hardest substance in the body, covers the crown; dentine, a bone-like layer, lies beneath; the pulp cavity in the centre contains nerves and blood vessels; cement holds the root in its socket.
- [ ] T7 Tooth decay (health link): bacteria in plaque on the teeth feed on sugars and release acid, which dissolves enamel and then dentine, forming a cavity. Prevention: less frequent sugary food and drink, brushing with fluoride toothpaste, and fluoridated water (fluoride hardens enamel; see "Minerals"), and regular dental check-ups.
- [ ] T8 Diagram: (a) the four types of teeth side by side, labelled incisor, canine, premolar, molar, with their roles; (b) a vertical section of a molar labelled crown, root, enamel, dentine, pulp cavity, nerves and blood vessels, gum, cement and jawbone.

Peristalsis (one process block)
- [ ] P1 Peristalsis is a wave of muscular contraction that passes along the wall of the gut, pushing food along. It happens from the oesophagus to the rectum, and also in the ureters.
- [ ] P2 How it works: the gut wall has an inner layer of circular muscle and an outer layer of longitudinal muscle (smooth, involuntary muscle). The circular muscle just behind the bolus contracts, narrowing the gut and squeezing the food forwards; the circular muscle just in front relaxes, widening the gut to receive it. The wave then moves along.
- [ ] P3 Peristalsis is automatic (involuntary) once food is swallowed, and works against gravity: a person can swallow while lying down or upside down.
- [ ] P4 It also mixes food with digestive juices and breaks it up, so it is part of mechanical breakdown; in the small intestine it keeps moving the contents past the absorbing surface.
- [ ] P5 Dietary fibre adds bulk that the muscles can grip and push against, so a high-fibre diet keeps peristalsis effective and prevents constipation (diet detail in "Diet").
- [ ] P6 Diagram: a section of the gut showing a bolus with contracted circular muscle (narrow) behind it and relaxed muscle (wide) in front, with an arrow for the direction of movement; label circular muscle, longitudinal muscle, bolus, contraction, relaxation.

The stomach (one block)
- [ ] S1 The stomach wall has three layers of smooth muscle (longitudinal, circular and an oblique, diagonal layer). Their contractions churn the food, breaking it up and mixing it thoroughly with gastric juice.
- [ ] S2 After a few hours of churning, food becomes chyme, a thick, creamy liquid. The sphincters at the entrance and exit stay closed during churning, so food is held in the stomach.
- [ ] S3 The pyloric sphincter relaxes now and then to let small amounts of chyme into the duodenum, so the small intestine is never overloaded and has time to digest and neutralise each portion.
- [ ] S4 The stomach's chemical role (acid and pepsin) is in "Chemical breakdown"; one phrase here.

Bile salts and emulsification (one process block)
- [ ] B1 Fats and oils do not mix with the watery contents of the gut; they form large droplets, so lipase (which works in water) can reach only the droplet surfaces.
- [ ] B2 Bile salts are made by the liver and enter the duodenum in bile. Each bile-salt molecule has one end that attracts water (hydrophilic) and one that attracts fat (hydrophobic). They gather on the surface of fat droplets, fat-loving end inwards, and with the churning of the gut they break the large droplets into many tiny droplets and stop them joining together again. This is emulsification; the mixture of tiny droplets in water is an emulsion.
- [ ] B3 Emulsification is mechanical (physical): the fat molecules are unchanged and no bonds are broken. It greatly increases the surface area of fat exposed to lipase, so fat is digested much faster.
- [ ] B4 Bile contains no enzymes. Its other roles (neutralising stomach acid and excreting bile pigments) are in "Chemical breakdown".
- [ ] B5 Everyday comparison (one bullet): washing-up liquid emulsifies grease in the same way, which is why bile salts are often compared to a detergent.
- [ ] B6 Diagram: one large fat droplet → after bile salts, many small droplets, each coated with bile-salt molecules; label "large surface area for lipase". Optional; may be combined with the fat model in "Chemical breakdown".

## 3. Traps

- Mechanical breakdown changes size, not molecules. Don't say bile "digests" fat or "breaks fat into fatty acids": that is lipase.
- Bile salts emulsify; bile pigments are wastes. Don't confuse them.
- The dental formula counts one side of the mouth only (upper over lower), so multiply by 2 for the total.
- Herbivore and carnivore dentition comparisons were asked on the previous syllabus; give T3's one comparison only, no other animals' formulae.
- Gum disease, braces, tooth whitening, root canals and fillings are not on the specification.
- Peristalsis is not swallowing; swallowing starts it. The epiglottis is mentioned in "The digestive system".
- Enzymes, pH, gastric juice and pancreatic juice belong to "Chemical breakdown". The roles of organs belong to "The digestive system".
- The diagram cues listed (T8, P6, B6) are the only ones wanted. No other diagram cues.
