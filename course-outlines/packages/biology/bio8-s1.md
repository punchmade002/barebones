## 1. Must cover

What biomolecules are (one block)
- [ ] B1 A biomolecule is a molecule made by living organisms. The four main groups are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids. They are organic: built around chains or rings of carbon atoms bonded to hydrogen. Water and minerals are also chemicals of life but are inorganic (covered in "Water" and "Minerals"). The elements in each biomolecule are listed in "Structures of biomolecules".
- [ ] B2 (assumed) Food supplies three things: energy (released in respiration, mainly from carbohydrates and lipids), raw materials for growth and repair (mainly proteins), and substances needed to regulate body processes (vitamins and minerals; the body makes its own enzymes and hormones from the food it takes in). One bullet; detail is in "Metabolic roles and nutritional sources".

Building and breaking: structural units (one block)
- [ ] U1 (assumed) A monomer is a small unit molecule; a polymer is a large molecule made of many monomers joined in a chain. Monosaccharides are the monomers of polysaccharides; amino acids are the monomers of polypeptides and proteins; nucleotides are the monomers of nucleic acids. A triglyceride is built from glycerol and three fatty acids but is not a repeating polymer.
- [ ] U2 (assumed) Condensation: two molecules join and a molecule of water is removed. This is how glucose units join to form starch, glycogen and cellulose, and how amino acids join to form proteins.
- [ ] U3 (assumed) Hydrolysis: a large molecule is split into smaller ones by adding water, e.g. starch hydrolysed to maltose, proteins to amino acids. Digestion is hydrolysis catalysed by enzymes (detail in "The Digestive System").
- [ ] U4 Formation of structural units: cells assemble biomolecules into the structures they are made of. Examples: cellulose fibres form plant cell walls; phospholipids and proteins form every cell membrane; keratin forms hair and nails; collagen forms skin, tendons and the framework of bone.
- [ ] U5 Diagram: a simple chain of linked monomers (e.g. glucose units) with arrows showing condensation (water out) joining them and hydrolysis (water in) splitting them.

Metabolism (one block)
- [ ] M1 Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions in an organism. Each reaction is controlled by a specific enzyme (detail in "Enzymes"). Metabolism is one of the characteristics of life.
- [ ] M2 Anabolism: reactions that build larger, more complex molecules from smaller ones. They need (absorb) energy. Examples: photosynthesis (glucose made from carbon dioxide and water); joining glucose into starch in plants or glycogen in the liver and muscles; joining amino acids into proteins.
- [ ] M3 Catabolism: reactions that break larger molecules into smaller ones. They release energy. Examples: respiration (glucose broken down to carbon dioxide and water); digestion of starch to maltose and glucose; breakdown of glycogen to glucose.
- [ ] M4 Energy transfer: energy released by catabolic reactions such as respiration is used to drive anabolic reactions. It is carried between them by the transfer molecule ATP (one sentence; detail in "Transfer Molecules"). Catabolism and anabolism therefore depend on each other.
- [ ] M5 Rearrangement into new molecules: cells convert one biomolecule into another to meet their needs. Examples: plants convert the glucose made in photosynthesis into starch (storage), cellulose (cell walls), sucrose (for transport), oils (e.g. stored in seeds) and, using nitrogen absorbed from the soil as nitrate, amino acids and proteins; in humans the liver converts excess glucose to glycogen, and excess carbohydrate is converted to fat for storage.
- [ ] M6 A short summary comparison: anabolism (small to large, energy used, e.g. photosynthesis, protein synthesis) vs catabolism (large to small, energy released, e.g. respiration, digestion). One comparison block or two paired bullets, not a repeat of M2–M3.

## 3. Traps

- Don't describe the structures of the biomolecules here; they belong to "Structures of biomolecules". Name them only.
- ATP and NAD⁺ belong to "Transfer Molecules": one sentence on ATP (M4) only, no NAD⁺.
- Enzyme mechanism (active site, specificity) belongs to "Enzymes".
- Digestion detail belongs to "The Digestive System"; U3 and M3 use digestion as an example only.
- Photosynthesis and respiration equations and stages belong to "Photosynthesis" and "Respiration". Name them as examples only; no stages.
- Don't confuse anabolic reactions with "anabolic steroids" (The Endocrine System).
- Students often say catabolism "uses" energy: it releases energy; anabolism uses it.
- No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define metabolism, anabolism and catabolism; give an example of each.
- Explain why photosynthesis is anabolic (builds large molecules from small ones; needs energy) and respiration catabolic.
- Explain the terms condensation and hydrolysis (assumed; useful in structure questions).
