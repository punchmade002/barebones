# Package: bio19-s6 — Dopamine and endorphins

  dopamine and endorphins in humans, taking into account the influence of lifestyle choices on

- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. "Discuss" means a balanced review: give

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] M1 Neurotransmitters released in the brain affect mood (how we feel) and behaviour (what we do), because they change how groups of neurons signal.

Dopamine
- [ ] P1 Dopamine is a neurotransmitter made by groups of neurons deep in the brain, from the amino acid tyrosine.
- [ ] P2 Reward and motivation: dopamine is released when we experience or expect something rewarding (food, social contact, achievement). It produces feelings of pleasure and satisfaction and reinforces the behaviour, so we are motivated to repeat it.
- [ ] P3 Movement and learning: dopamine also helps control smooth movement and is involved in learning and attention. Loss of dopamine-producing neurons causes Parkinson's disease (described in "Conduction across the synaptic cleft and its disruption"; one clause here).

Endorphins
- [ ] Q1 Endorphins are neurotransmitters (peptides) produced by the brain, including the pituitary gland and hypothalamus, in response to pain, stress and physical exertion.
- [ ] Q2 They bind to opioid receptors and reduce the sensation of pain; they also produce a feeling of wellbeing and reduce stress. They are the body's natural painkillers.

Lifestyle choices and their effects (discuss: effect, then consequence)
- [ ] L1 Non-medical drug use, stimulants: cocaine blocks dopamine reuptake and amphetamines increase dopamine release, so dopamine floods the reward pathway, producing an intense high.
- [ ] L2 Non-medical drug use, others: nicotine and alcohol also increase dopamine release in the reward pathway.
- [ ] L3 Non-medical drug use, opioids: heroin and other opioids bind to endorphin (opioid) receptors, producing pain relief and euphoria.
- [ ] L4 Tolerance and dependence: with repeated drug use the brain adapts, e.g. by reducing the number of dopamine receptors. More drug is needed for the same effect (tolerance), normal rewards feel less pleasurable, and stopping causes low mood and cravings (dependence and withdrawal). This is how addiction develops.
- [ ] L5 Exercise: sustained exercise raises endorphin levels and increases dopamine signalling, improving mood, reducing stress and anxiety, and reducing the perception of pain during exertion.
- [ ] L6 Dietary choices: foods high in sugar and fat trigger dopamine release in the reward pathway, which can drive overeating. A diet with enough protein supplies tyrosine, the amino acid dopamine is made from.
- [ ] L7 Balanced conclusion: activities such as exercise and social contact raise these neurotransmitters in ways that support long-term wellbeing, while drugs raise them far above normal levels and lead the brain to adapt, causing dependence. Mood and behaviour depend on many factors, not one neurotransmitter.

## 3. Traps

- Don't call dopamine "the pleasure chemical" or endorphins "happy hormones" as definitions; give their actual roles (P2, Q2).
- Don't claim specific foods (chocolate, chilli) "release endorphins"; that isn't in the checked facts.
- "Runner's high": don't name it as an established fact.
- The synapse mechanism of drug action is Higher Level material in "Conduction across the synaptic cleft and its disruption". Give the drug effects here in one sentence each, without enzymes or transporters beyond L1.
- Discuss needs both sides; avoid moralising language.
