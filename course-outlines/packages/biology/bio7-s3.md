## 1. Must cover

Each organelle item gives: structure; function; how the structure suits the function; where it
is found (plant, animal or both); whether a light microscope shows it. Group them into blocks
as shown.

Boundaries of the cell (structure-and-function block)
- [ ] W1 Cell wall: a rigid layer outside the cell membrane, made mainly of cellulose fibres in plants. It is fully permeable (lets water and dissolved substances pass freely). Functions: gives strength, support and a fixed shape; stops the cell bursting when it takes in water by osmosis, so the cell becomes firm (turgid), which supports non-woody plants. Found in plant cells (and in fungi, made of chitin, and bacteria); not in animal cells. Visible with the light microscope.
- [ ] W2 Cell membrane (plasma membrane): a very thin, flexible boundary around every cell, made of a double layer of phospholipids with proteins in it. It is selectively permeable: it controls which substances enter and leave; it holds the cell contents together; its proteins act as receptors that recognise hormones and other signals, and as markers that let cells recognise each other. Found in all cells, inside the wall in plant cells. Seen only as a line with the light microscope. Its structure and how materials cross it are in "Transport Across Membranes".

Inside the cell (structure-and-function block)
- [ ] Y1 Cytoplasm: everything inside the cell membrane except the nucleus: a watery, jelly-like fluid (cytosol) containing dissolved nutrients, salts and enzymes, in which the organelles are suspended. Function: site of many metabolic reactions, e.g. the first stage of respiration (glucose broken down to pyruvate) and anaerobic respiration, and the making of proteins at free ribosomes. Found in all cells.
- [ ] Y2 Vacuole: a fluid-filled sac surrounded by a single membrane. Mature plant cells have one large permanent central vacuole filled with cell sap (water containing sugars, salts and sometimes pigments, e.g. the red of beetroot). Functions: stores water, food and waste; takes in water by osmosis and pushes the cytoplasm against the wall, keeping the cell turgid and the plant upright (when water is lost, plants wilt). Animal cells have only small, temporary vacuoles (e.g. food vacuoles; some single-celled protists such as Amoeba have contractile vacuoles that pump out excess water). The plant vacuole is visible with the light microscope.

Energy organelles (structure-and-function block)
- [ ] M1 Mitochondrion (plural mitochondria): a rod- or sausage-shaped organelle with a double membrane. The outer membrane is smooth; the inner membrane is folded into cristae, which give a large surface area for the enzymes of the later stages of aerobic respiration; the fluid inside is the matrix. Function: site of most of aerobic respiration, releasing energy from food to make most of the cell's ATP.
- [ ] M2 Mitochondria are found in almost all eukaryotic cells, in plants as well as animals. Cells that need a lot of energy have many, e.g. muscle cells, liver cells, nerve cells and the middle section of sperm. They contain their own DNA and ribosomes. Too small to show detail with the light microscope; structure seen with the electron microscope.
- [ ] M3 Diagram: a mitochondrion in section labelled outer membrane, inner membrane, cristae and matrix.
- [ ] P1 Chloroplast: a disc-shaped organelle with a double membrane, containing stacks of flattened membrane sacs (thylakoids; a stack is a granum) that carry the green pigment chlorophyll, surrounded by a fluid called the stroma. Starch grains may be stored in the stroma.
- [ ] P2 Function: site of photosynthesis. Chlorophyll in the thylakoid membranes absorbs light energy (light-dependent stage); the stroma contains the enzymes that use it to make glucose from carbon dioxide (light-independent stage). The many stacked membranes give a large surface area for absorbing light. Detail of the stages is in "Photosynthesis".
- [ ] P3 Found only in plant cells (and algae) in parts exposed to light, especially the palisade cells of leaves and guard cells; not in root cells or in onion bulb cells, and never in animal cells. Visible as green discs with the light microscope. They contain their own DNA and ribosomes.
- [ ] P4 Diagram: a chloroplast labelled double membrane, thylakoids, granum, stroma and starch grain.

Control centre (structure-and-function block)
- [ ] N1 Nucleus: usually the largest organelle, round or oval, surrounded by a double membrane called the nuclear envelope. It contains the cell's DNA as chromatin (DNA wound around histone proteins).
- [ ] N2 Function: controls the cell's activities, because its DNA carries the genes that code for the cell's proteins, including the enzymes that control every reaction; it is where DNA is copied before cell division and where genes are copied into messenger RNA (mRNA).
- [ ] N3 (assumed) The nucleolus is a dense region inside the nucleus where ribosomes are made. One bullet.
- [ ] N4 Found in all eukaryotic cells except a few that lose it, e.g. mature human red blood cells. Visible with the light microscope, especially when stained.
- [ ] N5 Nuclear pore: one of many openings through the nuclear envelope, each lined with proteins that control what passes. mRNA and ribosome parts pass out to the cytoplasm; proteins (such as enzymes and histones), nucleotides and ATP pass in. The DNA is too large to leave and stays protected in the nucleus. Seen only with the electron microscope.
- [ ] N6 Chromosome: a long thread of DNA wound around proteins (histones), carrying many genes. During most of the cell's life, the chromosomes are long, thin and spread out as chromatin and can't be seen; before division they coil up tightly, becoming short and thick, and can be seen with the light microscope when stained. Human body cells have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). Function: carry genetic information and pass it to new cells and offspring. The detailed structure (chromatids, centromere) is in "Genetic Inheritance"; division is in "Cell Division and Cancer".
- [ ] N7 Ribosome: a very small organelle with no membrane, made of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and protein in two subunits. Function: the site of protein synthesis, where amino acids are joined in the order coded for by mRNA. Found in all cells, prokaryotic and eukaryotic, free in the cytoplasm; there are many in cells making a lot of protein. Seen only with the electron microscope, as dots. Translation in detail is in "DNA Replication, Protein Synthesis and Mutations".

Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus (Higher Level only) — one block
- [ ] R1 [HL] Endoplasmic reticulum (ER): a network of membrane-bound flattened sacs and tubes running through the cytoplasm, continuous with the outer membrane of the nuclear envelope. It forms channels that transport materials through the cell.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Rough ER is covered with ribosomes. Proteins made at these ribosomes enter the ER, where they are folded and transported; these are mainly proteins that will be secreted from the cell (e.g. digestive enzymes made by pancreas cells, antibodies) or built into membranes. Proteins made at free ribosomes stay in the cytoplasm. Cells that secrete a lot of protein have a lot of rough ER.
- [ ] R3 [HL] Smooth ER has no ribosomes. It makes lipids, including phospholipids for membranes and steroid hormones; in liver cells it helps to break down (detoxify) drugs and alcohol.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Golgi apparatus (Golgi body): a stack of flattened, curved membrane sacs, usually near the nucleus, with small membrane bubbles (vesicles) budding off it.
- [ ] R5 [HL] Function: receives proteins from the ER in vesicles; modifies them (e.g. adding carbohydrate to make glycoproteins); sorts and packages them into vesicles that carry them to where they are needed. Secretory vesicles move to the cell membrane, fuse with it and release their contents outside the cell (exocytosis). In plant cells the Golgi apparatus also makes materials for the cell wall.
- [ ] R6 [HL] The secretory pathway, in order: gene in the nucleus → mRNA leaves through a nuclear pore → protein made at a ribosome on the rough ER → folded and carried in the ER → transport vesicle → Golgi apparatus (modified and packaged) → secretory vesicle → fuses with the cell membrane → protein released, e.g. a digestive enzyme.
- [ ] R7 [HL] Both are found in plant and animal cells; both are seen only with the electron microscope.
- [ ] R8 [HL] Diagram: add rough ER (with ribosomes), smooth ER and the Golgi apparatus (with vesicles) to the animal-cell diagram (cue S3 below), in this block, with arrows showing the secretory pathway.

Plant and animal cells compared (comparison block)
- [ ] S1 Both have: cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus with nuclear pores and chromosomes, mitochondria, ribosomes.
- [ ] S2 Only plant cells have: a cellulose cell wall; a large permanent central vacuole; chloroplasts (in green parts). Plant cells usually have a fixed, regular (often rectangular) shape and store carbohydrate as starch; animal cells have a flexible, irregular or rounded shape, no wall, only small temporary vacuoles, and store carbohydrate as glycogen.
- [ ] S3 Diagram: a typical animal cell as seen with the electron microscope, labelled cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, nuclear envelope, nuclear pore, nucleolus, chromatin, mitochondrion and ribosomes.
- [ ] S4 Diagram: a typical plant cell (e.g. a palisade cell) as seen with the electron microscope, labelled cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, large vacuole, chloroplast, mitochondrion, nucleus, nuclear pore and ribosomes.
- [ ] S5 A table with each structure as a row and columns: structure, function, plant/animal/both, visible with light microscope (yes/no). Put the ER and Golgi rows only in the Higher Level block, not in this table.

## 3. Traps

- ER and Golgi apparatus are Higher Level only. Put them only in the R block, whose heading ends " (Higher Level only)"; keep them out of S1–S5, the diagrams S3–S4 and the table. Rough ER and vesicles are mentioned only in that block.
- Plant cells have mitochondria as well as chloroplasts; a common error is "plants photosynthesise instead of respiring".
- The cell wall is fully permeable; the cell membrane is selectively permeable. Don't swap them.
- Not all plant cells have chloroplasts (P3).
- Ribosomes don't contain chromosomes (a past-paper true/false point); ribosomes are made of rRNA and protein.
- RNA but no DNA is found in ribosomes and the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells; DNA is in the nucleus, mitochondria and chloroplasts.
- Membrane structure (phospholipid bilayer details, fluid mosaic model) and diffusion, osmosis and active transport belong to "Transport Across Membranes". W2 gives the composition in one phrase only.
- Mitosis and the stages of division belong to "Cell Division and Cancer"; chromosome structure detail to "Genetic Inheritance"; biomolecules to "Biomolecules — The Chemicals of Life"; respiration and photosynthesis stages to their own chapters.
- Lysosomes, centrioles, the cytoskeleton and plasmodesmata are not on the specification; don't add them.
- Diagram cues: M3, P4, R8 (Higher Level block), S3 and S4. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

Past papers (previous syllabus) asked these, and they remain on the specification:
- Draw and label a plant or animal cell as seen with the electron microscope.
- Give the function of a named organelle (mitochondrion, ribosome, nucleus, chloroplast, cell wall, vacuole, membrane).
- Name the organelle in which chlorophyll is found; where respiration's later stages take place.
- Name locations in a eukaryotic cell where RNA but no DNA is found.
- True or false: the ribosome contains the chromosomes.
- Give differences between plant and animal cells.
