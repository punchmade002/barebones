## 1. Must cover

Electron carriers: what they are and what they do (one block)
- [ ] N1 NAD⁺ is nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide; NADP⁺ is nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate, the same molecule with one extra phosphate group. Both are made in the body from niacin (vitamin B3).
- [ ] N2 Both are electron carriers (transfer molecules for electrons): they pick up high-energy electrons, together with hydrogen, in one reaction and hand them on to another reaction, then return to pick up more.
- [ ] N3 (assumed) Gaining electrons is reduction and losing electrons is oxidation ("OIL RIG": oxidation is loss, reduction is gain). NAD⁺ and NADP⁺ are the oxidised forms (empty carriers); NADH and NADPH are the reduced forms (loaded carriers).
- [ ] N4 Loading: each carrier accepts two electrons and a proton (H⁺): NAD⁺ + 2e⁻ + H⁺ → NADH, and NADP⁺ + 2e⁻ + H⁺ → NADPH. In respiration the electrons and hydrogen are removed from food molecules by enzymes called dehydrogenases.
- [ ] N5 Unloading: NADH and NADPH give up their electrons and hydrogen to the next reaction and become NAD⁺ and NADP⁺ again, so the same small supply is reused over and over, like ATP.
- [ ] N6 The electrons carry energy: when NADH's electrons are passed along an electron transport chain, the energy released is used to make ATP; when NADPH's electrons are added to carbon dioxide, they help build glucose. This "reducing power" is what makes the carriers useful.

NAD⁺ in respiration (one block; outline only)
- [ ] R1 NAD⁺ works in respiration, a catabolic pathway that breaks glucose down to release energy.
- [ ] R2 NAD⁺ collects electrons and hydrogen released as glucose is broken down: in glycolysis in the cytoplasm, and in the later stage (the Krebs or citric acid cycle) in the matrix of the mitochondrion. It becomes NADH.
- [ ] R3 NADH carries the electrons to the electron transport chain on the inner membrane of the mitochondrion. The energy released as the electrons pass along the chain is used to make most of the cell's ATP.
- [ ] R4 At the end of the chain the electrons, with hydrogen ions, are passed to oxygen, the final electron acceptor, forming water. NAD⁺ is released to collect more electrons.
- [ ] R5 Without oxygen the chain stops and NADH can't be unloaded there. In fermentation NADH instead passes its hydrogen to pyruvate (forming lactic acid or ethanol and carbon dioxide), which regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue making a little ATP. One bullet; the detail belongs to "Respiration".

NADP⁺ in photosynthesis (one block; outline only)
- [ ] P1 NADP⁺ works in photosynthesis, an anabolic pathway that builds glucose from carbon dioxide and water.
- [ ] P2 In the light-dependent stage, on the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast, light energises electrons in chlorophyll. These high-energy electrons pass along an electron transport chain to NADP⁺, which also takes up protons (H⁺) from the splitting of water (photolysis). It becomes NADPH.
- [ ] P3 NADPH carries the electrons and hydrogen to the light-independent stage in the stroma of the chloroplast, where they are added to carbon dioxide to make glucose, using energy from ATP.
- [ ] P4 NADP⁺ (and ADP + Pi) then return to the light-dependent stage to be loaded again.

Comparing the carriers (one comparison block, paired points)
- [ ] K1 Process: NAD⁺ in respiration; NADP⁺ in photosynthesis.
- [ ] K2 Type of pathway: NAD⁺ in a catabolic (breaking-down) pathway; NADP⁺ in an anabolic (building) pathway.
- [ ] K3 Where loaded: NAD⁺ in the cytoplasm and mitochondrial matrix, with electrons from glucose; NADP⁺ on the thylakoid membranes, with electrons from chlorophyll (replaced by electrons from water).
- [ ] K4 Where unloaded and final electron acceptor: NADH at the electron transport chain of the inner mitochondrial membrane, with oxygen as the final acceptor (forms water); NADPH in the stroma, with carbon dioxide as the final acceptor (forms glucose).
- [ ] K5 What the electrons' energy is used for: NADH's to make ATP; NADPH's to build glucose.
- [ ] K6 Structure: NADP⁺ has one extra phosphate group, which lets enzymes tell the two apart, so the carriers used for breaking down and for building up are kept separate.
- [ ] K7 ATP compared with both: ATP transfers energy (as a phosphate group); NAD⁺ and NADP⁺ transfer electrons and hydrogen. All three are reused, not used up.
- [ ] K8 Diagram: two loops side by side. Respiration loop: NAD⁺ → (electrons and H from glucose, glycolysis and Krebs cycle) → NADH → (electron transport chain, ATP made, electrons to oxygen) → NAD⁺. Photosynthesis loop: NADP⁺ → (electrons from chlorophyll, H⁺ from water, light-dependent stage) → NADPH → (light-independent stage, CO₂ → glucose) → NADP⁺.

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading. All key terms in this subsection are Higher Level; keep the key-term list to NAD⁺, NADP⁺, NADH, NADPH, electron carrier, reduction and oxidation, final electron acceptor.
- Don't swap them: NAD⁺ is respiration, NADP⁺ is photosynthesis. In this course NADP⁺ is studied only in photosynthesis; don't claim animals lack it.
- The reduced forms are written NADH and NADPH, never "NADH₂" or "NAD". The "+" belongs only to the oxidised forms.
- Some textbooks describe NADP⁺ first gaining two electrons to become NADP⁻ and then attracting a proton to become NADPH. That stepwise version belongs to "Photosynthesis" if used at all; here use N4.
- Don't describe the stages in detail: no Krebs-cycle intermediates, no acetyl CoA, no proton gradient or ATP synthase mechanism, no cyclic and non-cyclic pathways, no photosystems. These belong to "Respiration" and "Photosynthesis", which outline and model each two-stage process with reference to the transfer molecules.
- FAD and FADH₂ are not on the specification. Don't mention them.
- ATP's structure and roles belong to "ATP"; K7 gives the comparison only.
- Niacin is named only in N1; the vitamins topic belongs to "Vitamins".
- No other diagram cues beyond K8.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the transfer molecule that carries electrons in respiration and the one in photosynthesis.
- Describe the role of NADH (or NADPH) in the second stage of respiration (or photosynthesis).
- State what happens to the electrons and protons carried by NADPH in the light-independent stage, and which molecules return to the light-dependent stage (ADP + Pi and NADP⁺).
- State the role of oxygen at the end of the electron transport chain.
