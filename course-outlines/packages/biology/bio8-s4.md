## 1. Must cover

What minerals are (one block)
- [ ] D1 Minerals are inorganic elements, usually taken in as ions dissolved in water, that organisms need in small amounts for normal functioning. They cannot be made by the organism. Animals get them from food and drink; plants absorb them as ions dissolved in soil water through their root hairs.
- [ ] D2 Some minerals are built into biomolecules or structures: iron in haemoglobin, magnesium in chlorophyll, phosphorus in phospholipids, DNA and ATP, sulfur in some amino acids, iodine in thyroxine, and calcium (as calcium phosphate) in bone and teeth. Many others are not integrated into biomolecules but work as free ions in body fluids, e.g. sodium (Na⁺), potassium (K⁺), chloride (Cl⁻) and calcium (Ca²⁺) ions.
- [ ] D3 (assumed) Minerals differ from vitamins: minerals are inorganic elements; vitamins are organic compounds (see "Vitamins"). Neither supplies energy.

The four roles named in the specification (one block, one bullet group per role)
- [ ] R1 pH balance: phosphate and hydrogencarbonate ions act as buffers in cells and blood, taking up or releasing hydrogen ions so the pH stays almost constant (blood is kept at about pH 7.4). This matters because enzymes work only in a narrow pH range.
- [ ] R2 Regulation of enzyme systems: many enzymes need a mineral ion to work (a cofactor or activator). Examples: magnesium ions are needed by many enzymes that use ATP; zinc is part of many enzymes; chloride ions activate salivary amylase. Without the ion the enzyme is inactive, so mineral levels help control metabolic pathways.
- [ ] R3 Nerve impulse transfer: a nerve impulse travels as sodium ions move into and potassium ions move out of the neuron through channel proteins. Calcium ions entering the end of a neuron trigger the release of neurotransmitter at the synapse. Detail in "The Nervous System".
- [ ] R4 Muscle contraction: calcium ions released inside a muscle fibre after a nerve impulse arrives allow actin and myosin to interact, so the muscle contracts. Detail in "The Musculoskeletal System".
- [ ] R5 Other roles (one bullet): osmoregulation (sodium and chloride ions in body fluids affect the movement of water by osmosis; see "Water"), structure (calcium and phosphate in bones and teeth), oxygen transport (iron), and blood clotting (calcium).

Named minerals in humans: role, source and deficiency (one block; one bullet per mineral)
- [ ] H1 Calcium: forms bones and teeth (as calcium phosphate, with vitamin D needed for its absorption); needed for muscle contraction, nerve transmission and blood clotting. Sources: milk, cheese, yoghurt, green leafy vegetables, tinned fish eaten with the bones (e.g. sardines). Deficiency weakens bones (rickets in children, with low vitamin D; osteoporosis risk in later life).
- [ ] H2 Iron: part of haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells. Sources: red meat, liver, eggs, green leafy vegetables (e.g. spinach), beans and lentils, fortified breakfast cereals. Vitamin C helps absorb iron from plant foods. Deficiency causes anaemia: too little haemoglobin, so tiredness, weakness and pale skin. Women who menstruate need more iron than men.
- [ ] H3 Sodium: nerve impulses; controls water balance in body fluids. Sources: table salt, processed foods, crisps, bacon, cheese. Most people take in more than they need; excess salt is linked to high blood pressure.
- [ ] H4 Potassium: nerve impulses and muscle function, with sodium; fluid balance inside cells. Sources: bananas, potatoes, vegetables, fruit, milk.
- [ ] H5 Iodine: needed to make the hormone thyroxine in the thyroid gland, which controls metabolic rate (detail in "The Endocrine System"). Sources: seafood, seaweed, milk, iodised salt. Deficiency causes goitre (a swollen thyroid gland) and a low metabolic rate.
- [ ] H6 Phosphorus (as phosphate): bones and teeth; part of ATP, DNA, RNA and phospholipids; buffering (R1). Sources: milk, cheese, meat, fish, eggs, nuts.
- [ ] H7 Magnesium: bones; activates many enzymes (R2); muscle and nerve function. Sources: green leafy vegetables, nuts, wholegrain cereals.
- [ ] H8 Fluoride: hardens tooth enamel, making it more resistant to decay. Sources: fluoridated public water (as in Ireland), toothpaste, tea.

Minerals in plants (one block)
- [ ] G1 Plants absorb minerals from the soil water as ions through root hairs, often by active transport; they travel up the xylem (detail in "Transport in Plants").
- [ ] G2 Nitrogen, absorbed as nitrate, is needed to make amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids; shortage gives stunted growth and yellow leaves. Detail on how nitrogen reaches the soil is in "Nutrient Cycling".
- [ ] G3 Magnesium is part of chlorophyll; shortage causes yellowing leaves (less chlorophyll, less photosynthesis).
- [ ] G4 Phosphate is needed for ATP, DNA and phospholipids, and for root growth. Calcium forms calcium pectate, which holds neighbouring plant cell walls together.
- [ ] G5 Farmers add minerals (nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium) to soil in fertilisers. Sustainability link (one bullet): excess fertiliser washed into rivers and lakes causes eutrophication (detail in "Nutrient Cycling").

## 3. Traps

- Don't call minerals "organic" or say they provide energy.
- The specification's point is that minerals are "not necessarily" integrated into biomolecules: say some are and some aren't (D2); don't say none are.
- Vitamin D's role in calcium absorption and rickets belong to "Vitamins"; H1 mentions them in a phrase only.
- Nerve impulse and muscle contraction mechanisms belong to "The Nervous System" and "The Musculoskeletal System"; R3–R4 are one or two sentences each, no membrane potentials or sliding-filament detail.
- Thyroxine and the thyroid belong to "The Endocrine System"; eutrophication and the nitrogen cycle to "Nutrient Cycling".
- Don't give daily intake figures or supplement doses.
- No diagram cues: minerals need none.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name a mineral needed by plants and one needed by animals, and give the role of each.
- Give a food source of a named mineral (calcium, iron).
- Name a deficiency disease caused by lack of a mineral (anaemia, goitre).
- Explain the roles of named ions in nerve impulses and muscle contraction (new emphasis on this specification).
