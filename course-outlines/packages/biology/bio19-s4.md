# Package: bio19-s4 — Neurotransmission

- **loId:** `bio19-s4` · **chapterId:** `bio19` · **syllabusRef:** 2.4.7
- **Syllabus:** "neurotransmission: transmission of nerve impulse via neurons (sensory, motor,
  interneurons); role of neurotransmitters at a synapse in conduction of nerve impulses,
  involving movement of ions (detailed knowledge of electrochemistry not required)." Outcome 7:
  "explain the role of neurotransmitters at a synapse." Ordinary and Higher level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20.

## 1. Must cover

Transmission through a chain of neurons
- [ ] T1 A nerve impulse passes from neuron to neuron: receptor → sensory neuron → interneuron(s) → motor neuron → effector. Within a neuron the impulse is electrical; between neurons it is passed on chemically.
- [ ] T2 (assumed) The impulse is a wave of electrical change along the neuron's membrane caused by ions moving in and out of the neuron. Say no more about electrochemistry than this.

The synapse
- [ ] Y1 A synapse is the junction between two neurons, or between a neuron and an effector (e.g. a muscle fibre). The two cells don't touch: a tiny gap, the synaptic cleft, separates them.
- [ ] Y2 The neuron before the cleft is the presynaptic neuron; the one after it is the postsynaptic neuron. The presynaptic axon ends in a swollen synaptic knob containing mitochondria and vesicles of neurotransmitter.
- [ ] Y3 A neurotransmitter is a chemical released by a neuron that carries the impulse across a synapse. Examples: acetylcholine, noradrenaline, dopamine, serotonin.
- [ ] Y4 Diagram: a synapse labelling the presynaptic neuron, synaptic knob, mitochondria, vesicles, neurotransmitter molecules, synaptic cleft, receptor proteins on the postsynaptic membrane, postsynaptic neuron, and an arrow for the direction of the impulse.

How the impulse crosses (in numbered stages)
- [ ] X1 1. The impulse arrives at the synaptic knob.
- [ ] X2 2. This causes ions (calcium ions) to move into the knob.
- [ ] X3 3. The vesicles move to the presynaptic membrane, fuse with it and release neurotransmitter into the cleft.
- [ ] X4 4. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the cleft.
- [ ] X5 5. It binds to specific receptor proteins on the postsynaptic membrane; the receptor fits only its own neurotransmitter.
- [ ] X6 6. Binding makes ion channels in the postsynaptic membrane open, ions move in, and a new impulse starts in the postsynaptic neuron if enough neurotransmitter binds.
- [ ] X7 Mitochondria in the knob supply ATP for making and releasing neurotransmitter.

The roles of synapses
- [ ] F1 Impulses cross in one direction only, because only the presynaptic side has vesicles and only the postsynaptic side has receptors.
- [ ] F2 Weak stimuli may release too little neurotransmitter to start a new impulse, so synapses filter out unimportant signals.
- [ ] F3 One neuron can synapse with many others, so impulses can be spread to many cells or combined from many cells.
- [ ] F4 Synapses are where many drugs act (detail in "Conduction across the synaptic cleft and its disruption").

## 3. Traps

- No electrochemistry: no resting or action potential values, no sodium-potassium pump, no millivolts.
- How the neurotransmitter is removed from the cleft (enzymes, reuptake) and drug actions are Higher Level and belong to `bio19-s5`. Here end at X6 and F4.
- Dopamine's roles in mood belong to `bio19-s6`; here name it only as an example.
- Don't say the impulse "jumps" across the cleft.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define synapse and neurotransmitter; name a neurotransmitter.
- Label a diagram of a synapse; give the function of vesicles or the cleft.
- Describe how an impulse passes across a synapse.
- Explain why impulses travel in one direction only.
