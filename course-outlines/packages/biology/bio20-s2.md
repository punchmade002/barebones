# Package: bio20-s2 — Hormonal manipulation

- **loId:** `bio20-s2` · **chapterId:** `bio20` · **syllabusRef:** 2.4.11
- **Syllabus:** "uses of hormonal manipulations: sport, health, agriculture." Outcome 11:
  "recognise the impact of hormonal manipulations on organisms." Ordinary and Higher level.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. "Recognise the impact" means each use needs its
  purpose, how it works, and its benefits and risks.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] M1 Hormonal manipulation is the deliberate use of natural or synthetic hormones, or of drugs that block hormones, to change how an organism grows or functions.

Sport (one block)
- [ ] S1 Anabolic steroids are synthetic forms of testosterone. They increase muscle mass and strength and speed up recovery from training.
- [ ] S2 Risks of anabolic steroids: liver damage, increased risk of heart disease, reduced sperm production and infertility and shrinking of the testes in males, development of male characteristics in females, acne, and mood changes including aggression.
- [ ] S3 EPO (erythropoietin) is a hormone made by the kidneys that stimulates red blood cell production. Athletes misuse it to increase the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood and so their endurance. Risk: the blood thickens, increasing the risk of clots, heart attack and stroke.
- [ ] S4 These substances are banned in competitive sport by the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA); athletes are tested through urine and blood samples. Their use is unfair to other competitors.

Health (one block)
- [ ] D1 Insulin injections treat type 1 diabetes, in which the pancreas makes little or no insulin, so blood glucose would otherwise rise too high. Most insulin used today is human insulin made by genetically modified bacteria (detail in Genetic Engineering).
- [ ] D2 The contraceptive pill contains synthetic oestrogen and progesterone (or progesterone alone). These inhibit the release of FSH and LH, so no egg matures and ovulation is prevented.
- [ ] D3 Fertility treatment: hormones are given to stimulate the ovaries to release eggs (detail in Human Reproduction).
- [ ] D4 Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) supplies oestrogen (often with progesterone) after the menopause to relieve symptoms such as hot flushes and to reduce bone loss. It slightly increases the risk of some cancers and blood clots for some women.
- [ ] D5 Thyroxine tablets are given to people whose thyroid produces too little thyroxine.

Agriculture (one block, animals then plants)
- [ ] A1 Progesterone, given as a vaginal sponge or implant, is used to synchronise the breeding cycles of cattle and sheep, so a herd can be bred and give birth at the same time.
- [ ] A2 Growth-promoting hormones increase meat production in cattle and bovine somatotropin (BST) increases milk yield. Both are banned in the EU, including Ireland, though allowed in some other countries (e.g. BST in the USA), because of concerns about animal welfare and hormone residues in food.
- [ ] A3 Plant hormones: synthetic auxins (e.g. 2,4-D) are selective weedkillers that kill broad-leaved weeds in cereal crops by making them grow uncontrollably; auxin rooting powder makes cuttings form roots; ethene gas is used to ripen fruit such as bananas in storage after transport.

Evaluating the impact
- [ ] E1 A comparison block weighing benefits (better health, treatment of disease, food production, convenience) against costs (side effects, unfair advantage in sport, animal welfare, residues in food, environmental effects of weedkillers).

## 3. Traps

- Don't describe how plant hormones control normal growth or tropisms; give only the uses in A3.
- Contraception methods other than the pill belong to Human Reproduction; IVF detail too.
- How insulin lowers blood glucose is in "Glands, locations and hormones"; D1 gives the treatment only.
- Don't claim HRT is safe or unsafe for everyone; D4 states it as "for some women".
- Avoid naming brand names of drugs.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Give one use of hormones in sport, medicine and agriculture.
- Explain why anabolic steroids or EPO are banned; give a side effect.
- Explain how the contraceptive pill works.
- Evaluate the use of hormones in food production.
