# Package: bio35-s6 — Investigation: DNA-profile patterns

- **loId:** `bio35-s6` · **chapterId:** `bio35` · **syllabusRef:** 3.3.6
- **Syllabus:** Outcome 6: "investigate patterns using a DNA profile, use primary or secondary
  data to support conclusions." Ordinary and Higher level. No Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-29. Write as one full investigation in the
  NOTES_STANDARD "Investigation" block types: a planning block; a primary-data method block
  (running a gel with pre-prepared DNA samples from a school kit); a safety block; two worked
  secondary-data analyses (a forensic match and a paternity case), each as a Skills block; a
  results-and-conclusion block; an errors block. The specification names no kit or procedure;
  P1–P10 are the standard school procedure for agarose gel electrophoresis, kept general where
  kits differ ("as the kit instructions give"). What a profile is, STRs, two alleles per locus,
  and the rules for reading bands are in "DNA profiling" (Ordinary and Higher); the theory of
  extraction, PCR and electrophoresis is in "Generating a DNA profile" (Higher Level). Use them
  without re-teaching; this note gives the practical handling steps and the analysis.

## 1. Must cover

Planning (one block)
- [ ] A1 Aim: to compare DNA profiles in order to (a) find whether a suspect's DNA matches DNA from a crime scene and (b) find which of several men could be the biological father of a child. Data may be primary (a gel the student runs with DNA samples supplied in a school kit, already copied or cut and ready to load) or secondary (profiles given as diagrams, tables of band sizes or repeat numbers, or from a published case or simulation).
- [ ] A2 Hypotheses (examples to write): "The crime-scene DNA came from suspect B"; "Man X is the biological father of the child". The null outcome is an exclusion.
- [ ] A3 Variables: independent variable is the source of each DNA sample (each person); dependent variable is the position of each band (the distance it travels from the well, in mm, or its size read from the ladder); controlled variables are the gel (all samples on the same gel, same agarose and buffer), the volume of each sample loaded, the voltage and running time, and the staining time.
- [ ] A4 Controls: a DNA ladder (size marker) in one lane, so band sizes can be read and different gels compared; a lane loaded with loading dye and buffer but no DNA (negative control), which should show no bands, so any band there shows contamination; in a real case, a reference sample of known origin (positive control) run beside the unknowns.
- [ ] A5 Equipment and materials: electrophoresis tank with lid and electrodes, power pack, gel casting tray and comb, agarose powder and running buffer (as the kit instructions give), conical flask and microwave (or hot plate) to melt the agarose, heat-resistant glove, micropipette and tips (or fine plastic droppers), DNA samples with loading dye (crime scene, suspects; or mother, child, possible fathers), DNA ladder, DNA stain safe for schools (e.g. methylene blue) and a staining tray, white light box, ruler, gloves, safety glasses, camera or phone to record the gel.

Primary data: running the gel (one Investigation method block, numbered)
- [ ] P1 1. Mix agarose powder with running buffer in a conical flask and heat it, swirling, until it dissolves and the liquid is clear.
- [ ] P2 2. Let it cool until the flask can be held (about 55–60 °C), then pour it into the casting tray with the comb in place at one end. Leave it to set (it becomes firm and cloudy). Remove the comb gently, leaving a row of wells.
- [ ] P3 3. Place the gel in the tank with the wells at the negative (black) electrode end, and pour in running buffer until the gel is just covered.
- [ ] P4 4. Using a fresh micropipette tip for each sample, load the same volume of each sample into its own well: hold the tip just inside the well without piercing the bottom of the gel, and push the plunger slowly. Load the ladder in the first well and the no-DNA control in another. Record which sample is in which lane.
- [ ] P5 5. Put the lid on and connect the leads, black to black (negative) and red to red (positive): DNA "runs to red". Switch on at the voltage the kit instructions give. Bubbles rising from the electrodes show that current is flowing.
- [ ] P6 6. Run until the coloured loading dye has moved about three-quarters of the length of the gel (do not let it run off the end). Switch off the power pack before removing the lid.
- [ ] P7 7. Wearing gloves, move the gel to a staining tray, cover it with stain for the time the kit gives, then pour off the stain and rinse (destain) with water until the bands show clearly against a pale background.
- [ ] P8 8. View the gel on a white light box, photograph it beside a ruler, and measure the distance from the well to each band.
- [ ] P9 9. Repeat with a second gel, or compare with other groups' gels, to check the pattern is repeatable.
- [ ] P10 Option where no DNA kit is available (one bullet): food dyes loaded on an agarose gel separate into bands as they move in the electric field. This models the method (loading, running, band patterns), but dyes separate by their charge and size, not as DNA fragments.

Safety (one block)
- [ ] H1 Electricity and water: switch the power pack off before connecting or disconnecting leads or opening the lid; keep the lid on while running; don't touch the buffer while the current is on; keep hands and bench dry; use only low-voltage equipment designed for schools.
- [ ] H2 Hot agarose can boil over suddenly in a microwave and cause burns: heat in short bursts, swirl with a heat-resistant glove, point the flask away from people, and wear safety glasses.
- [ ] H3 Stains mark skin and clothing and some are harmful: wear gloves and glasses. Don't use ethidium bromide or UV light in school.
- [ ] H4 Handle micropipettes carefully, dispose of gels and tips as the teacher directs, and wash hands after the practical.

Secondary data, worked example 1: forensic matching (one Skills block)
- [ ] F1 Method: 1. Compare the crime-scene lane with each suspect's lane, one locus (or band) at a time. 2. A suspect matches only if every band is in the same position as a crime-scene band and there are no extra or missing bands. 3. One mismatched band at any locus excludes the suspect. 4. A single band at a locus means the person has two alleles with the same number of repeats.
- [ ] F2 Worked example (each number is the number of repeats of an allele; each allele is one band, and more repeats means a longer fragment, nearer the wells). Crime scene: locus 1, 12 and 15; locus 2, 8 only; locus 3, 10 and 13. Suspect A: 12 and 15; 8 and 9; 10 and 13. Suspect B: 12 and 15; 8 only; 10 and 13. Suspect C: 11 and 15; 8 only; 10 and 13.
- [ ] F3 Analysis: suspect A has an extra band (9) at locus 2, so is excluded; suspect C has 11 instead of 12 at locus 1, so is excluded; suspect B matches at all three loci. Conclusion: suspect B cannot be excluded and is very likely the source of the crime-scene DNA; A and C did not leave it. A real profile tests more than ten loci, which makes a chance match between unrelated people extremely unlikely (see "DNA profiling").

Secondary data, worked example 2: paternity (one Skills block)
- [ ] T1 Method: 1. At each locus, find which of the child's bands it shares with the mother (the maternal band). 2. The child's other band must have come from the father (the paternal band). 3. A man who lacks the paternal band at any locus is excluded as the father. 4. A man who has every paternal band cannot be excluded; he is very likely to be the father.
- [ ] T2 Worked example. Mother: locus 1, 10 and 14; locus 2, 7 and 9; locus 3, 11 and 12. Child: locus 1, 10 and 16; locus 2, 9 only (two alleles of 9); locus 3, 12 and 15. Man X: 13 and 16; 6 and 9; 15 only. Man Y: 16 and 17; 7 and 8; 11 and 15. Man Z: 12 and 16; 9 and 10; 13 and 14.
- [ ] T3 Analysis: the child's maternal bands are 10 (locus 1), 9 (locus 2) and 12 (locus 3), all present in the mother. So the paternal bands must be 16, 9 and 15. Man X has 16, 9 and 15, so he cannot be excluded. Man Y has no 9 at locus 2, so he is excluded. Man Z has no 15 at locus 3, so he is excluded. Conclusion: man X is very likely the biological father.
- [ ] T4 Diagram: a gel drawn for the paternity case, lanes for ladder, mother, child, X, Y, Z; each of the child's bands joined by a line to the matching band in the mother or in man X. Label wells, ladder, maternal band, paternal band. (One diagram cue for both worked examples; the forensic case can be drawn the same way.)

Results and conclusion (one block)
- [ ] D1 Record primary data as a labelled drawing or photograph of the gel and a table of each lane's bands (distance from the well in mm, or size read from the ladder). Bands at the same distance on the same gel are the same size.
- [ ] D2 Reading sizes: compare each band with the ladder; for a more exact size, plot a graph of the ladder's known fragment sizes (on a logarithmic scale) against distance travelled, and read the unknown bands' sizes from it.
- [ ] D3 Conclusion: state whether each person matches or is excluded, giving the locus or band that decides it, and say whether the results support the hypothesis. A match supports, but does not prove, that the DNA came from that person (see the limitations in "DNA profiling"); an exclusion is definite if the results are reliable.
- [ ] D4 Patterns to point out: identical twins give identical profiles; brothers and sisters share some but not all bands, because each inherits a random one of each parent's two alleles at each locus; every band of a child is found in at least one parent.

Sources of error and limitations (one block)
- [ ] E1 Contamination: DNA from the student or a reused tip gives extra bands (shown by bands in the no-DNA control lane).
- [ ] E2 Loading errors: piercing the well bottom loses sample; air bubbles or overfilling cause sample to spill into the next well, mixing lanes; unequal volumes give bands of different strength.
- [ ] E3 Running errors: wells at the positive end send the DNA off the gel into the buffer; too short a run leaves bands too close together to separate; too long a run sends small fragments off the end.
- [ ] E4 Smeared or faint bands come from too much or degraded DNA, or too short staining. Measuring band distances by eye and ruler has limited precision; repeat measurements or have a second person check.
- [ ] E5 Secondary data: check the source and whether profiles were run on the same gel; bands on different gels can be compared only through the ladder.

## 3. Traps

- Each band is one allele; a child has one band per locus from each parent (two bands, or one double-strength band if both alleles are the same).
- Exclusion needs only one mismatch; inclusion needs a match at every locus and is not absolute proof.
- In paternity, first remove the mother's contribution; don't compare the man's bands with all of the child's bands.
- "Run to red": samples at the black (negative) end. Shorter fragments travel further.
- Don't re-teach PCR, Taq polymerase, extraction or the theory of electrophoresis here (Higher Level, "Generating a DNA profile"), nor what STRs are ("DNA profiling"); give only the practical steps and the analysis.
- Ethical issues of DNA databases and paternity testing belong to "Ethics of genetic technologies".
- Diagram cue: T4 only. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Interpret a diagram of DNA profiles to identify which suspect matches a crime-scene sample, or which man could be the father, and explain the reasoning.
- Explain why identical twins have the same profile and why siblings share some bands.
- Describe how a gel is loaded and run (direction, electrodes, ladder) and give a safety precaution.
- Name a control and a source of error in a DNA-profile investigation.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "In a paternity case the mother's bands at three loci are 8 and 11, 5 and 6, 14 and 17; the child's are 11 and 12, 6 and 9, 14 and 15. Man P has 12 and 13, 9 and 9, 15 and 16. Man Q has 10 and 12, 7 and 9, 13 and 14. Which man could be the father? Explain." Model answer: maternal bands 11, 6, 14; paternal bands needed 12, 9, 15; P has all three, so cannot be excluded; Q lacks 15 at the third locus, so is excluded (T1 method). (Checked: mother has 11, 6 and 14; P has 12, 9 and 15; Q has 12 and 9 but not 15.)
2. 12 marks: "Describe how you would carry out an investigation to compare the DNA profile of a crime-scene sample with those of two suspects, using a school electrophoresis kit. Include a control, a safety precaution and how you would reach a conclusion." Model answer: A3 variables; A4 ladder and no-DNA control; P2–P8 main steps (wells at the negative end, equal volumes, run, stain, view); H1 or H2; F1 matching rule and D3.
3. 6 marks: "Explain why two brothers who are not twins have DNA profiles that are similar but not identical." Model answer: D4 (each inherits one of each parent's two alleles at each locus at random, so they share some bands but not all); identical twins are the exception, with identical profiles, because they come from one zygote.
