# Package: bio21-s3 — Nervous vs hormonal coordination

- **loId:** `bio21-s3` · **chapterId:** `bio21` · **syllabusRef:** 2.4.5
- **Syllabus:** outcome 5: "compare nervous and hormonal coordination." Ordinary and Higher
  level. "Compare" means similarities and differences, referring to both systems throughout.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] C1 Coordination is the process of linking stimuli with responses so the body works as a whole. Humans have two coordinating systems: the nervous system and the hormonal (endocrine) system.

Differences (write as paired points, nervous then hormonal, in one comparison block)
- [ ] D1 Nature of the message: electrical impulses along neurons, with chemical neurotransmitters at synapses vs chemical hormones.
- [ ] D2 Route: along neurons directly to particular cells vs in the blood to all parts of the body.
- [ ] D3 Speed: very fast (impulses arrive in a fraction of a second) vs slower (seconds to hours, as hormones travel in the blood).
- [ ] D4 Duration: short-lived effects that stop when impulses stop vs longer-lasting effects, because hormones stay in the blood until broken down (mainly in the liver).
- [ ] D5 Area affected: localised (only the muscle or gland the neuron reaches) vs widespread (every target cell with receptors for the hormone, possibly in many organs).
- [ ] D6 Effectors: muscles and glands vs target cells in organs and tissues.
- [ ] D7 Typical uses: rapid responses such as reflexes and movement vs long-term processes such as growth, metabolism, puberty and the menstrual cycle, and steady control of blood glucose.

Similarities (one block)
- [ ] S1 Both use chemical messengers that bind to specific receptors on target cells (neurotransmitters, hormones).
- [ ] S2 Both carry information from receptors or control centres to effectors, and both take part in homeostasis through negative feedback.

How the two systems work together (one block)
- [ ] W1 The hypothalamus links them: it is part of the brain but controls the pituitary gland.
- [ ] W2 In danger, nerve impulses make the adrenal glands release adrenaline, so a fast nervous response is followed by a longer-lasting hormonal one.
- [ ] W3 Worked example table as points: "Touching a hot plate: nervous, reflex, under a second, affects one arm" vs "Rising blood glucose: hormonal, insulin, minutes to hours, affects cells throughout the body".

## 3. Traps

- Don't repeat neuron or synapse structure (The Nervous System) or the list of glands and hormones (The Endocrine System); refer to them by title.
- Don't give exact speeds of nerve impulses or hormone half-lives.
- Don't say hormones "travel only to their target organ"; they travel everywhere in the blood and only target cells respond.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Give two (or three) differences between nervous and hormonal coordination.
- State one similarity.
- Say which system controls a named response and why.
