## 1. Must cover

Each item: Verb: "exact glossary meaning". Example from the specification → subsection title.

Giving information: recall and description (one block)
- [ ] V1 Identify: "recognise patterns, facts, or details; provide an answer from a number of possibilities; recognize and state briefly a distinguishing fact or feature". Example: identify the location of the major glands in the endocrine system → "Glands, locations and hormones".
- [ ] V2 Outline: "give the main points; restrict to essentials". Example: outline the concept of a microbiome → "The microbiome".
- [ ] V3 Describe: "develop a detailed picture or image of, for example a structure or a process, using words or diagrams where appropriate; produce a plan, simulation or model". Example: describe the basic structure and function of a DNA and RNA nucleotide → "Nucleotides, DNA and RNA".
- [ ] V4 Illustrate: "use examples to describe something". Example: illustrate and state Mendel's Laws → "Mendel's Laws".
- [ ] V5 Classify: "group things based on common characteristics". Example: identify and classify living things using classification principles, e.g. with a key → "Classification".
- [ ] V6 Organise: "arrange; to systematise or methodise". Example: organise and communicate research and investigative findings → "Communicating results".
- [ ] V7 Recognise: "identify facts, characteristics or concepts that are critical (relevant/appropriate) to the understanding of a situation, event, process or phenomenon". Example: recognise questions appropriate for scientific investigation → "Questioning and predicting".
- [ ] V8 Appreciate: "recognise the meaning of, have a practical understanding of". Example: appreciate the impact of modern technology on monitoring mother and foetus → "Prenatal and postnatal care".

Giving reasons: explanation and application (one block)
- [ ] V9 Explain: "give a detailed account including reasons or causes". Example: explain how reliability, accuracy, precision, error, fairness and safety have been considered in an investigation → "Designing and conducting investigations".
- [ ] V10 Relate: "associate, giving reasons". Example: relate the structures of the male and female reproductive systems to their functions → "The male reproductive system".
- [ ] V11 Justify: "give valid reasons or evidence to support an answer or conclusion". Example: draw and justify conclusions → "Producing and analysing data".
- [ ] V12 Apply: "select and use information and/or knowledge and understanding to explain a given situation or real circumstances". Example: apply learning and skills to solving problems in unfamiliar contexts → "Reflecting on investigations".
- [ ] V13 Use: "apply knowledge or rules to put theory into practice". Example: use a genome database to search for alleles that cause genetic diseases → "Bioinformatics: genome databases".
- [ ] V14 Predict: "give an expected result of an event; explain a new event based on observations or information using logical connections between pieces of information". Example: use models to predict the behaviour of a system → "Models".
- [ ] V15 Model: "represent an idea, structure, process or system through a variety of means such as words, diagrams, equations, physical models or simulations; use models to describe, explain, make predictions and solve problems, recognising that all models have limitations". Example: model the carbon cycle → "The carbon cycle".

Breaking down: analysis and comparison (one block)
- [ ] V16 Analyse: "study or examine something in detail, break down in order to bring out the essential elements or structure; identify parts and relationships, and to interpret information to reach conclusions". Example: critically analyse data to identify patterns and relationships → "Producing and analysing data".
- [ ] V17 Interpret: "use knowledge and understanding to recognise trends and draw conclusions from given information". No example; one line on its use with graphs and tables of given data.
- [ ] V18 Consider: "describe patterns in data; use knowledge and understanding to interpret patterns; make predictions and check reliability". Example: consider the evidence for evolution → "Evidence for evolution".
- [ ] V19 Compare: "give an account of the similarities and (or) differences between two (or more) items or situations, referring to both (all) of them throughout". Example: compare the use of pyramids of numbers and pyramids of biomass (Higher Level outcome) → "Pyramids of biomass".
- [ ] V20 Distinguish: "make the differences between two or more concepts or items clear". Example: distinguish between bacteria and fungi in terms of structure, nutrition and cellular nature → "Bacteria and fungi".
- [ ] V21 Examine: "consider an argument or concept in a way that uncovers the assumptions and relationships of the issue". Example: examine the role of infectious agents, environmental factors and genetic susceptibility in cancers → "Causes, prevention and treatment of cancer".

Making judgements (one block)
- [ ] V22 Discuss: "offer a considered, balanced review that includes a range of arguments, factors or hypotheses; opinions or conclusions should be presented clearly and supported by appropriate evidence". Example: discuss the importance of microorganisms in industries → "Microorganisms in industry".
- [ ] V23 Evaluate (data): "collect and examine data to make judgments and appraisals; describe how evidence supports or does not support a conclusion in an inquiry or investigation; identify the limitations of data in conclusions; make judgments about the ideas, solutions or methods". Example: evaluate primary or secondary data on the effects of human activity on species diversity → "Investigation: human activity and species diversity".
- [ ] V24 Evaluate (ethical judgement): "collect and examine evidence to make judgments and appraisals; describe how evidence supports or does not support a judgement; identify the limitations of evidence in conclusions; make judgments about the ideas, solutions or methods". Example: evaluate ethical and sustainability issues associated with the cycling of nutrients → "Ethics and sustainability of nutrient cycling".
- [ ] V25 Review: "re-examine deliberately or critically, usually with a view to approval or dissent; to analyse results for the purpose of giving an opinion". Example: review the skills and thinking used in an investigation → "Reflecting on investigations".
- [ ] V26 Reflect: "consider in order to correct or improve". Example: reflect on the skills and thinking used in an investigation → "Reflecting on investigations".

Finding out: investigating and practical work (one block)
- [ ] V27 Investigate: "observe, study, or make a detailed and systematic examination, in order to establish facts and reach new conclusions". Example: investigate factors affecting the growth of microorganisms → "Investigation: growth of microorganisms".
- [ ] V28 Explore: "observe, study, in order to establish facts". Example: explore the role of microbiomes in human health and in nutrient cycling in soils → "The microbiome".
- [ ] V29 Research: "inquire specifically, using involved and critical investigation". Example: research the use of enzymes in industries → "Industrial enzymes".
- [ ] V30 Pose: "put forward for consideration". Example: pose testable hypotheses → "Questioning and predicting".
- [ ] V31 Plan: "devise or project a method or a course of action". Example: design, plan and conduct investigations → "Designing and conducting investigations".
- [ ] V32 Design: "conceive, create and execute according to plan". Same example and subsection as V31.
- [ ] V33 Conduct: "perform an activity". Same example and subsection as V31.
- [ ] V34 Measure: "quantify changes in systems by reading a measuring tool". Example: measure abiotic factors such as temperature, aspect, wind speed and slope in a habitat → "Abiotic factors".
- [ ] V35 Produce: "bring into existence by intellectual or creative ability". Example: produce and select qualitative and quantitative data → "Producing and analysing data".

Confusable pairs (one comparison block, paired points; this is the only content beyond the glossary, and it must follow from the quoted meanings)
- [ ] P1 Outline vs describe: outline gives the main points only; describe gives a detailed picture, in words or diagrams.
- [ ] P2 Describe vs explain: describe says what something is or what happens; explain adds the reasons or causes.
- [ ] P3 Compare vs distinguish: compare refers to both items throughout and may give similarities as well as differences; distinguish makes only the differences clear.
- [ ] P4 Discuss vs evaluate: discuss gives a balanced review of a range of arguments supported by evidence; evaluate goes further, judging how far the evidence supports a conclusion, stating its limitations, and making a judgement.
- [ ] P5 Evaluate (data) vs evaluate (ethical judgement): the first judges data from an inquiry or investigation and its limitations; the second judges the evidence for an ethical judgement and its limitations.
- [ ] P6 Explore vs investigate: both observe and study to establish facts; investigate is a detailed, systematic examination that reaches new conclusions.
- [ ] P7 Review vs reflect: review re-examines critically to give an opinion of approval or dissent; reflect considers in order to correct or improve.

## 3. Traps

- Quote every meaning exactly, including punctuation and the specification's own wording ("judgments", "recognize" in Identify, "and (or)" in Compare). Copy from section 1, not from memory or from other subjects' glossaries; other Leaving Cert subjects define some verbs differently.
- The Describe entry in the specification ends "produce a plan, simulation or model". Keep it, even though it reads like part of another verb's meaning; don't move it to Design or Produce.
- Evaluate has two separate entries; give both, each with its own heading word in brackets.
- Don't add verbs that are not in the glossary (e.g. state, list, define, name, calculate, draw, suggest), even though exam questions may use them.
- Don't add mark allocations or claims about how the SEC marks a verb; the specification gives none.
- The example outcomes belong to their own subsections; give one phrase and the title only.
- No diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

No past papers on this specification. Every outcome begins with one of these verbs, and written-paper questions are expected to use them, so students must know what each requires. Expect short questions such as: what does a question beginning with a given verb require; and the verb in every longer question sets the depth of answer.

## 5. Practice questions to write

Short answers.
1. 3 marks: "What does the action verb 'evaluate' require you to do when evaluating data?" Model answer: V23 meaning: examine data to make judgements; describe how the evidence supports or does not support a conclusion; identify the limitations of the data; make judgements about the ideas, solutions or methods.
2. 6 marks: "Distinguish between the action verbs 'describe' and 'explain', and between 'compare' and 'distinguish'." Model answer: P2 and P3, using the glossary meanings (V3, V9, V19, V20).
3. 6 marks: "A question asks you to 'discuss the importance of microorganisms in industry'. State what 'discuss' requires, and how an answer to 'outline' the same topic would differ." Model answer: V22 (balanced review, range of arguments, clear conclusions supported by evidence) and V2 (main points only, restricted to essentials), as in P1 and P4.
