# Package: bio2-s5 — Reflecting on investigations

- **loId:** `bio2-s5` · **chapterId:** `bio2` · **syllabusRef:** U2.5
- **Syllabus:** Outcome 5: "review and reflect on the skills and thinking used in carrying out
  investigations, and apply their learning and skills to solving problems in unfamiliar
  contexts." Ordinary and Higher level (no Higher Level only blocks).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-22. The spec glossary defines "review" as
  re-examining critically "to analyse results for the purpose of giving an opinion" and
  "reflect" as "consider in order to correct or improve". This subsection owns evaluating an
  investigation and suggesting improvements, and the method for tackling unfamiliar
  investigations in exam questions. Error types are named here only by reference.

## 1. Must cover

Evaluating an investigation (one block)
- [ ] E1 Evaluating means judging how well the method answered the question and how far the data can be trusted, then saying what should change. It is a separate step from the conclusion.
- [ ] E2 Strengths: say what worked and why, e.g. variables well controlled with a water bath and buffer, enough replicates, a small range in repeat readings, results that agree with other groups.
- [ ] E3 Limitations: identify specific weaknesses and their effect on the results, e.g. a variable that could not be controlled, too few values of the independent variable to locate an optimum, a small sample, an end-point judged by eye, an anomaly that could not be repeated. Name each source of error as random or systematic (types in "Objectivity and data quality").
- [ ] E4 Confidence: state how confident you are in the conclusion and why, linking to the evidence (spread of data, repeats, fairness).
- [ ] E5 Improvements must be specific and say how each improves the data: e.g. "use a colorimeter instead of judging the colour by eye, to remove a subjective judgement and reduce random error"; "test at 2 °C intervals between 30 °C and 50 °C to locate the optimum more precisely"; "use five replicates instead of two, to reduce the effect of natural variation"; "calibrate the balance against a known mass, to remove a systematic error". "Be more careful" or "do it again" earns nothing.
- [ ] E6 Further work: an evaluation can suggest a new question or extension, e.g. testing the same enzyme at different pH values, or testing a different tissue. Investigation is iterative: results lead to a revised hypothesis or method.

Reflecting on skills and thinking (one block)
- [ ] R1 Reflection looks back at the investigator's own decisions, not only the data: why the question and hypothesis were chosen, how the method was planned, what problems arose and how they were solved, what changed during the work and why.
- [ ] R2 An investigative log (a dated lab notebook) records plans, decisions, changes, raw data, problems and ideas as the work happens. It makes reflection honest and specific, because it shows how the work actually unfolded rather than how it was meant to go.
- [ ] R3 Good reflection identifies skills used (planning, measuring, handling equipment, recording, analysing, working in a team, finding and using sources) and says which went well and which to improve next time.
- [ ] R4 An investigation that does not support its hypothesis, or gives unexpected results, is still valuable: explaining why is good scientific thinking.

Applying skills to unfamiliar contexts (one skills block: a step-by-step method for exam questions)
- [ ] U1 Exam questions describe investigations the student has never done. A method: (1) find the question being asked; (2) identify the independent variable (what was changed) and the dependent variable (what was measured); (3) list variables that should be controlled and how; (4) suggest a suitable control; (5) state a prediction using biological knowledge; (6) read the data or graph for the trend and any anomaly; (7) draw a conclusion and give a limitation and an improvement.
- [ ] U2 Worked example: "Does the length of time spent exercising affect pulse rate?" Independent variable: exercise time (e.g. 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 minutes of stepping); dependent variable: pulse rate in beats per minute, measured straight after exercise; controlled variables: type and pace of exercise, rest before starting, the way pulse is measured (same site, counted for the same time), time of day; control: resting pulse with no exercise; sample: a larger group chosen at random gives more reliable results, because fitness, age and health vary; ethics: informed consent, right to withdraw, exclude anyone with a health condition; expected trend: pulse rate increases with exercise time, because muscles respire faster and need more oxygen delivered by the blood, possibly levelling off.
- [ ] U3 Transferable checks for any unfamiliar context: is it a fair test? Is the sample big enough and random? Was there a control? Could a correlation have another cause? Are there ethical issues with the organisms or people used?

## 3. Traps

- Evaluation is not the conclusion: the conclusion states what was found; the evaluation judges the method and data.
- Improvements must be specific and give a reason (E5).
- Don't redefine random or systematic error, repeatability or reproducibility; refer to "Objectivity and data quality".
- Ethics and consent are defined in "Designing and conducting investigations"; U2 applies them in one phrase.
- The investigative log for the assessment component is described in "The Biology in Practice Investigation"; R2 describes logs generally.
- Don't explain heart structure or the circulatory system beyond the one reason in U2.
- No diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Suggest an improvement to a described method, or a source of error and how to reduce it.
- New to this specification, expect: questions set in unfamiliar contexts with stimulus material, asking students to identify variables, suggest a control, interpret data and evaluate the method.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "A student judged by eye when a starch–iodine mixture lost its blue-black colour. Identify the weakness in this method and suggest a specific improvement." Model answer: subjective end-point, random error, reduced precision (E3); colorimeter or colour standard, with the reason (E5).
2. 9 marks: "Explain why keeping an investigative log helps a student review and reflect on an investigation." Model answer: R1–R3; records decisions and changes as they happen; supports an honest account; identifies skills to improve.
3. 12 marks: "A student plans to investigate whether the length of time spent exercising affects pulse rate. Identify the independent and dependent variables, two controlled variables, a suitable control and one ethical consideration, and predict the result." Model answer: U2 in full.
