## 1. Must cover

Why water balance must be controlled (one block, three bullets)
- [ ] W1 Osmoregulation (defined in "Water") in humans is mainly done by the kidneys, by changing how much water is reabsorbed from the collecting ducts. The concentration of the blood plasma is the variable being controlled.
- [ ] W2 Water is gained from drink, food and respiration; it is lost in urine, sweat, breath (water vapour) and faeces. Gains and losses change from hour to hour, so the kidney must adjust urine to match.
- [ ] W3 If the blood becomes too concentrated, body cells lose water by osmosis and shrink; if it becomes too dilute, cells gain water and swell. Either upsets cell function, especially in the brain.

ADH and the collecting duct (one block)
- [ ] A1 ADH stands for antidiuretic hormone. Diuresis is the production of a large volume of dilute urine; ADH works against it, so it reduces urine volume. Use the gland facts by title ("Glands, locations and hormones"): one phrase that it is released from the pituitary gland.
- [ ] A2 The target cells are the cells lining the collecting ducts (and the last part of the distal convoluted tubule). ADH binds to receptors on these cells and causes water channel proteins (aquaporins) to be inserted into their membranes, so the wall of the duct becomes more permeable to water. Link: channel proteins are in "Metabolic roles and nutritional sources".
- [ ] A3 Because the collecting duct passes through the salty medulla (made salty by the loop of Henle; see "The nephron"), water moves out of the more permeable duct by osmosis into the medulla and is carried away in the blood capillaries. More ADH → more water reabsorbed → less water left in the duct → a small volume of concentrated urine.
- [ ] A4 Less ADH → fewer water channels → the duct wall is less permeable → less water reabsorbed → a large volume of dilute urine.
- [ ] A5 ADH changes water reabsorption only; it does not change the amount of salt or urea excreted. One bullet.

The feedback loop when the body is short of water (one process block, numbered)
- [ ] D1 1. Stimulus: the water concentration of the blood falls (its solute concentration rises), e.g. after heavy sweating, not drinking enough, eating salty food, or diarrhoea. 2. Receptors: special cells in the hypothalamus of the brain (osmoreceptors) detect the change. 3. The hypothalamus causes the pituitary gland to release more ADH into the blood. 4. ADH travels in the blood to the kidneys. 5. The collecting ducts become more permeable to water and more water is reabsorbed into the blood. 6. A small volume of concentrated, dark-yellow urine is produced. 7. The water concentration of the blood rises back towards normal, and ADH release falls again: negative feedback.
- [ ] D2 At the same time the hypothalamus causes the sensation of thirst, so the person drinks more. One bullet: drinking is the only way to replace lost water; the kidneys can only reduce further loss.

The feedback loop when the body has too much water (one process block, numbered)
- [ ] X1 1. Stimulus: after drinking a large volume of water, the water concentration of the blood rises (it becomes more dilute). 2. The hypothalamus detects the change. 3. The pituitary releases less ADH. 4. The collecting ducts become less permeable to water. 5. Less water is reabsorbed. 6. A large volume of dilute, pale urine is produced. 7. The blood's water concentration falls back to normal and ADH release returns to its usual level.

Examples and applications (one block, one bullet each)
- [ ] E1 Hot day or exercise: much water is lost in sweat, so more ADH is released and urine is small in volume and dark.
- [ ] E2 High salt intake: salt raises the solute concentration of the blood, so more ADH is released, more water is reabsorbed and a smaller volume of urine is produced; the extra salt is excreted in this smaller volume, and the person also feels thirsty.
- [ ] E3 Alcohol reduces the release of ADH, so the collecting ducts reabsorb less water and a large volume of dilute urine is produced; this causes dehydration, which contributes to the headache and thirst of a hangover.

Diagram (one bullet)
- [ ] G1 Diagram: a negative-feedback loop for water balance. Centre: normal blood water concentration. One branch: water concentration falls → hypothalamus detects → pituitary releases more ADH → collecting ducts more permeable → more water reabsorbed → small volume of concentrated urine → back to normal. Other branch: water concentration rises → less ADH → collecting ducts less permeable → less water reabsorbed → large volume of dilute urine → back to normal. Optionally a small sketch of a collecting duct passing through the salty medulla with arrows for water leaving by osmosis.

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading. ADH and aquaporin key terms are Higher Level material.
- Don't repeat the gland facts from "Glands, locations and hormones" (where ADH is made, where it is released from, the one-line function) beyond the single phrase in A1. Don't repeat the general negative-feedback definition from "Homeostasis as a stimulus-response model"; D1 step 7 uses the term.
- More ADH means less urine, not more. Students reverse this. State the direction in every bullet.
- ADH makes the duct more permeable to water; it does not "pump" water. Water moves by osmosis, because of the salty medulla.
- ADH is released when the blood is too concentrated (short of water), not when there is too much water.
- Don't teach aldosterone, renin, blood-pressure (volume) receptors, or diabetes insipidus: they are not on the specification. Don't say caffeine works by blocking ADH.
- Loop of Henle and nephron structure belong to "The nephron"; refer to "the salty medulla" without re-explaining how it forms.
- The diagram cue in G1 is the only one wanted. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Describe how ADH affects the volume of urine.
- State and explain the effect of a high salt intake (or of sweating) on urine volume.
- Name the part of the nephron that ADH acts on and the gland that releases it.
- Explain how ADH secretion is an example of negative feedback.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Describe how ADH affects the volume of urine produced." Model answer: A2–A3 (collecting ducts more permeable, more water reabsorbed by osmosis, small volume of concentrated urine) and A4 for the opposite.
2. 9 marks, Higher: "A student eats a very salty meal. State and explain the effect on the volume of urine she produces." Model answer: E2 with D1 steps 2–6: solute concentration rises, hypothalamus detects, more ADH, collecting ducts more permeable, more water reabsorbed, smaller volume; also thirst (D2).
3. 12 marks, Higher: "Explain how the body keeps the water concentration of the blood constant after drinking a large volume of water and on a hot day, and why this is an example of negative feedback." Model answer: X1 steps 1–7 and D1 steps 1–7 (with E1), ending with the response reversing the change. Must include the hypothalamus, pituitary, ADH and collecting duct, and the correct direction of urine volume in each case.
