## 1. Must cover

The light-independent reactions (one block, process; numbered stages)
- [ ] R1 Location: the stroma of the chloroplast, which contains the enzymes for these reactions. They need no light directly, but depend on the ATP and NADPH made in the light-dependent reactions on the thylakoids.
- [ ] R2 1. Carbon dioxide diffuses into the stroma (from the air spaces of the leaf, through the cell and the chloroplast's membranes) and is taken up (fixed) by combining with a carbon dioxide-accepting compound already present in the stroma.
- [ ] R3 2. NADPH and ATP from the light-dependent reactions enter the reactions.
- [ ] R4 3. Protons and electrons (that is, hydrogen) are transferred from NADPH to the carbon compounds made from CO₂. This adds hydrogen to the carbon (reduction), turning CO₂ into carbohydrate. NADPH becomes NADP⁺.
- [ ] R5 4. The energy to achieve this conversion comes from ATP, which is broken down to ADP + Pi.
- [ ] R6 5. Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is the product; six molecules of CO₂ are fixed for each molecule of glucose. The glucose is then used or converted to sucrose, starch and cellulose (see "Photosynthesis as an anabolic process").
- [ ] R7 6. ADP + Pi and NADP⁺ return to the light-dependent reactions on the thylakoid membranes, where they are reloaded to ATP and NADPH and can be re-used.
- [ ] R8 The reactions form a cycle, the Calvin cycle: most of the carbon compounds made are used to regenerate the CO₂-accepting compound, so the cycle can keep fixing CO₂, and the rest is built into glucose. Name the cycle in one bullet only; its intermediates aren't required.
- [ ] R9 Summary equation for the stage, in words: carbon dioxide + hydrogen (from NADPH) + energy (from ATP) → glucose, with NADP⁺ and ADP + Pi released.

Why the stage is sensitive to temperature and CO₂, and stops in the dark (one block)
- [ ] F1 Every step is catalysed by enzymes in the stroma, so the rate rises with temperature up to an optimum and falls when the enzymes are denatured. This is why temperature affects the rate of photosynthesis (see "Investigation: rate of photosynthesis").
- [ ] F2 CO₂ is the raw material of this stage, so its supply directly limits the rate of glucose production.
- [ ] F3 In darkness the light-dependent reactions stop, ATP and NADPH run out within a short time, and the light-independent reactions then stop too, even though they don't use light directly.

Modelling the two-stage process (one block; the model the outcome asks for)
- [ ] M1 Stage 1, the light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membranes): light energy, absorbed by chlorophyll, splits water and drives electron transfer, producing O₂ (released) and loading the transfer molecules: ADP + Pi → ATP and NADP⁺ → NADPH.
- [ ] M2 Stage 2, the light-independent reactions (stroma): the transfer molecules are unloaded: NADPH gives its protons and electrons to CO₂ and ATP gives the energy, producing glucose; NADPH → NADP⁺ and ATP → ADP + Pi.
- [ ] M3 The transfer molecules link the two stages, carrying energy (ATP) and electrons and hydrogen (NADPH) from stage 1 to stage 2, and returning empty (ADP + Pi, NADP⁺) from stage 2 to stage 1. Because they are recycled, the chloroplast needs only a small supply of each.
- [ ] M4 Tracing the atoms: the oxygen gas comes from water (stage 1); the carbon and the oxygen in glucose come from CO₂ (stage 2); the hydrogen in glucose comes from water, carried by NADPH. (State as given; the isotope evidence is in "Photosynthesis as an anabolic process".)
- [ ] M5 Diagram: the two-stage model. Draw a thylakoid (stage 1) on the left and the stroma (stage 2) on the right. Stage 1 inputs: light, H₂O; output: O₂. Stage 2 input: CO₂; output: glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆). Between the stages draw two loops of arrows: ATP from stage 1 to stage 2 and ADP + Pi back; NADPH from stage 1 to stage 2 and NADP⁺ back. Label each arrow with what it carries (energy; protons and electrons). This builds on the carrier loop in "NAD⁺ and NADP⁺"; draw the two-stage model here, not the loops alone.

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading. All key terms (light-independent reactions, Calvin cycle, carbon fixation, reduction of CO₂) are Higher Level.
- The specification says "protons and electrons are transferred from NADPH to CO₂". Teach this wording; "hydrogen" may be used as shorthand for a proton plus an electron. Don't write that "NADPH gives oxygen" or that CO₂ is "split".
- ATP gives energy, NADPH gives hydrogen (protons and electrons); students often swap these roles or say ATP gives hydrogen.
- It's ADP + Pi (and NADP⁺) that return, not "ATP returns". Don't say NADP⁺ is "used up".
- Don't call this the "dark stage" or say it happens only at night (F3).
- No RuBP, rubisco, glycerate 3-phosphate (GP), triose phosphate (G3P) or C4 and CAM plants: not on the specification.
- Light-dependent detail (photons, photolysis, cyclic and non-cyclic) belongs to "The light-dependent reactions"; M1 summarises it in one bullet only, for the model.
- The investigation of temperature and CO₂ belongs to "Investigation: rate of photosynthesis"; F1–F2 give the reason only.
- Respiration's two-stage model belongs to "Second-stage process"; don't model it here.
- No other diagram cues beyond M5.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- State where the light-independent stage takes place and why it is called light-independent.
- Name the two products of the light-dependent stage that are used in the light-independent stage and describe the role of each (ATP: energy; NADPH: protons and electrons).
- Name the two products of the light-independent stage that are regenerated and returned to the light-dependent stage (ADP + Pi, NADP⁺).
- Explain why the light-independent stage stops soon after a plant is placed in the dark.
