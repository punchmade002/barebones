## 1. Must cover

Excretion and the kidney's two jobs (one block)
- [ ] E1 (assumed) Excretion is the removal of the waste products of metabolism (defined in "Characteristics of living things"; use, don't redefine). The kidneys excrete urea, excess water, excess salts and excess hydrogen ions in urine. The lungs excrete carbon dioxide; sweat removes some water, salts and a little urea. One bullet for the other organs.
- [ ] E2 Urea is made in the liver from excess amino acids (deamination, detail in "Absorption, transport and storage"). It is carried dissolved in the blood plasma to the kidneys. It is toxic if it builds up, so it must be removed continuously.
- [ ] E3 "Transport and transfer between the internal and external environment": the blood (internal environment) carries wastes to the kidneys; the kidneys transfer them from the blood into urine; urine is passed out of the body (external environment). At the same time the kidneys return useful substances (glucose, amino acids, most of the water and salts) to the blood, so nutrients are not lost.
- [ ] E4 The kidneys have two roles: excretion (removing urea and other wastes) and osmoregulation and homeostasis (regulating the volume, water content, salt concentration and pH of the body fluids). Both are done by the same two processes: filtration of the blood, then reabsorption of what the body needs. What is not reabsorbed is urine.

The organs of the urinary system (one structure-and-function block; one bullet per organ)
- [ ] O1 Kidneys: a pair of bean-shaped organs at the back of the abdomen, one on each side of the vertebral column, just below the diaphragm; the lowest ribs partly protect them. The right kidney is slightly lower than the left, because the liver lies above it. Each is embedded in a layer of fat that cushions it against physical damage and helps hold it in place, and is covered by a tough outer membrane, the renal capsule. The adrenal glands sit on top of the kidneys (gland detail in "Glands, locations and hormones").
- [ ] O2 Blood supply: each kidney receives blood from a renal artery, a short, wide branch of the aorta, so blood arrives at high pressure and in large volume, which suits filtration. Filtered blood leaves by the renal vein, which joins the vena cava. Blood in the renal vein has less urea, and less oxygen and glucose, than blood in the renal artery, and more carbon dioxide (the kidney cells respire); it is the blood vessel with the lowest urea concentration in the body.
- [ ] O3 Ureters: two narrow muscular tubes, one from the renal pelvis of each kidney down to the bladder. Their walls contain smooth muscle that moves urine along by peristalsis (waves of contraction), so urine reaches the bladder whatever position the body is in, not just by gravity.
- [ ] O4 Urinary bladder: a hollow, muscular bag in the lower abdomen (pelvis) that stores urine. Its wall is elastic and folded, so it can stretch to hold a large volume and contract to empty. At its outlet two rings of muscle (sphincters) keep it closed: an inner one that works automatically (involuntary) and an outer one under conscious (voluntary) control, which young children learn to control.
- [ ] O5 Urethra: the tube that carries urine from the bladder out of the body. In males it is longer, runs through the penis, and also carries semen; in females it is short and opens just in front of the vagina.
- [ ] O6 Diagram: front view of the urinary system: two kidneys, renal arteries from the aorta, renal veins to the vena cava, ureters, bladder, sphincter muscles and urethra; show the adrenal glands on top of the kidneys if wanted.

Inside the kidney: cortex, medulla and renal pelvis (one structure-and-function block)
- [ ] K1 A kidney cut lengthways shows three regions. Cortex: the dark outer layer, under the renal capsule. Medulla: the paler inner layer, divided into cone-shaped renal pyramids. Renal pelvis: a funnel-shaped space on the inner (concave) side that collects urine and leads into the ureter.
- [ ] K2 Each kidney contains about one million nephrons, microscopic tubules that are the kidney's filtering units. Each nephron runs from the cortex down into the medulla and back; the tubules drain into collecting ducts that run through the pyramids and open into the renal pelvis. Name only; the parts of a nephron are Higher Level material in "The nephron".
- [ ] K3 Site of filtration: the cortex. Here blood is forced at high pressure through knots of capillaries (glomeruli) into the cup-shaped tops of the nephrons (Bowman's capsules). Small molecules (water, glucose, amino acids, salts, urea) are filtered out of the blood; blood cells and plasma proteins are too large and stay in the blood. The filtered liquid is called the filtrate.
- [ ] K4 Sites of reabsorption: the cortex (in the coiled tubules) and the medulla (in the loops of Henle and the collecting ducts). All the glucose and amino acids, most of the salts and most of the water are taken back into the blood; the remaining liquid, carrying urea and excess salts and water, is urine.
- [ ] K5 Renal pelvis: urine from the collecting ducts drains into the pelvis and then into the ureter. No filtration happens in the pelvis and urine is not changed there: it is a collecting space (see Traps).
- [ ] K6 Diagram: longitudinal section of a kidney labelled renal capsule, cortex, medulla, renal pyramid, renal pelvis, ureter, renal artery and renal vein, with the position of one nephron drawn in (from cortex into medulla) to show where filtration and reabsorption happen.

Regulation of body fluids by the kidney (one block)
- [ ] R1 Urine is mostly water, with urea, salts (mainly sodium chloride) and other wastes dissolved in it. Healthy urine contains no protein and no glucose, because protein is not filtered and glucose is all reabsorbed.
- [ ] R2 The kidneys keep the water content of the blood steady by changing the volume and concentration of urine. After drinking a lot of water, a large volume of dilute (pale) urine is produced. When water is short (little drinking, heavy sweating in exercise or hot weather, diarrhoea), a small volume of concentrated (dark yellow) urine is produced. The control is by the hormone ADH (Higher Level detail in "ADH and osmoregulation"); this block gives the effect only.
- [ ] R3 Salt balance: after a salty meal the kidneys excrete more salt; when salt is short they reabsorb more. Keeping the salt and water concentration of the blood steady stops body cells from gaining or losing water by osmosis (see "Water").
- [ ] R4 pH: the kidneys remove excess hydrogen ions from the blood in urine, helping to keep blood pH at about 7.4. One bullet; link to "Minerals" for buffers.
- [ ] R5 This is homeostasis: the kidneys keep the internal environment constant whatever is eaten or drunk (negative feedback is taught in "Homeostasis as a stimulus-response model").

Transport of urine from kidney to urethra (one block, as a numbered sequence)
- [ ] T1 1. Urine forms continuously in the nephrons and drains from the collecting ducts into the renal pelvis. 2. Peristalsis in the ureter carries it to the bladder in small spurts. 3. The bladder fills and its wall stretches; stretch receptors in the wall send nerve impulses that give the urge to urinate. 4. To urinate, the bladder's muscular wall contracts and the sphincters relax. 5. Urine leaves through the urethra. Urination is under voluntary control of the outer sphincter after early childhood.

Relating structure to function (one summary block; one paired point per structure)
- [ ] S1 Fat layer → protection; renal artery direct from the aorta → high-pressure blood for filtration; about a million nephrons → huge filtering surface; cortex full of capillary knots → filtration; long tubules and loops in the medulla → reabsorption; renal pelvis funnel → collects urine; muscular ureters → peristalsis; elastic bladder with sphincters → storage and controlled release; urethra → exit. One bullet per pair; no new facts beyond O1–K5.

Health and technology link (one block, three bullets)
- [ ] H1 Urine testing is used in diagnosis: glucose in urine is a sign of diabetes (blood glucose so high that not all of it can be reabsorbed); protein in urine is a sign of damage to the kidney's filters. Urine tests are also used to detect drugs, e.g. in sport.
- [ ] H2 Kidney failure: if the kidneys stop working, urea, salts and water build up in the blood and the person will die without treatment. Treatment is by dialysis or a kidney transplant.
- [ ] H3 Dialysis (technology): the patient's blood is pumped through a machine where it flows past a selectively permeable membrane with dialysis fluid on the other side. Urea and excess salts diffuse out of the blood into the fluid; the fluid contains glucose and salts at normal blood concentrations so these are not lost; blood cells and proteins are too large to cross the membrane. It must be repeated several times a week. A transplant gives a working kidney but the patient must take drugs to stop the immune system rejecting it (link to "Immunity" by title only).

## 3. Traps

- Excretion is not egestion: faeces are egested (never absorbed), urine is excreted (metabolic waste). Defined in "Characteristics of living things"; one phrase here.
- Urea is made in the liver, not the kidney. The kidney only removes it.
- The syllabus phrase "reabsorption in the cortex, medulla and renal pelvis" lists the three regions; teach that filtration is in the cortex, reabsorption in the cortex and medulla, and that the renal pelvis collects urine. Don't say filtration or reabsorption happens in the pelvis.
- Ureter (kidney to bladder, two of them) vs urethra (bladder to outside, one): the commonest error. Say both clearly.
- Keep nephron parts to the names in K2–K4 (glomeruli, Bowman's capsules, coiled tubules, loops of Henle, collecting ducts). Proximal and distal convoluted tubules, afferent and efferent arterioles, ultrafiltration pressure, active transport in the tubule and the filtrate-volume figures are Higher Level material in "The nephron"; don't put them here.
- ADH, the hypothalamus and the pituitary belong to "ADH and osmoregulation" (mechanism) and "Glands, locations and hormones" (gland facts). R2 says only that a hormone, ADH, controls it.
- Excretion in plants, and the skin as an excretory organ, were on the previous syllabus; don't teach them. E1 names sweat in one phrase only.
- The circulation route of the renal vessels belongs to "Circulation"; O2 gives only what the kidney needs.
- Dialysis and transplants are a health and technology link: keep to H2–H3; no peritoneal dialysis, tissue matching or donor statistics.
- The diagram cues listed (O6, K6) are the only ones wanted. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define excretion; distinguish excretion and egestion.
- State where urea is made and from which biomolecule.
- Label a kidney section: cortex, medulla (pyramid), renal pelvis, ureter; say in which region filtration happens and where named nephron parts are found.
- State the function of the fat around the kidneys; name the tube that carries urine to the bladder.
- Compare blood in the renal artery and renal vein.
- State how the volume of urine changes with water intake or sweating.
