## 1. Must cover

What a seed is (one block)
- [ ] W1 A seed is a fertilised, mature ovule. It contains an embryo (a new plant in miniature), a food store, and a protective coat (the testa). It is the product of sexual reproduction in flowering plants.
- [ ] W2 As the seed matures it loses most of its water and becomes dry and hard, and the embryo's activity slows almost to a stop. One bullet; don't name or explain dormancy.

Seed structures and their functions (one Structure-and-function block)
- [ ] S1 Testa: the seed coat, formed from the outer layers (integuments) of the ovule. It protects the embryo from mechanical damage, from attack by insects, fungi and bacteria, and from drying out.
- [ ] S2 Embryo: the young plant, which developed from the zygote by mitosis. It is made of the plumule, the radicle and one or two cotyledons.
- [ ] S3 Plumule: the embryonic shoot of the embryo; it will grow into the shoot (stem and leaves) of the new plant.
- [ ] S4 Radicle: the embryonic root of the embryo; it will grow into the first root of the new plant. It points towards the micropyle.
- [ ] S5 Cotyledon (assumed): a seed leaf, part of the embryo. In some seeds the cotyledons absorb and store the food; in others they pass food from the endosperm to the growing embryo.
- [ ] S6 Endosperm: a food store outside the embryo, formed by double fertilisation. It supplies the embryo with food (mainly starch; also oils and proteins in some seeds) when it starts to grow into a seedling, before the seedling's leaves can photosynthesise.
- [ ] S7 Micropyle and hilum (assumed, one bullet): the micropyle is a tiny pore in the testa (where the pollen tube entered the ovule); the hilum is the scar where the seed was attached to the inside of the fruit.
- [ ] S8 Food stored in seeds (one bullet): starch (e.g. cereal grains, beans), oils (e.g. sunflower seeds, oilseed rape) and protein (e.g. peas, beans). These food stores make seeds important human foods (cereals, pulses, vegetable oils) (link to "Metabolic roles and nutritional sources").

Two seed types compared (one block, paired points)
- [ ] T1 Monocotyledons have one cotyledon; dicotyledons have two. Endospermic seeds keep the endosperm as the food store when mature; non-endospermic seeds have absorbed the endosperm into the cotyledons as the seed developed.
- [ ] T2 Maize (a monocot): one cotyledon; large endosperm is the food store; the cotyledon passes food from the endosperm to the embryo. The maize grain is strictly a fruit, because its thin fruit wall is fused to the testa.
- [ ] T3 Broad bean (a dicot): two large cotyledons store the food; no endosperm in the mature seed.
- [ ] T4 Diagram: a broad bean seed opened (one cotyledon removed), labelled testa, micropyle, hilum, cotyledon, plumule, radicle.
- [ ] T5 Diagram: a vertical section of a maize grain, labelled fruit wall and testa (fused), endosperm, cotyledon, plumule, radicle.

Seeds and growth regulators in fruit growth (one block)
- [ ] G1 A growth regulator (plant hormone) is a chemical made in small amounts in one part of a plant that controls growth or development elsewhere. Developing seeds make growth regulators, mainly auxins (e.g. indoleacetic acid, IAA) and gibberellins.
- [ ] G2 After fertilisation the developing seeds release these growth regulators into the surrounding tissues. They stimulate the cells of the ovary wall (or, in some fruits, the receptacle) to divide and enlarge, so the fruit grows. Without fertilised seeds, most flowers simply wither and fall without forming a fruit.
- [ ] G3 Evidence: strawberry. The fleshy part of a strawberry is the swollen receptacle; the "pips" on its surface are tiny dry fruits, each with one seed. In experiments by J. P. Nitsch (published 1950), removing the pips stopped the receptacle growing; applying auxin to the de-seeded receptacle restored its growth, and removing pips from only one strip gave a strawberry that grew only where seeds remained.
- [ ] G4 The number and spread of seeds affect the size and shape of the fruit: fruits with few or unevenly spread fertilised seeds are small or misshapen (e.g. poorly pollinated strawberries and apples), which is one reason good pollination matters to growers.
- [ ] G5 Technology link: seedless fruits (parthenocarpy, fruit formation without fertilisation). Growers spray unpollinated flowers with auxins (e.g. tomatoes) or gibberellins so that the fruit grows without seeds; gibberellins are sprayed on seedless grape varieties to make the grapes larger. Some plants form fruit naturally without seeds, e.g. cultivated bananas, which are sterile (triploid) plants.

The role of seeds in plant reproduction (one block)
- [ ] R1 A seed carries the next generation: the embryo is a new individual formed by sexual reproduction, with half its genes from each parent, so seeds give genetic variation within the species (link to "Mitosis and meiosis").
- [ ] R2 The testa protects the embryo, and the food store supplies it until the seedling can photosynthesise.
- [ ] R3 Seeds (within fruits) allow the plant to spread to new places, away from the parent: see "Fruit and seed dispersal".
- [ ] R4 Dry seeds with a tough testa can survive conditions that would kill the parent plant (e.g. winter, drought), so they carry the species through to a new growing season. One bullet; don't use the word dormancy or describe germination.
- [ ] R5 Developing seeds make the growth regulators that cause the fruit to form (G2), and the fruit in turn protects and disperses the seeds.

## 3. Traps

- The plumule becomes the shoot and the radicle the root; don't swap them.
- The endosperm is outside the embryo; the cotyledons are part of the embryo.
- Broad bean seeds have no endosperm when mature; don't label one on the diagram.
- A seed is a mature ovule; a fruit is a mature ovary. Strawberry "pips" are fruits; the strawberry's flesh is receptacle, not ovary wall.
- Germination (conditions, stages, digestion of food reserves, epigeal and hypogeal growth) and seed dormancy were on the previous syllabus and are not on this specification. Describe the future role of plumule, radicle and food store in one phrase each, nothing more. Previous-syllabus germination questions must not be used.
- Cytokinins, ethene and abscisic acid are not required (outline Appendix A). Don't mention them. Ethene and fruit ripening are not "growth of fruit tissues".
- Tropisms and how auxin controls shoot growth are not on this specification; G1 defines auxin only as a fruit-growth regulator here. Synthetic auxin weedkillers, rooting powder and ethene ripening are in "Hormonal manipulation"; don't repeat them.
- Formation of the seed from the zygote and double fertilisation are in "Flower structure and pollination"; don't re-explain.
- The diagram cues T4 and T5 are all that are wanted. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Draw and label a seed, showing testa, plumule and radicle; give the function of each.
- Name the food store in a seed and give a food stored in it.
- Distinguish monocot and dicot seeds, or endospermic and non-endospermic seeds.
- Explain how seedless fruits may be produced (growth regulators).
- State which part of the flower forms the fruit and how seeds stimulate fruit growth.
