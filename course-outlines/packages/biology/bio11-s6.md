## 1. Must cover

What incomplete dominance is (one block)
- [ ] D1 What incomplete dominance looks like in results (the definition is in "Key concepts of information transfer"; don't restate it): the heterozygote's phenotype lies between those of the two homozygotes, e.g. pink snapdragons from red × white. So there are three phenotypes rather than two, and each genotype can be recognised from its phenotype; with complete dominance the heterozygote looks like the homozygous dominant (Tt is as tall as TT) and there are only two.
- [ ] D2 Why it happens (one bullet): in snapdragon flower colour, the red allele codes for a working enzyme that makes red pigment and the white allele codes for no working pigment enzyme; a plant with two red alleles makes enough pigment to be red, while a heterozygote has only one working copy, makes less pigment, and is pink. The alleles are not mixed or changed: red and white flowers reappear in the next generation (D5), which shows the genes themselves do not blend.
- [ ] D3 Symbols: because neither allele is dominant, don't use a capital and a lower-case letter of the same kind as if one were dominant. Use the letters the question gives. If choosing, use two different capital letters, e.g. R = allele for red, W = allele for white (so RR red, RW pink, WW white), and state in the key that the alleles show incomplete dominance. (Questions sometimes use B and b with a statement that B is incompletely dominant; follow the question's key.)

Worked example 1: the first cross, homozygous × homozygous (one block)
- [ ] D4 Snapdragon. Key: R = red, W = white, incomplete dominance. Parents' phenotypes: red × white. Parents' genotypes: RR × WW. Gametes: R × W. Offspring genotype: all RW. Offspring phenotype: all (100%) pink.

Worked example 2: heterozygote × heterozygote (one block, every line of the standard layout)
- [ ] D5 Pink × pink. Parents' genotypes: RW × RW. Gametes: R, W × R, W. Punnett square: RR, RW / RW, WW. Offspring genotypes: 1 RR : 2 RW : 1 WW. Offspring phenotypes: 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white (25% red, 50% pink, 25% white).
- [ ] D6 Diagram: the 2 × 2 Punnett square for RW × RW with gametes R and W on each side, boxes RR, RW, RW, WW, and each box labelled red, pink, pink or white (or shaded red, pink, white).
- [ ] D7 The key result: the phenotype ratio (1 : 2 : 1) is the same as the genotype ratio, because each genotype has its own phenotype. With complete dominance the same cross gives 3 : 1. So if a cross of two parents that look the same gives three phenotypes in a 1 : 2 : 1 ratio, the trait shows incomplete dominance.

Worked example 3: heterozygote × homozygote (one block)
- [ ] D8 Pink × white: RW × WW. Gametes: R, W × W. Offspring genotypes: 1 RW : 1 WW. Phenotypes: 1 pink : 1 white. Pink × red: RW × RR gives 1 RR : 1 RW, 1 red : 1 pink.

Second example: Andalusian fowl (one block)
- [ ] A1 In Andalusian chickens, feather colour shows incomplete dominance. Homozygous black × homozygous white gives all offspring with grey-blue feathers, called "blue". Blue × blue gives 1 black : 2 blue : 1 white. If black were instead completely dominant to white, the same blue-type (heterozygous) cross would give 3 black : 1 white.
- [ ] A2 Practical point: blue Andalusians can never be true-breeding, because every blue bird is heterozygous; breeders get all-blue chicks by crossing black with white birds.

Incomplete dominance combined with a second gene (one bullet, cross-reference)
- [ ] C1 A cross can combine an incompletely dominant gene with a completely dominant one (e.g. snapdragon flower colour with plant height). This is a two-gene cross: the worked example is in "Dihybrid crosses".

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading.
- Incomplete dominance is not "blending inheritance": the alleles stay separate and the parental phenotypes reappear in the F2 (D2, D5).
- Don't confuse incomplete dominance with codominance, in which both alleles are fully expressed side by side (e.g. AB blood group, where both A and B antigens are made). Codominance is not named on the specification; one bullet at most, inside D1, only to say AB blood group is not an example of incomplete dominance. No codominance crosses.
- The pink phenotype is the heterozygote; students wrongly write pink as RR or as a separate allele "P".
- With incomplete dominance, 1 : 2 : 1 is the phenotype ratio; with complete dominance, 1 : 2 : 1 is only the genotype ratio.
- Diagram cues: only D6. No other diagram cues.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Explain the term incomplete dominance.
- Given an incompletely dominant trait (e.g. feather colour in Andalusian fowl or flower colour in snapdragons), use a cross to give the genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring of two heterozygotes, with the ratio.
- State how the ratio would differ if one allele were completely dominant.
- Combine an incompletely dominant gene with a dominant one in a two-gene cross (taught in "Dihybrid crosses").

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Explain what is meant by incomplete dominance, using a named example." Model answer: the definition (heterozygote intermediate between the two homozygotes), D1–D2, with the snapdragon or Andalusian example; must not call it blending.
2. 12 marks, Higher: "In snapdragons, red flower (R) and white flower (W) show incomplete dominance. Using a genetic cross, show the genotypes and phenotypes of the offspring of two pink-flowered plants and give the phenotype ratio." Model answer: every line of the layout as D5; 1 RR red : 2 RW pink : 1 WW white.
3. 9 marks, Higher: "Two blue Andalusian chickens are crossed. Give the expected phenotype ratio and explain how this ratio would differ if black feathers were completely dominant to white." Model answer: A1 (1 black : 2 blue : 1 white vs 3 black : 1 white) with D7 reasoning.
