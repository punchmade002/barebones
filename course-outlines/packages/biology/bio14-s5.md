## 1. Must cover

Internal structure of the chloroplast (one block, structure and function)
- [ ] S1 Envelope: two membranes (outer and inner) surround the chloroplast. The inner membrane controls which substances enter and leave (e.g. CO₂ in, products of photosynthesis out), keeping the stroma's enzymes and conditions separate from the cytoplasm.
- [ ] S2 Thylakoids: flattened membrane sacs inside the chloroplast, separate from the envelope. Each encloses a fluid-filled thylakoid space (lumen). Thylakoids are stacked like piles of coins into grana (singular granum); grana are joined by single, unstacked thylakoid membranes (intergranal lamellae), so the whole system is one connected network of membranes.
- [ ] S3 The thylakoid membranes hold the pigments in their clusters and reaction centres, the electron transport chains and the enzyme ATP synthase: they are the site of the light-dependent reactions.
- [ ] S4 Stroma: the fluid around the thylakoids, containing the enzymes of the light-independent reactions, so it is the site of those reactions. It also contains starch grains (temporary store of glucose), lipid droplets, ribosomes and a small loop of DNA.
- [ ] S5 Own DNA and ribosomes: the chloroplast makes some of its own proteins, including some of the proteins of the thylakoid membrane and of the light-independent reactions. (One bullet; that this DNA is inherited separately from the nucleus belongs to "Nuclear and non-nuclear inheritance".)
- [ ] S6 Diagram: an electron-microscope-level drawing of a chloroplast in section, labelled outer membrane, inner membrane, stroma, granum, thylakoid, thylakoid space, intergranal lamella, starch grain, ribosomes and DNA. This extends the simpler chloroplast diagram in "Structures and organelles of animal and plant cells".

The electron transport chain in the thylakoid membrane (one block, structure and function)
- [ ] E1 An electron transport chain is a series of electron carriers that pass electrons from one to the next, each at a slightly lower energy level, releasing energy in small steps that can be used.
- [ ] E2 In the thylakoid membrane the chain is made of a number of large protein complexes embedded in (spanning) the membrane, linked by smaller mobile electron carriers that move within or along the membrane, carrying electrons from one complex to the next.
- [ ] E3 Some protein complexes contain the clusters of pigment molecules and the reaction-centre chlorophyll; others pass electrons on and use their energy. NADP⁺ is reduced to NADPH on the stroma side of the membrane, right where the light-independent reactions use it.
- [ ] E4 Embedding the complexes in a membrane holds them in a fixed order and close together, so electrons pass quickly and in the right sequence, and the energy released can be used to move protons across the membrane (T5).

The thylakoid proton gradient (two bullets only; mechanism in full in the mitochondrion subsection)
- [ ] T5 As electrons pass along the chain, protons (H⁺) are moved from the stroma into the thylakoid space; protons released by photolysis of water are also released there. The small, enclosed thylakoid space therefore builds up a high concentration of protons.
- [ ] T6 The protons flow back out of the thylakoid space into the stroma through ATP synthase in the thylakoid membrane, and the enzyme uses this flow to make ATP from ADP and Pi. The ATP is made on the stroma side, where the light-independent reactions need it. The same mechanism makes ATP in mitochondria; it is explained step by step in "The mitochondrion's internal structure and ATP synthase".

How the internal structure facilitates photosynthesis (one block, one structure–function point per bullet)
- [ ] F1 Large surface area: the many stacked thylakoids give a huge area of membrane for pigment clusters, electron transport chains and ATP synthase, so more light can be absorbed and more ATP and NADPH made.
- [ ] F2 Organisation of pigments: grouping many pigment molecules around each reaction centre funnels the energy of photons absorbed over a wide area, and over a range of colours, to the reaction centres.
- [ ] F3 Separate compartments: the thylakoid space is a small, enclosed compartment, so protons can be concentrated in it to drive ATP synthase; the thylakoid membrane is not freely permeable to protons, so the gradient is kept.
- [ ] F4 Two stages side by side: the light-dependent reactions on the membranes and the light-independent reactions in the stroma are next to each other, so ATP and NADPH are released into the stroma directly where they are used, and ADP + Pi and NADP⁺ return a short distance.
- [ ] F5 The stroma holds all the enzymes of the light-independent reactions in one compartment with their substrates, and stores the product temporarily as starch.
- [ ] F6 Own DNA and ribosomes let the chloroplast make some of its own proteins on the spot (S5).
- [ ] F7 Related (one bullet): leaf cells that do most photosynthesis, the palisade mesophyll, are packed with chloroplasts, which can move within the cell towards or away from the light.
- [ ] F8 Diagram: a section of a thylakoid membrane, with the stroma above and the thylakoid space below. Show a pigment cluster with its reaction-centre chlorophyll receiving light; electron carriers and protein complexes in the membrane with an arrow for electron flow; water being split on the thylakoid-space side (H₂O → 2H⁺ + ½O₂ + 2e⁻); H⁺ being moved into the thylakoid space and building up there; NADP⁺ + H⁺ → NADPH on the stroma side; and ATP synthase with H⁺ flowing out into the stroma and ADP + Pi → ATP. Label protein complex, mobile electron carrier, thylakoid membrane, thylakoid space, stroma, ATP synthase.

## 3. Traps

- Every block is Higher Level only; mark every heading. Key terms (thylakoid space, intergranal lamella, electron transport chain, protein complex, mobile electron carrier, ATP synthase) are Higher Level.
- In the chloroplast, protons build up inside the thylakoid space, not in the stroma and not between the two envelope membranes. ATP is made in the stroma. (In mitochondria protons build up in the intermembrane space; that comparison is made in "The mitochondrion's internal structure and ATP synthase".)
- The electron transport chain is in the thylakoid membrane, not in the envelope membranes and not in the stroma.
- The specification's step-by-step proton wording ("charge difference which attracts protons to the complex, given by donor molecules") is written for the mitochondrion and is taught there; don't repeat it here. T5–T6 are the only proton bullets.
- The stages themselves belong to "The light-dependent reactions" and "The light-independent reactions"; here, link each structure to its job without re-teaching the stages.
- The basic chloroplast diagram and the Ordinary Level description belong to "Structures and organelles of animal and plant cells"; S6 is the Higher Level version with extra labels. Leaf structure belongs to "Transport in Plants".
- Chloroplast origin by endosymbiosis is not on the specification; don't add it.
- No other diagram cues beyond S6 and F8.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Label a diagram of a chloroplast (granum, thylakoid, stroma, membranes) and state the stage of photosynthesis that takes place in each part.
- Name the location of the electron transport chain in photosynthesis.
- Explain how the internal structure of a chloroplast suits it to photosynthesis.
