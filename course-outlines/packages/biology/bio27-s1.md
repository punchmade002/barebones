## 1. Must cover

Human nutrition and the four stages (one block)
- [ ] P1 (assumed) Humans are heterotrophs (defined in "Characteristics of living things"; one phrase) and omnivores: they eat both plant and animal material.
- [ ] P2 Ingestion: taking food into the body through the mouth.
- [ ] P3 Digestion: the breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed. It is both mechanical (physical breakdown into smaller pieces) and chemical (hydrolysis of large molecules by enzymes).
- [ ] P4 Why digestion is necessary: large molecules such as starch, proteins and fats cannot pass through the wall of the gut or through cell membranes; digestion converts them to small soluble molecules (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol) that can be absorbed into the blood and used by cells to build the body's own molecules or for respiration.
- [ ] P5 Absorption: the passage of the small soluble products of digestion, with water, vitamins and minerals, through the wall of the gut into the blood or lymph (mainly in the small intestine).
- [ ] P6 (assumed) Assimilation: the use of absorbed molecules by body cells, e.g. amino acids built into proteins, glucose respired. One bullet; detail in "Absorption, transport and storage".
- [ ] P7 Egestion: the removal of undigested and unabsorbed material (mainly dietary fibre), as faeces, through the anus. Egestion is not excretion: faeces were never part of the body's metabolism (except bile pigments, see "Chemical breakdown"), whereas excretion removes metabolic waste (see "Characteristics of living things").

The alimentary canal (one block)
- [ ] C1 The alimentary canal (gut) is a continuous muscular tube from the mouth to the anus. Food passes in order through the mouth, pharynx (throat), oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum and anus.
- [ ] C2 Its wall contains two layers of smooth muscle, circular and longitudinal, which move food along by peristalsis (detail in "Mechanical breakdown"). Its inner lining makes mucus, which lubricates food and protects the lining from digestive enzymes and acid.
- [ ] C3 Sphincters are rings of muscle that control movement between parts: e.g. at the entrance and exit of the stomach (the exit one is the pyloric sphincter) and at the anus.

The organs of the alimentary canal (one structure-and-function block; one bullet per organ: position and role)
- [ ] O1 Mouth: food is ingested, chewed by the teeth and mixed with saliva from three pairs of salivary glands; the tongue shapes it into a ball (bolus) and pushes it back for swallowing. Starch digestion begins here.
- [ ] O2 Oesophagus (gullet): a muscular tube that carries food from the pharynx, down through the chest behind the trachea and through the diaphragm, to the stomach, by peristalsis. No digestion or absorption happens in it. During swallowing the epiglottis covers the entrance to the trachea so food goes down the oesophagus (one phrase; breathing tract detail in "Gas exchange and the breathing mechanism").
- [ ] O3 Stomach: a J-shaped muscular bag in the upper left of the abdomen, just below the diaphragm. It stores food for a few hours, churns it with gastric juice (acid and a protease) into a thick liquid called chyme, kills most bacteria in food, and begins protein digestion. The pyloric sphincter releases chyme a little at a time into the small intestine.
- [ ] O4 Small intestine: the longest part of the gut, coiled in the abdomen. Its first part, the duodenum, receives bile from the liver and pancreatic juice from the pancreas, and most chemical digestion happens there. The later parts (the jejunum and the ileum; students need to name the duodenum and ileum) complete digestion and absorb most digested food and water through millions of villi.
- [ ] O5 Large intestine: wider but shorter than the small intestine. Its parts are the caecum, with the appendix (a small dead-end tube with no digestive role in humans) attached, the colon and the rectum. The colon absorbs water and some minerals from the remaining material, and is home to many bacteria (detail in "Absorption, transport and storage" and "The microbiome").
- [ ] O6 Rectum: the last part of the large intestine, which stores faeces until egestion.
- [ ] O7 Anus: the opening at the end of the gut, closed by sphincter muscles; the outer sphincter is under voluntary control, so egestion can be delayed.

The associated glands and organs (one structure-and-function block; one bullet each)
- [ ] G1 Salivary glands: three pairs, around the mouth and below the jaw, releasing saliva into the mouth through ducts. Saliva contains water, mucus and the enzyme salivary amylase.
- [ ] G2 Liver: the largest internal organ, in the upper right of the abdomen just below the diaphragm. Its digestive role is to make bile. It also receives the absorbed products of digestion through the hepatic portal vein, stores glycogen and deaminates amino acids (detail in "Absorption, transport and storage").
- [ ] G3 Gallbladder: a small sac under the liver that stores and concentrates bile, and squeezes it through the bile duct into the duodenum when fatty food arrives there. It makes no bile itself.
- [ ] G4 Pancreas: a soft, pinkish gland behind the stomach, lying in the curve of the duodenum. It makes pancreatic juice (enzymes for starch, protein and fat, and sodium hydrogencarbonate), which flows through the pancreatic duct into the duodenum. It is also an endocrine gland making insulin (in "Glands, locations and hormones"); a gland with both ducted (exocrine) and ductless (endocrine) roles.
- [ ] G5 The liver, gallbladder and pancreas are associated organs: food never passes through them; their secretions reach the gut through ducts. Food does pass through the mouth, stomach and intestines.

Diagram (one bullet)
- [ ] D1 Diagram: the human alimentary canal and its associated glands, front view, labelled mouth, salivary glands, tongue, oesophagus, diaphragm, liver, gallbladder, bile duct, stomach, pyloric sphincter, pancreas, pancreatic duct, duodenum, ileum, caecum, appendix, colon, rectum and anus. Draw the gut as one continuous tube; the liver above the stomach on the body's right; the gallbladder under the liver; the pancreas in the loop of the duodenum.

Summary of the journey (one block; one bullet per stage)
- [ ] S1 A one-line summary of what happens in each part, in order, with the stage of nutrition: mouth (ingestion; mechanical and chemical digestion begins) → oesophagus (transport) → stomach (storage, churning, protein digestion begins) → duodenum (most chemical digestion, with bile and pancreatic juice) → ileum (digestion completed; absorption) → colon (water absorption) → rectum (storage of faeces) → anus (egestion). No enzyme names or pH values in this block; they are in "Chemical breakdown".

## 3. Traps

- Associated organs (liver, gallbladder, pancreas, salivary glands) are not part of the canal; food does not pass through them. Students draw food going through the liver.
- The gallbladder stores bile; the liver makes it.
- The large intestine is wider but shorter than the small intestine: "large" refers to width.
- Excretion vs egestion: faeces are egested. Don't call the large intestine an excretory organ.
- The appendix has no digestive role in humans; don't give it one.
- Comparative dentition (herbivore and carnivore teeth) and the dental formula belong to "Mechanical breakdown"; enzyme names and pH to "Chemical breakdown"; villus structure to "Absorption, transport and storage". This subsection names roles in a phrase only.
- The pancreas's insulin role belongs to "Glands, locations and hormones"; one phrase in G4.
- The diagram cue in D1 is the only one wanted. No other diagram cues.
