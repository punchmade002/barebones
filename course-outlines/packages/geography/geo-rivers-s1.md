## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The river and its course
- [ ] R1 River terms: source, mouth, tributary, confluence, drainage basin (catchment), watershed, distributary, estuary. Exams ask students to match these to labelled maps or diagrams.
- [ ] R2 Long profile: the smooth, concave slope from source to mouth, steep near the source and gentle near the mouth. Three courses (stages): upper (youthful), middle (mature), lower (old). State which landforms belong to which course. Exams ask students to identify the course from a labelled profile.
- [ ] R3 River energy depends on volume (discharge) and gradient; energy left after overcoming friction is used for erosion and transport. Volume increases downstream as tributaries join.
- [ ] R4 Vertical erosion (deepening) dominates in the upper course; lateral erosion (widening) dominates in the middle and lower courses. Headward erosion lengthens a valley at its source. One bullet each.

Erosion processes (each: what drives it, how it works)
- [ ] E1 Hydraulic action. Exams ask students to explain it briefly.
- [ ] E2 Abrasion (corrasion), including the drilling of potholes by pebbles swirled in eddies. Exams ask students to explain it briefly.
- [ ] E3 Attrition: load becomes smaller and more rounded downstream.
- [ ] E4 Solution (corrosion): dissolving of soluble rock such as limestone.

Transport and deposition
- [ ] T1 Four methods of transport: traction, saltation, suspension, solution.
- [ ] T2 Competence (the largest particle a river can carry) and capacity (the total load it can carry); both rise sharply in flood. One bullet each, no graphs or figures.
- [ ] T3 Causes of deposition: a fall in velocity (entering a lake or sea, the inside of a bend, receding floodwater, a gentler gradient) or a load too great for the river's energy.
- [ ] T4 Deposits are sorted by size (heaviest first, finest last) and the load is rounded. This contrasts with unsorted, angular glacial till (one bullet; till itself is in `geo-glaciation-s1`).

Drainage patterns (each: shape, the rock or relief that causes it, example from section 2 where given)
- [ ] P1 Dendritic.
- [ ] P2 Trellised.
- [ ] P3 Radial.
- [ ] P4 Deranged.
- [ ] P5 Centripetal (rivers flow inward to a central lake or basin).
- [ ] P6 One bullet on how each pattern looks on an Ordnance Survey map (exams ask students to identify trellised and dendritic patterns on a map). Method only; no grid references.

Landforms of fluvial erosion (each: formation in numbered stages, example from section 2, diagram cue)
- [ ] L1 V-shaped valley and interlocking spurs (upper course).
- [ ] L2 Waterfall, plunge pool and gorge (upper course).
- [ ] L3 Rapids (alternating bands of hard and soft rock) and potholes. Short block; no example.

Landforms of erosion and deposition
- [ ] L4 Meander: river cliff on the outer bank (erosion), point bar / slip-off slope on the inner bank (deposition), helicoidal flow. State that a meander is formed by both erosion and deposition; exams ask students to classify it.
- [ ] L5 Oxbow lake and meander scar.

Landforms of fluvial deposition (each: formation, example, diagram cue)
- [ ] D1 Floodplain (alluvium).
- [ ] D2 Levees, including the raised river bed.
- [ ] D3 Delta: conditions needed; flocculation; distributaries; topset, foreset and bottomset beds; types (arcuate, bird's-foot, estuarine); lake deltas.
- [ ] D4 Estuary and its mudflats and salt marsh (deposition in the tidal river mouth where fresh and salt water meet). Short block.
- [ ] D5 Braided channel: a river split by islands of deposited sand and gravel (eyots/braid bars) where the load is large and discharge varies. One or two bullets; no example.

Classifying and explaining
- [ ] X1 One bullet listing the landforms by process: erosion (V-shaped valley, interlocking spurs, waterfall, gorge, potholes, rapids); erosion and deposition (meander, oxbow lake); deposition (floodplain, levee, delta, point bar, estuary mudflats). Exams test this classification repeatedly.
- [ ] X2 For "the role of erosion processes in the formation of one fluvial landform" questions, the waterfall block must name which erosion process acts at each stage (hydraulic action and abrasion undercutting the soft rock and scouring the plunge pool). Put this into L2 itself; no separate block.

## 3. Traps (known errors)

- `NOTES_STANDARD.md`'s worked example names "much of the Shannon basin" (dendritic) and "the rivers draining the Wicklow Mountains" (radial). These are not checked; leave both out and describe dendritic and radial patterns without an example.
- Don't use Poulanass Waterfall, Glendalough: it falls from a glacial hanging valley and belongs to `geo-glaciation-s1`.
- Interlocking spurs are fluvial (upper course). Truncated spurs are glacial; don't mention them except as "not to be confused with".
- Meanders are formed by **both** erosion and deposition. A floodplain and a delta are deposition; a waterfall and a V-shaped valley are erosion.
- Meanders are not caused by the Coriolis effect. Don't mention it.
- Point bar (river deposition, inside of a bend) is not the same as terracettes (soil creep, mass movement) or river terraces (rejuvenation, `geo-rivers-s2`).
- Levees: the **coarsest** material is dropped first, beside the channel. Don't say the finest.
- Knickpoints, incised meanders and river terraces belong to `geo-rivers-s2` (rejuvenation). Peneplains and Davis's cycle belong to `geo-rivers-s3`. Dams, canalisation and flood defences belong to `geo-rivers-s4`. Leave them all out here.
- Glacial lakes (tarns, ribbon lakes) and fluvio-glacial features are in `geo-glaciation-s1`. Weathering and mass movement on valley sides are in `geo-rocks-weathering-s3` and `-s5`: one clause in L1 is enough.
- Give no velocities, Hjulström-curve values, delta sizes or meander dimensions. None are checked.
- Do not copy grid references, map names or photograph descriptions from past papers.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Matching labelled map, photo or diagram features to: tributary, confluence, meander, oxbow lake, levee, floodplain, delta, V-shaped valley, waterfall, point bar.
- Identifying trellised and dendritic drainage patterns on an OS map.
- Locating a V-shaped valley, meander, levees, oxbow lake or confluence on an OS map (contours forming a V pointing upstream; widely spaced contours on the floodplain).
- Classifying landforms as formed by erosion, deposition, or both.
- True/false on which course (upper, middle, lower) a labelled part of a long profile shows.
- Explaining hydraulic action and abrasion briefly.
- Matching processes to features (river erosion to waterfall; river deposition to levee or point bar).
- 30 marks: "Examine the role of the processes of erosion in the formation of one fluvial landform" (waterfall, V-shaped valley or meander answers, focused on named erosion processes).
- 30 marks: explaining the formation of one landform of fluvial erosion or deposition with a diagram.
