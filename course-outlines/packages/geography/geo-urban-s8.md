# Package: geo-urban-s8 — The future of urbanism

- **loId:** `geo-urban-s8` · **chapterId:** `geo-urban` · **syllabusRef:** 5.6
- **Syllabus:** Students should study "• the future of urbanism. Issues related to the cities of
  the future."
- **Scope:** Higher Level asks "discuss two issues facing cities of the future" (sometimes
  "in the developed world") almost every second year: each issue must carry about 7–8 SRPs on
  its own, with named cities and checked facts. Write each issue as its own block: the issue,
  why it matters for the future, named examples, and the responses. Cover developed-world
  issues (with Irish and European examples) and developing-world issues.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-21. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The trend
- [ ] F1 The world keeps urbanising: UN shares for 1950, 2018 and 2050; growth concentrated in Asia and Africa; more and larger megacities; Ireland's own urban growth planned under the NPF. Facts from section 2 only.

Issues facing cities of the future (each a block)
- [ ] I1 Housing supply and affordability: demand from population growth, smaller households and migration; high rents and prices in Dublin and other successful cities; homelessness; responses (compact growth, higher density, apartments near transport, public and cost-rental housing, Housing for All). Developing world: slum growth (s7).
- [ ] I2 Transport and sustainable mobility: car dependence, congestion and emissions; responses: mass transit (MetroLink planned, BusConnects, Grand Paris Express), cycling cities (Copenhagen), low-emission zones (London ULEZ), the "15-minute city" (Paris), electric vehicles, car-free districts (Vauban, Freiburg).
- [ ] I3 Climate change and the environment: cities produce most emissions and are exposed to sea-level rise, flooding and heat waves (urban heat island); coastal cities at risk (Dublin Bay, Cork's tidal flooding); responses: climate action plans and emissions targets (Ireland's Climate Act 2021), flood defences, green roofs and green infrastructure, energy-efficient buildings and district heating, renewable energy, planned eco-cities (Masdar City).
- [ ] I4 Urban sprawl versus compact cities: sprawl wastes land and energy; compact growth policy (NPF targets); brownfield regeneration; height and density debates; green belts.
- [ ] I5 Ageing populations: more older people in developed cities; need for accessible housing, transport, health care and age-friendly design; fewer workers paying taxes.
- [ ] I6 Technology and smart cities: sensors, data and digital services to manage traffic, energy and waste (Songdo, South Korea; Smart Dublin); risks (cost, privacy, digital divide).
- [ ] I7 Changing work and city centres after COVID-19: remote and hybrid working; empty offices and shops; commuting less; city centres converting offices to homes and leisure; remote-working hubs in towns (Our Rural Future, from s2).
- [ ] I8 Shrinking and declining cities: deindustrialised cities losing population (Detroit, s5); vacant land reused for parks and urban farming; managed decline.
- [ ] I9 Social inequality and segregation: rich and poor areas drifting apart; gentrification; gated communities; immigrant concentrations (Paris banlieues); need for social mix and inclusive planning.
- [ ] I10 Governance: who runs the city region; Dublin split between four councils; the 2023 Citizens' Assembly recommendation for a directly elected Dublin mayor; Limerick's directly elected mayor (2024); city regions such as the Métropole du Grand Paris (2016).
- [ ] I11 Resources and services: water supply (Dublin's proposed Shannon water pipeline; São Paulo's 2014–2015 drought, from s7), energy demand (data centres), waste.
- [ ] I12 Developing-world futures: megacities growing fastest in Asia and Africa; slums, water, sanitation, informal work, climate vulnerability of low-lying delta and coastal cities (Kolkata, Mumbai, from s7); innovative low-cost solutions (Curitiba's bus rapid transit).

Ireland's urban future (one block, pulling the issues together)
- [ ] R1 Irish cities to 2040: about 1 million more people by 2040 (NPF); the regional cities to grow by at least half; Housing for All targets; Dublin's MetroLink and Cork's proposed light rail; data centres and electricity; climate targets. Each point one bullet with its checked fact.

## 3. Traps

- **Each issue must be a distinct issue.** Exams mark "two issues" separately; don't let housing and sprawl, or transport and climate, overlap in the same points.
- UN figures are projections: write "projected about 68% by 2050", not as fact. Don't give future megacity counts beyond the 2018 figure.
- The Climate Act target is **51% by 2030** (relative to 2018) and **net zero by 2050**. Don't give sector targets.
- Masdar City: only "begun 2006, low-carbon aim scaled back". Songdo: give no population. Copenhagen: no cycling percentage. The 15-minute city is an idea associated with Carlos Moreno, promoted by Anne Hidalgo; don't say it is fully built.
- No Dublin-specific NPF population target, no MetroLink or Cork flood-scheme dates or costs.
- Don't present Dublin as having a directly elected mayor (only recommended, 2023).
- This subsection draws its examples from s4–s7; the writer should name them briefly as future issues and not re-teach their case studies (problems in s5, strategies in s6, developing cities in s7).
- Don't copy data-response values (urban population projections for named regions or for Ireland in past papers).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Higher 30 marks: discuss two issues facing cities of the future (housing, transport, climate/environment, sprawl, ageing, technology, social inequality). Also "in the developed world".
- Short questions: one challenge associated with increasing urban populations; one reason why more people live in urban areas than rural areas; graphs of the global urban population share.

## 5. Practice questions to write
