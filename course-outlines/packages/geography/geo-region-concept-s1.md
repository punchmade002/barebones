# Package: geo-region-concept-s1 — Physical regions

- **loId:** `geo-region-concept-s1` · **chapterId:** `geo-region-concept` · **syllabusRef:** 2.1
- **Syllabus:** Statement: "A region is an area of the earth's surface, which can be identified by
  selected criteria operating at a variety of scales. Single or multiple indices may be used to
  study these regions." Students should study "physical regions: climatic regions, in particular
  the cool temperate oceanic" (national setting "Irish climate", international setting "North
  West Europe") and "geomorphological regions including: Karst landscapes (for example Burren),
  Munster ridge and valley (for example South Munster), Northern European plain." Higher Level
  outcome: "explain and illustrate, at different scales, the concept of a region as identified
  by selected criteria."
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

This is the first subsection of the chapter, so it also carries the general idea of a region
(items R1–R5). Later subsections use those terms without redefining them.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea of a region
- [ ] R1 Definition: a region is an area of the earth's surface that shares one or more characteristics (criteria) that make it different from the areas around it.
- [ ] R2 Criteria used to define regions: physical (climate, relief, rock type), administrative (government), cultural (language, religion), socio-economic (wealth, employment, development) and urban (a city and its hinterland). The five types are the five subsections of this chapter; one bullet listing them.
- [ ] R3 Scale: regions exist at different scales, from a local area (a county or a city's hinterland) through national regions (the West of Ireland) to continental or global regions (North West Europe, the Islamic world). Exams ask students to "explain and illustrate at different scales".
- [ ] R4 Single vs multiple indices: a region can be defined by one criterion (e.g. rock type alone gives a karst region) or several combined (e.g. a peripheral region defined by income, unemployment and out-migration together).
- [ ] R5 Boundaries: physical and cultural regions usually merge into each other through transition zones rather than ending at a sharp line; administrative regions have exact, legally fixed boundaries. Regions also change over time (detail in `geo-region-european`).

Climatic regions
- [ ] C1 Climatic region: a large area with similar temperature, precipitation and seasonal pattern, usually also with similar natural vegetation and soils. The world can be divided into climatic regions; this subsection studies the cool temperate oceanic in detail.
- [ ] C2 Exam vocabulary of climate types (one bullet, names only): cool temperate oceanic (Ireland, North West Europe), warm temperate oceanic (the Mediterranean type), hot desert. Students must recognise each as a climatic region in matching questions. Their full study is in the Atmosphere–Ocean option; don't describe them here beyond one clause each.
- [ ] C3 Characteristics of the cool temperate oceanic climate, using the Irish figures in section 2: mild winters, cool summers, small annual range of temperature, rain in every month, no dry season, changeable weather, prevailing south-westerly winds.
- [ ] C4 Why Ireland has this climate (each a separate bullet): latitude (mid-latitudes, so moderate sun angle); the North Atlantic Drift (warm ocean current that keeps winters mild and coasts ice-free); maritime influence (the sea heats and cools slowly, so the range is small); prevailing south-westerly winds blowing off the Atlantic; frequent Atlantic depressions and their fronts bringing rain; relief rainfall on western mountains.
- [ ] C5 Variation within Ireland: west wetter than east (relief rain, first landfall of depressions; the east lies in a rain shadow); more wet days in the west; the south-east is the sunniest part; the north is slightly cooler than the south; coasts milder in winter than the inland Midlands. Figures in section 2.
- [ ] C6 North West Europe as the international setting: the same climate covers Ireland, Britain, north-west France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Denmark and coastal Norway, because all face the Atlantic and the North Atlantic Drift and lie in the path of westerly depressions.
- [ ] C7 Transition eastward: moving east across Europe, away from the Atlantic, winters become colder, summers warmer, the annual range larger and rainfall lower, with a summer maximum (continental influence). This shows a climatic region has a transition zone, not a line, as its boundary (links R5).
- [ ] C8 Climate shapes the region's other features: deciduous forest (oak, ash) is the natural vegetation; brown earth soils; grass grows for most of the year, which favours pastoral farming (cattle, dairying) in Ireland. One or two bullets; soils are taught in `geo-rocks-weathering`.

Geomorphological regions
- [ ] G1 Geomorphological region: an area with a distinctive landscape produced by the same rock type, geological structure or landforming processes. Three settings below.
- [ ] G2 Karst region, the Burren: Carboniferous limestone; landforms (limestone pavement with clints and grikes, turloughs, caves, few surface rivers because drainage is underground); glaciation exposed the pavement and left erratics; the distinctive land use and plant life that follow from the rock (section 2). Formation of each landform is in `geo-rocks-weathering-s4`: name the landforms with one clause each, don't re-teach formation.
- [ ] G3 Munster ridge and valley region (South Munster): Armorican folding about 300 million years ago produced east–west anticlines and synclines; Old Red Sandstone ridges and Carboniferous limestone valleys after differential erosion; named ridges and valleys; rivers flowing east along the valleys then turning south (trellised drainage); drowned valleys at the coast form rias. Then the human consequences: towns, routes and fertile farmland in the valleys, rough grazing, forestry and bog on the ridges. Formation detail is in `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s5`; give it in at most three bullets.
- [ ] G4 The Northern European Plain: a vast lowland, mostly low and flat to gently rolling, stretching from western France across Belgium, the Netherlands, northern Germany, Denmark and Poland into Russia. Shaped by glacial deposits (moraines, outwash sands, lakes) in the north and fertile loess on its southern edge; crossed by large rivers flowing north to the North Sea and Baltic; the Netherlands' reclaimed polders lie on it. Human consequences: fertile farmland, easy movement for transport, dense population and major cities.
- [ ] G5 Comparison: the three geomorphological regions are defined by different criteria: rock type (Burren), geological structure (Munster) and relief and surface deposits (Northern European Plain). One short comparison block. Exams ask "how geomorphology can be used to define regions".

## 3. Traps

- The Burren was glaciated. Don't say its karst survived because ice never reached it (a known marking-scheme error).
- The Cliffs of Moher are sandstone, siltstone and shale, not karst limestone. Don't use them as a karst landform; name them only as part of the Geopark.
- Don't give the Burren's area or the National Park's area: sources disagree.
- Munster ridges are anticlines of sandstone; valleys are synclines of limestone. Never reverse them.
- Warm temperate oceanic is the Mediterranean climate, not Ireland's. Ireland is cool temperate oceanic. Exams use warm temperate oceanic and hot desert as distractors.
- The Wicklow Mountains (granite) and the Leinster batholith appear in exams as geomorphological regions. One bullet at most, naming them as granite uplands; their landforms are in `geo-rocks-weathering-s4`.
- Don't call the Mezzogiorno or the West of Ireland physical regions in matching answers: they are peripheral (socio-economic) regions (s4).
- The Paris Basin is a core region (s4) and a sedimentary basin (`geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s4`); don't use it as the Northern European Plain example.
- Weather systems (depressions, fronts, anticyclones) are taught in `geo-weather-climate`; name them only. Other world climates are in the Atmosphere–Ocean option.
- Don't take climate figures from past-paper answers; use only section 2.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Short questions (every year): matching types of region to definitions or examples. Expected answers here: climatic region = cool temperate oceanic, warm temperate oceanic, the Mediterranean, hot desert; geomorphological region = the Burren, Munster ridge and valley, the Northern European Plain, the Wicklow Mountains, the Leinster batholith. A geomorphological region is one with a unique landscape based on rock type, structure or relief.
- Matching maps of regions to region types.
- Higher 30 marks: how climate (or culture) can be used to define regions, with examples. Expected answer: definition, the cool temperate oceanic characteristics with Irish figures, the controls, variation within Ireland, North West Europe and the eastward transition, links to vegetation and farming.
- Ordinary 30 marks: describe and explain two region types from climatic, geomorphological, language and religion. Expected answer for this subsection: climatic (cool temperate oceanic) and/or geomorphological (the Burren or Munster).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Using a climatic region you have studied, explain how climate can be used to identify a region." Model answer: cool temperate oceanic only, about 15 SRPs: definition; temperature and rainfall figures; controls (latitude, North Atlantic Drift, south-westerlies, depressions, relief); west–east variation in Ireland; North West Europe and its eastward transition; vegetation and farming. No geomorphology.
2. 30 marks, Higher: "Describe and explain two geomorphological regions you have studied." Model answer: the Burren (karst) and Munster ridge and valley, with the landscape features and their human consequences; no formation detail beyond one bullet per landform.
3. 4 marks: "Explain what is meant by a region, and give one example of a region at a local scale and one at a continental scale." Model answer: R1 definition; Co. Clare (or the Burren); North West Europe.
