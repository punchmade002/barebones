# Package: geo-economic-environment-s4 — Sustainable economic development

- **loId:** `geo-economic-environment-s4` · **chapterId:** `geo-economic-environment` · **syllabusRef:** 4.5
- **Syllabus:** students should study "sustainable economic development so as to control its
  environmental impact. Students should examine past experiences, future prospects and the
  necessity for environmental impact studies". National settings: "National issues, the role
  of the EPA. Depletion of fish stocks, mining sites." Studied areas, each with its own block of
  facts: **the EPA**, **environmental impact assessment**, **depletion of fish stocks** (past

  Bord na Móna**, and **the Aral Sea** as a global past experience. Higher 30-mark questions ask
  "how economic activities can be developed sustainably to control their environmental impact".

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] S1 Definition (Brundtland Report, 1987): development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
- [ ] S2 Three pillars: environmental protection, economic growth, social fairness. Sustainable development balances all three.
- [ ] S3 Principles: use renewable resources no faster than they regenerate; use non-renewables efficiently and recycle; keep pollution within what the environment can absorb; the polluter pays; the precautionary principle (act before harm is proven when the risk is serious).
- [ ] S4 Global framework: Rio Earth Summit (1992) and Agenda 21; UN Sustainable Development Goals (2015, 17 goals for 2030).

Control: the EPA (syllabus setting; full block)
- [ ] E1 Establishment and status: set up under the Environmental Protection Agency Act 1992; independent public body; headquarters at Johnstown Castle, Co. Wexford.
- [ ] E2 Licensing: large industries (e.g. pharmaceutical and chemical plants, power stations, intensive pig and poultry farms, cement works, mines) and waste facilities need an EPA licence that sets emission limits; this is integrated pollution control (one licence covering air, water, waste and noise).
- [ ] E3 Monitoring: water quality of rivers, lakes, estuaries, groundwater and bathing waters; air quality network; greenhouse gas inventory and projections; drinking water supplied by Uisce Éireann.
- [ ] E4 Enforcement: inspections; Office of Environmental Enforcement (2003); prosecution of polluters; oversight of local authorities' environmental work.
- [ ] E5 Reporting and advice: State of the Environment report every four years; advice to government and the public; research funding; radiation protection (radon).
- [ ] E6 Limits: the EPA cannot be everywhere; diffuse pollution from thousands of farms is hard to police; local authorities and other bodies share responsibility.

Control: environmental impact assessment (the "necessity for environmental impact studies")
- [ ] I1 What it is: a study, required before planning permission is decided for large projects, that predicts a project's effects on the environment and how to reduce them. The developer's report was long called an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS); it is now an Environmental Impact Assessment Report (EIAR).
- [ ] I2 Legal basis: EU EIA Directive (1985), applied in Irish planning law; required for projects above set sizes (motorways, power stations, wind farms, mines and quarries, large intensive farms, pipelines, ports, airports).
- [ ] I3 Contents: description of the project; the existing environment (baseline); likely effects on people, plants and animals, soil, water, air, climate, landscape, archaeology and heritage, traffic and noise; alternatives considered; mitigation measures; non-technical summary for the public.
- [ ] I4 Process: the developer prepares it; the public and bodies (e.g. the National Parks and Wildlife Service) make submissions; the planning authority or An Coimisiún Pleanála (formerly An Bord Pleanála) decides, often with conditions.
- [ ] I5 Why it is necessary: prevents damage before it happens instead of paying for clean-up; considers alternatives; gives the public a say; is legally required (Ireland was fined over Derrybrien because no proper assessment was done).

Past experiences: depletion of fish stocks (full case)
- [ ] F1 Fish are renewable only if catches stay below the rate at which stocks breed and grow (maximum sustainable yield).
- [ ] F2 Causes of overfishing: modern technology (sonar, satellite navigation, factory ships, huge nets), too many boats, subsidies, catching young fish before they breed, discards, illegal fishing.
- [ ] F3 Grand Banks cod collapse, Newfoundland (past experience; full facts in section 2).
- [ ] F4 North Sea herring collapse and closure in the 1970s, and recovery after the fishery reopened under strict quotas.
- [ ] F5 Management in EU and Irish waters: Common Fisheries Policy (1983): total allowable catches (TACs) divided into national quotas; mesh size and minimum landing sizes; closed areas and seasons; vessel decommissioning; 2013 reform (fishing at maximum sustainable yield; landing obligation ending discards); monitoring by satellite and by the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority and the Naval Service; marine protected areas; aquaculture as an alternative. Keep the CFP to its conservation role; its economic impact on Ireland is in `geo-economic-eu`.
- [ ] F6 Irish context: mackerel is the most valuable Irish catch, landed mainly at Killybegs; Irish fishing communities depend on stocks, so conservation causes hardship (link to "Conflicts between economic interests and environmental interests").

Past experiences and future prospects: mining sites (full case)
- [ ] M1 Past experience without controls: Avoca, Co. Wicklow, acid mine drainage still polluting the river long after mining ended.
- [ ] M2 Present practice: Tara Mines, Navan; operates under an EPA licence; tailings stored in a lined tailings facility; water monitoring; restoration plans.
- [ ] M3 Rehabilitation after closure: Lisheen, Co. Tipperary (closed 2015, site rehabilitated); new mines need an EIAR and a closure-and-restoration plan with a financial bond before they open.

Peatlands: from exploitation to restoration (national issue)
- [ ] P1 Past: large-scale industrial peat harvesting by Bord na Móna since the 1940s for power stations and fuel, removing raised bogs, releasing stored carbon and destroying habitats.
- [ ] P2 Change: end of peat-fired power and of peat harvesting (2020–2021); rewetting and rehabilitation of cutaway bogs; reuse for wind farms, solar, biodiversity and recreation (Lough Boora); a "just transition" for Midland workers.

Global past experience: the Aral Sea
- [ ] A1 Rivers feeding the Aral Sea diverted from the 1960s to irrigate cotton; the sea shrank, salinity rose, fishing collapsed, ports were left far from water, dust storms spread salt and pesticides, local health suffered. Partial recovery of the northern sea after a dam (Kok-Aral, 2005).

Sustainable approaches in other activities (one block; one or two bullets each)
- [ ] O1 Energy: switch to renewables (see "Burning fossil fuels and using alternative energy sources").
- [ ] O2 Agriculture: agri-environment schemes (REPS 1994, now ACRES); Origin Green; nitrates rules; results-based farming in the Burren (BurrenLIFE).
- [ ] O3 Forestry: replant after felling, mixed native broadleaves, not on deep peat.
- [ ] O4 Tourism: visitor limits, ecotourism, managed access (Skellig Michael; full facts in "Conflicts between economic interests and environmental interests").
- [ ] O5 Waste: reduce, reuse, recycle; levies and deposit schemes; circular economy.
- [ ] O6 Polluter pays: carbon tax, landfill levy, plastic bag levy, EU Emissions Trading System.

Future prospects
- [ ] FP1 Climate neutrality by 2050 (EU and Ireland); offshore wind; retrofitting homes; electric vehicles and public transport; circular economy; cutting agricultural emissions; restoring bogs and planting forests as carbon sinks; the UN SDGs by 2030.
- [ ] FP2 Obstacles: cost; short-term economic pressure; jobs in traditional industries; enforcement; global cooperation needed.

## 3. Traps

- The Brundtland Report (1987) defined sustainable development; the Rio Earth Summit (1992) was a separate later event.
- The EPA does not grant planning permission; local authorities and An Coimisiún Pleanála do. The EPA licenses emissions.
- An EIS is written by the developer (often by consultants), not by the EPA or the planning authority.
- The Grand Banks cod are off Canada (Newfoundland), not in EU waters. The moratorium date is 2 July 1992; keep "about 30,000" jobs.
- The CFP's effect on the Irish economy (quotas, Ireland's share, Brexit) belongs to `geo-economic-eu`; the conflict between fishermen and conservation belongs in "Conflicts between economic interests and environmental interests". Here, fish stocks are a sustainability case: causes of depletion and management measures.
- Mining methods and the Tara case are taught in `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`; here focus on control, licensing and rehabilitation.
- The 80-mark Optional Unit essays on sustainability (resource use, fair trade, justice, self-reliance) belong to `geo-option-global-interdependence`. Don't write fair trade or self-reliance blocks here.
- Don't give tonnages, fleet numbers, quota figures or catch values: none are checked.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Higher 30 marks (asked more than once): examine how economic activities can be developed sustainably to control their environmental impact; discuss how the environmental impact of economic activity can be controlled by a sustainable approach. Expected answer: definition, the EPA's role, environmental impact assessment, then sustainable management of named activities (fishing with past experience, mining sites, energy, agriculture, peat), future prospects.
- Short questions: what is meant by sustainable development; the role of the EPA; why environmental impact studies are needed.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Examine how economic activities can be developed sustainably in order to control their environmental impact." Model answer: definition; EPA licensing and monitoring; EIA; fish stocks (Grand Banks lesson, CFP measures); mining (Avoca vs Tara vs Lisheen); one line of future prospects. About 15 SRPs.
2. 30 marks, Higher: "Discuss the role of the Environmental Protection Agency and of environmental impact assessment in controlling the environmental impact of economic activity." Model answer: the EPA's establishment, licensing, monitoring, enforcement and reporting; EIA contents, process and necessity, with Derrybrien as the cost of not doing it.
3. 30 marks, Ordinary: "Explain how the depletion of fish stocks can be prevented, with reference to examples you have studied." Model answer: causes of overfishing, Grand Banks collapse, North Sea herring recovery, CFP and Irish monitoring measures. About 10 SRPs.
