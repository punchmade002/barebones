## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

What a volcano is
- [ ] V1 Magma (molten rock below the surface) vs lava (magma that reaches the surface). A volcano is an opening (vent) in the crust through which magma, gases and fragments escape, and the landform built around it.
- [ ] V2 Anatomy of a volcano, for diagram matching: magma chamber, main vent (pipe/conduit), secondary (parasitic) vent, crater, cone of alternating layers of lava and ash, lava flow, ash cloud, volcanic bombs. One labelled diagram cue covering all of these.
- [ ] V3 Materials ejected: lava; gases (water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide); pyroclastic material or tephra (ash, lapilli or cinders, volcanic bombs, pumice). Exams ask for two pyroclastic materials and two gases.
- [ ] V4 Hazards made of these materials: pyroclastic flows (fast, very hot avalanches of gas, ash and rock); lahars (volcanic mudflows of ash and water, e.g. from melted snow or ice); ash fall.
- [ ] V5 Basic (basaltic) lava vs acidic lava: basic lava is low in silica, hot, runny (low viscosity), gases escape easily, so eruptions are gentle (effusive); acidic lava is high in silica, cooler, viscous, traps gas, so eruptions are explosive. Link only: low-viscosity lava builds shield volcanoes, viscous lava builds steep cones (the landforms are in s3).
- [ ] V6 Activity: active (has erupted recently and may erupt again soon), dormant (has not erupted for a long time but could erupt again), extinct (not expected ever to erupt again). Each with an example.

Global distribution
- [ ] D1 Most volcanoes lie in narrow belts along plate boundaries, not at random. One sentence per boundary type on why; the boundary processes themselves are taught in `geo-plate-tectonics-s3`.
- [ ] D2 The Pacific Ring of Fire: a belt of volcanoes (and earthquakes) around the edges of the Pacific Ocean, mainly along destructive boundaries. It contains about three-quarters of the world's active volcanoes. Exams ask what it is and what occurs there.
- [ ] D3 Constructive (divergent) boundaries: basic magma rises where plates separate; mostly under the sea along mid-ocean ridges; Iceland is where the Mid-Atlantic Ridge rises above sea level.
- [ ] D4 Destructive (convergent) boundaries where oceanic crust is subducted: the descending plate melts, and acidic magma rises to form explosive volcanoes in fold-mountain chains (e.g. the Andes) or island arcs (e.g. Japan, Indonesia).
- [ ] D5 Continental–continental collision and transform (conservative) boundaries: few or no volcanoes.
- [ ] D6 Hot spots: plumes of magma rising from deep in the mantle, away from plate boundaries. The plate moves over a stationary hot spot, producing a chain of volcanoes that get older away from the hot spot. Exams ask for a named hot spot.
- [ ] D7 Ireland today: in the middle of the Eurasian plate, far from a boundary, so no active volcanoes.

Ireland's volcanic past (the syllabus's national setting)
- [ ] A1 Antrim basalt extrusion and the opening of the Atlantic: as North America and Greenland separated from Europe, the crust stretched and cracked; basic lava poured out of long fissures (fissure eruptions) in north-east Ireland, flow upon flow, about 60 million years ago. Explain the cause and setting here; the landforms (Antrim plateau, Giant's Causeway, columnar jointing) are in s3, so name them only.

Effects of volcanic activity
- [ ] N1 Negative effects: loss of life; destruction of buildings, farmland and infrastructure by lava, pyroclastic flows and lahars; ash fall collapsing roofs, destroying crops and contaminating water; ash clouds disrupting air travel; volcanic tsunamis; gases and ash cooling the global climate for a year or two; economic losses and evacuation.
- [ ] N2 Positive effects: fertile soils from weathered volcanic ash and lava; geothermal energy and hot water; tourism (volcanoes, geysers, hot springs); minerals and building materials (e.g. sulphur, pumice, basalt); creation of new land.
- [ ] N3 At least one full case study with date, place, plate setting, events, effects and response, enough for a 30–40 mark answer: Mount St Helens 1980 (section 2). Shorter supporting examples for named effects from section 2.

Prediction and reducing the effects
- [ ] P1 Volcanoes give warning signs because magma must move upwards before an eruption.
- [ ] P2 Monitoring methods: seismometers (swarms of small earthquakes as magma rises); ground deformation (tiltmeters, GPS and satellite radar measure swelling or bulging of the cone); gas emissions (changes in amount and type, especially sulphur dioxide); temperature (thermal imaging and satellites detect heating of the ground or crater lakes); changes in groundwater and springs.
- [ ] P3 Studying the history of past eruptions and their deposits to estimate how often a volcano erupts and which areas are at risk; hazard maps.
- [ ] P4 Limits of prediction: scientists can often forecast that an eruption is likely, but not its exact time, size or style.
- [ ] P5 Reducing the effects: monitoring by volcano observatories; hazard zoning (keeping settlement out of high-risk zones); evacuation plans and warning systems; diverting or slowing lava flows (barriers, channels, cooling with water); public education.
- [ ] P6 A named example of a successful forecast (Pinatubo 1991) and of lava control (Heimaey 1973), from section 2.

## 3. Traps (known errors)

- Magma is below ground; lava is on the surface. Don't use them interchangeably.
- Dormant ≠ extinct. Dormant can erupt again; extinct is not expected to. Mount Fuji is dormant, not extinct. Kilimanjaro: do not use (its status is disputed).
- Basalt is a volcanic (extrusive) rock, not plutonic. Antrim's basalt came from fissure eruptions, not from a single cone volcano.
- Hawaii is **not** on a plate boundary; it is a hot spot. Iceland is on a constructive boundary **and** over a hot spot.
- The Ring of Fire is around the **Pacific**, not the Atlantic. Do not give a percentage other than "about three-quarters" (75%) of active volcanoes, the figure agreed with `geo-plate-tectonics-s3`, and do not copy the percentage or any figure from a past paper.
- Mount St Helens killed 57 people, not thousands; the 1980 eruption was a lateral (sideways) blast after a landslide, not a vertical one.
- Recent eruptions in past papers (Litli-Hrútur 2023, Agung, Anak Krakatau, Kilauea 2024, Hunga Tonga 2022) came with figures in the question. Do not use any of those figures.
- Volcano landform types (shield, composite, cinder cone, caldera, lava plateau, volcanic plug) and plutonic features (batholith, dyke, sill, laccolith) belong to `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s3`. Here mention only that lava type controls cone shape. Exams that ask "What is a caldera?" are answered in s3.
- How plates move, convection currents, subduction and what happens at each boundary are in `geo-plate-tectonics-s2` and `-s3`. Give one cause sentence per boundary type, no more.
- Geothermal energy as a human interaction with the rock cycle is a full option in `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`. Here it is one positive effect, one bullet.
- Igneous rock formation (basalt, granite) is in `geo-rocks-weathering-s1`.
