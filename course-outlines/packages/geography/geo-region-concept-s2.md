## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] A1 Administrative region: an area with fixed, legally defined boundaries, set up by a government to organise services, elections and decision-making. Its boundaries are exact lines on a map, unlike the transition zones of physical and cultural regions.
- [ ] A2 Why governments create them: to deliver services close to people (housing, roads, planning), to organise elections fairly, to collect statistics, and to channel funding (including EU regional funds).
- [ ] A3 The hierarchy of scales, one bullet each, smallest to largest: townland and electoral division (census and local units); local authority area (county or city council); constituency; regional assembly area; the State; the European Union (supranational).

Ireland: counties and local councils
- [ ] I1 Counties: the main traditional administrative unit; 26 counties in the Republic, 32 on the island. Provinces (Leinster, Munster, Connacht, Ulster) are historic and have no administrative function today.
- [ ] I2 County divisions: some counties were divided for administration because of size or population. Dublin was split in 1994 into three new county council areas plus Dublin City; Tipperary was divided into North and South Riding councils until they were merged in 2014.
- [ ] I3 Local councils and corporations: "corporations" were the old name for city governments (e.g. Dublin Corporation), renamed city councils. Since the Local Government Reform Act 2014 there are 31 local authorities: county councils, city councils and city and county councils (section 2). The same Act abolished town councils, replacing them with municipal districts, and merged Limerick and Waterford city and county councils and the two Tipperary councils.
- [ ] I4 Functions of local councils: housing, roads and traffic, planning permission and development plans, libraries, fire service, environment and waste, parks and amenities. Funded partly by the Local Property Tax and commercial rates. Councillors are elected every five years.
- [ ] I5 Boundaries change with population: Cork city boundary extended in 2019 to take in growing suburbs. One bullet. Dublin's council structures in detail are in `geo-region-european` (2.4, urban growth and the expansion of city regions); point there.
- [ ] I6 Regional assemblies: three regional assemblies since 2015 (Northern and Western; Southern; Eastern and Midland) prepare regional plans and manage EU regional funds. Earlier (2000–2006) Ireland was divided into two regions for EU funding: the Border, Midland and Western (BMW) region, which kept the highest level of EU aid (Objective 1), and the Southern and Eastern region. Link to s4 (peripheral regions).

Ireland: constituencies
- [ ] E1 A Dáil constituency is an electoral region that elects TDs (members of the Dáil) by proportional representation (PR-STV); each elects 3 to 5 TDs.
- [ ] E2 Constitution: roughly equal representation, between one TD per 20,000 and one per 30,000 people. So constituency boundaries are redrawn after each census as population shifts.
- [ ] E3 The Electoral Commission (set up 2023; before that, constituency commissions) reviews the boundaries. Its 2023 review gave 43 constituencies electing 174 TDs (first used in the 2024 general election).
- [ ] E4 Constituencies often cross county boundaries, because a county's population may not fit a 3-to-5-seat constituency, e.g. Sligo–Leitrim. Growing areas (Dublin and its commuter belt) gain seats; declining areas lose them. This shows administrative boundaries changing over time.
- [ ] E5 Other scales of electoral region (one bullet): local electoral areas for council elections; three European Parliament constituencies (Dublin, Midlands–North-West, South).

France: départements
- [ ] F1 Origin: created in 1790 during the French Revolution to replace the old provinces, weaken regional loyalties and centralise power in Paris. They were drawn so every place could reach the main town (chef-lieu) within about a day's ride on horseback.
- [ ] F2 Today there are 101 départements: 96 in metropolitan France and 5 overseas. Each has a number (originally alphabetical) used, for example, as the first two digits of postcodes; Paris is 75.
- [ ] F3 Many are named after natural features such as rivers and mountains, e.g. Bouches-du-Rhône, Haute-Garonne, Seine-Maritime, Pyrénées-Atlantiques.
- [ ] F4 Government: each has a prefect (préfet) appointed by the national government, and an elected departmental council responsible for social welfare, roads and lower secondary schools (collèges).
- [ ] F5 The French hierarchy of scales: about 35,000 communes (the smallest, each with a mayor); départements; regions. In 2016 the number of metropolitan regions was cut from 22 to 13 by merging, to create larger regions able to compete in Europe. Regions handle economic development, regional transport and upper secondary schools (lycées).
- [ ] F6 Comparison with Ireland: France has three tiers of local and regional government; Ireland is highly centralised, with weaker local government. One short comparison block.

## 3. Traps

- Provinces are not administrative regions with councils; don't list functions for them.
- Don't say there are 26 local authorities or that each county has one council: Dublin has four, Cork and Galway each have a city and a county council, and Limerick, Waterford and Tipperary each have one.
- A constituency is an electoral region, not a council area; its boundaries are not the same as county boundaries.
- Don't give constituency counts or seat totals other than 43 and 174 (older figures, e.g. 39 constituencies and 160 TDs, were correct only for the 2016 and 2020 elections; leave them out).
- Don't give a figure for the number of NUTS regions in Ireland: the classification has been revised.
- Départements are not the same as French regions. Exams call a département "an administrative region in France". Keep 101 départements and 13 metropolitan regions distinct.
- The expansion of Dublin and its council structures as a story of urban growth belongs to `geo-region-european` (2.4); the BMW region as a peripheral region is developed in s4 and in `geo-region-ireland`. Here, one bullet each.
- Never paste figures from past-paper answers.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Matching (most years): an administrative region = a named county (Co. Longford, Co. Leitrim, Co. Meath, Co. Cavan, Co. Wexford), a county council (Longford County Council), a constituency (Sligo–Leitrim), or "an administrative region in France" (a département).
- Matching a definition: an administrative region is one involved in governing the functions and organisation of an area.
- No long question on administrative regions has been set in recent years, but a 30-mark "explain how administrative regions operate at different scales" is in syllabus scope.

Model answer: the hierarchy of scales; Irish local authorities (2014 reform, functions, Dublin's 1994 division); constituencies (rules, Electoral Commission, cross-county example); French départements and regions (origin, number, functions, 2016 reform). About 15 SRPs.
2. 6 marks: "Explain why constituency boundaries in Ireland change over time." Model answer: E2–E4.
3. 4 marks: "Name two levels of local or regional government in France and state one function of each." Model answer: départements (social welfare/roads/collèges) and regions (economic development/lycées) or communes (mayor, local services).
