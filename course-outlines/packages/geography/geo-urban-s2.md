## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Patterns
- [ ] R1 Rural settlement: farmhouses, hamlets and villages in the countryside; pattern = the way buildings are spread over the land. Three patterns: dispersed, clustered (nucleated), linear (ribbon).
- [ ] R2 Dispersed settlement: individual farmhouses scattered, each on its own farm. Why: Irish farms are family-owned and each family lives on its holding; consolidation of holdings after the Land Acts (Land Commission, Congested Districts Board) moved houses out of clusters onto individual farms; fertile, well-drained lowland with plentiful water allows houses anywhere; pastoral farming needs a farmer close to the animals. It is the dominant Irish rural pattern.
- [ ] R3 Clustered (nucleated) settlement: buildings grouped together. Why: route focus (crossroads), bridging point, church, castle or estate gates, defence, water supply at a spring or well (wet-point site), dry-point site above flood land, a harbour (fishing village), services (shop, pub, school, Garda station). Types: villages; estate villages; clachans.
- [ ] R4 Clachans and rundale: clusters of farmhouses of related families, without a church or shop, worked land in common in scattered strips (rundale, with infield and outfield); common in the west before the Famine; broke up as population fell and holdings were consolidated and houses moved onto individual farms (CDB and Land Commission). A few survive in the west.
- [ ] R5 Linear settlement: houses strung along a line: a road, a coast road, a valley floor, a river or a raised ridge (esker, drumlin edge); avoids marshy or steep land.
- [ ] R6 Absence of settlement: uplands (steep, exposed, poor soils), bogs, flood plains, lakes, forestry plantations. One bullet each; exams ask it on OS maps.
- [ ] R7 A European comparison: planned rural settlement on the Dutch polders (village hierarchy round Emmeloord, which s3 uses for central place theory: one line here and cross-refer); the Mezzogiorno's hilltop villages and the 1950 land reform that scattered new farmhouses on the lowlands (point to `geo-region-european-s2`; one bullet).

Ribbon development and one-off housing
- [ ] B1 Ribbon development: houses built one plot deep along roads leading out of towns and villages, and along rural roads generally.
- [ ] B2 Causes: cheaper sites than in towns; road frontage gives access and services (electricity, water mains, phone/broadband) along the road; farmers selling roadside sites; people wanting rural living near a town; car ownership; historically weak planning control; tax incentives and the property boom of the Celtic Tiger.
- [ ] B3 Problems: traffic hazards from many entrances onto fast roads; costly to service (school buses, post, waste collection, water and sewers); septic tanks polluting groundwater and private wells; loss of scenic landscape; car dependence; town centres decline as people live outside them; blurred boundary between town and country.
- [ ] B4 Advantages and the rural point of view: keeps rural populations, schools, GAA clubs and shops alive; lets families live near relatives and farms; cheaper housing.

Planning strategies in rural areas (each strategy a bullet, then evaluation)
- [ ] S1 The planning system: planning permission from the local authority; appeals to An Bord Pleanála; the County Development Plan (made every six years under the Planning and Development Act 2000) sets zoning, rural housing policy, design rules and protected areas; Local Area Plans for towns and villages.
- [ ] S2 National level: the National Spatial Strategy (2002) and the National Planning Framework (2018) (both summarised in s6; here only their rural housing aims); Sustainable Rural Housing Guidelines (2005): distinguish areas under strong urban influence (where rural houses are limited to people with a local economic or social need) from structurally weak areas (where rural housing is encouraged to keep population).
- [ ] S3 Typical County Development Plan rural policies: local-needs criteria; restricting new entrances onto national and regional roads; directing new housing into villages and serviced sites; design guidelines (siting, materials, hedgerows); minimum site sizes for septic tanks; protecting scenic areas and views; zoning around towns.
- [ ] S4 Septic tank regulation: registration and inspection of domestic wastewater systems (Water Services (Amendment) Act 2012; EPA).
- [ ] S5 Rural development programmes to sustain rural communities: LEADER (EU, from 1991); CLÁR; the Town and Village Renewal Scheme (2016); Our Rural Future (2021–2025); remote working hubs. Rural depopulation and the decline of services (post offices, Garda stations, pubs) as the problem they address.
- [ ] S6 Evaluation: tension between keeping rural communities alive and preventing sprawl; local-needs conditions criticised as unfair and tested at EU level; planning has reduced but not stopped ribbon development; villages need sewage treatment to take more housing.

## 3. Traps

- **Clustered = nucleated.** Linear = ribbon when it extends out of a town along approach roads. Don't present them as four separate patterns.
- A **clachan** is a cluster of farmhouses with no church, shop or other services; a village has services. Don't call a clachan a village.
- Rundale is a **landholding system**, not a settlement pattern; mention it only to explain clachans.
- Don't give numbers of one-off houses, planning refusals, rural households or septic tanks other than "about half a million" (EPA estimate).
- Don't say local-needs rules were "abolished" or "illegal": only the 2013 EU ruling on the Flemish rule is checked.
- Don't name past-paper OS map areas or give grid references. The OS-map skill (identifying patterns and absence of settlement with grid references) is in `geo-mapwork-s1`.
- Rural-to-urban migration and its impact on rural areas (donor regions, ageing, brain drain) belong to `geo-population-migration`. Mention rural depopulation only as the reason for S5.
- Urban sprawl and pressure on rural land use by **cities** is in s4 and s5. Here keep to dispersed rural housing and ribbon development.
- The NPF and NSS are described in full in s6 (urban planning). Here only their rural-housing aims.
- Historic farmsteads (ringforts) belong to s1.

- Name three rural settlement patterns on an OS map, give grid references and explain each (skill in `geo-mapwork-s1`; explanations here).
