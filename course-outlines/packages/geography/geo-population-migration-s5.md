- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and

- **Studied areas:** (1) Ireland: recent immigration, religious change, racism and the Irish

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Terms
- [ ] T1 Ethnic group (shared culture, language, ancestry, traditions or religion); race (a group classed by physical traits; a social idea with no biological basis for ranking people); religion. Multicultural, multi-ethnic society.
- [ ] T2 Racism and discrimination; xenophobia; Islamophobia; segregation and ghettos (migrants clustering for support, cheap housing and chain migration, or forced by discrimination); assimilation vs integration vs multiculturalism as government approaches.

How issues arise (the general mechanisms; each one bullet)
- [ ] M1 Competition for jobs, housing and services, especially in recessions; fear that wages are undercut.
- [ ] M2 Visible difference (skin colour, dress such as the hijab) makes minorities targets of prejudice.
- [ ] M3 Religious practice in public space: places of worship, dress, food (halal), holy days, faith schools; clashes with secular or majority-religion norms.
- [ ] M4 Residential segregation and concentration in deprived areas, which reinforces poverty and separation.
- [ ] M5 Language barriers in schools, work and services.
- [ ] M6 Political exploitation: far-right and anti-immigration parties and movements; misinformation on social media.
- [ ] M7 Second-generation issues: children born in the host country who face discrimination and identity conflict.
- [ ] M8 Positive outcomes too: cultural enrichment (food, music, sport, festivals), new places of worship, intercultural schools, mixed marriages; one bullet only.

Case studies (each: the migration, the issues that arose, and the government policy response)
- [ ] C1 Ireland: from a mostly white, Catholic society to a diverse one since the 1990s; religious change (growth of Orthodox Christianity, Islam, Hinduism and "no religion"); schools and the baptism barrier; racism and hate crime; anti-immigration protests and the Dublin riot (2023); the Traveller community as an indigenous ethnic minority.
- [ ] C2 Irish government policy: equality legislation; the Irish Human Rights and Equality Commission; the Migrant Integration Strategy; the National Action Plan Against Racism; hate-offences legislation; school admission reform; Educate Together and multi-denominational schools; recognition of Traveller ethnicity; evaluation (gaps, direct provision's isolating effect from `geo-population-migration-s4`).
- [ ] C3 France: immigration from Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia after the Second World War and Algerian independence; a large Muslim population; laïcité (strict separation of religion and state); the 2004 headscarf law and the 2010 face-covering ban; segregated banlieues of high-rise estates with high unemployment; the 2005 and 2023 riots; terrorist attacks (2015) and rising Islamophobia; the far-right National Front / National Rally. Government approach: assimilation (everyone is a French citizen; no official ethnic statistics).
- [ ] C4 Germany: Gastarbeiter (guest workers) recruited from the 1950s to 1973, especially from Turkey; expected to go home but many stayed and brought families; long denied citizenship; segregated districts (e.g. Kreuzberg in Berlin); citizenship reform in 2000 and 2024; the 2015 refugee arrivals and the rise of the anti-immigration AfD.
- [ ] C5 Britain: the Windrush generation from the Caribbean from 1948; racism in housing and work; the Notting Hill riots (1958); race relations laws; the Windrush scandal (2018).
- [ ] C6 Northern Ireland: the Plantation of Ulster brought Protestant settlers from Scotland and England onto land taken from Catholic Irish; centuries of religious and political division; the Troubles; the Good Friday Agreement; segregated housing and schools today. Keep this to the migration origin and its religious legacy.
- [ ] C7 The Roma: Europe's largest ethnic minority, historically migratory; discrimination in housing, education and employment; segregated schooling in parts of central and eastern Europe; expulsions (France, 2010).

## 3. Traps

- Do not name Irish suburbs as "ghettos" of particular nationalities (a model answer on the past paper names Ballymun and Tallaght this way without evidence). Describe residential concentration without naming areas, except Kreuzberg in Berlin and the Paris banlieues (Clichy-sous-Bois).
- Do not name mosque planning disputes in Ireland; none is checked.
- The Dublin riot's trigger was a knife attack; do not give the attacker's nationality or speculate about motive, and do not name the victims.
- Laïcité dates from the 1905 law; the headscarf law is 2004 (schools); the face-covering ban is 2010 (public places). Don't swap them.
- Travellers are an indigenous Irish ethnic minority, not migrants; include them only as part of Irish ethnic policy and say so.
- Partition, nationalism and the full Northern Ireland conflict belong to `geo-option-culture-identity`. Here only the Plantation as a migration and its religious legacy.
- Racial groupings, multi-racial societies and colonialism's effect on race belong to `geo-option-culture-identity`. Migration policy (asylum, direct provision, citizenship referendum) belongs to `geo-population-migration-s4`; refer to it.
- Avoid stereotypes: present tensions as arising from circumstances, discrimination and politics, and always pair issues with policy responses.
