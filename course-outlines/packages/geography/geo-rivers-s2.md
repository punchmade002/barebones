# Package: geo-rivers-s2 — Fluvial adjustment to base level

- **loId:** `geo-rivers-s2` · **chapterId:** `geo-rivers` · **syllabusRef:** 1.6
- **Syllabus:** "Students should study the way in which landforms result from a combination of
  crustal uplift (in response to isostatic readjustment) and denudation by surface processes,
  and that sometimes landscapes illustrate that these opposing forces are temporarily out of
  balance." Studied item: **fluvial adjustment to base level**. National setting: "Rivers of
  N.W. Ireland." Statement: "All landforms represent a balance between endogenetic and
  exogenetic forces; this balance may change through time."
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-19. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Base level and the graded river
- [ ] B1 Base level: the lowest level to which a river can erode. Sea level is the ultimate base level; a lake, a band of resistant rock or the main river (for its tributary) gives a temporary (local) base level. Base level is defined in `geo-glaciation-s2` I7: extend it here with the temporary types, without repeating the basic definition word for word.
- [ ] B2 Graded (equilibrium) profile: a smooth, concave long profile where the river's energy is just enough to carry its load, so erosion and deposition are balanced. A change in base level upsets this balance.

Rejuvenation: causes
- [ ] C1 Rejuvenation: a fall in base level relative to the land gives the river renewed energy for vertical erosion. Exams ask for this name for renewed erosion in a river's lower course.
- [ ] C2 Cause 1, a eustatic fall in sea level: during a glacial period water is locked up in ice sheets, so sea level falls worldwide.
- [ ] C3 Cause 2, isostatic uplift: land depressed under ice rises (rebounds) when the ice melts, raising it relative to sea level. Refer to `geo-glaciation-s2`; don't re-teach isostasy.
- [ ] C4 Cause 3, tectonic uplift of the land by earth movements.
- [ ] C5 Cause 4, an increase in discharge (e.g. wetter climate, or a river gaining a tributary's water by river capture) gives more energy. One bullet; mark it as a less common cause.
- [ ] C6 Rejuvenation begins at the mouth and works upstream by headward erosion, so for a long time the river has two graded sections: a new, steeper lower section and the old upper section. This is the syllabus's "temporarily out of balance" landscape.

Landforms of rejuvenation (each: what it is, formation in numbered stages, example from section 2 where given, diagram cue)
- [ ] K1 Knickpoint: a break of slope on the long profile where the new and old graded profiles meet; often marked by a waterfall or rapids; it retreats upstream over time. Exams ask what a knickpoint is.
- [ ] K2 Incised meanders: renewed vertical erosion cuts existing meanders down into the land. Two types: ingrown meanders (asymmetrical cross-section, one gentle and one steep side, slower downcutting with some lateral erosion) and entrenched meanders (symmetrical, steep-sided gorge, rapid downcutting).
- [ ] K3 River terraces: remnants of an old floodplain left as flat steps above the new floodplain after the river cuts down. Paired terraces (at the same height on both sides, rapid downcutting) and unpaired terraces (at different heights, downcutting while the river migrates sideways). Exams use "paired terraces" as a label.
- [ ] K4 Gorge cut by deep incision into an uplifted landscape. One or two bullets.
- [ ] K5 Reading a diagram: the number of rejuvenations equals the number of knickpoints on the long profile, or the number of terrace levels above the present floodplain on a cross-section. Exams ask how many times rejuvenation has taken place in a diagram.
- [ ] K6 The rejuvenation landforms students name when asked for "features not shown": knickpoint, waterfall/rapids at the knickpoint, incised meanders, river terraces, gorge. One bullet.

Rise in base level and the coast
- [ ] S1 A rise in base level (sea level rising, or land sinking) reduces the river's energy: the lower course is drowned and deposition increases. Name rias and estuaries; ria formation is in `geo-glaciation-s2`.
- [ ] S2 Coastal landforms caused by a change in base level, one bullet naming them only: raised beaches (fall in base level) and rias (rise). Exams ask for two. Their formation is in `geo-glaciation-s2`.
- [ ] S3 Setting: rivers of north-west Ireland were rejuvenated as the land rose isostatically after the Midlandian ice melted. One bullet; no named river.

## 3. Traps

- Rejuvenation follows a **fall** in base level (relative to the land). A **rise** causes drowning and deposition, not rejuvenation.
- Isostatic = the land moves; eustatic = the sea moves. Don't swap them. Don't re-explain isostasy: `geo-glaciation-s2` teaches it.
- River terraces (rejuvenation) are not terracettes (soil creep, `geo-rocks-weathering-s5`) and not kame terraces (fluvio-glacial, `geo-glaciation-s1`).
- A knickpoint is the break of slope itself; the waterfall or rapids are what is often found at it. Don't define a knickpoint as "a waterfall".
- An ordinary waterfall (hard rock over soft) is in `geo-rivers-s1`; here the waterfall is caused by the change in base level, whatever the rock.
- Incised meanders are not ordinary meanders: they are cut down into solid rock with steep valley sides, not on a floodplain.
- Don't give sea-level falls, uplift rates, terrace heights or knickpoint retreat rates. None are checked. Don't use the Thames terrace names or counts.
- Ria and raised beach formation, and the north–south contrast in Ireland, are in `geo-glaciation-s2`. Name them only.
- Peneplains and Davis's cycle are in `geo-rivers-s3`. Mention only that rejuvenation interrupts a cycle of erosion, in one clause at most.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name the process by which a river in its lower course begins to erode again after a change in base level (rejuvenation).
- One or two reasons why base level changes / why rejuvenation occurs (eustatic fall, isostatic uplift, tectonic uplift).
- Explain what a knickpoint is.
- Label a diagram of rejuvenation features: knickpoint, waterfall, incised meander, paired terraces.
- Name two rejuvenation features not shown in a diagram.
- Count the number of rejuvenations shown in a diagram.
- Name two coastal landforms formed by an adjustment to base level (raised beaches, rias).
- 30 marks (possible): explain the formation of landforms caused by fluvial adjustment to base level.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Examine how a fall in base level can affect a river and its landforms." Model answer: what base level and rejuvenation are, two or three causes, then knickpoint, incised meanders and paired terraces each explained in stages with a diagram cue and the checked examples. About 15 SRPs.
2. 8 marks: "Explain what a knickpoint is and describe how it changes over time." Model answer: break of slope where old and new graded profiles meet, waterfall or rapids at it, headward retreat, eventual disappearance as the whole river grades to the new base level.
3. 4 marks: "Name two causes of river rejuvenation." Model answer: a eustatic fall in sea level and isostatic uplift of the land (tectonic uplift accepted).
