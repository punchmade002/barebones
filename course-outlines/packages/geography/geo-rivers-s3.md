# Package: geo-rivers-s3 — Cyclic landscape development and peneplains

- **loId:** `geo-rivers-s3` · **chapterId:** `geo-rivers` · **syllabusRef:** 1.6
- **Syllabus:** "Students should study the way in which landforms result from a combination of
  crustal uplift (in response to isostatic readjustment) and denudation by surface processes,
  and that sometimes landscapes illustrate that these opposing forces are temporarily out of
  balance." Studied item: **cyclic landscape development and peneplains**. National setting:
  "Planation surfaces in Munster." Statement: "All landforms represent a balance between
  endogenetic and exogenetic forces; this balance may change through time."
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-19. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] I1 A landscape is shaped by uplift (endogenetic) and denudation (exogenetic) acting against each other over very long periods; when uplift stops, denudation slowly lowers the land towards base level. Use the terms without redefining them (they are defined in `geo-glaciation-s2`).
- [ ] I2 Cyclic landscape development: the landscape passes through a sequence of stages from uplift to near-flatness, and a new uplift (or fall in base level) starts the sequence again.

Davis's cycle of erosion (the "geographical cycle")
- [ ] D1 Who and what: W. M. Davis's model; it assumes rapid uplift followed by a long period of stability while rivers and weathering wear the land down. Landscape depends on "structure, process and stage (time)".
- [ ] D2 Youth: uplifted plateau with high relief; steep gradients, vertical erosion, deep V-shaped valleys, waterfalls and rapids, wide flat interfluves (areas between rivers) not yet eroded; few tributaries.
- [ ] D3 Maturity: maximum relief; valleys widen by lateral erosion; meanders and floodplains develop; the drainage network is fully developed; interfluves are reduced to ridges.
- [ ] D4 Old age: relief is low; very wide floodplains, large meanders, oxbow lakes, levees; rivers flow sluggishly close to base level; divides are lowered to gentle rises.
- [ ] D5 Peneplain: the end product, a gently undulating surface of low relief close to base level ("almost a plain"). Exams ask what a peneplain is.
- [ ] D6 Monadnocks: isolated residual hills of more resistant rock rising above the peneplain.
- [ ] D7 Interruption: uplift, isostatic rebound or a fall in sea level rejuvenates the rivers and starts a new cycle before the old one is finished (one bullet; rejuvenation landforms are in `geo-rivers-s2`). A landscape with remnants of more than one cycle is polycyclic.
- [ ] D8 Planation surface: a flat erosion surface cut across rocks of different types and structures. An uplifted peneplain survives as a plateau or as summits at similar heights (accordant summits) that cut across folded rock.
- [ ] D9 Criticisms: uplift and denudation happen at the same time, not one after the other (Walther Penck's alternative); the time needed is far too long to observe; the model ignores climate change, glaciation and isostatic response to erosion; few if any true peneplains exist today at sea level. Paired with a strength: it gives a simple, useful framework for describing landscapes by stage.

Planation surfaces in Munster (case study)
- [ ] M1 Structure, in one or two bullets only: folding about 300 million years ago (Variscan or Armorican) formed east–west ridges of resistant Old Red Sandstone (upfolds, anticlines) and valleys of softer Carboniferous limestone (downfolds, synclines). Folding itself is taught in `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s5`; don't re-teach it.
- [ ] M2 Planation: over a very long period of denudation the region was worn down towards a near-level surface cutting across the folds.
- [ ] M3 Evidence: flat or gently sloping upland surfaces and summit levels at similar heights cutting across the folded rocks of different types.
- [ ] M4 Uplift and rejuvenation: later uplift raised the planation surface; rivers were rejuvenated and cut down again; renewed erosion removed the softer limestone faster, so the ridge-and-valley relief was re-exposed (differential erosion).
- [ ] M5 Drainage evidence: the main rivers flow east along the limestone valleys and then turn sharply south, cutting through the sandstone ridges to the sea. The usual explanation is superimposed drainage: the rivers first flowed south across an older surface lying above the present rocks and kept their courses as they cut down into the folded rocks below.
- [ ] M6 The Munster landscape as "temporarily out of balance": uplift has outpaced denudation, so an old surface survives at height while rivers are still adjusting.

## 3. Traps

- A peneplain is an erosion surface of low relief **near base level**; an uplifted peneplain is no longer near base level. Say "former" or "uplifted" when describing the Munster surfaces.
- Don't confuse a peneplain with a floodplain (a depositional surface beside a river) or a plateau of lava (Antrim, `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s3`).
- Davis's stages (youth, maturity, old age) describe a whole landscape. Don't confuse them with the upper, middle and lower courses of one river (`geo-rivers-s1`), though the landforms overlap. Name the landforms of each stage; don't re-explain their formation.
- Rejuvenation landforms (knickpoints, incised meanders, terraces) are in `geo-rivers-s2`; isostasy is in `geo-glaciation-s2`; the folding mechanism is in `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s5`. Refer to them only.
- Superimposed drainage is the usual explanation for the southward turns of the Munster rivers, not a proven fact. Present it as "the usual explanation".
- Don't name the Lee or the Bandon as making the same turn, and don't name individual Munster ridges or give heights or dates for the planation surfaces. None are checked.
- Don't give Davis's later publication dates or Penck's dates.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- 30 marks (possible): explain cyclic landscape development, or describe how a landscape illustrates the balance between uplift and denudation, with reference to Munster.
