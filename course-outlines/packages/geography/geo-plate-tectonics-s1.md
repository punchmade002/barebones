## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The layers (each: what it is made of, solid or liquid, depth or thickness from section 2)
- [ ] L1 Overview: the earth is made of concentric layers, from the outside in: crust, mantle, outer core, inner core. Density and temperature increase towards the centre. Diagram cue: a labelled cross-section of the earth with all layers and the two discontinuities.
- [ ] L2 Crust: the thin, solid outer layer. Two types (L3, L4).
- [ ] L3 Continental crust: thicker, lighter (less dense), mainly granite-type rock; rich in silica and aluminium (old name **sial**); can be very old.
- [ ] L4 Oceanic crust: thinner, denser, mainly basalt; rich in silica and magnesium (old name **sima**); much younger than continental crust, because it is continually created and destroyed (link only; the process is in s2 and s3).
- [ ] L5 The Moho (Mohorovičić discontinuity): the boundary between the crust and the mantle, where earthquake waves speed up suddenly.
- [ ] L6 Mantle: the thickest layer, of dense rock rich in iron and magnesium; upper and lower mantle.
- [ ] L7 Asthenosphere: the semi-molten, plastic layer in the upper mantle on which the plates move. Molten rock in the mantle is called **magma** (lava only once it reaches the surface).
- [ ] L8 Lithosphere: the rigid outer shell made of the crust plus the solid top of the upper mantle. It is broken into plates (the plates themselves are in s2).
- [ ] L9 Gutenberg discontinuity: the boundary between the mantle and the core.
- [ ] L10 Outer core: liquid iron and nickel; movement of this liquid metal generates the earth's magnetic field (needed later for palaeomagnetism in s2).
- [ ] L11 Inner core: solid iron and nickel; the hottest part of the earth; solid despite the heat because of the enormous pressure.
- [ ] L12 The core is made of iron and nickel (exams ask for the two minerals/metals by name).

Heat and movement inside the earth
- [ ] H1 Source of internal heat: decay of radioactive elements, plus heat left over from the earth's formation.
- [ ] H2 Convection currents: circular movements of semi-molten rock in the mantle. Heated rock near the core becomes less dense and rises; near the top of the mantle it spreads out sideways, cools, becomes denser and sinks again. State the process only; how it moves plates is in s2.
- [ ] H3 Endogenic (internal) forces: forces that come from inside the earth, powered by its internal heat (plate movement, folding, faulting, volcanic activity, earthquakes). Contrast with exogenic (external) forces such as weathering and erosion. Exams ask for a true/false and a short explanation of "endogenic forces".

How we know (one block)
- [ ] K1 Evidence from earthquake (seismic) waves: waves change speed and direction as they pass through layers of different density; this is how the discontinuities were found.
- [ ] K2 P-waves (primary) pass through solids and liquids; S-waves (secondary) pass only through solids. S-waves do not pass through the outer core, which shows it is liquid. This leaves an S-wave shadow zone on the far side of the earth from an earthquake.

## 3. Traps

- Magma vs lava: molten rock in the mantle is **magma**; it is called lava only after it erupts at the surface. Ordinary Level asks this directly.
- Lithosphere vs asthenosphere: the lithosphere is the rigid shell (crust plus upper-mantle top) that forms the plates; the asthenosphere is the semi-molten layer beneath it. Exams ask which name fits "upper mantle and crust" (lithosphere). Don't call the asthenosphere "liquid"; it is semi-molten or plastic.
- The outer core is liquid and the inner core is solid. Never swap them.
- The Moho separates crust and mantle; the Gutenberg discontinuity separates mantle and core. Don't swap them.
- Continental crust is thicker but less dense; oceanic crust is thinner but denser. This density difference is why oceanic crust subducts (state that in s3, not here).
- Don't give mantle temperatures, crust percentages of the earth's volume, or seismic wave speeds: none are checked.
- Only describe what convection currents are here. Plate movement, ridge push and slab pull belong to `geo-plate-tectonics-s2`; boundaries to `geo-plate-tectonics-s3`.
- Earthquake waves are used here only as evidence for the earth's structure. Earthquake causes, focus, epicentre, measurement and prediction belong to `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes`.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Labelled diagram of the earth's layers: match letters to crust, mantle, outer core, inner core; at Higher Level also lithosphere, asthenosphere, oceanic crust and continental crust.
- The name of the boundary between crust and mantle (Moho), and what "Moho" means.
- The two main metals of the core (iron and nickel).
- The name of the layer made of crust plus upper mantle (lithosphere).
- The name of molten rock in the mantle (magma, not lava).
- True/false: convection currents are circular movements of semi-molten rock in the mantle; endogenic forces act inside the earth.
- Short explanation of endogenic forces.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Describe the structure of the earth's interior." Model answer: one point each for crust (two types), Moho, mantle with asthenosphere, lithosphere, outer core (liquid), inner core (solid), with a depth for each.
2. 4 marks: "Explain the difference between the lithosphere and the asthenosphere." Model answer: rigid crust plus top of upper mantle vs semi-molten plastic layer below on which plates move.
3. 4 marks: "Explain briefly what is meant by endogenic forces." Model answer: internal forces powered by the earth's heat; named examples (folding, faulting, volcanic activity, earthquakes); contrast with exogenic forces.
