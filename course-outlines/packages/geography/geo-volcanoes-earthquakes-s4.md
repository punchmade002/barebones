## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Sedimentary structures
- [ ] T1 Sedimentary rocks are laid down in layers called strata (beds). One sentence only on how they form (deposition, compaction, cementation); the full rock cycle is in `geo-rocks-weathering-s1`.
- [ ] T2 Bedding planes: the boundaries between one layer and the next, roughly horizontal when laid down. They mark a pause or change in deposition.
- [ ] T3 Joints: cracks at right angles to the bedding planes, formed as sediment dries and shrinks, and by stresses and the removal of overlying rock. Bedding planes and joints divide the rock into blocks.
- [ ] T4 Dip (the angle and direction the strata tilt) and strike. Earth movements tilt strata; the landform depends on whether the layers are horizontal, gently dipping or steeply dipping. How the strata come to be tilted, folded or domed is in s5; here only the attitude of the layers.

Why structures matter to landforms
- [ ] W1 Lines of weakness: water, ice and weathering exploit bedding planes and joints, so rock breaks into blocks along them and erosion is concentrated there.
- [ ] W2 Differential erosion: where resistant layers (e.g. sandstone, limestone) alternate with weak layers (e.g. shale, clay), the weak layers are worn away faster and the resistant ones stand out as cliffs and caps.
- [ ] W3 Permeability: water passes down joints and bedding planes in permeable or pervious rock and flows over impermeable rock; springs emerge where a permeable layer sits on an impermeable one (spring line).

Landforms (each: what it is, how the structure produces it in numbered stages, example from section 2, diagram cue)
- [ ] L1 Plateau / tabular upland on horizontal strata: a resistant cap rock protects weaker layers beneath, giving a flat top and steep edges.
- [ ] L2 Stepped slopes: alternating hard and soft horizontal layers erode at different rates, giving a profile of cliffs (hard rock) and gentler slopes (soft rock, often covered by scree).
- [ ] L3 Mesa and butte: as the edges of a plateau retreat, it is broken into flat-topped hills with cap rock; a mesa is wider than it is high; a butte is a smaller, narrower remnant.
- [ ] L4 Escarpment and cuesta on gently dipping strata: a steep scarp slope cut across the edges of the layers and a long, gentle dip slope following the tilt of the resistant layer; vales on the weak rock between cuestas.
- [ ] L5 Hogback on steeply dipping strata: a narrow ridge with scarp and dip slopes of nearly equal steepness.
- [ ] L6 Effects of bedding planes and joints on smaller landforms, one bullet each and named only: limestone pavement (clints and grikes), caves along bedding planes, waterfalls over horizontal cap rock, sea caves and arches along joints. Their full formation is elsewhere (see Traps).

Settings (use as the place-based case studies)
- [ ] S1 The Dartry–Cuilcagh upland (Co. Sligo, Leitrim, Cavan, Fermanagh): horizontal Carboniferous strata of limestone, shale and sandstone; flat-topped uplands with steep cliffed edges and stepped slopes; Benbulben and Cuilcagh in full; karst and cave features beneath named only.
- [ ] S2 The Paris Basin: a saucer-shaped basin of sedimentary layers of different resistance, dipping gently in towards Paris; resistant layers form a series of outward-facing escarpments (cuestas) with clay vales between.
- [ ] S3 The Brecon Beacons, Wales: gently dipping Old Red Sandstone forming flat-topped summits and steep north-facing escarpments with gentle south-facing dip slopes.

## 3. Traps (known errors)

- Bedding planes are horizontal (parallel to the layers); joints are vertical (across the layers). Don't swap them.
- A sill (s3) is an igneous sheet along a bedding plane; it is not a sedimentary structure.
- Benbulben's cap is limestone; Cuilcagh's cap is sandstone. Don't mix them.
- Escarpment vs dip slope: the scarp slope is steep and cuts across the layers; the dip slope is gentle and follows the layers.
- The Weald, folding, doming and faulting belong to `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s5`. Here strata are described only as horizontal or dipping.
- Karst landforms (limestone pavement, swallow holes, caves, stalactites) are taught in full in `geo-rocks-weathering-s4`; the Marble Arch upland is its setting. Name them here in one bullet as effects of joints and bedding planes; do not explain their formation.
- Waterfalls are in `geo-rivers-s1`; sea caves and arches in the coastal chapter. Name only.
- Sedimentary rock formation and Irish rock types are in `geo-rocks-weathering-s1`.
- Don't give heights other than those in section 2.
