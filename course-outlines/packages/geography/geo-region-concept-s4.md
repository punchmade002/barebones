## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] X1 Socio-economic region: an area defined by its level of wealth and development, measured with several indices together (multiple indices, link s1 R4): income or GDP per head, unemployment, the mix of primary, secondary and tertiary jobs, migration, age structure, education and services.
- [ ] X2 The core–periphery idea: wealth, people and investment concentrate in a core; the periphery loses people and capital to it. Advantages build on each other in the core (cumulative causation), so the gap tends to widen unless governments or the EU intervene.

Core regions
- [ ] C1 Definition: a region with a strong, growing economy, high incomes, and a concentration of population, investment and decision-making.
- [ ] C2 Characteristics, one bullet each: high GDP per head and low unemployment; a large tertiary (services) sector, with finance, headquarters and high-tech industry; most foreign investment; net in-migration of young, educated workers, giving a young population; dense, well-developed infrastructure (motorways, airports, ports, rail); universities and research; usually contains the capital city and government.
- [ ] C3 Causes: accessibility, a large market and labour force, early start, government and capital functions, and the agglomeration of firms that attracts more firms.
- [ ] C4 Problems of success: congestion, high house prices, urban sprawl, pressure on services. One or two bullets.
- [ ] C5 Examples: the Greater Dublin Area (link `geo-region-ireland`); the Paris Basin (link `geo-region-european`); the European core, the "Blue Banana". Facts in section 2.

Peripheral regions
- [ ] P1 Definition: a region distant (physically or economically) from the core, with a weaker economy and fewer opportunities.
- [ ] P2 Characteristics, one bullet each: remoteness and poor access; difficult physical conditions (mountains, high rainfall, poor soils, short growing season); dependence on the primary sector (small farms, fishing, forestry); low incomes and higher unemployment or underemployment; net out-migration of young adults, leaving an ageing population and falling numbers in rural areas; fewer services; small, scattered settlements.
- [ ] P3 Responses: EU regional and cohesion funds, state agencies, improved roads and broadband, decentralisation of government offices, tourism. One or two bullets; detail in the case-study chapters.
- [ ] P4 Examples: the West of Ireland (Northern and Western region), the former BMW region; the Mezzogiorno; northern Norway; north-west Scotland. Facts in section 2.

Less-developed regions
- [ ] L1 Definition: a region whose level of development is well below the national or EU average. In EU regional policy, a "less developed region" is one with GDP per head below 75% of the EU average; these receive the most EU aid.
- [ ] L2 The overlap: most less-developed regions in Europe are also peripheral (the Mezzogiorno; much of eastern Europe such as Romania and Bulgaria). Ireland as a whole received the highest level of EU aid (Objective 1) until 1999; from 2000 only the BMW region kept that status (2000–2006). One bullet linking to s2 I6.
- [ ] L3 At a global scale, less-developed regions are developing countries; India is the syllabus's continental example (`geo-region-continental`). The Mezzogiorno's development history (the Cassa per il Mezzogiorno, set up in 1950) is in `geo-region-european`. One bullet each.

Regions of industrial decline
- [ ] D1 Definition: a region whose economy grew on heavy, traditional industry (coal mining, iron and steel, shipbuilding, textiles) and has since lost much of it.
- [ ] D2 Causes: coal seams exhausted or too costly to work; cheaper imports and competition from newly industrialised countries; replacement of coal by oil and gas; out-of-date plant; automation needing fewer workers.
- [ ] D3 Effects: high unemployment (especially among older male workers); derelict factories, mines and spoil heaps; polluted land; out-migration; falling services and a poor image, which deters new investment.
- [ ] D4 Responses: retraining; clearing and cleaning derelict land; attracting new light industry and services; universities and technology parks; reusing industrial heritage for tourism and culture; EU funds.
- [ ] D5 European examples with facts (section 2): the Sambre–Meuse valley, Belgium; the Ruhr, Germany; the South Wales coalfield. Name only: Nord–Pas-de-Calais, France.
- [ ] D6 Irish examples: shipbuilding decline in Belfast; the decline of the Dublin docklands with containerisation and its regeneration through the International Financial Services Centre (IFSC). Evidence of industrial decline in Ireland's own economy is in `geo-economic-activities`; one pointer.

Comparing core and peripheral regions
- [ ] K1 Economic activity in core vs peripheral regions, as paired points: primary (large, commercial, efficient farms on good land vs small, fragmented farms on poor land, more fishing and forestry); secondary (concentrations of manufacturing and multinational investment vs few, small, often grant-aided factories); tertiary (finance, headquarters, government, universities, major ports and airports vs tourism and public services). Exams set this as a 30-mark question.

## 3. Traps

- Peripheral regions have net **out**-migration; core regions net **in**-migration (true/false favourite).
- Core regions attract industry; regions of industrial decline lost theirs. Don't call the Ruhr a core region in matching answers: exams class the Ruhr and the Sambre–Meuse as regions of industrial decline.
- The Paris Basin is a core region, not a region of industrial decline or a physical region in matching.
- Peripheral is not the same as less-developed: a peripheral region can be in a rich country (the West of Ireland, northern Norway). Make the distinction in one bullet.
- Don't give GDP per head, incomes, unemployment rates or population figures for the West of Ireland, the Mezzogiorno or the GDA: the case-study packages set those. Use only section 2.
- Don't name Greater Dublin Area population totals or percentage shares; give only the County Dublin and State figures.
- Past-paper answers in this bank contain invented percentages and wrong examples (e.g. the Ruhr as a core region). Never copy them.
- Full case studies (physical, economic, human processes) belong to `geo-region-ireland`, `geo-region-european` and `geo-region-continental`. Here, at most two or three bullets per example region.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Matching (most years): core region (the Paris Basin, the Greater Dublin Area); peripheral region (the West of Ireland, the BMW region, the Mezzogiorno, northern Norway); region of industrial decline (the Sambre–Meuse valley, the Ruhr, the South Wales coalfields). Definition of a peripheral region.
- True/false: core regions are well-developed and attract industry (true); peripheral regions have net immigration (false); core regions have net emigration (false); high unemployment and abandoned factories mark regions of industrial decline (true).
- Ordinary 30 marks: name a core region and explain why it is core; name a peripheral region and explain why it is peripheral.
- Higher 30 marks: how economic activity in core regions differs from that in peripheral regions.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Contrast the primary, secondary and tertiary activities of a core region and a peripheral region you have studied." Model answer: paired primary, secondary and tertiary contrasts using the Greater Dublin Area and the West of Ireland (or the Paris Basin and the Mezzogiorno), with causes. About 15 SRPs.
2. 30 marks, Higher: "Examine the causes and effects of industrial decline in a region you have studied." Model answer: the Ruhr or the Sambre–Meuse valley only: original industries, causes of decline, effects, responses.
3. 30 marks, Ordinary: "Describe the main characteristics of (i) a core region and (ii) a peripheral region, naming an example of each." Model answer: Greater Dublin Area and West of Ireland, about 7 SRPs each.
