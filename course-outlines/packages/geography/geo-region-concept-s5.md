## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] N1 Node: a central point where routes meet (road, rail, sea, air), usually a town or city.
- [ ] N2 Nodal (functional, city or urban) region: a city together with the surrounding area linked to it by flows of people, goods, services and information. Unlike the other regions in this chapter, it is defined by connections and movement, not by sameness.
- [ ] N3 Hinterland: the area around a town or city that it serves and that depends on it for jobs, shopping, services and markets (also called its sphere of influence or urban field). The urban area is the continuously built-up part; the hinterland extends far beyond it.
- [ ] N4 Commuter belt: the part of the hinterland from which people travel daily to work in the city.

How the city and its hinterland interact (one bullet each)
- [ ] F1 Jobs: commuters travel in from the hinterland every day.
- [ ] F2 Services: the city provides shops, hospitals, third-level colleges, entertainment and offices that smaller places cannot support; the hinterland's people travel in to use them.
- [ ] F3 Goods: the hinterland supplies food, water, raw materials and labour; the city supplies manufactured goods and services. A port's hinterland is the area whose trade passes through it.
- [ ] F4 Information and media: local newspapers, radio and TV stations serve the hinterland.

Measuring a hinterland
- [ ] M1 Methods, one bullet each: commuting data from the census; the catchment area of a hospital, college or secondary school; the circulation area of a local newspaper or reach of a local radio station; bus and rail routes and timetables; delivery areas of businesses; home addresses of shoppers or of members of a sports club.
- [ ] M2 The hinterland's edge is not a sharp line: hinterlands of neighbouring towns overlap, and different services give different boundaries. Larger cities have larger hinterlands, and a capital city's hinterland for some services is the whole country.
- [ ] M3 Hinterlands change over time: motorways, car ownership, faster rail and remote working extend commuter belts; new shopping centres or services in smaller towns can shrink a city's hinterland.

Irish cities (settings)
- [ ] I1 Dublin: capital and primate city, far larger than any other Irish city. Nodal: the focus of the national motorway network (radial routes meeting at the M50 ring road), the main rail lines, Dublin Port and Dublin Airport. Its commuter belt extends into Meath, Kildare, Wicklow and Louth, with fast-growing commuter towns. For some functions (government, national media, specialist hospitals, the main airport) its hinterland is the whole country.
- [ ] I2 Problems in Dublin's urban region: urban sprawl, long commutes and congestion, high house prices. One or two bullets; the growth of the city region and its council structures belong to `geo-region-european` (2.4) and its land use and planning to `geo-urban`.
- [ ] I3 Cork: Ireland's second city, at the head of Cork Harbour on the River Lee; its port and pharmaceutical industry around the harbour; its hinterland covers much of Munster, with commuter towns around the harbour.
- [ ] I4 Galway: the main city of the West, serving a large rural hinterland including Connemara; a centre of the medical devices industry and higher education.
- [ ] I5 Limerick and Waterford: regional cities serving the Mid-West and the South-East. One bullet, names only.
- [ ] I6 Planning to balance city regions: the National Planning Framework (Project Ireland 2040, published 2018) aims to direct more growth to Cork, Limerick, Galway and Waterford to counterbalance Dublin. One bullet.

European cities (settings)
- [ ] E1 Paris: primate city of France, at the centre of the Paris Basin. Radial motorways and high-speed TGV lines meet there, so its hinterland reaches across France. Detail is in `geo-region-european` (Paris Basin, urban development); give two or three bullets.
- [ ] E2 London: capital of the United Kingdom; a global financial centre whose commuter belt covers much of south-east England.
- [ ] E3 Brussels: capital of Belgium and seat of the main EU institutions, so its influence extends across the EU.
- [ ] E4 Rotterdam: Europe's largest port; its hinterland is the whole Rhine basin, linked by river barges, rail and pipelines. A good example of a port hinterland crossing national borders.
- [ ] E5 Milan: the main city of northern Italy's core (Lombardy) and Italy's financial centre. Name only: Munich, Berlin, Rome (exam matching examples of urban regions).
- [ ] E6 The Randstad, Netherlands: a ring of cities (Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague, Utrecht) forming one large urban region around a rural "Green Heart". A polycentric city region, in contrast to the single-centre Paris and Dublin regions.

## 3. Traps

- True/false favourites: the hinterland is the area around (beyond the built-up area of) a town or city that it serves: true. A nodal region is characterised by good, not poor, transport: false.
- A nodal region is a functional region (defined by links to a centre), not a formal region defined by uniform features. Don't describe it as an area where everything is the same.
- Don't give population figures for Cork, Limerick, Galway or Waterford, or for Dublin city and suburbs: only the County Dublin and State figures are checked.
- The European Parliament sits in both Brussels and Strasbourg; don't say all EU institutions are in Brussels.
- Central place theory, threshold and range, the urban hierarchy and Christaller belong to `geo-urban` (5.4). Mention "hinterland" and "primate city" only.
- Urban land use, urban problems in depth and planning belong to `geo-urban`; Dublin's council structures and the expansion of city regions to `geo-region-european` (2.4). One pointer each.
- In matching questions, a named city (Limerick, Ennis, Milan, Munich, Berlin, London, Rome, Brussels) is the urban region; don't match a city to "administrative" or "core" when "urban region" is offered.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Matching (most years): urban/city region = a named Irish or European city or town (Limerick, Ennis, Milan, Munich, Berlin, London, Rome, Brussels). An urban region is defined as one with high population density in cities and towns.
- True/false on the hinterland and on nodal regions and transport.
- No long question on nodal regions has been set in recent years; a 30-mark "examine the relationship between a city and its hinterland" is in syllabus scope.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Examine the relationship between an urban area and its hinterland, with reference to an Irish or European city you have studied." Model answer: Dublin only: definitions, its nodality, commuter belt and named commuter towns, services and national hinterland, two-way flows, how the hinterland is measured, changes over time, problems. About 15 SRPs.
2. 6 marks: "Explain what is meant by a nodal region and describe one way the extent of a city's hinterland can be measured." Model answer: N1–N2 and one method from M1.
3. 2 marks: "True or false: (a) A city's hinterland can be mapped using the circulation area of its local newspaper. (b) A nodal region is defined by features that are the same throughout it." Model answer: (a) true, (b) false (it is defined by links to a central node), each with a one-line reason.
