# Package: geo-region-ireland-s1 — A Core Irish Region: the Greater Dublin Area

- **loId:** `geo-region-ireland-s1` · **chapterId:** `geo-region-ireland` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2
- **Syllabus:** "two contrasting Irish regions: the study of each region should include –
  physical processes, e.g. climate, soils, relief, drainage – economic processes - primary
  activities e.g. agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining/energy - secondary activities e.g.
  patterns in manufacturing activities - tertiary activities e.g. tourism and transport – human
  processes, e.g. language, religion, urban and rural development, population dynamics."
  Statement: "The study of regions show how economic, human, and physical processes interact in
  a particular area." This subsection is the **core** region of the pair; `geo-region-ireland-s2`
  is the contrasting peripheral region (the West).
- **Exam weight:** Higher Level 30-mark questions are set on every heading (one primary activity,
  secondary activity, one tertiary activity, one urban area, population distribution, physical
  factors and agriculture), each answered with about 15 SRPs. Every heading below therefore needs
  at least 15 distinct, specific points. Ordinary Level 40-mark questions ask: name one
  primary/secondary/tertiary activity, explain the region's advantages for it, describe one
  problem or challenge it faces. A 20/30-mark sketch map of the region is also common.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The region
- [ ] R1 Definition: the Greater Dublin Area (GDA) = Dublin City and County Dublin (four local authorities: Dublin City, Fingal, South Dublin, Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown) plus Counties Meath, Kildare and Wicklow. Location on the east coast, facing Britain and Europe across the Irish Sea.
- [ ] R2 Why it is a core region: large, dense, growing population; most of the State's wealth, jobs, multinational investment, government and services; best infrastructure; in-migration; self-reinforcing growth (cumulative causation). One short block; the general theory of core and peripheral regions is in `geo-region-concept-s4`.
- [ ] R3 Sketch-map items: outline of the four counties; River Liffey (also Boyne); Dublin city plus towns such as Navan, Naas, Bray; relief: Wicklow Mountains or the lowland plain of Meath/Kildare. Write a short "what to draw" bullet list (no grid references).

Physical processes
- [ ] P1 Climate: cool temperate oceanic; prevailing south-westerly winds; the east lies in the rain shadow of the Wicklow Mountains and the uplands further west, so it is the driest part of Ireland; relatively sunny; slightly greater temperature range than the west (warmer summers, cooler winters, more frost) because it is farther from the Atlantic; heavier rain and snow on the Wicklow uplands. Link each point to its effect on farming, tourism or settlement.
- [ ] P2 Relief: low-lying, gently undulating lowland covering most of Meath, Kildare and north Co. Dublin (part of the Central Lowlands, Carboniferous limestone covered in glacial deposits); the Wicklow and Dublin Mountains to the south (Leinster granite batholith, Caledonian, with quartzite peaks); glacial landforms (U-shaped valleys of Glendalough and Glenmalure, the Curragh outwash plain, eskers); coastal features (Howth Head, Dublin Bay). Effects: lowland easy for farming, building and transport; uplands for forestry, water supply, recreation.
- [ ] P3 Soils: deep, fertile, well-drained brown earths on limestone boulder clay in Meath, Kildare and Fingal (best farmland in Ireland); free-draining sandy/gravel soils of the Curragh; raised bog (the Bog of Allen, Kildare); acidic, leached podzols and blanket bog on the granite uplands of Wicklow. Effects on land use.
- [ ] P4 Drainage: River Liffey (rises in the Wicklow Mountains, flows through Kildare, enters the sea at Dublin Bay, has the Poulaphouca reservoir); River Boyne (Meath); smaller Dublin rivers (Dodder, Tolka); the Royal and Grand Canals linking Dublin to the Shannon; reservoirs supplying Dublin's water (Poulaphouca/Blessington Lakes, Vartry at Roundwood); water-supply pressure and the proposed pipeline from the River Shannon; urban flood risk from paved surfaces (one bullet; flooding processes are in `geo-rivers-s4`).

Primary activities
- [ ] A1 Agriculture: factors (climate, relief, soils, huge nearby market, Dublin Port, government/EU policy, technology); farm types: large, commercial, highly productive farms; beef cattle fattening and tillage in Meath and Kildare; horticulture (field vegetables, glasshouses) in Fingal around Rush and Lusk; bloodstock in Kildare (the Curragh, the Irish National Stud); sheep on Wicklow uplands.
- [ ] A2 Agriculture problems: urban sprawl and high land prices take farmland; competition and pressure on farmers to sell for housing; part-time farming near the city; labour costs and shortages for horticulture; competition from imported vegetables; CAP changes.
- [ ] A3 Forestry: Wicklow among Ireland's most forested counties (the "Garden of Ireland"); conifer plantations (mainly Sitka spruce) on poor upland soils; Coillte (State forestry company, 1988) has its head office in Newtownmountkennedy, Co. Wicklow; recreation forests near Dublin; national forest cover is low (about 11%).
- [ ] A4 Fishing: small part of the region's economy. Howth, a State fishery harbour centre (Dublin Bay prawns, whitefish); Dublin Bay. Problems: EU quotas, Brexit loss of quota, falling fleet. Keep brief.
- [ ] A5 Mining and energy: Tara Mines at Navan (zinc and lead, Europe's largest zinc mine); sand and gravel quarrying from glacial deposits; granite quarrying (Ballyknockan). Energy: the region is Ireland's largest energy market; Poolbeg (gas-fired generation and the Dublin Waste-to-Energy incinerator); Turlough Hill pumped-storage station; Poulaphouca hydro; Arklow Bank offshore wind farm; former peat cutting in the Bog of Allen (Bord na Móna has ended peat harvesting); data centres' heavy electricity demand.

Secondary activities
- [ ] S1 Pattern: the GDA is Ireland's largest manufacturing region; older industries near Dublin Port and the inner city (brewing, food, port-related) declined after the 1970s; newer high-tech and pharmaceutical plants are in suburban business parks and towns close to the M50 and motorways (Grange Castle, Leixlip, Blanchardstown).
- [ ] S2 Named firms: Intel at Leixlip, Co. Kildare (since 1989, Ireland's largest private investment); Pfizer at Grange Castle, Clondalkin; Guinness at St James's Gate (1759). Use one or two as a case study.
- [ ] S3 Factors (each a full block of points, as exams ask any two of these): **markets** (about 2 million people in the region; EU single market via Dublin Port and Airport); **labour** (large, young, educated workforce; universities: Trinity College Dublin, UCD, DCU, TU Dublin, Maynooth University; immigrant workers); **infrastructure** (Dublin Port, Dublin Airport, motorways radiating from the M50, Port Tunnel, high-capacity broadband and electricity); **government policy** (IDA Ireland, low corporation tax of 12.5%, now 15% for very large multinationals; EU membership); **raw materials** (few local raw materials; high-tech industries import components and ship light, high-value products by air; food and drink firms process farm produce from the region's hinterland); agglomeration/clustering.
- [ ] S4 Problems/challenges: high costs (land, housing, wages), traffic congestion, housing shortage for workers, competition from lower-cost countries, dependence on multinationals, global corporation tax changes, strain on electricity and water supply.

Tertiary activities
- [ ] T1 Tourism factors and attractions: Ireland's main gateway (Dublin Airport and Port); capital-city heritage and culture (Guinness Storehouse, Book of Kells at Trinity College, museums, Croke Park); conference and business tourism (the Convention Centre Dublin); Brú na Bóinne (Newgrange), Co. Meath, a UNESCO World Heritage Site; Glendalough and the Wicklow Mountains National Park; Ireland's Ancient East brand; good accommodation and transport.
- [ ] T2 Tourism challenges: hotel shortages and high prices, overcrowding at top sites, congestion, competition from other city-break destinations.
- [ ] T3 Transport as a tertiary activity and as an advantage: Dublin Port (Ireland's busiest port; ferries to Holyhead), Dublin Airport (Ireland's busiest, second runway 2022), radial motorways (M1, M3, M4, M7, M11) joined by the M50 orbital motorway, Dublin Port Tunnel (2006), DART (1984), Luas (2004), commuter rail, Dublin Bus and BusConnects, planned MetroLink.
- [ ] T4 Transport problems: traffic congestion, long commutes from Meath, Kildare and Wicklow, car dependence caused by low-density sprawl; responses (public transport investment, cycle lanes, the NTA's Greater Dublin Area Transport Strategy 2022–2042).
- [ ] T5 Other services in brief: the International Financial Services Centre (IFSC, 1987) and Docklands ("Silicon Docks": Google and other tech firms' European headquarters); government, health and education services concentrated in the capital.

Human processes
- [ ] H1 Population size and distribution (Census 2022 figures in section 2): about 2.1 million, about 40% of the State; highest densities in Dublin city and suburbs, then the commuter towns along motorways and rail lines, lowest on the Wicklow uplands. Exams ask to "account for the distribution of population", so give a reason for each high- and low-density area (relief, soils, jobs, transport).
- [ ] H2 Population dynamics: steady growth from natural increase (young population, more births than deaths) and in-migration from the rest of Ireland and from abroad; young age structure and low dependency compared with the West; growing multicultural population; growth spreading outward into the commuter counties of Meath, Kildare and Wicklow.
- [ ] H3 Urban development of Dublin: site (lowest bridging point of the Liffey, sheltered bay; Viking settlement from 841); Norman, Georgian (Wide Streets Commissioners, 1757) growth; 20th-century suburban expansion and the western new towns of Tallaght, Clondalkin and Lucan (Wright Report, 1967); high-rise Ballymun flats (1960s, later demolished and regenerated); inner-city decline and renewal (IFSC 1987, Temple Bar, Docklands); expansion of commuter towns. Exams ask "factors influencing the development of one urban area", so organise this as factors (site, route focus, port, capital-city functions, industry, planning).
- [ ] H4 Urban problems: urban sprawl, housing shortage and high rents/prices, homelessness, traffic, pressure on water supply. Generic urban theory belongs to `geo-urban`; keep it Dublin-specific.
- [ ] H5 Rural development: rural areas of Meath, Kildare and Wicklow are under urban pressure, not decline: commuter villages, one-off and urban-generated housing, rising land prices, loss of farmland, new schools and services needed; contrast with rural decline in the West.
- [ ] H6 Language: English-dominant region; Irish spoken by a minority but large absolute numbers; growth of Gaelscoileanna; the Ráth Chairn Gaeltacht, Co. Meath, founded in 1935 by families from Connemara; immigration has made the region multilingual (Polish is the most common foreign language spoken at home in Ireland).
- [ ] H7 Religion: Roman Catholic majority but the lowest Catholic share in Ireland and the highest share with no religion; historic Church of Ireland presence (Christ Church and St Patrick's cathedrals); immigration has added Muslim, Orthodox and other communities; secularisation.

## 3. Traps

- The GDA is Dublin, Meath, Kildare and Wicklow. Do not include Louth (Drogheda) or call it "the Dublin Region" only; the Eastern and Midland Regional Assembly area is larger and is not this region.
- Do not give a length for the Liffey or the Boyne, or rainfall/sunshine/growing-season figures beyond the table: competing values circulate.
- Killybegs, Castletownbere, Rossaveel etc. are not in the GDA; name them only as the other fishery harbour centres.
- Poolbeg's tall chimneys belong to the old ESB oil-fired station (closed); don't describe them as working.
- Bord na Móna peat: say harvesting has ended, give no date.
- Intel: no employment number; Google: no staff number; Dublin Airport: only the rounded 2023 figure.
- Never copy figures from past-paper charts (tourist percentages, canal-crossing counts, IDA job tables, Dublin Port hectares, MetroLink budget). They are data-response material, not facts.
- Isostasy, glacial landform formation, the Leinster batholith's formation and the Bull Island/Sutton tombolo formation are taught in chapters 1–6. Reference them in one clause; don't re-teach.
- The general core–periphery model, Gaeltacht boundary change since 1850 (`geo-region-european-s4`), Dublin council structures and city-region expansion (`geo-region-european-s4`) and generic urban theory (`geo-urban`) belong elsewhere. Mention only what the GDA needs.
- Ráth Chairn is a Gaeltacht inside the GDA; don't say the region has no Gaeltacht.
- Keep the West's facts (Galway medtech, Knock, Corrib gas, Wild Atlantic Way) out of this file except as a one-line contrast.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Sketch map (20 marks Higher, 30 marks Ordinary): outline of the region, one river, one/two urban centres, one relief feature.
- 30 marks Higher: two factors influencing secondary activity (government policy, labour, markets, infrastructure, raw materials) in an Irish region; expected answer: GDA manufacturing with Intel/pharma examples, each factor developed in about 7–8 SRPs.
- 30 marks Higher: two factors influencing one tertiary activity (tourism or transport); physical factors influencing one primary activity; agriculture with any two of relief, climate, markets; physical factors for agriculture in two contrasting Irish regions (this region gives the "favourable" half).
- 30 marks Higher: factors influencing the development of one urban area (Dublin); account for population distribution in the region.
- Ordinary 40 marks: name a primary/secondary/tertiary activity; explain the region's advantages; describe one problem or challenge.
- Ordinary 15 marks: name a core region and explain why it is core.
- Short questions: why the east is sunnier/drier than the west (rain shadow, distance from the Atlantic); why Dublin has the highest disposable income (high-paid jobs, multinationals, services); why Dublin has traffic congestion.
