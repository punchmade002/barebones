# Package: geo-region-european-s2 — A Peripheral European Region: the Mezzogiorno

- **loId:** `geo-region-european-s2` · **chapterId:** `geo-region-european` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2
- **Syllabus:** "two contrasting European regions. Students can choose one region from
  Scandinavia and/or one from western/central Europe (including the United Kingdom) and / or
  one from the Mediterranean. The study of the region should include – physical processes e.g.
  climate, soils, relief, and drainage – economic processes - primary activities e.g.
  agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining/energy - secondary activities e.g. patterns in
  manufacturing activities - tertiary activities, e.g. transport and tourism. – human
  processes, e.g. language, religion, urban and rural development and population dynamics."
  The Mezzogiorno is the Mediterranean (peripheral) choice, contrasting with the Paris Basin
  in `geo-region-european-s1`.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.
- **Scale:** each heading (physical; primary; secondary; tertiary; human) must carry a
  30-mark Higher answer of about 15 SRPs. Explain how the physical environment, history and
  government policy interact to keep the region peripheral, and what has changed.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The region
- [ ] R1 Definition and extent: southern Italy and the islands; the eight regions (section 2); meaning of "Mezzogiorno" ("midday", the midday sun); share of Italy's area and population.
- [ ] R2 Why it is peripheral: distance from the EU core and Italian markets, mountainous relief, Mediterranean climate stresses, history (feudal estates, unification), organised crime, emigration; lower GDP per head and higher unemployment than the North (the North–South divide, "questione meridionale"). One block; details follow.
- [ ] R3 Sketch-map skill: checked rivers, urban centres, relief features and agricultural or industrial areas students can draw and name.

Physical processes
- [ ] P1 Climate: Mediterranean: hot, dry summers (subtropical high pressure moves north); mild, wet winters (Atlantic depressions); figures in section 2; the sirocco; summer drought and water shortage; intense autumn/winter downpours and flash floods; rainfall higher on the Apennines than on the lowlands. Link to farming (irrigation, drought-tolerant crops).
- [ ] P2 Relief: mostly mountains and hills; the Apennines run the length of the peninsula; Calabrian uplands; small coastal lowlands (named); steep slopes limit farming, transport and settlement.
- [ ] P3 Tectonic and volcanic activity: the African and Eurasian plates; earthquakes (Messina 1908, Irpinia 1980, L'Aquila 2009); volcanoes (Vesuvius, Etna, Stromboli, Campi Flegrei); fertile volcanic soils. Brief: point to `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes` packages and reuse their facts; don't re-teach processes.
- [ ] P4 Soils: thin, stony upland soils; terra rossa on limestone; fertile volcanic soils (Campania, Etna) and alluvial lowland soils; soil erosion from deforestation, overgrazing and winter downpours (calanchi badlands); mudflows (Sarno 1998, reuse).
- [ ] P5 Drainage: short, steep rivers; seasonal regime (torrents in winter, dry beds in summer: fiumare); few navigable rivers; water transfer by aqueduct; dams and irrigation schemes.

Economic processes: primary
- [ ] A1 Traditional agriculture: latifundia (large estates of absentee landlords) worked by landless labourers; extensive wheat and sheep; small, fragmented hill farms; low productivity.
- [ ] A2 Land reform (1950) and the Cassa per il Mezzogiorno (1950–1984): redistribution of estates, new farmhouses and villages on reclaimed lowlands, malaria eradication, drainage, irrigation, roads.
- [ ] A3 Modern agriculture: durum wheat on the Tavoliere; olives (Puglia); citrus (Sicily); vines; tomatoes and market gardening on the irrigated Campanian plain; specialised, export-oriented Mediterranean produce; agri-food processing.
- [ ] A4 Problems facing agriculture: drought and water shortage; soil erosion; Xylella fastidiosa killing Puglia's olive trees; ageing farmers and rural depopulation; exploitation of migrant farm labour (caporalato); dependence on CAP support.
- [ ] A5 Forestry: historic deforestation of the Apennines; reafforestation (e.g. the Sila) under the Cassa; summer wildfires.
- [ ] A6 Fishing: Mediterranean fishing, Mazara del Vallo; traditional tuna fishing in Sicily; overfishing. Brief.
- [ ] A7 Energy: the Val d'Agri oilfield (Basilicata); gas pipelines landing in Sicily and Puglia; growth of wind and solar power in Puglia and Sicily.

Economic processes: secondary
- [ ] M1 Reasons for weak industrial development: distance from markets, poor infrastructure, small local market and low incomes, lack of capital and enterprise, organised crime (extortion), lack of raw materials.
- [ ] M2 The growth-pole policy of the Cassa: state-led heavy industry (steel at Taranto; petrochemicals at Brindisi and in eastern Sicily); the Bari–Brindisi–Taranto industrial triangle; "cathedrals in the desert" (capital-intensive plants, few jobs, few local linkages).
- [ ] M3 Car industry: Alfasud/Fiat at Pomigliano d'Arco (near Naples); Fiat at Melfi (Basilicata); closure of Termini Imerese (Sicily, 2011).
- [ ] M4 Decline and problems: the Taranto steelworks and its pollution; closures; the informal (black) economy.
- [ ] M5 Recent policy: EU cohesion funding (less-developed regions); the single Special Economic Zone for the South (2024); Italy's post-Covid recovery plan earmarking at least 40% of funds for the South; aerospace and agri-food as growth sectors.
- [ ] M6 Factors questions: be ready to explain secondary activity by labour, transport, raw materials, markets, infrastructure, government policy using the facts above, with the challenge each factor creates.

Economic processes: tertiary
- [ ] T1 Tourism: why tourists come (climate, coast, volcanoes, heritage, food); named UNESCO and coastal sites; the Costa Smeralda; agritourism; problems (seasonality, coastal overdevelopment and illegal building, crime, poor transport).
- [ ] T2 Transport: the Autostrada del Sole; the slow upgrading of the A2 to Reggio Calabria; rail (high-speed only to Naples/Salerno); Gioia Tauro container port; ferries to Sicily, Sardinia, Greece and Albania; the Strait of Messina crossing. Importance of transport to development (the region's remoteness is partly a transport problem).
- [ ] T3 Services and the public sector: large public-sector and informal employment; weaker private services than the North.

Human processes
- [ ] H1 Population distribution: dense coastal lowlands and cities (Campania) vs sparse interior uplands (Basilicata); reasons (relief, water, soils, jobs, history of hilltop settlement).
- [ ] H2 Population dynamics: high historic emigration (to the Americas from the late 19th century; to northern Italy, Germany, Switzerland and Belgium after the Second World War); continuing out-migration of young graduates; fertility, once high, now very low; ageing and depopulation of inland villages (€1 house schemes); inward migration across the Mediterranean (Lampedusa).
- [ ] H3 Language: Italian; strong regional dialects (Neapolitan, Sicilian); historic minority languages (Albanian/Arbëreshë, Greek/Griko, Sardinian, Catalan in Alghero).
- [ ] H4 Religion: Roman Catholic, with strong traditional practice and religious festivals (San Gennaro, Naples).
- [ ] H5 Culture as a whole (religion, language, politics, music/dance, sport): the tarantella; Neapolitan song; football (SSC Napoli); Neapolitan pizza; regional autonomy of Sicily and Sardinia; clientelism and organised crime in politics. One block.
- [ ] H6 Urban development: Naples as the largest city of the South: historic centre, overcrowding (Spanish Quarter), peripheral estates (Scampia), the waste crisis, crime; Palermo; rapid unplanned growth fed by rural migration; illegal building.
- [ ] H7 Rural development: hilltop villages (defence, escape from malarial lowlands); land-reform villages and farmhouses on the plains; Matera's sassi (cave dwellings evacuated in the 1950s, now a UNESCO site and 2019 European Capital of Culture); depopulation and abandonment of interior villages.
- [ ] H8 Factors in urban growth (Naples): site on the Bay of Naples, port, capital of the former Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, fertile volcanic hinterland, rural in-migration.

## 2. Checked facts and examples (the only named places, dates and figures you may use)

| R1 | Eight regions: Abruzzo, Molise, Campania, Puglia (Apulia), Basilicata, Calabria, Sicily, Sardinia. "Mezzogiorno" means "midday". About 40% of Italy's area and about one-third of its population: about 20 million people (2020s), out of an Italian population of about 59 million (2024). Sicily and Sardinia are autonomous regions with a special statute. |
| R2 | The Mezzogiorno produces only about 22% of Italy's GDP; its GDP per head is a little over half that of the Centre-North (recent years). Unemployment is roughly two to three times the North's rate (recent years). The South (the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies) became part of a united Italy in 1861; the North–South gap widened after unification. |
| R3 sketch items | Rivers: Volturno (Campania; the longest river of the southern mainland), Ofanto (Puglia), Basento (Basilicata). Urban centres: Naples, Bari, Taranto, Brindisi, Palermo, Catania, Reggio Calabria. Relief: Apennines; Tavoliere di Puglia (lowland around Foggia); Campanian plain; plain of Catania; the Sila (Calabria); Mount Etna; Vesuvius. Agricultural/industrial areas: the Tavoliere (durum wheat); the Bari–Brindisi–Taranto industrial triangle. |
| P1 | Lowland summer (July) averages around 25°C or more; coastal winter (January) averages about 9–12°C. Most rain falls between October and March. Annual rainfall is under 600 mm on parts of the Puglia lowlands and Sicily, much higher on the Apennines. Sirocco: a hot, dry, dusty wind from the Sahara. |
| P2 | Apennines: highest point Corno Grande (Gran Sasso, Abruzzo), 2,912 m. Mount Etna, Sicily: about 3,300 m, Europe's highest active volcano. Lowlands: Tavoliere di Puglia (the largest plain of peninsular southern Italy), Campanian plain around Naples, plain of Catania (Sicily's largest plain). |
| P3 | Messina earthquake and tsunami, 28 December 1908: Messina and Reggio Calabria destroyed; tens of thousands killed. Irpinia earthquake, Campania, 23 November 1980: thousands killed. L'Aquila, Abruzzo, 6 April 2009: magnitude 6.3, about 300 deaths (reuse `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s2`). Vesuvius buried Pompeii and Herculaneum in AD 79; it last erupted in 1944. Etna and Stromboli are active. Campi Flegrei: a volcanic caldera west of Naples where the ground rises and falls (bradyseism) around Pozzuoli. Fertile volcanic soils on Vesuvius and Etna support vineyards and orchards. |
| P4 | Terra rossa: red clay soil on limestone, e.g. Puglia. Calanchi: gullied badland slopes on clay in Basilicata. Sarno, Campania, 5–6 May 1998: saturated volcanic ash slid as mudflows, about 160 killed (reuse `geo-rocks-weathering-s5`). |
| P5 | Fiumare: wide, dry, stony riverbeds in Calabria and Sicily that flood after heavy rain. The Apulian Aqueduct (Acquedotto Pugliese), built in the early 20th century, carries water from the Sele river springs in Campania across the Apennines to Puglia. |
| A1 | Latifundia: large estates owned by absentee landlords, worked by landless day labourers (braccianti), used for extensive wheat growing and sheep grazing. |
| A2 | Land reform laws of 1950 expropriated parts of large estates and gave plots to peasants. Cassa per il Mezzogiorno (Fund for the South), 1950–1984: land reclamation, irrigation dams, aqueducts, roads; from the late 1950s it also funded industry. Malaria was eradicated from the lowlands after the Second World War. |
| A3 | Tavoliere di Puglia, around Foggia: Italy's main durum wheat area (for pasta). Puglia is Italy's leading olive-oil region. Sicilian blood oranges grown on the plain of Catania near Etna. San Marzano tomatoes grown on the plain near Vesuvius. Lemons on the Amalfi coast and around Sorrento. Puglia and Sicily are major wine producers. |
| A4 | Xylella fastidiosa: a bacterial disease of olive trees detected in the Salento (southern Puglia) in 2013; it has killed millions of olive trees. Caporalato: illegal gangmaster hiring and exploitation of farm labourers, many of them migrants. |
| A5 | Reafforestation of the Sila plateau, Calabria. |
| A6 | Mazara del Vallo, Sicily: one of Italy's main fishing ports. Traditional tuna trapping (the mattanza) in Sicily, e.g. Favignana; now largely ended. |
| A7 | Val d'Agri, Basilicata: Italy's largest onshore oilfield and one of the largest onshore oilfields in western Europe. Gas pipelines from Algeria (landing at Mazara del Vallo) and Libya (landing at Gela) reach Sicily; the Trans Adriatic Pipeline (TAP), carrying gas from Azerbaijan, lands in Puglia and began flowing in 2020. Puglia and Sicily are among Italy's leading regions for wind and solar power. |
| M2 | Taranto steelworks: built by the state firm Italsider in the 1960s; one of Europe's largest steelworks (later Ilva, now Acciaierie d'Italia). In 2012 a court ordered parts of the plant seized because of pollution linked to illness in Taranto. Petrochemicals at Brindisi and at the Priolo–Augusta complex near Siracusa (Sicily); refinery at Gela (Sicily). "Cathedrals in the desert": large, capital-intensive state plants that created few jobs and few local spin-off firms. |
| M3 | Alfasud plant at Pomigliano d'Arco near Naples, opened 1972, now builds the Fiat Panda. Fiat's Melfi plant, Basilicata, opened in the early 1990s (now Stellantis). Fiat's plant at Termini Imerese, Sicily, closed in 2011. Leonardo builds Boeing 787 fuselage sections at Grottaglie, near Taranto. |
| M4 | Organised crime: Cosa Nostra (Sicily), 'Ndrangheta (Calabria), Camorra (Campania), Sacra Corona Unita (Puglia); extortion payments (pizzo) deter investment. Lavoro nero: undeclared work in the informal economy. |
| M5 | For EU cohesion policy 2021–27, most of the South, including Campania, Puglia, Basilicata, Calabria and Sicily, is classed as "less developed regions" (GDP per head below 75% of the EU average). A single Special Economic Zone (ZES Unica) covering the whole South began in January 2024, with tax credits and simplified permits. Italy's post-Covid National Recovery and Resilience Plan reserves at least 40% of its territorially allocated funds for the South. |
| T1 | UNESCO sites: Pompeii and Herculaneum (1997); the Amalfi Coast (1997); the Valley of the Temples, Agrigento (1997); the trulli of Alberobello, Puglia (1996); the sassi of Matera (1993); the historic centre of Naples (1995). The Costa Smeralda, north-east Sardinia, was developed as a luxury resort from the 1960s by the Aga Khan. Taormina, Sicily. Agriturismo: farm-stay tourism. | T2 | Autostrada del Sole (A1, Milan–Rome–Naples), completed 1964. A2 Salerno–Reggio Calabria motorway: notorious for decades of slow, costly upgrading. Ferries cross the Strait of Messina between Villa San Giovanni/Reggio Calabria and Messina; ferries from Bari and Brindisi to Greece and Albania. High-speed rail reaches Naples and Salerno; the Naples–Bari line is being upgraded. |
| H1 | Campania: about 400 people per km², one of Italy's most densely populated regions. Basilicata: about 55 people per km² and only about 540,000 people (2020s). |
| H2 | Large-scale emigration from the late 19th century to the USA and Argentina; after the Second World War to the industrial cities of northern Italy (Turin, Milan) and to Germany, Switzerland and Belgium. Lampedusa, an Italian island south of Sicily, is a main landing point for migrants crossing the Mediterranean. |
| H3 | Neapolitan and Sicilian dialects. Arbëreshë: Albanian-speaking communities in Calabria and Sicily, settled from the 15th century. Griko: Greek dialect in the Salento (Puglia) and southern Calabria. Sardinian. Catalan spoken in Alghero, Sardinia. |
| H4 | Feast of San Gennaro, patron saint of Naples, with the ritual of the liquefaction of his blood. |
| H5 | The tarantella: a fast folk dance of southern Italy, named after Taranto. Neapolitan song, e.g. "'O sole mio". SSC Napoli won Serie A in 1987, 1990, 2023 and 2025. The art of the Neapolitan pizza maker was added to UNESCO's intangible heritage list in 2017. |

| H8 | Naples: on the Bay of Naples beside Vesuvius; capital of the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies until 1861. |

## 3. Traps

- The Mezzogiorno is southern Italy and its islands only. Don't include Rome or Lazio in it.
- Don't give the share of the Mezzogiorno that is upland as a percentage (textbook figures vary): say "mostly mountainous or hilly".
- Don't give exact death tolls for Messina 1908 or Irpinia 1980 (sources disagree): "tens of thousands" and "thousands" only.
- Strait of Messina bridge: long proposed, approved and challenged repeatedly; don't state that it is built, under construction or cancelled.
- The Po valley, the "industrial triangle" of Milan–Turin–Genoa and Fiat's Turin plants are northern Italy, the contrast, not the Mezzogiorno. The South's own triangle is Bari–Brindisi–Taranto; keep the two apart.
- Past-paper model answers for this chapter include invented Mediterranean statistics (tourist totals, percentages of world orange output, GDP figures). Use none of them.
- Volcano and earthquake processes, Vesuvius 79 AD, L'Aquila and Sarno details are in the `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes` and `geo-rocks-weathering` packages: reuse the facts given here only, and don't re-teach the processes.
- Migration theory and EU migration policy belong to `geo-population-migration`; CAP and EU regional funds' effects on Ireland belong to `geo-economic-eu`. Here, EU policy only as it affects the Mezzogiorno.
- The Paris Basin (core contrast) is `geo-region-european-s1`. When a question asks for two contrasting European regions, the notes must make the contrast explicit, but don't repeat Paris Basin facts in this file.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Outline/sketch map of a European region: named river, two urban centres, named relief feature, named agricultural or industrial area.
- 30 marks: impact of climate or relief on primary activity in two contrasting European regions (s1 and s2 together); development of agriculture with reference to relief, climate, markets.
- 30 marks: development of secondary activity with reference to two factors (labour, transport, raw materials, markets, infrastructure, government policy).
- 30 marks: tourism or transport as a tertiary activity; importance of transport to economic development in two contrasting regions.
- 30 marks: population distribution throughout a European region; factors in the growth of one urban area.
- Ordinary 40 marks: name a primary/secondary/tertiary activity, the region's advantages for it, and one challenge it faces.
- Ordinary 30 marks: the culture of a region (religion, language, politics, music/dance, sport/games).
