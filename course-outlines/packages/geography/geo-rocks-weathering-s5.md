## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] I1 Mass movement (mass wasting): the downslope movement of weathered material (regolith, soil, rock, mud, peat, snow) under the influence of gravity, without a transporting agent such as a river or glacier. Weathering supplies the material.
- [ ] I2 Forces on a slope: gravity pulls material downslope; friction and cohesion (and plant roots) resist. Movement happens when the downslope force exceeds the resistance.
- [ ] I3 Classification by speed (slow / fast) and by water content (dry / wet); also by type of movement (heave, slide, flow, fall). Exams ask whether named processes are fast or slow.
- [ ] I4 Mass movement varies in space (steep, wet, unvegetated slopes) and in time (after heavy rain, snowmelt, earthquakes, seasonal freeze-thaw). This is the syllabus's "intensity and frequency" point.

Slow processes (each: what it is, how it works, where, evidence, diagram cue)
- [ ] SL1 Soil creep: the slowest; a few millimetres to centimetres a year. Soil particles are lifted at right angles to the slope by wetting (swelling) or freezing (frost heave), then drop vertically on drying or thawing, so each cycle moves them a little downslope. Evidence: terracettes (small steps on grassy slopes), bent tree trunks, tilted fences, walls and poles, soil piled up behind walls, cracked roads.
- [ ] SL2 Solifluction: in periglacial areas, the surface layer thaws in summer above permanently frozen ground (permafrost); the saturated layer cannot drain and flows slowly downhill, forming lobes and terraces. Widespread in Ireland at the end of the last glaciation.

Fast processes (each: what it is, causes, example from section 2, diagram cue)
- [ ] F1 Rockfall: blocks loosened by freeze-thaw (or undercut by the sea or a river) fall freely from a steep cliff, collecting as scree (talus) at the foot.
- [ ] F2 Landslide (rockslide): a mass of rock or debris slides suddenly along a flat plane, such as a bedding plane or joint sloping in the same direction as the slope, often after heavy rain lubricates the plane or an earthquake shakes it.
- [ ] F3 Slump (rotational slide): saturated material, often clay over or under permeable rock, slides along a curved (concave) slip plane and rotates backward, leaving a crescent-shaped scar (scarp) at the top, a back-tilted block and a bulging toe. Common on clay cliffs undercut by the sea.
- [ ] F4 Mudflow: fine material (clay, silt, weathered rock, volcanic ash) saturated by heavy rain or snowmelt flows rapidly downhill like a liquid, often along valleys; can bury settlements.
- [ ] F5 Lahar: a volcanic mudflow of ash and water (rain, or melted snow and ice on the volcano); can travel very fast and far.
- [ ] F6 Bog burst (peat slide): blanket bog on a slope becomes saturated after prolonged or intense rain; the peat's weight increases and its base loses grip on the underlying mineral soil or rock, so the peat bursts and flows downhill as a black slurry; blocks rivers, kills fish, destroys roads, bridges and land. Common in the uplands of western and north-western Ireland.
- [ ] F7 Avalanche: a sudden, rapid slide of snow and ice down a steep mountain slope, triggered by heavy snowfall, rapid thaw, or disturbance; also ice avalanches when part of a glacier collapses.

Factors governing mass movement (each: how it increases or reduces movement; exams ask two and an explanation)
- [ ] FA1 Gradient (slope angle): the steeper the slope, the greater the downslope pull of gravity and the faster the movement; gentle slopes favour creep, steep slopes falls and slides.
- [ ] FA2 Water content: some water helps cohesion, but saturation fills the pores, adds weight, reduces friction between particles and acts as a lubricant, so material flows; heavy or prolonged rain and snowmelt trigger most fast movements.
- [ ] FA3 Type and structure of rock and soil: clay becomes slippery and plastic when wet; jointed or bedded rock dipping toward the slope slides; permeable rock over impermeable clay or shale concentrates water at the junction (Antrim).
- [ ] FA4 Vegetation: roots bind soil and plants absorb water; bare slopes move more easily.
- [ ] FA5 Climate and weathering: freeze-thaw loosens rock; heavy seasonal rainfall; periglacial climates give solifluction.
- [ ] FA6 Undercutting and loading: erosion by the sea or a river at the base of a slope removes support; extra weight at the top (rock, buildings, spoil) increases the downslope force.
- [ ] FA7 Tectonic activity: earthquakes and volcanic eruptions shake loose material and trigger landslides and lahars.
- [ ] FA8 Human activity: one bullet only, pointing to `geo-rocks-weathering-s7` (road cuttings, building on slopes, drainage, overgrazing, deforestation).

Case studies (enough facts for a 30-mark answer on one process; use section 2 only)
- [ ] CS1 Antrim coastal landslides (national setting): geology and process.
- [ ] CS2 Bog bursts in N.W. Ireland (national setting): Pollatomish, plus the historic Knocknageeha example (Co. Kerry, not N.W., but the classic Irish bog burst).
- [ ] CS3 Italy (international setting): Sarno mudflows; Vajont landslide.
- [ ] CS4 South America (international setting): Armero lahar; Vargas mudslides.
- [ ] CS5 Recent glacier-collapse events in the Alps (Blatten, Marmolada): glacier retreat as a new cause of mass movement.

## 3. Traps (known errors)

- A marking-scheme answer refers to a "Cummeragh Mountain bogburst in Donegal (2003)". No such event is checked; it confuses other places. Do not use it. The 2003 Irish events in the table are Pollatomish (Co. Mayo) and Derrybrien (Co. Galway, in `geo-rocks-weathering-s7`).
- Do not give slope-angle thresholds (e.g. "30–45 degrees") as facts; they are not checked. Say "steep" and "gentle".
- Do not give creep rates beyond "millimetres to centimetres a year".
- Fast vs slow, as exams test it: soil creep and solifluction are slow; rockfall, landslide, slump, mudflow, lahar, bog burst and avalanche are fast.
- Slump is rotational (curved slip plane); a landslide/rockslide moves along a flat plane. Keep them separate.
- Human causes of mass movement and its management belong to `geo-rocks-weathering-s7` (Derrybrien, Meenbog, overgrazing, deforestation). Here, name human activity as a factor in one bullet only. Aberfan is in `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`.
- Freeze-thaw as a weathering process is taught in `geo-rocks-weathering-s3`: name it here as the cause of rockfall without repeating the stages.
- Coastal cliff erosion and coastal landforms belong to `geo-sea`; river erosion to `geo-rivers`. Undercutting is named here as a factor only.
- Do not copy the details of past-paper news articles beyond the facts in the table.
