# Package: geo-glaciation-s2 — Isostasy

- **loId:** `geo-glaciation-s2` · **chapterId:** `geo-glaciation` · **syllabusRef:** 1.6
- **Syllabus:** "Students should study the way in which landforms result from a combination of
  crustal uplift (in response to isostatic readjustment) and denudation by surface processes,
  and that sometimes landscapes illustrate that these opposing forces are temporarily out of
  balance." Studied item: **isostasy**. Statement: "All landforms represent a balance between
  endogenetic and exogenetic forces; this balance may change through time."
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-19. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] I1 Endogenetic forces (internal, e.g. uplift) vs exogenetic forces (surface processes: weathering, erosion, deposition). Landscapes reflect the balance between them.
- [ ] I2 The crust floats on the denser mantle (asthenosphere), like a block of wood in water. Isostasy is the state of balance between them.
- [ ] I3 Isostatic depression: the weight of an ice sheet presses the crust down.
- [ ] I4 Isostatic rebound (recovery, uplift): when the ice melts, the crust slowly rises. Rebound lags far behind melting and is still continuing in some places.
- [ ] I5 Denudation also changes the load: erosion removes weight, so the crust rises; heavy deposition (e.g. at deltas) adds weight, so it sinks.
- [ ] I6 Eustatic change: a worldwide change in sea level. During glaciation, water locked up in ice lowered sea level; meltwater raised it again. Distinguish it clearly from isostatic (land-level) change.
- [ ] I7 Base level: the lowest level to which a river can erode, normally sea level. A relative change between land and sea alters base level.
- [ ] I8 Out of balance: where uplift is faster than denudation, or sea level rises faster than the land, the landscape shows features that do not match present conditions. These are the syllabus's "temporarily out of balance" landscapes.

Coastal landscape
- [ ] C1 Uplift relative to sea level (emergent coastline): raised beaches. Formation; features found on them (old cliff line, wave-cut notch, sea caves and stacks above the present tide reach, beach shingle, a flat former wave-cut platform).
- [ ] C2 Sea level higher relative to land (submergent coastline): rias (drowned river valleys), fjords (drowned glacial troughs), drowned drumlins.
- [ ] C3 The north–south contrast in Ireland: thicker ice and more rebound in the north, with raised beaches; drowned coastlines in the south-west.

Fluvial landscape
- [ ] R1 A fall in base level gives the river renewed erosive power. This is **rejuvenation**, the name exams ask for.
- [ ] R2 Landforms of rejuvenation, one sentence each, naming only: knickpoint (and waterfall), incised meanders, river terraces. Their full formation is in `geo-rivers-s2`; don't repeat it.
- [ ] R3 A rise in base level drowns the lower course: rias, estuaries.

## 3. Traps

- Isostatic change = the **land** moves; eustatic change = the **sea** moves. Never swap them.
- A ria is a drowned **river** valley (V-shaped in cross-section, winding); a fjord is a drowned **glacial** valley (U-shaped, deep, often with a shallow threshold at its mouth). Keep them separate.
- Don't give uplift rates or ice thicknesses: none are checked.
- Glacial landform formation (corries, drumlins etc.) is in `geo-glaciation-s1`. Refer to drumlins and fjords only as drowned features.
- Cyclic landscape development and peneplains (Munster planation surfaces) belong to `geo-rivers-s3`. Leave them out here.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Naming two coastal features formed by isostatic adjustment in a marked area of a map. Expected answers: raised beaches and related features in an uplifted area; rias, fjords and drowned drumlins in a submerged one.
- The name of the process that renews erosion in a river's old stage (rejuvenation).
- True/false on endogenic forces (they are internal forces from within the earth).
- 30 marks: "Describe and explain how isostasy impacts on the coastal landscape or on the fluvial landscape."
