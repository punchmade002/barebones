## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Glaciers and the Irish Ice Age
- [ ] G1 Ice Age context: Ireland was glaciated during the Pleistocene; two main cold stages, the older Munsterian and the more recent Midlandian.
- [ ] G2 Ice sheet (continental, covers whole landscapes, e.g. Antarctica, Greenland) vs valley glacier (confined to a valley, fed from corries).
- [ ] G3 Formation of glacier ice: snow → firn (névé) → glacier ice by compaction and air expulsion.
- [ ] G4 Glacier budget: accumulation zone, ablation zone, equilibrium line.
- [ ] G5 Advance and retreat of the snout: depends on the balance of accumulation and ablation; ice still flows forward during retreat.
- [ ] G6 Movement: basal slip (meltwater lubrication), internal deformation (plastic flow), rotational movement in a corrie; fastest at the centre surface.
- [ ] G7 Skill: average rate of snout movement = distance ÷ number of years. Use your own invented round numbers and say they are illustrative.

Erosion
- [ ] E1 Plucking.
- [ ] E2 Abrasion, producing striations, polished rock and rock flour.
- [ ] E3 Freeze-thaw on rock above and beside the ice supplies debris.

Landforms of erosion (each: formation in numbered stages, example from section 2, diagram cue)
- [ ] L1 Corrie (cirque) and tarn.
- [ ] L2 Arête.
- [ ] L3 Pyramidal peak (horn).
- [ ] L4 U-shaped valley (glacial trough), including truncated spurs.
- [ ] L5 Hanging valley and its waterfall.
- [ ] L6 Ribbon lake (overdeepening), and paternoster lakes (a chain of rock-basin lakes along a valley floor, separated by bars of harder rock).
- [ ] L7 Fjord.
- [ ] L8 Roche moutonnée (smooth stoss side by abrasion, rough lee side by plucking).
- [ ] L9 Striations (direction of ice flow).

Transport
- [ ] T1 Supraglacial, englacial and subglacial load.
- [ ] T2 Glacial load is angular and unsorted.

Landforms of glacial deposition (each: formation, example, diagram cue)
- [ ] D1 Till / boulder clay: unsorted, unstratified, angular; boulder clay plains.
- [ ] D2 Moraines: lateral, medial, terminal (end), recessional, ground.
- [ ] D3 Drumlins: shape, orientation, swarms ("basket of eggs" landscape), drowned drumlins.
- [ ] D4 Erratics.

Landforms of fluvio-glacial deposition (each: formation, example where given, diagram cue)
- [ ] F1 Sorted and stratified deposits: how fluvio-glacial material differs from till. Exams ask students to classify features as glacial erosion, glacial deposition or fluvio-glacial.
- [ ] F2 Esker.
- [ ] F3 Outwash plain (sandur).
- [ ] F4 Kame and kame terrace.
- [ ] F5 Kettle hole and kettle lake.

## 3. Traps (known errors)

- Do not copy figures from past papers, e.g. the 2022 Stein Glacier distance. Make up your own round numbers for the rate-of-movement skill and label them illustrative.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Matching photos or diagrams to: U-shaped valley, corrie/tarn, arête, fjord, drumlin, erratic, striations, lateral moraine, interlocking spurs (fluvial, as a distractor).
- True/false: striations are carved by ice; U-shaped valleys are erosional, not depositional; eskers are fluvio-glacial; deranged drainage occurs on glacial deposits.
- Classifying features as glacial erosion, glacial deposition or fluvio-glacial.
- Classifying lake types as glacial, fluvial or karst (tarn, ribbon lake and kettle lake are glacial).
- Labelling a diagram of six glacial landforms; explaining plucking.
- Did a glacier advance or retreat; what makes a snout advance; average annual rate of movement.
- Naming two landforms of glacial deposition.
- 30/40 marks: explaining the formation of one or two landforms with diagrams.
