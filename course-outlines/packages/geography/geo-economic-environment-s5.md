# Package: geo-economic-environment-s5 — Conflicts between economic interests and environmental interests

- **loId:** `geo-economic-environment-s5` · **chapterId:** `geo-economic-environment` · **syllabusRef:** 4.5
- **Syllabus:** students should study "conflicts that may develop between local and global
  economic interests and environmental interests. Students should be familiar with the issues
  relating to at least two examples." National setting: "Appropriate national examples e.g.
  Irish fish stocks, tourism, and heritage." International setting: "Appropriate global
  examples." Higher questions ask for **two examples**; Ordinary questions ask for one conflict
  "between people". Write every studied area below as a self-contained case (the two sides, who
  is involved, what happened, outcome), so a student can pick any two.
- **Studied areas:** (1) Corrib gas, Co. Mayo; (2) Irish fish stocks; (3) Mullaghmore, the
  Burren (tourism); (4) Skellig Michael (tourism and heritage); (5) the M3 and the Hill of Tara
  (heritage); (6) turf cutting on protected raised bogs; (7) wind farms; (8) dairy expansion and
  water and climate; (9) data centres and electricity; global: (10) the Amazon rainforest,
  Brazil; (11) lignite mining in Germany (Hambach Forest, Lützerath); (12) the Three Gorges Dam.
  Items 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 are the main cases and need the fullest blocks.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] C1 Why conflicts arise: economic interests (jobs, profit, energy, tax revenue, regional development, infrastructure) vs environmental interests (habitats, landscape, heritage, water and air quality, health, safety, the climate); local vs national vs global interests.
- [ ] C2 Who is involved (stakeholders): developers and companies, government and State agencies, local communities (often divided among themselves), environmental groups, farmers and fishermen, tourists, the EU and the courts.
- [ ] C3 How conflicts are resolved: planning permission and appeals, environmental impact assessment, court cases, EU law (Habitats and Birds Directives, Special Areas of Conservation), compensation, compromise (route changes, limits, conditions), sustainable management.

National examples (each a full case: background, economic side, environmental side, events, outcome)
- [ ] N1 Corrib gas, Co. Mayo: economic case (energy security, a domestic gas supply, revenue, construction jobs) vs local safety and environmental objections (a high-pressure raw-gas pipeline near homes, Sruwaddacon Bay protected estuary, peat, Carrowmore Lake drinking water); Shell to Sea; the Rossport Five; route changed and a tunnel built; gas flowed 2015; Shell sold out 2018.
- [ ] N2 Irish fish stocks: fishermen and coastal communities (jobs, Killybegs and the other harbours) vs conservation through EU quotas; much of the catch in Irish waters taken by other EU fleets; Brexit cut Irish quotas; decommissioning of vessels; the 2007 ban on salmon drift-net fishing (commercial fishermen vs conservation and angling tourism); the north-east Atlantic mackerel dispute with Iceland and the Faroes; fish farming vs wild salmon (sea lice).
- [ ] N3 Mullaghmore, the Burren, Co. Clare (tourism): the OPW's planned interpretative (visitor) centre in the Burren National Park; jobs and visitor spending vs a fragile limestone landscape and rare plants; the Burren Action Group; the Supreme Court ruling of 1993; final refusal in 2000 and restoration of the site.
- [ ] N4 Skellig Michael, Co. Kerry (tourism and heritage): UNESCO World Heritage monastic site; boat operators and Star Wars tourism vs the fragile site, steep steps, safety and seabird colonies; daily visitor limit and licensed boats.
- [ ] N5 The M3 motorway and the Hill of Tara, Co. Meath (heritage): commuter congestion and regional access vs the archaeological landscape of the Tara–Skryne valley; protests; Lismullin; motorway opened 2010.
- [ ] N6 Turf cutting on protected raised bogs: rights and cheap fuel for rural families vs EU Habitats Directive protection and carbon storage; the ban on 53 raised-bog SACs; compensation; protests.
- [ ] N7 Wind farms: renewable targets, landowner income and community funds vs visual impact, noise, shadow flicker, property values, birds, peat stability (Derrybrien); offshore wind vs fishing grounds and seascapes.
- [ ] N8 Dairy expansion after milk quotas ended (2015): farm incomes and food exports vs water quality (nitrates) and greenhouse gas emissions; the agricultural emissions ceiling.
- [ ] N9 Data centres: investment, multinational jobs and Ireland's tech economy vs huge electricity demand, grid strain and climate targets; EirGrid's limit on new connections in Dublin.

Global and European examples
- [ ] G1 The Amazon rainforest, Brazil (full case): economic drivers (cattle ranching, soya, logging, mining at Carajás, hydro dams such as Belo Monte, roads such as the Trans-Amazonian Highway) vs environmental interests (biodiversity, carbon store, rainfall recycling, indigenous peoples); deforestation trend with checked figures; Chico Mendes; protection measures (Forest Code, soy moratorium, Amazon Fund, indigenous reserves); swings in policy under different governments; global interest (climate) vs Brazil's development needs.
- [ ] G2 Lignite mining in Germany: RWE's open-cast mines in the Rhineland vs forest, villages and climate; Hambach Forest protests and the 2018 court ruling; Lützerath cleared in 2023; coal exit by 2038.
- [ ] G3 Three Gorges Dam, China: clean electricity, flood control and navigation vs over a million people relocated, flooded heritage and farmland, sediment trapping, ecological change (reuse `geo-rivers-s4`).
- [ ] G4 Global vs local in climate policy (one bullet): developing countries' right to develop vs the global need to cut emissions; fossil-fuel exporting countries' interests.

Map-based skill
- [ ] M1 Explaining how conflict may arise from locating a development (e.g. a wind farm or quarry) at a chosen map site: identify the groups for and against and their reasons. Invented, illustrative maps only.

## 2. Checked facts and examples (the only named places, dates and figures you may use)

Do not name any place, date or figure that is not on this list.

| Item | Example(s) |
|---|---|
| N1 Corrib | Corrib gas field, about 80 km off north-west Co. Mayo: discovered 1996; gas first flowed December 2015; processed at the Bellanaboy terminal; for a time supplied over half of Ireland's gas (from `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`). Local opposition to piping unprocessed, high-pressure gas onshore near homes at Rossport; the Shell to Sea campaign (from 2005) argued the gas should be processed at sea. The "Rossport Five" were jailed in June 2005 for refusing to obey a court order not to obstruct pipeline work and were released after 94 days. The onshore pipeline route was changed to keep it further from houses, and a tunnel was bored under Sruwaddacon Bay, a protected estuary (Special Area of Conservation), to carry it. Shell sold its share in the project in 2018. Protests were policed heavily for years; the dispute divided the local community. |
| N2 fish stocks | Killybegs, Co. Donegal: Ireland's largest fishing port (pelagic fleet: mackerel, herring). Six State Fishery Harbour Centres: Killybegs, Castletownbere, Dingle, Rossaveel, Dunmore East, Howth. Ireland's exclusive economic zone: 200 nautical miles. Common Fisheries Policy 1983: EU-wide TACs divided into national quotas; Irish fishermen have long argued that Ireland's share of the catch in its own rich waters is too small. The EU–UK Brexit trade agreement (December 2020) reduced Irish quotas (give no percentage) and led to a scheme paying owners to decommission (scrap) vessels. Ireland banned drift-net fishing for wild salmon at sea from 2007, because wild salmon numbers returning to Irish rivers had fallen sharply; commercial salmon fishermen received compensation; anglers and conservation groups supported the ban. From 2010 Iceland and the Faroe Islands set themselves large mackerel quotas as mackerel moved north-west into their waters; the EU, with strong support from Irish fishermen, protested; in 2013 it imposed trade measures on the Faroes over herring, banning imports of Faroese herring and mackerel; the dispute eased in 2014. Salmon farms in western bays: sea lice from farms are blamed by anglers for harming wild salmon and sea trout (name no farm or bay). |
| N3 Mullaghmore | The Burren, Co. Clare: Carboniferous limestone pavement, rare Arctic-alpine and Mediterranean plants side by side (spring gentian, mountain avens); Mullaghmore is a hill of folded, terraced limestone; the Burren National Park protects the area near Mullaghmore (from `geo-region-concept-s1`). In the early 1990s the Office of Public Works (OPW) began building an interpretative centre and car park near Mullaghmore. Supporters: tourism jobs and spending in a poor rural area; better visitor management. Opponents (the Burren Action Group): traffic, sewage and visitor pressure would damage a fragile, unspoilt landscape; centres should be in nearby villages. In 1993 the Supreme Court ruled that the State had to obtain planning permission like any other developer; work stopped. In 2000 An Bord Pleanála refused permission, and the partly built site was cleared and restored. |
| N4 Skellig | Skellig Michael, about 12 km off the Iveragh Peninsula, Co. Kerry: an early Christian monastery of stone beehive huts on a steep rocky island; UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1996; managed by the OPW. Access only by licensed boats in summer, with visitor numbers capped at about 180 a day; landings depend on the weather. Filming for the Star Wars films (2014 onwards) greatly increased demand. Seabird colonies (puffins, Manx shearwaters, storm petrels) breed on the island; Little Skellig holds one of the world's largest gannet colonies. Conservationists objected to filming and rising visitor numbers; boat operators and local businesses want more access. |
| N5 Tara | Hill of Tara, Co. Meath: ancient ceremonial site, traditional seat of the High Kings of Ireland. The M3 motorway (Clonee to north of Kells) was routed through the Tara–Skryne valley, about 2 km from the Hill. Supporters: relieve congestion on the N3 for the fast-growing commuter towns of Meath (Navan, Kells), cut journey times to Dublin. Opponents (campaign groups, archaeologists, protesters camped on the route): destruction of an archaeological landscape, dozens of sites found during excavation. In 2007 a large prehistoric ceremonial enclosure was found at Lismullin and declared a national monument; it was excavated and recorded, then the road went ahead. The M3 opened in 2010 (from `geo-weather-climate-s3`). |
| N6 turf | EU Habitats Directive, 1992: Ireland designated raised bogs as Special Areas of Conservation (SACs). After a ten-year derogation ended, turf cutting was banned on 53 raised-bog SACs from 2011. Compensation: about €1,500 a year for 15 years per turf cutter, or relocation to a bog not protected. The Turf Cutters and Contractors Association organised protests and continued cutting on some bogs. Raised bogs are rare in Europe and store large amounts of carbon; Ireland holds a large share of the EU's remaining active raised bog (give no percentage). |
| N7 wind | Derrybrien, Slieve Aughty Mountains, Co. Galway, 16 October 2003: a peat slide of about 450,000 m³ during wind-farm construction killed fish in the Owendalulleegh River; in 2019 the EU Court of Justice fined Ireland €5 million plus €15,000 a day over the missing environmental impact assessment (from `geo-rocks-weathering-s7`). Climate Action Plan: 80% renewable electricity by 2030 and at least 5 GW of offshore wind. |
| N8 dairy | Milk quotas abolished on 1 April 2015; the dairy herd grew strongly afterwards. Agriculture produces over a third of Ireland's greenhouse gas emissions. Sectoral ceilings (2022) require agriculture to cut emissions by 25% by 2030. EPA (2016–2021): only about half of Irish rivers, lakes and estuaries in good or high ecological status; agriculture the main pressure. |
| N9 data centres | Data centres used about a fifth of Ireland's metered electricity in 2023 (CSO); most are in the Dublin region (from `geo-region-ireland-s1`). In 2022 EirGrid said it would not connect new data centres in the Dublin area until about 2028 because the grid could not supply them. |
| G1 Amazon | The Amazon is the world's largest tropical rainforest, about 60% of it in Brazil. Annual deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon (PRODES satellite monitoring): about 27,800 km² in 2004; cut by over 80% to about 4,600 km² by 2012; rose again to about 13,000 km² in 2021; fell sharply from 2023. Most cleared land becomes cattle pasture; soya farming spreads in the south. The Trans-Amazonian Highway (begun 1970) opened the forest to settlers; clearings along roads form a "fishbone" pattern (Rondônia, from `geo-weather-climate-s2`). Carajás, Pará: the world's largest iron-ore mine (Vale). Belo Monte dam on the Xingu River, completed 2019, opposed by indigenous groups including the Kayapó. Chico Mendes, a rubber tapper and union leader who campaigned to protect the forest, was murdered at Xapuri, Acre, on 22 December 1988. Brazil's Forest Code requires landowners in the Amazon to keep 80% of their land as forest (often not enforced). Soy moratorium 2006: major traders agreed not to buy soya from newly cleared Amazon land. Amazon Fund 2008: donors (mainly Norway, also Germany) pay for reduced deforestation. Deforestation fell under President Lula (2003–2010 and from 2023) and rose under President Bolsonaro (2019–2022). Belém, Pará, hosted the COP30 climate conference in November 2025. |
| G2 Germany | RWE's Hambach open-cast lignite mine, North Rhine-Westphalia: expanding into the ancient Hambach Forest; protesters occupied tree houses for years; in October 2018 a German court halted clearing, and the remaining forest was later spared under the coal phase-out. The village of Lützerath was cleared of protesters in January 2023 to extend the Garzweiler lignite mine. Germany's 2020 law ends coal-fired power by 2038 at the latest. |
| G3 Three Gorges | Yangtze River, China: world's largest power station by installed capacity; dam completed 2006, fully operational 2012; over one million people relocated; flood control downstream; traps sediment (from `geo-rivers-s4`). Farmland, towns and archaeological sites were flooded by the reservoir (give no reservoir length). |

## 3. Traps

- Each case needs both sides. A conflict answer that describes only the damage is not about conflict.
- The Corrib formation, extraction and field facts belong in `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`; here use them only to set up the conflict.
- Fish-stock depletion as a sustainability issue (Grand Banks, CFP conservation measures) belongs in "Sustainable economic development"; the CFP's economic effects on Ireland belong in `geo-economic-eu`. Here, only the conflict between economic and conservation interests.
- Mullaghmore is in Co. Clare (the Burren); the Burren is a protected landscape, not a quarrying area. Do not invent a final visitor-centre location.
- Skellig Michael is a UNESCO World Heritage Site; do not say Little Skellig can be landed on (it cannot), and give no visitor figure other than "about 180 a day".
- The Lismullin site was excavated and recorded and the road built over it; don't say it was preserved in place or that the route was changed.
- Derrybrien is in Co. Galway, not Co. Clare.
- The Amazon figures are rounded PRODES figures for the Brazilian Amazon only; keep the years exactly as given and give no later yearly figure.
- Chico Mendes was murdered in 1988; the Rio Earth Summit was 1992. Don't merge them.
- Lützerath and Hambach are in Germany's Rhineland lignite area, not the Black Triangle (that is the acid-rain example in "Burning fossil fuels and using alternative energy sources").
- Deforestation as part of the 80-mark interdependence essays belongs to `geo-option-global-interdependence`; keep the Amazon here as a conflict of interests.
- Do not use past-paper model-answer examples or figures (e.g. invented wind-farm or hotel sites).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Higher 30 marks: discuss how conflict may develop between economic and environmental interests as a result of the exploitation of resources, with reference to two examples. Expected answer: two developed cases (e.g. Corrib gas and Irish fish stocks, or the Amazon and Mullaghmore), each with both sides, the groups involved and the outcome.
- Ordinary 30 marks (asked repeatedly): name one example of how the development of economic activities has resulted in conflict between people; describe and explain the conflict.
- Map-based (Ordinary and Higher): locate a wind farm or similar development on an Ordnance Survey map and explain how conflict may arise.
