# Package: geo-urban-s6 — Urban planning and renewal

- **loId:** `geo-urban-s6` · **chapterId:** `geo-urban` · **syllabusRef:** 5.6
- **Syllabus:** Students should study "• the effectiveness of urban planning strategies and urban
  renewal in solving urban problems". National setting: "Strategic urban plans." International
  setting: "Case studies from developed world cities" (shared with the s5 problems).
- **Scope:** The key verb is **effectiveness**: every strategy needs what it did, the evidence of
  success, and its failures or criticisms. Exams ask for the effectiveness of planning in solving
  two of housing, transport and pollution, so write a block for each of those three problems as
  well as the renewal case studies. Case studies: **Dublin Docklands**, **Temple Bar**,
  **Ballymun**, **Limerick Regeneration**, **Adamstown**, the national strategic plans (NSS,
  NPF), and **London Docklands** as the developed-world case outside Ireland.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Terms and approaches
- [ ] A1 Urban planning: controlling the use and development of land through development plans, zoning, planning permission and public investment. Urban renewal: improving and rehabilitating existing run-down areas, keeping the community in place. Urban redevelopment: clearing an area completely and rebuilding, often with new uses and new people. Gentrification as a side-effect (better-off people move in, prices rise, original residents are displaced).
- [ ] A2 Tools: development plans and zoning; tax incentives in designated areas; special development agencies (DDDA, LDDC); Strategic Development Zones (fast-track planning for areas of national importance); social and affordable housing quotas on new developments (Part V); derelict sites and vacant sites levies; public transport investment; new towns; regeneration companies with community participation.

Strategic urban plans (national setting)
- [ ] P1 Advisory Regional Plan for Dublin (Myles Wright, 1967): western new towns. Effectiveness: housed rapid growth, but towns were built before services, jobs and transport (Tallaght's Luas and shopping centre came decades later), causing sprawl and absence of community.
- [ ] P2 National Spatial Strategy (2002): gateways and hubs to balance growth away from Dublin. Effectiveness: weakened by too many designated centres, the 2003 decentralisation of civil servants to towns not in the plan, and the property boom; Dublin continued to sprawl; replaced in 2018.
- [ ] P3 National Planning Framework (Project Ireland 2040, 2018): about 1 million more people by 2040; half of growth in the five cities; compact growth on brownfield and infill land; Regional Spatial and Economic Strategies; Urban Regeneration and Development Fund. Effectiveness: too early to judge fully; housing supply has lagged.
- [ ] P4 Housing policy: Housing for All (2021); Rent Pressure Zones (2016); Part V social and affordable housing. Effectiveness: completions rose but have stayed below need; record homelessness; high rents.
- [ ] P5 Transport strategy: Luas, Luas Cross City, DART, Port Tunnel, BusConnects, Dublin Bikes, cycle lanes, the GDA Transport Strategy 2022–2042, planned MetroLink. Effectiveness: Luas and the Port Tunnel succeeded; MetroLink repeatedly delayed (Metro North shelved in 2011); congestion persists because land use is still dispersed.
- [ ] P6 Pollution policy: smoky coal ban (1990, nationwide 2022), plastic bag levy, Ringsend upgrade, Clean Air Strategy (2023). Effectiveness: smoke and smog cured, but traffic nitrogen dioxide and particulates from solid fuel remain problems.

Urban renewal and redevelopment case studies (each a block: problem, actions with dates, results, criticisms)
- [ ] R1 Dublin Docklands: derelict docks after containerisation; Urban Renewal Act 1986 tax incentives; Custom House Docks Development Authority; IFSC (1987); Dublin Docklands Development Authority (1997–2016); flagship projects; "Silicon Docks" tech firms; Luas extension; Strategic Development Zone (2014). Results: tens of thousands of jobs (no number), new homes, culture venues. Criticisms: early schemes favoured offices and expensive apartments; local residents got few of the jobs; social housing slow; gentrification; the DDDA was damaged by its role in the Irish Glass Bottle site purchase (2006) during the property bubble.
- [ ] R2 Temple Bar: area bought up by CIÉ for a planned bus station; artists and small businesses moved in on short leases; the bus plan was dropped; Temple Bar Properties (1991) regenerated it as a cultural quarter, with Dublin as European City of Culture 1991. Results: cultural venues, restored buildings, tourism. Criticisms: became dominated by pubs and night-time tourism; few permanent residents.
- [ ] R3 Ballymun: 1960s high-rise flats; isolation, poor maintenance, unemployment, drugs; Ballymun Regeneration Ltd (1997); towers demolished 2004–2015; new low-rise homes, Main Street, arts centre, IKEA. Effectiveness: housing quality improved and residents were involved; but the recession stalled the town centre, promised Metro North was shelved, unemployment stayed high.
- [ ] R4 Limerick Regeneration: estates of concentrated disadvantage; the 2007 Fitzgerald report; Limerick Regeneration agencies (2007), later integrated into Limerick City and County Council; demolition, new homes, social and economic programmes. Criticisms: slow, demolition outpaced rebuilding, populations of the estates fell.
- [ ] R5 Adamstown, South Dublin: a Strategic Development Zone (2001) planned as a new suburb with its own railway station and with schools and services built in phases alongside housing. Effectiveness: model of planned phasing, but the 2008 crash halted building for years.
- [ ] R6 Living City Initiative (2015) and smaller-city renewal (tax relief to restore old buildings in the centres of Dublin, Cork, Limerick, Galway, Waterford, Kilkenny); Cork Docklands as the next large regeneration (one bullet each).
- [ ] R7 London Docklands (developed-world case): docks closed 1960s–80s; London Docklands Development Corporation (1981–1998), an unelected body with planning powers and an Enterprise Zone; Canary Wharf; Docklands Light Railway; London City Airport; Jubilee Line extension. Results: a second financial centre, jobs and housing. Criticisms: top-down, bypassed local councils; jobs went to commuters; locals priced out; transport came late.
- [ ] R8 Paris (one bullet, facts from `geo-region-european-s1`): the five new towns took growth from the city; the grands ensembles became problem estates; the Grand Paris Express aims to link the banlieues.

Overall evaluation
- [ ] E1 Conclusions for a 30-mark answer: planning works best when housing, jobs, services and transport are delivered together and residents are involved; flagship property-led schemes create jobs and buildings but not always benefits for local people; national plans are undermined when other policies (tax incentives, decentralisation) pull the other way.

## 3. Traps

- **Renewal ≠ redevelopment.** Renewal improves what is there and keeps the community; redevelopment clears and rebuilds. Ballymun and Limerick combined both. The Docklands was mainly redevelopment.
- **The IFSC was set up in 1987; the DDDA in 1997.** Don't merge them into one date.
- The Irish Glass Bottle figure (about €412 million, 2006) is the only checked Docklands money figure. Give no job totals, rents or apartment prices.
- Homelessness: only "over 15,000 in emergency accommodation (2025)". No other housing statistics.
- NPF figures must match the other packages (half of growth in five cities; at least 50% growth in each regional city by 2040). Don't give a Dublin-specific NPF growth target.
- MetroLink, Luas Cork and BusConnects' later phases are **planned/proposed**: no opening dates or costs.
- London Docklands: the corporation's dates are 1981–1998. Don't claim Canary Wharf is the tallest building in the UK (it no longer is).
- Problems themselves (causes and effects of congestion, decay, sprawl, heritage loss, pollution) are s5. Here: strategies and how effective they were. Cities of the future are s8; developing-world solutions (site and service, upgrading, Curitiba) are s7.
- Past-paper answers invent figures and policies (e.g. a statutory "Dublin Green Belt"). Ireland has no statutory Dublin green belt.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Higher 30 marks: the effectiveness of urban planning strategies in solving two of housing, transport, pollution, with urban areas studied.
- Higher 30 marks: the effectiveness of urban renewal in solving urban problems (Docklands, Ballymun, Temple Bar, Limerick).
- Ordinary: explain briefly what is meant by urban renewal / urban redevelopment; describe one solution to urban sprawl; measures to solve traffic congestion.
