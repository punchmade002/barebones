# Package: geo-urban-s1 — Pre-historic and historic settlement

- **loId:** `geo-urban-s1` · **chapterId:** `geo-urban` · **syllabusRef:** 5.4
- **Syllabus:** Statement: "Settlements can be identified in relation to site, situation and
  function." Students should study "the locational characteristics for • pre-historic and

- **Scope:** Elective Unit 5 (Human). Higher Level 30-mark answers need about 15 SRPs, so each

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Key ideas
- [ ] K1 Site (the actual land the settlement is built on: e.g. dry point, wet point, defensive hill, river loop, bridging point, sheltered harbour) vs situation (its position relative to the surrounding area: routes, resources, hinterland). Function (the economic and social purpose of a settlement). Define all three, with one-line Irish examples.
- [ ] K2 Locational factors common to all periods: water supply; defence; fertile, well-drained land; shelter; routeways and fords/bridging points; resources (fish, timber, stone); religious or political importance. Each later block applies these to one period.

Phases of settlement in Ireland (each a block: dates, people, site factors, what survives, examples)
- [ ] P1 Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age) hunter-gatherers: first people in Ireland; temporary camps on coasts, lakeshores and river banks for fishing and gathering; Mount Sandel the earliest known house site.
- [ ] P2 Neolithic (New Stone Age) first farmers: forest clearance, fields, permanent farmsteads; megalithic tombs (court, portal/dolmen, passage, wedge tombs) on high, visible ground; the Céide Fields; Brú na Bóinne.
- [ ] P3 Bronze Age and Iron Age: metalworking; standing stones, stone circles, fulachta fia (cooking sites beside streams); hillforts and promontory forts for defence; crannógs (artificial islands in lakes); royal sites (Tara, Navan Fort). Dún Aonghasa as a cliff-edge stone fort.
- [ ] P4 Early Christian (early medieval) period, from the 5th century: ringforts (rath, lios; stone versions cashel, caher, dún) as dispersed defended farmsteads on well-drained hillsides; souterrains; monasteries as the first proto-towns (sited on routeways, rivers, eskers, or remote islands); round towers, high crosses, holy wells; place-name evidence (kil/cill, rath, lis, dun).
- [ ] P5 Viking settlement, from the 9th century: first raids, then fortified ship bases (longphorts) that became Ireland's first true towns; sited on sheltered estuaries and harbours at river mouths for trade and access by sea; Dublin, Waterford, Wexford, Limerick, Cork; Norse place names.
- [ ] P6 Norman (Anglo-Norman) settlement, from 1169: conquest of the east and south; motte and bailey first, then stone castles; walled towns with gates, market squares, charters, burgage plots, parish churches and abbeys; sited at bridging points and on navigable rivers in fertile lowlands, for defence and control of land and trade; manor villages. Examples: Kilkenny, Trim, Carrickfergus, Athenry, Fethard.
- [ ] P7 Later medieval: tower houses (fortified homes of local lords, 15th–16th centuries); Gaelic Ireland largely without towns.
- [ ] P8 Plantation towns, 16th–17th centuries: Laois–Offaly, Munster and Ulster plantations; planned towns with a central square or "diamond", a wide main street, a market house and an Established (Protestant) church; walled Derry.
- [ ] P9 18th-century landlord (estate) towns and Georgian towns: improving landlords laid out planned towns beside their demesnes, with wide streets, squares, malls and market houses (Westport, Birr, Kenmare, Adare); Georgian Dublin and Limerick (wide streets, squares, red-brick terraces).
- [ ] P10 19th century: canals and railways (first railway 1834) created railway junction towns and seaside resorts; mills and industrial villages; workhouses after 1838; the Famine (1845–49) and emigration emptied much of the rural west; Belfast's industrial growth.
- [ ] P11 20th and 21st centuries: State-planned new towns (Shannon; Dublin's western new towns); suburban expansion and commuter towns. One block only; urban growth and planning are taught in s4–s6.
- [ ] P12 How historic phases show in a modern Irish town today (the "historic development of Irish towns" setting): street pattern, walls and gates, castle, cathedral, market square, Georgian terraces, mills, railway station. Use one worked town: Kilkenny (Norman core, cathedral, Medieval Mile) or Galway (Norman walled port, merchant houses, Spanish Arch). The changing functions of the same town over time are in s3; don't duplicate.

## 3. Traps

- **Ringforts are early Christian (early medieval) farmsteads**, not Norman, not Viking and not prehistoric (same rule as `geo-mapwork-s1`). Mottes and walled towns are Norman. Fulachta fia are Bronze Age. Megalithic tombs are mainly Neolithic.
- **The Vikings founded Ireland's first towns; monasteries were proto-towns, not towns.** Don't say the Normans founded Dublin, Waterford or Limerick: they captured and expanded Viking towns.
- Derry's walls are the only **complete** city walls; Athenry and Fethard have among the most complete **town** walls. Don't swap these.
- Don't give construction dates for individual ringforts, tower houses or round towers, a ringfort total beyond "over 40,000", or populations for any historic town.
- Don't use towns from past-paper OS maps or aerial photographs (Clonmel, Foxford, Bantry, Sligo, Oughterard, Cavan) as worked map examples or say why they developed "at this location". Clonmel appears only as the Bianconi fact in P10.
- Tara and Navan Fort are royal/ceremonial sites, not towns.
- The OS-map method (naming antiquities from the legend, six-figure grid references, three phases on a map) is in `geo-mapwork-s1`; historical maps as sources are in `geo-mapwork-s2`. Give only a one-line cross-reference.
- Rural settlement patterns (dispersed, clustered, linear, clachans) are s2. Urban hierarchy and changing functions are s3. Dublin's full urban development (Georgian to Docklands) is in `geo-region-ireland-s1`; here only Dublin's Viking founding and the Wide Streets Commissioners.
- Don't copy past-paper model answers (some invent dates and figures for named towns).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Name, locate on an OS map and explain three phases of historical settlement (prehistoric, early Christian, Norman, plantation or later). The explanation needs the period, the people and the reason for the site (map skill in `geo-mapwork-s1`; content here).
- Explain reasons why a town developed at its location (bridging point, route focus, defence, river, coast or harbour, fertile hinterland, religious site). The reasons are the same locational characteristics as K2–P9.
- Higher 30 marks: the locational characteristics of pre-historic and historic settlements; the historic development of an Irish town.
- Ordinary: match a historic feature to its period; name two historic settlement features.
