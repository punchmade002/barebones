# Package: geo-region-european-s1 — A Core European Region: the Paris Basin

- **loId:** `geo-region-european-s1` · **chapterId:** `geo-region-european` · **syllabusRef:** 2.2
- **Syllabus:** "two contrasting European regions. Students can choose one region from
  Scandinavia and/or one from western/central Europe (including the United Kingdom) and / or
  one from the Mediterranean. The study of the region should include – physical processes e.g.
  climate, soils, relief, and drainage – economic processes - primary activities e.g.
  agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining/energy - secondary activities e.g. patterns in
  manufacturing activities - tertiary activities, e.g. transport and tourism. – human
  processes, e.g. language, religion, urban and rural development and population dynamics."
  Statement: "The study of regions show how economic, human, and physical processes interact in
  a particular area." The Paris Basin is the western/central European (core) choice; it pairs
  with the Mezzogiorno in `geo-region-european-s2`.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.
- **Scale:** each of the four headings (physical; primary; secondary; tertiary; human) must
  carry a 30-mark Higher answer of about 15 SRPs. Write one block per must-cover group, dense
  with the checked facts below, and always explain *why* (the interaction of physical,
  economic and human processes), not just *what*.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The region
- [ ] R1 Location and extent: a large, low sedimentary basin in northern France, centred on Paris, open to the English Channel in the north-west and ringed by older uplands (section 2). It covers several French administrative regions; Île-de-France (the Paris region) is its core.
- [ ] R2 Why it is a core region: capital-city functions, the largest concentration of population, wealth, industry and services in France, fertile farmland, the focus of the national transport network, and a central position in the EU. One block; the details follow under each heading.
- [ ] R3 Sketch-map skill: what to show and name on an outline map of the region (river, two urban centres, relief feature, agricultural or industrial area). List the checked items students can draw; no grid references, no past-paper wording.

Physical processes
- [ ] P1 Climate: transitional between cool temperate oceanic in the west and more continental in the east (annual temperature range increases eastwards; colder winters, warmer summers inland); moderate rainfall spread through the year; Paris figures from section 2. Link climate to farming (long growing season, warm summers ripen cereals and grapes; dry harvest weather).
- [ ] P2 Relief and geology: a saucer-shaped basin of sedimentary layers (limestone, chalk, clay, sands) dipping gently towards Paris; youngest rocks at the centre, older at the rim; outward-facing escarpments (cuestas) with clay vales between on the eastern side; mostly low and gently rolling. Point to `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s4` for how cuestas form; don't re-teach it.
- [ ] P3 Soils: limon (fine, wind-blown loess-type loam) on the plateaux such as the Beauce and Brie, among the most fertile soils in Europe; thin calcareous soils on chalk (Champagne); heavier, wetter clay soils in the vales and the west, suited to grass. How soils were improved (fertilisers turned the dry chalk plain of Champagne into productive arable land).
- [ ] P4 Drainage: the Seine and its tributaries (Marne, Oise, Aube, Yonne) converge on Paris; gentle gradients make them navigable; the lower Seine meanders to its estuary at Le Havre; the Loire crosses the south of the region at Orléans. Permeable chalk and limestone store groundwater (aquifers) and give dry valleys. Flooding of the Seine (1910, 2016) and upstream regulating reservoirs.

Economic processes: primary
- [ ] A1 Agriculture overview: France is the EU's largest agricultural producer; the Paris Basin is its main arable region. Physical factors (relief, limon soils, climate) explained as advantages.
- [ ] A2 Cereal farming in the Beauce (wheat, barley, maize; "granary of France"), plus sugar beet and oilseed rape on the plateaux; large, highly mechanised farms with high yields.
- [ ] A3 Viticulture in Champagne: chalk soils, slopes of the escarpment, protected name; Reims and Épernay; value added by the Champagne houses (link to secondary).
- [ ] A4 Dairy and pastoral farming on wetter clay soils (Normandy in the west; Brie); market gardening near Paris for the huge urban market.
- [ ] A5 Human and economic factors in farming: the Paris market and Rungis; remembrement (consolidation of small scattered plots into large farms); co-operatives and agribusiness; the CAP (France receives the largest share of CAP spending); rural depopulation as farms mechanised.
- [ ] A6 Forestry: large forests on the poorer sandy soils near Paris, used for timber and recreation.
- [ ] A7 Fishing: minor in this region; one bullet saying so, no named port.
- [ ] A8 Energy: nuclear power dominates French electricity; named stations on the Seine, the Loire and the Normandy coast; oil refining and petrochemicals at the Seine estuary (imported crude). Few local fossil fuels.

Economic processes: secondary
- [ ] M1 Why manufacturing concentrated in and around Paris: the largest market and labour force, capital and head offices, the hub of road, rail, river and air transport, government and research.
- [ ] M2 Car industry: Renault and PSA (now Stellantis) plants in the Paris region; the closure of Renault's Boulogne-Billancourt plant (1992) as an example of industry leaving the inner city for suburban and provincial sites.
- [ ] M3 The Lower Seine industrial axis: Le Havre and Rouen ports, oil refining and petrochemicals; HAROPA port grouping (Le Havre, Rouen, Paris).
- [ ] M4 Food and drink processing: sugar beet, cereals, Champagne production.
- [ ] M5 High-technology and research: the Paris-Saclay cluster south-west of Paris.
- [ ] M6 Government policy changing the pattern: decentralisation from the 1950s–60s restricting new industry in Paris and steering it to the provinces; DATAR (1963); the eight métropoles d'équilibre (1964); the five new towns (villes nouvelles) around Paris. Results: Paris lost manufacturing share but kept head offices and research.
- [ ] M7 Factors questions: be ready to explain secondary activity by labour, transport, raw materials, markets, infrastructure and government policy (each factor gets its own bullet group in the notes, using the facts above).

Economic processes: tertiary
- [ ] T1 Paris as a primate city and service centre: government, finance, head offices, universities, international organisations (OECD, UNESCO); La Défense business district; Île-de-France's share of French population and output.
- [ ] T2 Tourism: why tourists come (heritage, culture, shopping, events, theme parks, Champagne and the countryside); named attractions; Disneyland Paris; Paris 2024 Olympic and Paralympic Games; problems (congestion, seasonality, pressure on sites).
- [ ] T3 Transport: radial network centred on Paris (roads, rail); TGV high-speed rail (first line 1981); Eurostar through the Channel Tunnel (1994); Charles de Gaulle and Orly airports; the Seine waterway and Paris river port; the Périphérique; RER and the Grand Paris Express; importance of transport to the region's economic development.
- [ ] T4 Rungis wholesale market as a link between primary and tertiary activity (one bullet; don't repeat A5).

Human processes
- [ ] H1 Population distribution: the dense core of Île-de-France and the City of Paris vs low rural densities in the outer basin; reasons (jobs, services, transport vs farm mechanisation). Exams ask "account for population distribution" in a European region.
- [ ] H2 Population dynamics: rural-to-urban migration into Paris ("Paris and the French desert", 1947); a young, in-migrant age structure in the Paris region; out-migration of retirees; immigration from former colonies (Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia) and southern Europe after the Second World War.
- [ ] H3 Language: French; standard French grew from the speech of the Paris region; immigrant languages in the suburbs. No figures.
- [ ] H4 Religion: traditionally Roman Catholic; the 1905 law separating church and state (laïcité); a large Muslim minority, mainly of North African origin; secularisation.
- [ ] H5 Culture as a whole (for "culture of a region" questions: religion, language, politics, music/dance, sport): centralised politics; major sporting events and clubs; Paris as a centre of art, fashion and cuisine. One block, facts from section 2.
- [ ] H6 Urban development of Paris: growth on the Seine crossing (Île de la Cité); Haussmann's rebuilding (1853–1870); the Périphérique; post-war grands ensembles in the banlieues; the 1965 plan and five new towns; La Défense; Métropole du Grand Paris (2016); the Grand Paris Express.
- [ ] H7 Urban problems: congestion, high housing costs, social segregation and deprivation in the banlieues (Seine-Saint-Denis; the 2005 riots), urban sprawl.
- [ ] H8 Factors in urban growth (exams ask "factors which influenced the development of one urban area"): site (Seine crossing, island), situation (centre of the basin, river convergence), transport, administration (capital), primary activity (rich farmland hinterland), industry and services. One block mapping each factor to Paris.
- [ ] H9 Rural development: larger farms and fewer farmers, rural depopulation in the outer basin (part of the "diagonale du vide" idea), commuter villages near Paris, second homes and rural tourism.

## 3. Traps

- The Vosges, Ardennes, Armorican Massif and Massif Central are the **rim** of the basin, not uplands inside it. A sketch map may show them on the edge, but say they bound the region. Several past-paper model answers place the Vosges "in" the region; avoid that wording. The Beauce is the safest named lowland.
- Reims is not on the Marne (Épernay is). Le Havre is at the Seine's mouth, Rouen upstream of it. Lyon is not in the Paris Basin.
- The "Blue Banana" core axis (south-east England to northern Italy) does **not** include Paris; don't use it to explain the Paris Basin's core status.
- Lorraine (iron ore, steel) and the Nord coalfield (Lille) lie on the margins of the basin and are regions of industrial decline. Don't present them as the core of the Paris Basin's industry.
- Airbus's main assembly is in Toulouse, not the Paris Basin. Don't use it.
- Fishing: don't name a fishing port; none is checked for this region.
- Don't give Champagne bottle numbers, farm-size averages, tourist totals or GDP per head figures: none are checked. Don't copy any figures from past-paper answers (they include invented statistics).
- Escarpment formation and the geology of cuestas belong to `geo-volcanoes-earthquakes-s4`; describe the relief and link it to land use only.
- The general idea of core and peripheral regions and French départements as administrative regions belongs to `geo-region-concept`. The Mezzogiorno (peripheral contrast) is `geo-region-european-s2`; the EU's history is `geo-region-european-s4`; CAP and EU policy effects on **Ireland** belong to `geo-economic-eu`.
- Don't use the 2026 specification's terms or topics.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Outline/sketch map of a European region (not in Ireland): a named river, two named urban centres, a named relief feature (upland or lowland), a named agricultural or industrial region. Expected: Seine; Paris and Rouen/Le Havre/Reims; the Beauce or an edging upland; the Beauce (cereals) or Champagne (vines).
- 30 marks: development of agriculture with reference to two of relief, climate, soils, markets.
- 30 marks: impact of climate (or relief) on primary activity in two contrasting European regions (pairs with s2).
- 30 marks: development of secondary activity with reference to two of labour, transport, raw materials, markets, infrastructure, government policy.
- 30 marks: development of one tertiary activity (tourism or transport); importance of transport to economic development in two contrasting regions.
- 30 marks: factors in the growth/development of one urban area (Paris); population distribution throughout the region.
- Ordinary 40 marks: name a primary/secondary activity, explain the region's advantages for it, describe one challenge it faces.
- Ordinary 30 marks: the culture of a region (religion, language, politics, music/dance, sport/games).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Account for the development of agriculture in a European region (not in Ireland) that you have studied, with reference to relief and climate." Model answer: Paris Basin only; about 15 SRPs split between relief/soils (low, gently rolling basin, limon, mechanisation, large farms, Beauce cereals, Champagne chalk slopes) and climate (Paris temperatures and rainfall, warm summers, dry harvests, eastward continentality, northern limit of vines).
