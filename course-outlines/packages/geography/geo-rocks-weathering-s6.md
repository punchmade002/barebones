## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Conditions
- [ ] C1 Aeolian processes: erosion, transport and deposition by wind. Most effective in hot and cold deserts and on coasts, where there is loose, dry, fine material, little vegetation to bind it, and strong winds.
- [ ] C2 Why wind is weaker than water or ice: air is far less dense, so wind can move only sand and finer particles, and only close to the ground.

Processes of wind erosion
- [ ] E1 Deflation: wind lifts and blows away loose, dry, fine particles (dust, silt, sand), lowering the land surface.
- [ ] E2 Abrasion (sandblasting): sand grains carried by the wind strike rock surfaces and wear them away. Most effective within about a metre or two of the ground, where most sand is carried.
- [ ] E3 Attrition: wind-blown grains collide with each other and become smaller, rounder and frosted.

Transport
- [ ] T1 Suspension: fine dust carried high in the air, sometimes for thousands of kilometres (dust storms).
- [ ] T2 Saltation: sand grains bounce along the surface in short hops; most wind-blown sand moves this way.
- [ ] T3 Surface creep: larger grains are pushed and rolled along by the impact of saltating grains.

Landforms of wind erosion (each: what it is, formation in stages, example where given, diagram cue)
- [ ] L1 Deflation hollow: a depression formed where deflation removes loose material down to the water table or to a resistant layer.
- [ ] L2 Desert pavement (reg): a surface of closely packed stones left behind after deflation has blown away the fine material between them, protecting the ground below.
- [ ] L3 Ventifacts: stones with flat, polished faces and sharp edges cut by sandblasting from the prevailing wind direction.
- [ ] L4 Yardangs: long, streamlined ridges of rock lying parallel to the prevailing wind, separated by grooves abraded in softer rock.
- [ ] L5 Zeugens: tabular, flat-topped ridges where a hard cap rock overlies softer rock; abrasion cuts down along joints in the cap and undercuts the soft rock beneath. They form where the rock layers are horizontal and the joints run across the wind.
- [ ] L6 Mushroom (pedestal) rocks: rock pillars undercut near the base by sandblasting, which is concentrated near the ground, leaving a wider top.

Wind deposition (one short block)
- [ ] D1 Deposition occurs when wind speed drops or meets an obstacle. Desert landscapes: erg (sand sea), reg (stony desert), hamada (bare rock).
- [ ] D2 Barchan dunes: crescent-shaped dunes, with horns pointing downwind; gentle windward slope, steep slip face on the lee side; they migrate downwind.
- [ ] D3 Seif (linear) dunes: long ridges roughly parallel to the dominant wind direction, formed where winds blow from two directions.
- [ ] D4 Loess: thick deposits of fine wind-blown dust, often from the edges of former ice sheets or deserts; it forms very fertile soils.

Human link and Ireland
- [ ] H1 Wind erosion of soil where vegetation is removed: the Dust Bowl. One block; see the Trap on duplication with s7.
- [ ] H2 In Ireland, wind erosion is limited by the damp climate and vegetation cover. The main aeolian landforms are coastal sand dunes (taught in `geo-sea-s1`) and blowouts where dune vegetation is damaged (taught in `geo-sea-s2`). Name them in one bullet.

## 3. Traps (known errors)

- Wind abrasion is concentrated near the ground; mushroom rocks are undercut at the base, not the top.
- Barchan horns point downwind; the slip face is on the sheltered (lee) side.
- Yardangs are parallel to the wind; zeugens have a hard cap on soft rock. Don't swap them.
- Do not give wind speeds, dune heights or depths: none are checked.
- Coastal sand dune formation and marram grass are taught in `geo-sea-s1`; dune damage and management in `geo-sea-s2`. One bullet here, no formation stages.
- The Dust Bowl appears in `geo-rocks-weathering-s7` as an overcropping case study. Here give it at most two bullets, as an example of wind erosion only; the full case study (causes, effects, responses) is in s7.
- Salt crystallisation and exfoliation in deserts are weathering, taught in `geo-rocks-weathering-s3`; don't repeat them.
