## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Land-use zones in the modern city
- [ ] Z1 Land-use zones: CBD (central business district); zone in transition (inner city, mixed old housing, warehouses, small industry, decay and renewal); inner residential (older terraced housing); suburbs (lower density, semi-detached, newer); industrial zones (port, inner-city, suburban industrial estates and business parks); open space and recreation; transport land (roads, rail, port, airport); rural–urban fringe.
- [ ] Z2 The CBD in detail: highest land values; tall buildings; department stores, chain stores and specialist shops; offices, banks, head offices; government and cultural buildings; few residents; pedestrian streets; daytime crowding and congestion; vertical zoning (shops on ground floor, offices above). Dublin's CBD (Grafton Street, Henry Street, O'Connell Street).
- [ ] Z3 Models of urban land use (each: what it shows, its city, one strength and one limitation; a diagram cue): Burgess concentric zone model (1925, Chicago); Hoyt sector model (1939: sectors along transport routes, high-class residential sector away from industry); Harris and Ullman multiple nuclei model (1945: several centres, e.g. suburban shopping centres, airport, business parks). Dublin fits multiple nuclei and sectors better than concentric rings.
- [ ] Z4 Dublin's land-use zones as the Irish city case study: CBD on both sides of the Liffey; Georgian core now offices; inner-city docklands and north inner city (transition, renewal); older inner suburbs; outer suburbs and the western new towns; industrial and business parks along the M50; the port and airport; open space (Phoenix Park, Dublin Mountains, Dublin Bay).
- [ ] Z5 Paris as the world city: CBD and government quarter in the inner arrondissements; La Défense office district; Haussmann's boulevards; banlieues with grands ensembles; five new towns; the Périphérique as the boundary of the inner city.

Changes in land use and planning issues
- [ ] M1 Industrial land use moving out: old industry beside the port, canals and railways in the inner city (brewing, biscuits, distilling) closed or moved to suburban industrial estates and business parks near motorways; reasons (space, cheaper land, road access, less congestion, planning policy, new industries need clean modern sites); inner-city brownfield sites then redeveloped.
- [ ] M2 Residential land use moving out: inner-city tenements cleared (20th century); new suburbs and new towns; sprawl into commuter counties; now a return to inner-city apartment living (Docklands, regeneration). Issues: density, height, social mix.
- [ ] M3 Commercial land use moving out: suburban shopping centres and retail parks near motorway junctions (car access, free parking, cheap land); out-of-town office parks; effects on the city centre (vacant units, loss of trade) and responses (pedestrianisation, regeneration, limits on retail park size). Paris: La Défense, Renault's Boulogne-Billancourt closure as industry leaving the inner city.
- [ ] M4 Planning issues arising: zoning; brownfield vs greenfield development; height and density; transport linking new zones; mixing land uses; conflict between business, residents and heritage.

Land values and social stratification
- [ ] V1 Land values (bid-rent theory, Alonso, 1964): accessibility is greatest at the centre, so competition makes land dearest there (the peak land value intersection, PLVI); values fall with distance; commerce outbids industry, industry outbids housing; secondary peaks at suburban centres, ring-road junctions and along main roads. Effects: tall buildings and intensive use at the centre; large, low-density uses at the edge.
- [ ] V2 Social stratification (segregation): residential areas separate by income, class, ethnicity, age and family stage; causes (land and house prices, social housing allocation, planning, transport, choice and discrimination); results (affluent areas vs areas of concentrated disadvantage; gated developments; gentrification of inner-city areas).
- [ ] V3 Dublin example: affluent south-east suburbs vs lower-income areas in parts of the north inner city and some outer social-housing estates; Ballymun as a case of concentrated social housing (regeneration in s6); gentrification around the Docklands.
- [ ] V4 Paris example: wealthy western districts vs poorer north-eastern banlieues (Seine-Saint-Denis); immigrant concentration in grands ensembles; the 2005 riots.

The expansion of cities and pressure on rural land use
- [ ] E1 Causes: population growth and in-migration; demand for cheaper, larger houses; car ownership and motorways; high city house prices; jobs moving to the suburbs.
- [ ] E2 Pressures on rural land at the rural–urban fringe: loss of farmland; rising land prices encourage farmers to sell; hobby farms and horse paddocks; commuter villages with new estates; roads, retail parks, business parks, data centres, landfill and quarries; recreation (golf courses); loss of habitats and hedgerows; flooding from paved surfaces; conflicts between farmers, residents and developers; villages losing their character and community.
- [ ] E3 Dublin example: sprawl into Meath, Kildare and Wicklow; commuter towns; one-off housing in the commuter belt; data centres on the city edge.
- [ ] E4 Responses: green belts (London's Metropolitan Green Belt), new towns (Britain's New Towns Act 1946; Paris's five new towns; Dublin's western new towns), compact growth and brownfield targets (NPF, in s6), development plans zoning land.

## 3. Traps

- **Burgess = concentric (1925, Chicago); Hoyt = sector (1939); Harris and Ullman = multiple nuclei (1945).** Don't swap names or dates, and don't attribute any model to Christaller (s3).
- Don't present a model as describing Dublin exactly; say how Dublin fits or departs from it.
- Gentrification is when better-off people move into and renovate a poorer inner-city area, displacing lower-income residents; don't confuse it with urban renewal (rebuilding) or redevelopment (clearance and new building), which are s6.
- No land values, rents, house prices or density figures are checked: none in the notes.
- Paris facts must match `geo-region-european-s1` exactly. Don't add arrondissement-level income figures.
- Don't name Dublin estates as deprived other than Ballymun (which is named for its regeneration) and the general "north inner city". Avoid stigmatising named places.
- Urban sprawl and absence of community as **problems** belong to s5; planning strategies and their effectiveness (NPF, Docklands, Ballymun regeneration) to s6. Here: the land-use pattern, why uses moved, land values, segregation and rural-fringe pressure. Rural ribbon development is s2.
- Don't copy past-paper figures (e.g. Dublin Port's land area from a stimulus article).
