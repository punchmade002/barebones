# Package: geo-economic-environment-s3 — Environmental pollution

- **loId:** `geo-economic-environment-s3` · **chapterId:** `geo-economic-environment` · **syllabusRef:** 4.5
- **Syllabus:** students should study "environmental pollution at a local/national and global
  scale". International setting: "Relevant global issues, e.g. global warming." Studied areas,
  each a self-contained case study (cause, effects, how it could be reduced) because Ordinary
  questions ask for "one example of environmental pollution caused by economic activity":
  **global warming** (global), **water pollution from agriculture in Ireland** (national/local),
  **marine oil pollution** (global, with the Exxon Valdez and Prestige), **plastic and waste
  pollution** (local and global), and **ozone depletion** (global, shorter).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Ideas
- [ ] D1 Pollution: the release by human activity of substances or energy (heat, noise, light) into the environment in amounts that harm living things, health or property.
- [ ] D2 Types: air, water (fresh and marine), land/soil, noise, visual.
- [ ] D3 Scales: local (one river, one town), national (a country's water quality), global (the whole atmosphere or ocean). Pollution does not respect borders (transboundary pollution; acid rain is the European example, taught in "Burning fossil fuels and using alternative energy sources").
- [ ] D4 Point sources (a factory pipe, a sewage outfall) vs diffuse sources (runoff from many farms, traffic).
- [ ] D5 Which economic activities pollute: agriculture, manufacturing, energy generation, transport, mining, tourism, households and urban waste.

Global scale: global warming (syllabus setting; full 30-mark case study)
- [ ] W1 The natural greenhouse effect: greenhouse gases let incoming short-wave solar radiation through but absorb outgoing long-wave radiation, keeping the earth habitable.
- [ ] W2 The enhanced greenhouse effect: human activity adds greenhouse gases, trapping more heat.
- [ ] W3 Sources by gas: CO2 (burning fossil fuels, deforestation, cement); methane (cattle and sheep, rice paddies, landfill, gas leaks); nitrous oxide (nitrogen fertilisers, slurry); CFCs and other fluorinated gases.
- [ ] W4 Evidence: rising CO2 concentration; rising global temperature; record years; retreating glaciers and shrinking Arctic sea ice; rising sea level.
- [ ] W5 Global effects: sea-level rise (thermal expansion and melting land ice) threatening low-lying countries and deltas; more intense heatwaves, droughts, floods and storms; glacier retreat threatening water supply; coral bleaching; species shifts and extinctions; crop failures in vulnerable regions; climate refugees.
- [ ] W6 Effects on Ireland: warmer temperatures; wetter winters and more flooding; risk of summer drought; stronger storms and coastal erosion; changes to farming and to marine species; threat to low-lying coastal towns.
- [ ] W7 Ireland's emissions profile: agriculture is the largest source (over a third), then transport and energy; high emissions per person for an EU country.
- [ ] W8 Global responses: UNFCCC (1992, Rio); Kyoto Protocol (1997); Paris Agreement (2015) aims; annual COP meetings.
- [ ] W9 EU and Irish responses: EU Emissions Trading System; European Green Deal (climate neutral by 2050; 55% cut by 2030); Ireland's Climate Act 2021 (51% by 2030, net zero by 2050), carbon tax, Climate Action Plan (renewables, retrofitting, electric vehicles, agricultural measures).
- [ ] W10 How individuals and communities can reduce emissions: energy efficiency, insulation, public transport, cycling, less food waste, renewable heating.

National/local scale: water pollution from agriculture in Ireland (full case study)
- [ ] N1 Cause: nitrogen and phosphorus from slurry, manure and chemical fertilisers wash off fields into rivers and lakes, especially after heavy rain or when spread on wet or frozen ground; silage effluent; also sewage from towns and septic tanks.
- [ ] N2 Process (eutrophication): nutrients feed rapid growth of algae (algal blooms); when the algae die, bacteria decomposing them use up the dissolved oxygen; fish and other life suffocate (fish kills); water turns green and smells; some blue-green algae are toxic.
- [ ] N3 Effects: loss of fish and other wildlife; damage to angling and tourism; drinking water needs costly treatment; nitrates in groundwater; coastal waters affected (estuaries, algal mats).
- [ ] N4 Scale in Ireland: EPA findings on water quality; agriculture the main pressure, then urban wastewater; the dairy expansion since 2015 raised nitrogen levels in the south and east.
- [ ] N5 Example: Lough Neagh's toxic blue-green algal bloom in 2023.
- [ ] N6 Reduction: EU Nitrates Directive (1991) and Ireland's Nitrates Action Programme (closed periods for spreading slurry, limits on nitrogen per hectare, slurry storage, buffer strips beside watercourses); EU Water Framework Directive (2000) targets; upgrading of urban wastewater treatment; EPA monitoring; farm advisory programmes.

Global scale: marine oil pollution
- [ ] O1 Causes: tanker accidents, drilling blow-outs, illegal tank washing at sea, pipeline leaks.
- [ ] O2 Exxon Valdez (1989) case: cause, effects, response (US Oil Pollution Act 1990, double hulls).
- [ ] O3 Prestige (2002), off Galicia, Spain: cause, effects on fishing and coast.
- [ ] O4 Deepwater Horizon (2010): one or two lines reusing `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`.
- [ ] O5 Effects in general: oiled seabirds and mammals, smothered shorelines, fishing bans, tourism losses; clean-up methods (booms, skimmers, dispersants) and prevention (double-hulled tankers, routing ships away from coasts).

Local and global: plastic and waste pollution
- [ ] L1 Causes: single-use packaging, litter, illegal dumping, landfill; plastics take hundreds of years to break down; microplastics.
- [ ] L2 Effects: litter in towns and countryside; wildlife entanglement and ingestion; ocean garbage patches; microplastics in food chains; landfill leachate and methane.
- [ ] L3 Irish responses: plastic bag levy (2002); deposit return scheme (2024); recycling and the waste hierarchy (reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, dispose); EPA waste licensing; Dublin Waste-to-Energy incinerator at Poolbeg.

Global scale: ozone depletion (shorter block)
- [ ] Z1 CFCs (from aerosols, fridges, air-conditioning) destroyed stratospheric ozone, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation; Antarctic ozone hole; effects (skin cancer, eye cataracts, damage to plankton and crops).
- [ ] Z2 Response: Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs; the ozone layer is recovering. Often cited as the most successful global environmental agreement.

Also (one bullet each)
- [ ] X1 Air pollution in towns: traffic (NO2 and fine particles) and home solid-fuel burning; link to smoke-free zones in "Burning fossil fuels and using alternative energy sources".
- [ ] X2 Mining pollution: acid mine drainage at Avoca (from `geo-rocks-weathering-s2`).
- [ ] X3 Noise and visual pollution: airports, motorways, quarries, wind farms, pylons.

## 3. Traps

- The ozone hole is not the cause of global warming. CFCs are greenhouse gases as well as ozone destroyers, but the two problems have different mechanisms and different treaties (Montreal vs Kyoto/Paris).
- Acid rain belongs in "Burning fossil fuels and using alternative energy sources" (the syllabus pairs it with fossil fuels). Here, one bullet naming it as a transboundary example.
- Smog and the Dublin coal ban belong in the same neighbouring subsection; refer to them in one bullet only.
- Lough Neagh is in Northern Ireland and is the largest lake on the island; Lough Corrib is the largest in the Republic.
- Keep the 2023 Lough Neagh bloom as a single event; do not claim it has been solved.
- The USA signed but never ratified Kyoto. Don't say it was a full Kyoto party.
- Exxon Valdez is in Alaska (Prince William Sound), not the Gulf of Mexico; Deepwater Horizon is in the Gulf of Mexico.
- Global warming as an interdependence issue (with deforestation and desertification, 80-mark essays) belongs to the Global Interdependence option (`geo-option-global-interdependence`). Keep this subsection to pollution, its causes, effects and reduction.
- Don't give temperature or sea-level projections for 2100, or any figures from past-paper emissions charts.
- Sustainable development, the EPA's full role and environmental impact assessment belong in "Sustainable economic development". Mention the EPA here only as monitoring water quality and licensing waste.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Ordinary 40 marks (asked repeatedly): name one example of environmental pollution caused by economic activity; explain its causes and effects; describe how it could be reduced. Each case study above must stand alone for this question.
- Ordinary: one negative impact of CO2 emissions; two ways greenhouse gas emissions can be reduced; drawing a graph of emissions data.
- Higher 30 marks: environmental pollution at a local/national or global scale (cause, effects, management), usually answered with global warming or agricultural water pollution.
