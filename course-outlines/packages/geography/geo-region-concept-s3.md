## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

The idea
- [ ] K1 Culture: the shared way of life of a group (language, religion, customs, music, sport, sense of identity). A cultural region is an area where people share one or more of these traits, which sets them apart from neighbouring areas.
- [ ] K2 Language and religion are the two traits the syllabus uses; they are easiest to map (census data on language and religion).
- [ ] K3 Cultural regions rarely match political borders: one state can contain several cultural regions (Belgium, Switzerland), and one cultural region can cross several states (the Basque Country, the Islamic world). Their edges are usually transition zones (link to s1, R5).

Language regions in Ireland: the Gaeltacht
- [ ] L1 The Gaeltacht: the officially recognised areas where Irish is (or was) the community language. It is not continuous: scattered pockets, mainly along the western seaboard. Name the seven counties and the main areas (section 2).
- [ ] L2 Why it survived in the west: remote, mountainous, poor farmland, poor communications; these areas had least contact with English-speaking trade, towns and administration. So the Gaeltacht overlaps with the peripheral West of Ireland (s4).
- [ ] L3 The two Meath Gaeltachtaí were created in the 1930s by resettling Irish-speaking families from Connemara: a cultural region created deliberately by the State.
- [ ] L4 State support defines and sustains the region: Irish is the first official language under the 1937 Constitution; Údarás na Gaeltachta (1980) promotes employment and the language there; Raidió na Gaeltachta (1972) and TG4 (1996) broadcast in Irish; the Official Languages Act 2003; Irish-medium schools; summer colleges bring learners into the Gaeltacht.
- [ ] L5 Threats: out-migration of young people, in-migration of English speakers and holiday homes, English-language media and tourism. Census figures show a gap between the number who say they can speak Irish and the number who use it daily (section 2).
- [ ] L6 The shrinking and redrawing of Gaeltacht boundaries since 1850 is in `geo-region-european` (2.4). Point there in one bullet; give no history here.

Language regions abroad: Belgium
- [ ] B1 Belgium is divided by a language border running east–west across the country: Dutch-speaking (Flemish) Flanders in the north; French-speaking Wallonia in the south; a small German-speaking community in the east; Brussels, the capital, an officially bilingual (French and Dutch) region that lies inside Flanders but is mostly French-speaking.
- [ ] B2 Origin: the line marks the old boundary between Germanic- and Latin-speaking peoples. It was fixed by law in 1962–63.
- [ ] B3 Political consequences: Belgium became a federal state in 1993, with three regions (Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels-Capital) and three language communities (Flemish, French, German-speaking), each with its own parliament. Schools, media and political parties are divided by language.
- [ ] B4 Tension: in 1968 the university at Leuven split, and the French-speaking section moved to a new town, Louvain-la-Neuve, in Wallonia; after the 2010 election Belgium went 541 days without a new government because Flemish and French-speaking parties could not agree.
- [ ] B5 The language divide matches an economic divide: Wallonia's coal and steel industries in the Sambre–Meuse valley declined (s4), while Flanders, with the port of Antwerp and newer industries, became the wealthier region, and many Flemish people resent transfers of money to Wallonia.

Other European language regions (one bullet each; exams use them in matching)
- [ ] O1 The Basque Country: Basque (Euskara) is unrelated to any other European language; the region crosses the border of Spain and France.
- [ ] O2 Catalonia, north-east Spain: Catalan language; capital Barcelona; a strong independence movement (an unofficial independence referendum was held in 2017).
- [ ] O3 Wales (Welsh) and Brittany (Breton): Celtic-language regions. Switzerland: four national languages (German, French, Italian, Romansh). Name only; the Swiss case study is in the Culture and Identity option.

Religious regions in Ireland
- [ ] P1 The Republic is mainly Roman Catholic, but the share is falling and the number with no religion is rising (Census 2022 figures in section 2). Growth of other religions through immigration (e.g. Orthodox Christians, Muslims).
- [ ] P2 Northern Ireland: the Plantation of Ulster (from 1609) brought Protestant settlers from Britain, creating a Protestant majority in much of the north-east of Ulster. Religion became linked to political identity: mostly Catholic nationalists and mostly Protestant unionists.
- [ ] P3 At a local scale, religion defines segregated residential areas in Belfast, separated in places by "peace walls" (peace lines). Census 2021 showed, for the first time, more Catholics than Protestants in Northern Ireland.
- [ ] P4 The interaction of religion, culture and politics in Northern Ireland (partition, the Troubles, the Good Friday Agreement) is taught in `geo-region-european` (2.3). Here, one bullet pointing there.

Religious regions abroad: the Islamic world
- [ ] M1 Islam was founded by the Prophet Muhammad in Arabia in the 7th century; Mecca and Medina, in present-day Saudi Arabia, are its holiest cities. The Islamic calendar begins with the Hijra (Muhammad's move from Mecca to Medina) in 622.
- [ ] M2 Extent: the Islamic world stretches from Morocco and North Africa through the Middle East and Central Asia to Pakistan, Bangladesh and Indonesia. Indonesia has the largest Muslim population of any country. Muslims are almost a quarter of the world's population, so Islam is the second-largest religion.
- [ ] M3 How it spread: by Arab conquest across North Africa and into Spain (711); by trade along routes to South and South-East Asia; later by the Ottoman Turks into the Balkans.
- [ ] M4 What makes it a region: a shared faith (the Five Pillars: declaration of faith, prayer five times a day facing Mecca, giving to charity, fasting in Ramadan, pilgrimage to Mecca, the Hajj); Arabic as the language of the Qur'an; mosques and minarets in the landscape; Islamic law (Sharia) shaping the laws of some states; shared customs of dress, food and calendar.
- [ ] M5 Divisions within the region: Sunni Muslims are the large majority; Shia Muslims are the majority in Iran and Iraq. Rivalry between them adds to conflict in the Middle East.
- [ ] M6 Boundary zones and change: Muslim communities in the Balkans (Bosnia and Herzegovina, Albania, Kosovo) are a legacy of Ottoman rule; Spain was under Muslim (Moorish) rule until the fall of Granada in 1492; migration has created large Muslim communities in western Europe (North Africans in France, Turks in Germany, Pakistanis and Bangladeshis in Britain).

Religious regions in Europe
- [ ] Y1 Three broad Christian regions: Roman Catholic south and centre (Italy, Spain, Portugal, Poland, Ireland, southern Germany); Protestant north (Scandinavia, northern Germany, Britain); Orthodox east and south-east (Greece, Russia, Romania, Serbia).
- [ ] Y2 Origins: the split between the Catholic and Orthodox churches (Rome and Constantinople); the Protestant Reformation, begun by Martin Luther in 1517 in Germany. Vatican City, in Rome, is the centre of the Catholic Church.
- [ ] Y3 Religion and conflict in Europe: the break-up of Yugoslavia in the 1990s set Orthodox Serbs, Catholic Croats and Muslim Bosniaks against each other in Bosnia. One bullet.

## 3. Traps

- Brussels is officially bilingual but lies geographically inside Flanders; don't call it part of Wallonia.
- Flemish is the Dutch spoken in Belgium; don't call it a separate language from Dutch in a way that suggests it is unrelated.
- Don't give Gaeltacht population figures or Gaeltacht-only census figures: none are checked. Use only the national Irish-speaker figures in section 2.
- Don't give the 2016 census Irish-language figures quoted in past-paper answers.
- Don't give the number of Muslims worldwide as a number; use "almost a quarter".
- Islam is not confined to the Arab world: most Muslims live in Asia (Indonesia, Pakistan, Bangladesh). Don't equate "Arab" with "Muslim".
- Don't say Catholics are a majority in Northern Ireland: Census 2021 showed them as the largest group, not over half.
- Gaeltacht boundary history (1850 onwards, 1926 and 1956 definitions) belongs to `geo-region-european` (2.4); Northern Ireland's political interaction and partition belong to `geo-region-european` (2.3); language policy, minority languages and church–state relations in depth belong to the Culture and Identity option. Keep each to one pointer bullet.
- The Mezzogiorno is not a cultural region in exam matching; it is a peripheral region (s4). The Basque Country, Catalonia, the Gaeltacht, Dingle and the Islamic world are cultural regions.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Matching (most years): a cultural region (Basque Country, Catalonia, the Gaeltacht, Dingle as part of the Gaeltacht), a region defined by religion (the Islamic world), a cultural region in Ireland defined by language (the Gaeltacht). Definition: a region where people share common traits such as language, religion and traditions.
- Higher 30 marks (set twice): how culture can be used to define regions, with examples. Expected answer: definition, the Gaeltacht, Belgium, and/or the Islamic world or Irish religious regions, each with specific facts.
- Ordinary 30 marks: describe and explain cultural regions defined by language or by religion.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 30 marks, Higher: "Examine how language can be used to define regions, with reference to examples you have studied." Model answer: the Gaeltacht (location, reasons for survival, State support, threats, census contrast) and Belgium (language border, federal structure, tensions, economic divide). About 15 SRPs. No religion.
2. 30 marks, Higher: "Examine how religion can be used to define regions, with reference to examples you have studied." Model answer: the Islamic world (origin, extent, spread, defining traits, Sunni–Shia division, European edges) and religious regions in Ireland (Census 2022 change in the Republic, Northern Ireland's religious geography). About 15 SRPs. No language.
3. 4 marks: "Name two language regions in Belgium and state the language spoken in each." Model answer: Flanders (Dutch/Flemish), Wallonia (French); Brussels bilingual and the German-speaking east are acceptable alternatives.
