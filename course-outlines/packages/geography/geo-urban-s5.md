# Package: geo-urban-s5 — Urban problems in developed-world cities

- **loId:** `geo-urban-s5` · **chapterId:** `geo-urban` · **syllabusRef:** 5.6
- **Syllabus:** Statement: "Problems can develop from the growth of urban centres." Students
  should study "• urban problems of traffic movement and congestion • urban decay and urban
  sprawl and the absence of community • heritage issues in urban areas • environmental
  quality". National setting: "Appropriate examples from Irish towns and cities."
  International setting: "Case studies from developed world cities."
- **Scope:** Each problem (traffic, decay, sprawl, absence of community, heritage, environmental
  quality) is a separate block: causes, effects, a named case with checked facts, and the
  responses in brief. Exams ask "discuss any two/three of these problems". Main case study:
  **Dublin**. Developed-world cases outside Ireland: **Detroit** (decay), **Paris banlieues**
  (absence of community), **London** (congestion charge). Limerick for decay. Judging how well
  planning and renewal solve the problems is s6: here give responses as a bullet or two per
  problem only.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-20. The writer works only from this package and
  `course-outlines/NOTES_STANDARD.md`.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Traffic movement and congestion
- [ ] T1 Causes: rising car ownership; low-density suburbs and commuter towns poorly served by public transport; radial roads converging on a historic centre with narrow streets and few river crossings; journeys to work and school at the same peak times; delivery and heavy goods vehicles; jobs and shopping dispersed to suburbs reachable only by car; historically weak rail investment.
- [ ] T2 Effects: lost time and productivity; stress; air and noise pollution; carbon emissions; road accidents; slower buses; businesses and tourism harmed; less space for pedestrians and cyclists.
- [ ] T3 Dublin case: why it is congested (sprawl, car dependence, radial routes, Liffey crossings, late arrival of light rail, port traffic formerly through the centre).
- [ ] T4 Measures (Ordinary asks "measures that can be taken"; each a bullet): public transport (DART, Luas, commuter rail, BusConnects, planned MetroLink); bus lanes/quality bus corridors; park and ride; cycle lanes and Dublin Bikes; integrated ticketing (Leap card); orbital roads and bypasses (M50); Port Tunnel and HGV management; pedestrianisation; parking charges and limits; congestion charging (London); car-sharing, remote working, staggered hours; land-use planning that puts homes near jobs and transport.

Urban decay
- [ ] D1 Definition and causes: physical, economic and social decline of part of a city, usually the inner city: old industries and port jobs close; better-off residents move to suburbs; buildings left derelict; reduced rates income; poor maintenance of social housing; dereliction attracts crime and vandalism; road schemes and planning blight.
- [ ] D2 Effects: unemployment and poverty; derelict sites; population decline; drug and crime problems; poor services; stigma; cycle of decline.
- [ ] D3 Dublin's inner city in the 1970s–80s: dock and industrial jobs lost, tenements cleared, population moved to the suburbs, dereliction (renewal in s6).
- [ ] D4 Limerick: concentrated disadvantage in social-housing estates (Moyross, Southill, St Mary's Park, Ballinacurra Weston); a 2006 crime prompted a government report (2007) and the Limerick Regeneration programme (evaluated in s6).
- [ ] D5 Detroit as the developed-world case study: car-industry city; population peak and fall; suburbanisation and "white flight"; abandoned houses and factories; 2013 bankruptcy; signs of recovery downtown. (Shrinking cities as a future issue is in s8.)

Urban sprawl
- [ ] S1 Definition: the uncontrolled outward spread of low-density housing and other development over rural land at the edge of a city.
- [ ] S2 Causes: population growth; high inner-city prices; demand for houses with gardens; car ownership and motorways; planning that zoned greenfield land; developers' cheaper greenfield sites; tax incentives in the Celtic Tiger.
- [ ] S3 Effects: long commutes and congestion; loss of farmland and habitats (s4 E2); costly services spread thin; car dependence; dormitory towns; city-centre decline; higher emissions.
- [ ] S4 Dublin case: sprawl into the commuter counties; unfinished "ghost estates" after the 2008 crash.
- [ ] S5 Solutions (one bullet each; effectiveness in s6): green belts; compact growth and brownfield development (NPF); higher densities near public transport; new towns; regeneration of inner-city sites.

Absence of community
- [ ] C1 Causes: new estates built quickly without schools, shops, community centres or public transport; high-rise flats with no shared space; long commutes leaving little time for local life; transient renting populations; demolition of old inner-city communities when tenements were cleared; social segregation.
- [ ] C2 Effects: isolation, loneliness, anti-social behaviour, little sense of belonging or local organisation.
- [ ] C3 Examples: Ballymun's tower blocks (Dublin); commuter estates and ghost estates; the Paris banlieues (grands ensembles, unemployment, 2005 riots).
- [ ] C4 Responses: community facilities built with housing (phasing conditions); regeneration with resident participation; mixed tenure; sports clubs, family resource centres.

Heritage issues
- [ ] H1 Urban heritage: historic buildings, streetscapes, archaeological sites, monuments and their setting; value for identity, tourism and the economy (exam asks one benefit of an urban heritage site).
- [ ] H2 Threats: demolition for offices and roads; neglect and dereliction; inappropriate new buildings and heights out of scale; traffic vibration and pollution; tourist pressure.
- [ ] H3 Dublin cases: loss of Georgian houses on Lower Fitzwilliam Street to the ESB (1960s); the Wood Quay Viking site and the civic offices (1970s); the Moore Street 1916 terrace; positive reuse in Temple Bar and the Docklands warehouses.
- [ ] H4 Protection: Record of Protected Structures and Architectural Conservation Areas (Planning and Development Act 2000); national monuments; An Taisce and heritage groups; UNESCO World Heritage listing (one international urban example).
- [ ] H5 Kilkenny's Medieval Mile as heritage tourism in a smaller Irish city (one bullet; history in s1).

Environmental quality
- [ ] E1 Air quality: smoke from coal fires, traffic emissions (nitrogen dioxide, particulates); Dublin's 1980s smog and the 1990 ban on smoky coal; the ban extended nationwide in 2022.
- [ ] E2 Water quality: sewage treatment (Ringsend), storm overflows, Dublin Bay; urban rivers.
- [ ] E3 Waste: litter, landfill, the plastic bag levy (2002), recycling, the Poolbeg waste-to-energy plant.
- [ ] E4 Noise and visual pollution; graffiti; derelict sites (Derelict Sites levy, one bullet).
- [ ] E5 Green space and biodiversity: parks (Phoenix Park), canals, Dublin Bay; loss of green space to infill.
- [ ] E6 Urban flooding from paved surfaces and rivers (Dodder flooding, 1986); urban heat island (one bullet).

## 3. Traps

- **Urban decay ≠ urban sprawl.** Decay is decline inside the city (usually the inner city); sprawl is uncontrolled growth at the edge. Keep their causes separate.
- **Urban renewal ≠ redevelopment** (both are s6): don't evaluate them here.
- Detroit: give only the checked figures (about 1.85 million in 1950, about 640,000 in 2020); don't give vacant-house counts or debt totals.
- Wood Quay: don't give a protest march size or a date of the final court ruling. Moore Street: don't describe later court cases.
- The coal ban: don't give a number of lives saved. The London charge: don't give its price (it changes).
- Don't claim any Dublin site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site; Ireland's two are Brú na Bóinne and Skellig Michael.
- Past-paper model answers in this chapter contain errors (e.g. calling the London orbital motorway "the M25" in Dublin, invented "Dublin Green Belt strategy"). Dublin's orbital road is the **M50**; Ireland has no statutory Dublin green belt.
- Don't copy data-response figures (car modal share, canal-crossing counts, Luas Cork projections).
- Developing-world city problems (slums, informal sector) are s7; cities of the future and shrinking cities as a future trend are s8; the evaluation of planning strategies is s6; land-use change is s4.

- Higher 30 marks: discuss two of heritage, planning and environmental issues arising from urban growth.
- Short questions: one benefit of a heritage site in an urban area; one challenge of growing urban populations.
