# Package: pe-physical-activity-inclusion-s2 — Addressing barriers to physical activity

- **loId:** `pe-physical-activity-inclusion-s2` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-physical-activity-inclusion` · **syllabusRef:** 7.2
- **Not prescribed for June 2027.** Topic 7 is not one of the two topics the SEC prescribed
  for the 2027 examination (circular S51/25). Write it after the prescribed chapters.
- **Syllabus:** "discuss ways in which barriers to participation might be/have been addressed
  by the individuals themselves, their representative groups and/or voluntary and statutory
  organisations". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: the three levels
  (individuals, representative groups, voluntary and statutory organisations), each with what
  they do and examples; then worked responses for at least three groups, each barrier matched
  to a response.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] L1 Individuals themselves: self-advocacy, forming or joining groups and clubs, becoming coaches or leaders and role models, campaigning, using social media to raise awareness, choosing and adapting activities.
- [ ] L2 Representative groups: organisations that represent a group's interests and run or lobby for opportunities (e.g. disability sport organisations, women-in-sport bodies, older people's organisations, anti-racism bodies). They provide programmes, training, advice, awareness campaigns and lobbying.
- [ ] L3 Voluntary organisations: run by volunteers or charities; clubs and community groups (e.g. Special Olympics clubs, local walking groups, community sports clubs).
- [ ] L4 Statutory organisations: set up by law and funded by the state (e.g. Sport Ireland, Local Sports Partnerships, local authorities, the Department of Education); they provide funding, policy, facilities and programmes.
- [ ] M1 Matching responses to barriers: cost → subsidised or free programmes, equipment loan; facilities → accessible design and grants; lack of trained coaches → inclusion training for coaches; confidence → beginner and single-sex sessions, buddy systems; transport → local and school-based provision; attitudes → awareness campaigns and role models; media coverage → campaigns for visibility.
- [ ] W1 Worked responses for women, people with disabilities and one other group (e.g. older adults or ethnic minority groups): barrier → who addressed it → how → effect.
- [ ] E1 Evaluating the responses: what has worked, what gaps remain (rural provision, funding, reliance on volunteers).

## 3. Traps

- Statutory (state, set up by law) and voluntary (charity, volunteers) are different; keep examples correct.
- Do not repeat the list of barriers from "Supports and barriers for selected groups"; name the barrier briefly and focus on the response.

## 4. What exams ask

- Examine how women or another group might tackle the barriers to participation (Higher, after a participation chart).
- Explain two ways coaches can adapt activities to be more inclusive (overlaps "Adapted physical activity").

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Discuss ways in which barriers to participation for one group have been addressed by representative groups and by statutory organisations."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Describe two ways a barrier to participation for people with a disability could be overcome."
