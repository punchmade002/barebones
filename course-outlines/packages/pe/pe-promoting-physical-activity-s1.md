## 1. Must cover

Concepts of physical activity (each: definition, key features, two examples)
- [ ] K1 Physical activity: any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that uses energy, on a continuum from healthy-lifestyle activity to competing to win (the specification's framing).
- [ ] K2 Play: spontaneous, freely chosen, enjoyable activity done for its own sake, with flexible or self-made rules, mainly associated with children (e.g. chasing games in a playground).
- [ ] K3 Leisure and recreation: activity chosen in free time for enjoyment, relaxation and wellbeing, with little emphasis on competition (e.g. a walk, a leisurely cycle, a swim).
- [ ] K4 Physical education: planned, structured learning in, through and about physical activity within the school curriculum, taught by a qualified teacher.
- [ ] K5 Mass-participation sports: organised events in which large numbers of people of mixed ability take part at the same time, usually for personal challenge or charity rather than to win (e.g. city marathons, fun runs, parkrun, sportives).
- [ ] K6 Outdoor and adventure activities: activities in the natural environment that involve challenge, managed risk and uncertainty; land-based (hill walking, orienteering, rock-climbing, mountain biking) and water-based (canoeing, kayaking, sailing, surfing).
- [ ] K7 Sport: organised, competitive physical activity governed by formal rules and a governing body, with an emphasis on performance and winning.

Personal benefits
- [ ] P1 Physical: lower risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, some cancers and obesity; stronger bones (lower osteoporosis risk); better fitness, sleep and weight management.
- [ ] P2 Mental: improved mood and self-esteem, reduced stress, anxiety and symptoms of depression, better concentration; enjoyment and a sense of achievement.

Social benefits
- [ ] S1 Making friends and belonging to a group; teamwork, communication and leadership skills; social outlet; community cohesion; reduced isolation; positive use of time; meeting people from different backgrounds.

Economic benefits
- [ ] E1 For society: lower healthcare costs, fewer sick days and higher productivity, employment in the sport and leisure industry, tourism and event income, the value of volunteering.
- [ ] E2 For the individual: lower medical costs, career opportunities, lower costs of free activities such as walking compared with health treatment.

Life stages (benefits that matter most at each)
- [ ] L1 Children: motor-skill development, healthy growth, social skills, play.
- [ ] L2 Adolescents (teenagers): self-esteem and body image, managing stress, friendships and belonging, establishing lifelong habits.
- [ ] L3 Adults: weight and disease prevention, stress relief, social contact, work productivity.
- [ ] L4 Older adults: balance and strength to prevent falls, bone density, independence, social contact and reduced loneliness, cognitive health.

Guidelines
- [ ] G1 Irish guidelines: children and young people at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity every day; adults at least 30 minutes of moderate activity on five days a week (150 minutes a week); older adults the same, with balance and strength activities.

## 3. Traps

- Mass-participation sport is about many participants at once, not about popularity on television.
- When asked for outdoor and adventure activities "other than water activities", exclude canoeing, kayaking, sailing, rowing and surfing.
- Economic benefits are financial, not "fitter people"; keep the three types of benefit distinct.
- Supports and barriers belong to "Physical activity participation"; national policies to "Physical activity promotion".

## 4. What exams ask

- Define or explain each concept (sport, physical education, leisure and recreation, mass-participation sports, outdoor and adventure); match concepts to descriptions with examples; name four outdoor and adventure activities other than water activities.
- Outline social benefits (for teenagers, adults); economic benefits; personal benefits at two life stages; benefits for children and for teenagers; health benefits for older adults with named activities.
- Identify four benefits of physical activity participation.
