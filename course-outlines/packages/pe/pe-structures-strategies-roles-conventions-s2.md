# Package: pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions-s2 — Roles and relationships

- **loId:** `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions-s2` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions` · **syllabusRef:** 3.2
- **Syllabus:** "Investigate the demands of different roles in terms of the physical, personal
  and technical qualities needed for a successful performance"; "identify weaknesses of
  opponent/s in order to exploit them". The topic introduction adds the importance of
  affiliation, team building and responsible leadership. Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher
  Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what a role is; physical,
  personal and technical demands, with worked examples for three contrasting roles; team roles
  and relationships (affiliation, team building, leadership); identifying and exploiting
  opponents' weaknesses. The non-playing roles of coach/choreographer and official have their
  own subsections; here focus on performer roles.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] R1 Role: a performer's specific position, function and responsibilities within an activity or team (e.g. goalkeeper, centre-back, point guard, setter in volleyball, lead dancer).
- [ ] R2 Physical demands: the fitness components the role needs (e.g. a midfielder's cardio-respiratory endurance; a goalkeeper's power, agility and reaction time).
- [ ] R3 Personal demands: psychological and interpersonal qualities (e.g. concentration, decision-making, communication, leadership, composure, resilience, responsibility).
- [ ] R4 Technical demands: the skills and tactical knowledge the role needs (e.g. a goalkeeper's handling and distribution; a point guard's ball-handling and passing; a setter's volleying accuracy).
- [ ] R5 Worked analysis of three contrasting roles in named activities (e.g. a soccer goalkeeper, a Gaelic football midfielder, a basketball point guard), each under physical, personal and technical headings.
- [ ] R6 Demands on a performer other than fitness (an Ordinary pattern "you may not refer to fitness"): personal and technical demands only.

Relationships within a team
- [ ] T1 Affiliation: belonging to a team, club or group and identifying with it; formal affiliation to a club or governing body also gives access to competitions, insurance and coaching.
- [ ] T2 Team building: developing trust, communication and cohesion (task cohesion: working together towards a goal; social cohesion: getting on together) through shared goals, team activities and clear roles.
- [ ] T3 Responsible leadership: a captain or leader who communicates, sets an example, supports team-mates, keeps discipline and puts the team's goals first.
- [ ] T4 How clear roles and good relationships improve performance (players know their responsibilities, cover for each other, communicate effectively).

Identifying and exploiting opponents' weaknesses
- [ ] W1 How weaknesses are identified: scouting, video analysis of previous matches, notational statistics, observation early in a game.
- [ ] W2 Types of weakness: technical (e.g. a weak backhand or weaker foot), physical (e.g. slow defenders, poor stamina late in a game), tactical (e.g. a high defensive line, poor marking at set pieces), psychological (e.g. loses composure under pressure).
- [ ] W3 Exploiting them: a matched strategy for each type (e.g. play balls behind a slow, high defensive line; serve to the weak backhand; run at a tiring defender late in a game).

## 3. Traps

- The coach/choreographer and official roles are separate subsections; do not cover their demands here beyond a one-line cross-reference.
- Structures and strategies themselves are taught in "Structures, strategies and compositional elements"; here link a strategy only to the weakness it exploits.
- Keep physical, personal and technical demands under separate headings; do not mix fitness into "personal".

## 4. What exams ask

- In a named activity, identify two weaknesses an opposition team could have and suggest a strategy to exploit one.
- Identify a performer role and describe two demands on it (not fitness).
- Analyse the physical and technical demands of a performer in a named activity (Higher case study).
- Discuss the personal and technical qualities needed for successful sports coaching (overlap with the coach subsection).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Analyse the physical, personal and technical demands of a performer role in a physical activity of your choice." Model: one named role; at least two demands under each heading, each explained.
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Identify two weaknesses an opposing team could have and suggest a strategy to exploit one of them." Model: two distinct weaknesses; one specific strategy linked to one weakness.
