# Package: pe-ethics-fair-play-s5 — Best practice for the use of supplements

- **loId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play-s5` · **chapterId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 6.5
- **Syllabus:** "investigate the uses of dietary supplements designed to optimise performance
  in one of their selected physical activities". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level
  only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what a dietary supplement
  is; the ethical and anti-doping issue (inadvertent doping); best practice before using one;
  common supplements, their uses and the activities they suit; a worked investigation for one
  selected activity. The nutritional role and general challenges of supplements are taught in
  `pe-performance-demands` ("Diet, nutrition and hydration"); here focus on safe, ethical use
  and the investigation.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] S1 Dietary supplement: a product taken in addition to the normal diet (powder, tablet, drink, gel) to supply nutrients or other substances intended to improve health or performance.
- [ ] S2 The risk: supplements are not regulated like medicines; some are contaminated with, or contain undeclared, prohibited substances. Under strict liability the athlete is responsible for a positive test, so supplement use can lead to an anti-doping violation.

Best practice
- [ ] B1 "Food first": meet needs through a balanced diet before considering supplements.
- [ ] B2 Needs assessment by a qualified professional (sports dietitian or doctor): is there a genuine need, and will it help this athlete?
- [ ] B3 Evidence: use only supplements with good scientific evidence for the athlete's activity.
- [ ] B4 Risk minimisation: choose batch-tested products from third-party testing programmes (which reduces but does not remove risk), check every ingredient against the Prohibited List, keep the packaging, batch number and receipt, never buy online from unknown sources.
- [ ] B5 Correct dose and timing; trial in training, never for the first time in competition; monitor side effects.
- [ ] B6 Young athletes: supplements are generally not recommended for under-18s; focus on diet.

Common supplements and their uses (each: what it does, which activity it suits, a challenge)
- [ ] U1 Protein (powders, bars): muscle repair and growth after resistance or high-volume training; convenient; often unnecessary if the diet has enough protein.
- [ ] U2 Creatine: increases muscle phosphocreatine stores, improving repeated short maximal efforts and recovery between them (sprinting, team games, weightlifting); can cause weight gain from water retention.
- [ ] U3 Caffeine: stimulant that can reduce perceived effort and improve alertness and endurance; side effects include anxiety, poor sleep and stomach upset; permitted but monitored.
- [ ] U4 Sports drinks and energy gels: fluid, electrolytes and carbohydrate during long events.
- [ ] U5 Vitamin and mineral supplements (e.g. iron, vitamin D): only to correct a diagnosed deficiency.

Investigation for one selected activity
- [ ] I1 Structure: the activity's demands (duration, intensity, energy systems); the supplements used in it and their claimed benefits; the evidence; the risks; best-practice guidance; a conclusion on whether and how a performer should use them (e.g. a team-game player: creatine for repeated sprints, protein for recovery, sports drinks in matches; with food-first and batch-tested caveats).

## 3. Traps

- Exam questions may exclude creatine ("you may not use creatine"); the notes must give at least three other supplements with function and effect.
- Do not present supplements as necessary or risk-free.
- Prohibited drugs are in "Drugs and sport"; do not list steroids or EPO as supplements.

## 4. What exams ask

- Complete a table of three sports supplements, their function and performance effect (excluding creatine).
- Name two sports nutrition supplements and explain how one optimises performance.
- Name a supplement and justify why athletes use it; name two food supplements other than protein with reasons; outline two challenges of taking supplements.
- Investigate the uses of a supplement in one selected activity.
