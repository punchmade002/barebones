# Package: pe-learning-skill-technique-s1 — Defining a skilled performance

- **loId:** `pe-learning-skill-technique-s1` · **chapterId:** `pe-learning-skill-technique` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 1.1
- **Syllabus:** "identify the characteristics of a skilled performance"; "discuss the
  difference between skill and ability". The specification's own definition: "A skilled
  performance is one in which a sequence of movements is performed in a fluent and controlled
  manner, the right options are selected and the skills and techniques used fully reflect the
  performer's ability and experience. Technique is the way in which these skills are executed."
  Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what skill and technique
  are; characteristics of a skilled performance; ability; skill versus ability; types of skill
  (assumed knowledge used later in "Skill acquisition"). Apply points to named activities
  (e.g. a basketball jump shot, a Gaelic football solo, a tennis serve).

## 1. Must cover

Skill and technique
- [ ] S1 Skill: a learned ability to bring about a predetermined result with maximum certainty and the minimum outlay of time and/or energy (Knapp, see section 2). Skill is learned through practice; it is goal-directed.
- [ ] S2 Technique: the way a skill is executed, i.e. the specific movement pattern. Two performers can have the same skill (e.g. a free throw) with different techniques.
- [ ] S3 The specification's definition of a skilled performance (fluent, controlled sequence; right options selected; skills reflect the performer's ability and experience). A skilled performance therefore includes decision-making, not only movement quality.

Characteristics of a skilled performance (each with a short named-activity illustration)
- [ ] C1 Consistent: the outcome can be repeated reliably under pressure.
- [ ] C2 Accurate/precise: achieves the intended outcome (e.g. lands the ball where intended).
- [ ] C3 Fluent and well co-ordinated: movements flow smoothly from one phase to the next, good timing.
- [ ] C4 Controlled: the performer is in control of body and implement throughout.
- [ ] C5 Efficient (economy of movement): no wasted movement or energy. One bullet only; the full treatment is in "Analysing skill: movement and quality perspectives".
- [ ] C6 Aesthetically pleasing: looks good to the observer.
- [ ] C7 Adaptable: correct decisions and right option selected; skill adapted to the situation (anticipation, reading the play).
- [ ] C8 Learned and goal-directed: the result of practice, with a clear intended outcome.
- [ ] C9 Recognising a skilled versus an unskilled (novice) performer by these features: e.g. novice is jerky, inconsistent, slow to decide.

Ability
- [ ] A1 Ability: an inherited (innate), stable and enduring trait that a person brings to the learning of skills; abilities underpin skills and set potential.
- [ ] A2 Two broad groups of ability (assumed): perceptual-motor abilities (e.g. reaction time, co-ordination, balance, spatial awareness) and physical proficiency abilities (e.g. strength, flexibility, stamina). Name the classification as in section 2.
- [ ] A3 Each skill draws on several abilities (e.g. a tennis serve needs co-ordination, timing, power, flexibility); a specific ability can support many skills.

Skill versus ability
- [ ] D1 Skill is learned; ability is largely inherited.
- [ ] D2 Skill can be developed rapidly with practice; ability is relatively stable and changes slowly.
- [ ] D3 Skills are built on abilities; ability determines how quickly and how far a skill can be developed.
- [ ] D4 Natural ability alone is not enough: without practice, coaching, feedback and psychological preparation, ability is not turned into skilled performance (the 2025 Ordinary "Can someone succeed with just ability?" angle). A less gifted performer who practises well can outperform a more gifted one who does not.

Types of skill (assumed; needed to choose practice types in "Skill acquisition")
- [ ] T1 Open (environment unpredictable, externally paced, e.g. a pass in a game) versus closed (stable environment, self-paced, e.g. a free throw, a gymnastic vault).
- [ ] T2 Discrete (clear beginning and end, e.g. a penalty kick), serial (discrete elements linked, e.g. a triple jump), continuous (no clear start or end, e.g. running, swimming).
- [ ] T3 Gross (large muscle groups, e.g. a shot-put) versus fine (small, precise movements, e.g. a snooker shot or dart throw).
- [ ] T4 Self-paced versus externally paced. Present all four as continua: most skills sit between the extremes.

Applying to a named activity
- [ ] P1 Identify three skills from one activity and explain why each benefits performance (e.g. in basketball: the jump shot scores points, the bounce pass retains possession past a defender, the defensive slide prevents penetration).

## 3. Traps

- Do not define ability as "how good someone is at a sport"; that is skill level.
- Do not say ability cannot change at all: it is relatively stable, influenced by genetics and early development.
- Stages of learning, practice types and principles of effective practice belong to "Skill acquisition". Types of skill are taught here; refer to them there without re-teaching.
- Feedback types belong to "Psychological preparation" (`pe-performance-demands`). Do not list them here.
- Economy of movement and creative application are taught in full in "Analysing skill: movement and quality perspectives". One bullet each here, as characteristics.
- Do not copy past-paper athletes (e.g. the skilled multi-sport athlete in the 2024 Higher case) or newspaper headlines.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define skill; define ability; explain the term creative application of skill.
- Identify two or four characteristics of a skilled performance, sometimes for highly skilled athletes in a photograph.
- Are skill and ability the same thing? Justify.
- Can someone succeed with just natural ability? Explain.
- Identify three skills from a named activity and explain why each benefits performance.
- Short questions (8 marks) and parts of 40-mark questions; Higher Level expects each characteristic explained, not just named.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Identify and explain four characteristics of a skilled performance in a named physical activity." Model: four distinct characteristics (e.g. consistency, fluency, efficiency, correct decision-making), each explained with a specific example from one named activity.
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Discuss the difference between skill and ability, using examples from physical activity." Model: learned vs inherited, changes with practice vs stable, skill built on several abilities, one worked example; must not list characteristics of skilled performance.
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Can a performer succeed with natural ability alone? Explain your answer." Model: no; ability must be developed by practice, coaching and feedback; one example.
