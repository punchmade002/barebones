# Package: pe-promoting-physical-activity-s4 — Pathways to excellence in physical activity

- **loId:** `pe-promoting-physical-activity-s4` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-promoting-physical-activity` · **syllabusRef:** 5.4
- **Syllabus:** "suggest ways in which pathways between school- and community-based physical
  activity/sport might be strengthened"; "analyse current provision and support for excellence
  in performance in the three selected physical activities". Ordinary and Higher level; no
  Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what a pathway is and the
  long-term athlete development idea; strengthening school–community pathways; provision and
  support for excellence (structures, funding, coaching, sports science, competition); a worked
  analysis for one Gaelic game and one Olympic sport.

## 1. Must cover

Pathways
- [ ] P1 A pathway to excellence: the route and supports by which a participant progresses from first involvement to high-level performance (school/club → development squads → national/international).
- [ ] P2 Long-term athlete development (LTAD, Balyi): age- and stage-appropriate development through the stages FUNdamentals, Learn to Train, Train to Train, Train to Compete and Train to Win, with Active for Life for everyone. Early specialisation can cause drop-out and injury.

Strengthening school–community pathways
- [ ] S1 Club coaches running sessions in schools, and teachers coaching in clubs.
- [ ] S2 Taster sessions and "bring a friend" days that move pupils from school PE into local clubs.
- [ ] S3 Shared facilities and coordinated fixtures so school and club demands don't clash.
- [ ] S4 Information on local clubs given to pupils; club open days; Transition Year sports-leadership and coaching courses.
- [ ] S5 Local Sports Partnerships linking schools and clubs; school-based clubs affiliated to NGBs.
- [ ] S6 Communication between school and club coaches about workload to avoid overtraining talented young players.

Provision and support for excellence
- [ ] E1 Structures: clubs, county or regional development squads, provincial and national underage squads, academies (e.g. rugby provincial academies), college sports scholarships.
- [ ] E2 Funding: Sport Ireland funding to NGBs for high performance; direct athlete funding through Sport Ireland's International Carding Scheme; university sports scholarships; sponsorship.
- [ ] E3 Coaching and sports science: qualified high-performance coaches, strength and conditioning, physiotherapy, performance analysis, nutrition and psychology, many delivered through the Sport Ireland Institute.
- [ ] E4 Facilities: national centres (e.g. the Sport Ireland Campus at Abbotstown, the National Aquatic Centre), county grounds, indoor arenas.
- [ ] E5 Competition: regular high-level competition at underage and senior level, international fixtures and training camps.
- [ ] E6 Analysing provision: strengths and weaknesses of the current supports for each selected activity (e.g. depth of the voluntary club system in Gaelic games; reliance on a small number of facilities and funding concentrated on carded athletes in Olympic sports); suggestions for improvement.

## 3. Traps

- Gaelic games players are amateurs; do not describe them as paid professionals.
- Do not invent funding amounts or carding levels.
- Adapted pathways for athletes with disabilities are in `pe-physical-activity-inclusion` ("Adapted physical activity").
- The NGB's general role is in "Physical activity promotion"; here focus on excellence.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline or describe two ways pathways between school and community sport can be strengthened.
- Analyse the provision and support for excellence in a named activity (8 marks, Higher).
- Outline two ways an NGB supports high performance (case study, e.g. swimming); how an NGB might use funding to support high performance.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Analyse the provision and support for excellence in performance in a named physical activity of your choice." Model: pathway structures, funding, coaching and sports science, competition; one strength and one weakness.
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Describe two ways in which pathways between school sport and community sport can be strengthened."
