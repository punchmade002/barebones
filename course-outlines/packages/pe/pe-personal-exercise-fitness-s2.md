# Package: pe-personal-exercise-fitness-s2 — Aerobic training methods

- **loId:** `pe-personal-exercise-fitness-s2` · **chapterId:** `pe-personal-exercise-fitness` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (aerobic training and what each requires):** continuous training (constant
  intensity; purpose; FITT formula; intermediate slow distance 20–60 mins; long slow distance
  over 60 mins); interval training (readiness; work:recovery ratios; interval duration; FITT);
  fartlek training (readiness; terrain; interval duration; FITT); aerobics/step aerobics
  (technical description of skills; routine; transition; timing and rhythm; training zones;
  FITT); spinning (spinning techniques; workout intensity; session design; training zones;
  FITT); indoor rowing (time; distance; targets; resistance; session design).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write one block per method in the
  specification's order, covering exactly the elements the specification lists for that method,
  plus one worked example session with FITT. Training zones and principles in general are in
  "Applying training principles to each method"; apply them here.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] C1 Continuous training: constant intensity in the aerobic zone without rest; purpose (cardio-respiratory endurance, aerobic base, body composition); intermediate slow distance of 20–60 minutes and long slow distance of more than 60 minutes; FITT example (3–5 times a week, about 60–80% of maximum heart rate, 30 minutes, running).
- [ ] I1 Interval training: readiness (a fitness base and correct technique before high-intensity intervals); work:recovery ratios (e.g. 1:1 or 1:2 for aerobic and lactic intervals, 1:3 or more for maximal efforts); interval duration (short for speed and anaerobic power, longer for aerobic capacity); FITT example (e.g. 6 × 3 minutes hard with 3 minutes easy jog).
- [ ] F1 Fartlek training: readiness; terrain (hills, sand, grass, trails varying the load); interval duration (unstructured or structured bursts); FITT example (e.g. 30 minutes with sprints between markers and hill efforts).
- [ ] A1 Aerobics and step aerobics: technical description of basic steps (e.g. basic step, V-step, grapevine, knee lift, step touch; step up and down with the whole foot on the step); routine (blocks of moves combined); transitions (smooth changes between moves); timing and rhythm (moves in time with the music's beats, e.g. 32-count blocks); training zones (keeping heart rate in the aerobic zone); FITT.
- [ ] S1 Spinning: techniques (seated climb, standing climb, seated flat, sprints, correct bike set-up with saddle height so the knee is slightly bent at the bottom of the pedal stroke); workout intensity (resistance and cadence, heart-rate zones or perceived exertion); session design (warm-up, intervals of climbs and sprints, cool-down); training zones; FITT.
- [ ] R1 Indoor rowing: technique (catch, drive legs–body–arms, finish, recovery arms–body–legs); time and distance (e.g. 20-minute steady piece or a 2,000 m test); targets (pace per 500 m, stroke rate); resistance (damper setting); session design (warm-up, intervals or steady state, cool-down).
- [ ] W1 One worked aerobic session for a named goal (illustrative, with FITT and a warm-up and cool-down).

## 3. Traps

- The rowing drive is legs, body, arms; the recovery is arms, body, legs.
- Readiness must be addressed for interval and fartlek training, as the specification lists it.

## 4. What exams ask

- Training methods for named components; continuous, interval and fartlek definitions and examples; work:recovery ratios; FITT applied to a method.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Compare continuous, interval and fartlek training for a games player, using FITT for one of them."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Describe how a spinning session could be designed to improve cardio-respiratory endurance."
