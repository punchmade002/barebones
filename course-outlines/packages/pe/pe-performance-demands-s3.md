# Package: pe-performance-demands-s3 — Performance-related fitness

- **loId:** `pe-performance-demands-s3` · **chapterId:** `pe-performance-demands` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 2.3
- **Syllabus:** "define the components of performance-related fitness: agility, balance,
  co-ordination, power, speed and reaction time". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level
  only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. One block per component: definition, forms,
  two named activities where it is decisive and why. Tests belong in "Assessing fitness and the
  principles of training".

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] R1 Agility: the ability to change the position or direction of the body quickly and with control, while keeping balance (e.g. evading a tackle in rugby, a badminton player recovering to the centre of the court).
- [ ] R2 Balance: the ability to keep the body's centre of mass over its base of support. Static balance (stationary, e.g. a handstand) and dynamic balance (while moving, e.g. a gymnast on the beam, a hurler on the run).
- [ ] R3 Co-ordination: the ability to use two or more body parts (or senses and body parts) together smoothly and efficiently (e.g. hand–eye co-ordination in striking a sliotar; the arm–leg timing of front crawl).
- [ ] R4 Power: the ability to apply strength at speed; power = strength × speed (e.g. a high-jump take-off, a shot-put, a smash).
- [ ] R5 Speed: the ability to move the body, or a part of it, from one point to another in the shortest possible time; speed = distance ÷ time (e.g. a 100 m sprint, a fast break in basketball). Includes limb speed (e.g. arm speed in a throw).
- [ ] R6 Reaction time: the time between the presentation of a stimulus and the start of the response (e.g. a sprinter reacting to the gun, a goalkeeper reacting to a shot). Distinguish movement time (from starting to completing the movement) and response time (reaction time + movement time).
- [ ] R7 Two examples per component of where it is needed in one named activity (a 2026 Ordinary pattern: "identify two examples of where reaction time is needed").
- [ ] R8 Identifying the main PRF component for a described athlete or event and explaining why (case-study tables).

## 3. Traps

- Reaction time ends when movement starts; it does not include the movement itself.
- Agility includes a change of direction with control; running fast in a straight line is speed.
- Strength is health-related in the specification; power is performance-related.
- Tests belong in "Assessing fitness and the principles of training"; training methods in "Designing a fitness plan".
- Do not reuse case-study athletes from past papers.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define agility and outline its importance in a named activity.
- Define reaction time and give two examples of where it is needed in one activity.
- Identify the main PRF component for named athletes or events in a table and explain each, with another activity where it matters.
- Identify two PRF components important in a named activity and justify them.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Identify two performance-related components of fitness important in a named physical activity and justify their importance." Model: two components defined and linked to specific moments in the activity.
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Define reaction time and give two examples of where it is needed in one physical activity." Model: definition as in R6; two distinct situations in one activity.
