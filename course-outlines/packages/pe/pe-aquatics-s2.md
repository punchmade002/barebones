# Package: pe-aquatics-s2 — Lifesaving

- **loId:** `pe-aquatics-s2` · **chapterId:** `pe-aquatics` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (skills and techniques):** entry into water/entries into unknown conditions;
  strokes/propelling over a distance to casualty or with casualty; life-saving kick/side
  stroke; approaching casualty and manoeuvring a casualty when turning; contact/non-contact tows
  using rigid/non-rigid aids/conserving energy/breath/heat; two different types of rescue.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: the rescuer's priorities and
  the order of rescue methods (safest first); types of casualty; entries; approach strokes and
  the lifesaving kick; approaching and turning a casualty; non-contact and contact tows with and
  without aids; landing and aftercare (brief); conserving energy; two worked rescues.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] P1 Priorities: the rescuer's own safety first; shout for help and alert emergency services; keep the casualty in sight; choose the safest method.
- [ ] P2 Order of rescue methods, safest first: talk (shout instructions), reach (with an arm, pole or towel from the bank), throw (a buoyant aid or rope), wade (only if safe), row (a boat), swim (with an aid), tow (contact tow, only if trained and necessary).
- [ ] C1 Types of casualty: weak swimmer, non-swimmer (panicking, may grab the rescuer), injured swimmer, unconscious casualty.
- [ ] E1 Entries: slide-in entry (unknown depth or conditions; safest), step-in entry (known depth, clear water; keeps eyes on casualty), straddle entry (known deep water; head stays above water to watch casualty), compact jump (from a height into known deep water).
- [ ] E2 Unknown conditions: always enter feet-first, slowly, testing depth.
- [ ] S1 Approach strokes: head-up front crawl or head-up breaststroke so the casualty stays in sight; pace to conserve energy for the return.
- [ ] S2 Lifesaving kick (inverted breaststroke kick on the back) and sidestroke: used when towing because the arms are occupied.
- [ ] A1 Approaching the casualty: stop at a safe distance (about 2–3 m), talk to reassure, offer an aid; if grabbed, submerge to break free.
- [ ] A2 Manoeuvring and turning a casualty: from the front, turn the casualty onto their back with the face out of the water; turn smoothly while keeping the face clear.
- [ ] T1 Non-contact tows: the rescuer and casualty both hold a rigid aid (e.g. a rescue tube or float) or a non-rigid aid (e.g. a towel or clothing); keeps the rescuer out of reach of a panicking casualty.
- [ ] T2 Contact tows: chin tow or cross-chest/arm tow, keeping the casualty's face clear of the water; for tired or unconscious casualties.
- [ ] T3 Conserving energy, breath and heat: steady pace, breathe regularly, rest on an aid, minimise movement in cold water.
- [ ] L1 Landing and aftercare: help the casualty out with support; check breathing; recovery position; keep warm; seek medical help (secondary drowning risk) (brief).
- [ ] R1 Worked rescue 1, reach or throw rescue: a weak swimmer near the edge; rescuer lies flat, reaches with a pole or throws a buoyant aid, talks the casualty in.
- [ ] R2 Worked rescue 2, swimming rescue with an aid: slide-in entry, head-up approach, stop and offer the aid, non-contact tow with the lifesaving kick, land the casualty.

## 3. Traps

- Rescuer safety comes first; never recommend a contact rescue as the first option.
- Feet-first entry into unknown water; no diving.

## 4. What exams ask

- Lifesaving appears as a named activity in written answers (safe practice, roles, communication, first aid).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Describe the order of rescue methods a lifesaver should consider, and explain why the order matters."
