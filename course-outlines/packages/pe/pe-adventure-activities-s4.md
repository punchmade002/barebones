# Package: pe-adventure-activities-s4 — Rock-climbing

- **loId:** `pe-adventure-activities-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-adventure-activities` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (outdoors or indoor climbing wall, single pitch):** ability to use equipment and
  implement safe procedures; basic knots; correct use of climbing calls and communication;
  climbing bottom and top rope (single and multiple anchors), rope management, belaying,
  hand/finger/foot jams, 3-point and/or correct use of 2-point contact points, lay backing,
  undercling, bridging; climbing overhang and/or mantle shelf; traversing feet and hands;
  abseiling straight demonstrating ability to lock off when collecting equipment part-way down.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: equipment; safety checks;
  knots; belaying and rope systems; climbing calls; movement techniques (each with key
  points); overhangs and mantle shelf; traversing; abseiling; safety and conventions; demands.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] E1 Equipment: harness, helmet, climbing shoes, rope, belay device, locking karabiners, slings and anchors, chalk.
- [ ] E2 Pre-climb safety checks (buddy check): harness correctly fitted and buckled, knot tied and dressed, belay device and locking karabiner correctly loaded and locked, helmet on.
- [ ] K1 Figure-of-eight follow-through knot to tie the rope to the harness, with a stopper knot; checked by the partner.
- [ ] K2 Other basic knots: clove hitch (attaching to an anchor), overhand on the bight.
- [ ] B1 Top rope: the rope runs from the climber up through an anchor at the top and down to the belayer; a fall is short.
- [ ] B2 Bottom rope (as the specification calls it): the belay and anchor at the bottom of the route.
- [ ] B3 Single and multiple anchors: multiple anchors share the load so that one failure is backed up.
- [ ] B4 Belaying: managing the rope through a belay device so the climber is protected; the brake hand never leaves the rope; take in slack as the climber rises; lock off to hold a fall; lower smoothly.
- [ ] B5 Rope management: flake (lay out) the rope so it runs freely; keep it clear of feet and tangles.
- [ ] C1 Climbing calls, in sequence: "Safe" (climber secure), "Belay on?"/"On belay" (belayer ready), "Climbing" / "Climb on", "Take in" (remove slack), "Slack" (give rope), "Take" (hold my weight), "Lower" / "Lowering", "Rope below!" (warning). Calls are short, clear and acknowledged.
- [ ] M1 Three-point contact: two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand, in contact at all times while moving one limb; two-point contact used correctly for dynamic moves.
- [ ] M2 Footwork: weight over the feet, use the edge or toe of the shoe precisely, straight arms to save energy, hips close to the wall.
- [ ] M3 Jams: wedging hands, fingers or feet into cracks to make a hold.
- [ ] M4 Lay back (lay backing): pulling on a vertical edge with the hands while pushing the feet against the wall in the opposite direction.
- [ ] M5 Undercling: pulling up on the underside of a hold while the feet push, keeping the body close.
- [ ] M6 Bridging: legs spread across two opposite walls (as in a corner), pushing out with the feet to rest or move up.
- [ ] M7 Overhang: keep arms straight, feet high and hips close to the wall, move quickly between rests.
- [ ] M8 Mantle shelf: pushing down on a ledge with the hands (pressing up like getting out of a pool), then bringing a foot up onto it.
- [ ] M9 Traversing: moving sideways with hands and feet, used for warm-up and bouldering; keep three points of contact and the body balanced over the feet.
- [ ] A1 Abseiling: descending a fixed rope using a friction device, feet flat and wide on the rock, body leaning back roughly perpendicular to the rock, the control hand below; lock off part-way down to free both hands to collect equipment; a safety backup (prusik or separate belay).
- [ ] S1 Safety and conventions: qualified supervision; checking anchors; never stand directly below a climber; helmets outdoors; weather and rock conditions; respect for the crag and access rules.
- [ ] D1 Demands: muscular endurance and strength of fingers, forearms, shoulders and core; flexibility; balance; concentration and managing fear and anxiety; trust and communication between climber and belayer.

## 3. Traps

- The belayer's brake hand never leaves the rope.
- Climbing calls must be in the right order and acknowledged; give them exactly as listed.
- Do not recommend lead climbing; the specification sets single-pitch top/bottom roping.

## 4. What exams ask

- Rock-climbing is used as a named activity in written answers (safety checks, communication, anxiety, muscular endurance, roles of belayer and climber).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Describe the safety checks and communication a climber and belayer should use before and during a top-rope climb."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Explain two climbing techniques that help a climber conserve energy."
