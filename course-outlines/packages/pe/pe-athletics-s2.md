# Package: pe-athletics-s2 — Running events

- **loId:** `pe-athletics-s2` · **chapterId:** `pe-athletics` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus:** running events sprints, middle distance, long distance, hurdles; skills and
  techniques: starts and finishes; upper body action (head and arms); lower body action
  (legs); overall efficiency.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: the running events and
  their demands; starts (crouch start and standing start); sprint technique (upper body, lower
  body, phases: drive, acceleration, maximum velocity, maintenance); middle and long distance
  technique and pacing; hurdles technique; finishes; overall efficiency and common faults; race
  tactics; rules.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] E1 Events and demands: sprints (100 m, 200 m, 400 m; ATP-PC and lactic acid systems, speed, power, reaction time); middle distance (800 m, 1500 m; lactic acid and aerobic, speed endurance, tactics); long distance (5000 m, 10,000 m and beyond; aerobic, cardio-respiratory endurance, pacing); hurdles (speed, flexibility, rhythm).
- [ ] S1 Crouch start (sprints, in blocks): "On your marks" (hands behind the line, shoulder-width, front knee near the line, back knee on the ground), "Set" (hips raised slightly above the shoulders, shoulders over the hands, legs bent at roughly 90 degrees front and 120 degrees back), gun (drive out low with powerful leg extension and an opposite arm drive).
- [ ] S2 Standing start (middle and long distance): one foot forward, body leaning slightly, weight on the front foot, no hands on the track; on "Go" drive forward.
- [ ] S3 Drive and acceleration: body low and leaning forward at first, gradually becoming upright over about 20–30 m.
- [ ] U1 Upper body in sprinting: head still and in line with the spine, eyes forward, relaxed face and shoulders, arms bent at about 90 degrees driving from the shoulder (hand from hip to chin level), no crossing the body.
- [ ] L1 Lower body in sprinting: high knee lift, foot lands on the ball under the hips, full extension of the drive leg (hip, knee and ankle), heel pulled up towards the bottom on recovery, short ground contact.
- [ ] D1 Middle and long distance: more upright, relaxed running; lower knee lift and arm drive; mid-foot landing; rhythmic breathing; economy of movement.
- [ ] D2 Pacing: even pace or negative split (second half faster); avoid going out too fast (lactic acid build-up); kick for the finish.
- [ ] H1 Hurdles: attack the hurdle from a set distance; lead leg driven up straight with the knee leading; trail leg brought round to the side with the knee high and the foot turned out; lean the trunk forward and use the opposite arm to the lead leg; run off the hurdle quickly; consistent stride pattern between hurdles (e.g. three strides).
- [ ] F1 Finishes: run through the line without slowing; dip the torso at the line (the torso, not the head or arms, counts in a race finish).
- [ ] O1 Overall efficiency: relaxed running, minimal side-to-side movement, good posture, rhythm; economy of movement.
- [ ] O2 Common faults: arms crossing the body, overstriding and heel striking, tense shoulders, leaning back, slowing before the line.
- [ ] T1 Tactics for middle distance: position near the front on the inside, avoid being boxed in, respond to surges, timing of the final kick.
- [ ] R1 Rules: one false start leads to disqualification at senior level; lane rules (sprints stay in lanes; 800 m breaks into the inside lane after the first bend); obstruction.
