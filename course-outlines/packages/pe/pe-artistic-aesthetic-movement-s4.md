# Package: pe-artistic-aesthetic-movement-s4 — Dance

- **loId:** `pe-artistic-aesthetic-movement-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-artistic-aesthetic-movement`
  · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus:** learners perform an individual and a group dance in which they demonstrate
  consideration of: technical ability (posture, alignment, co-ordination, balance, strength,
  flexibility, mobility and control); overall choreographic design, including choreographic
  content (use of space, e.g. levels and pathways, timing and relationship to other dancers),
  choreographic construction (motif and variation) and choreographic form (e.g. canon, unison,
  binary, rondo); dance style (e.g. contemporary, folk, modern, ballet, jazz, tap, ethnic,
  traditional); use of props and music; overall sense of performance, including the ability to
  communicate artistically and aesthetically with an audience.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: the individual and group
  dance requirement; technical ability (each quality defined for dance); choreographic content;
  choreographic construction; choreographic form (each form defined with a simple example);
  dance styles (one line each); props and music; performance and communication; the
  choreographic process; safe practice; demands.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] R1 Requirement: an individual dance and a group dance.
- [ ] T1 Technical ability for dance: posture (upright, lifted carriage), alignment (body parts in correct line, e.g. knees over toes), co-ordination, balance, strength, flexibility, mobility (range at the joints) and control (stopping, holding and moving precisely).
- [ ] C1 Choreographic content: use of space (levels high/medium/low, pathways straight/curved/zigzag, directions, formations), timing (speed, rhythm, stillness), relationships to other dancers (mirroring, contrast, leading and following, contact).
- [ ] C2 Choreographic construction: a motif (a short movement phrase that expresses the theme) developed through variation (changing its size, speed, level, direction, body part or adding repetition).
- [ ] F1 Choreographic form: unison (dancers perform the same movement at the same time); canon (dancers perform the same movement one after another, as in a round); binary (A B: two contrasting sections); rondo (A B A C A: a main section returning between contrasting sections); also ternary (A B A) as an extra example.
- [ ] S1 Dance styles (one line each): contemporary, folk, modern, ballet, jazz, tap, ethnic and traditional (Irish traditional dance is a familiar example of traditional dance).
- [ ] P1 Props and music: props extend movement or tell the story (e.g. a chair, scarf, ribbon); music sets mood, tempo and structure; movement matched to musical phrasing and accents; silence can be used for effect.
- [ ] Q1 Performance and communication: projection, focus (where the eyes look), facial expression, energy, commitment and confidence; communicating the theme artistically and aesthetically to an audience.
- [ ] Z1 The choreographic process: choose a stimulus or theme; improvise and select movement; create motifs; develop and structure them into a form; rehearse; refine with feedback and video.
- [ ] Y1 Safe practice: warm-up and cool-down, suitable floor surface and footwear, space between dancers, correct technique for lifts and landings, hydration.
- [ ] D1 Demands: flexibility, balance, strength, muscular endurance, co-ordination, rhythm, spatial awareness, confidence and expression, teamwork in group dance.

## 3. Traps

- Canon is sequential, unison is simultaneous; do not swap them.
- Aesthetic and artistic criteria and general compositional elements are taught elsewhere; this subsection applies them to dance.

## 4. What exams ask

- Dance is a common aesthetic activity in written answers (compositional elements, aesthetic and artistic criteria, methods of analysing choreography, comparing two activities).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Explain three choreographic devices or forms a choreographer could use in a group dance, with an example of each."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Explain motif and variation in dance."
