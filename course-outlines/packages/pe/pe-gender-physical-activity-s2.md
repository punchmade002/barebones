## 1. Must cover

Body image
- [ ] B1 Body image: a person's perceptions, thoughts and feelings about their own body, including how they think others see it. It can be positive or negative and is not the same as how the body actually looks.
- [ ] B2 Body ideals: the "ideal" body promoted for women is typically slim and toned; for men, lean and muscular. Few people match these ideals.

How body image influences participation
- [ ] F1 Females: self-consciousness about appearance, being watched or judged, sweating, and revealing kit leads some girls to avoid or drop out of activity, especially in adolescence.
- [ ] F2 Females: fear of becoming "too muscular" can steer girls away from strength training and contact sports.
- [ ] M1 Males: pressure to be muscular can push boys towards weight training and away from activities seen as unmasculine; boys who feel too small, too heavy or unathletic may avoid team sports and changing rooms.
- [ ] M2 Both: negative body image is a barrier to joining and staying; positive body image supports participation.

How participation influences body image
- [ ] P1 Positive: regular activity improves fitness, competence and self-esteem, and shifts the focus to what the body can do rather than how it looks (body functionality).
- [ ] P2 Negative: some activities emphasise weight or appearance (gymnastics, dance, distance running, weight-category sports, bodybuilding) and can lead to unhealthy dieting, disordered eating or excessive exercise.
- [ ] P3 Muscle dysmorphia in males (believing one's body is too small despite being muscular) as an extreme effect (assumed; one bullet).

Media representations of the body
- [ ] R1 Media (TV, magazines, advertising, social media) show narrow, idealised bodies: thin and toned women, lean muscular men; images are often edited or filtered.
- [ ] R2 Female athletes are more often shown for appearance, sexualised poses or personal life; male athletes for power, strength and action.
- [ ] R3 Social media lets young people compare themselves with idealised images constantly, including from influencers and fitness accounts.
- [ ] R4 Male athletes in aesthetic activities and female athletes in power and contact sports are less often shown, reinforcing ideas of which activities suit which gender.

Debate: do media representations help or hinder participation?
- [ ] D1 Hinder: unattainable ideals lower body satisfaction; young people who don't match them feel excluded; activity is framed as a way to change appearance rather than for enjoyment and health, which is a weak and fragile motivation; sexualised images of women can discourage girls.
- [ ] D2 Help: images of fit, healthy athletes can inspire; increasing coverage of diverse body types and of women's sport shows that bodies of many shapes perform well; athletes speaking about body confidence challenge ideals.
- [ ] D3 A reasoned conclusion: the effect depends on whether the images focus on performance and diversity (positive) or on appearance and narrow ideals (negative).

Responses
- [ ] Q1 Media literacy education (understanding editing and selection); showing a wide range of body types; focusing coverage on skill and achievement; comfortable kit choices; PE that values effort and enjoyment over appearance.

## 3. Traps

- Body image is a perception, not body shape or weight.
- Keep the debate balanced: both sides, then a conclusion.
- Hegemonic masculinity and femininity are defined in "Gender socialisation and its impact on participation"; refer to them here only as the source of the ideals.
- Do not give clinical detail or statistics on eating disorders; one bullet each for P2 and P3.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define body image; explain it using an example.
- Discuss how body image may influence physical activity participation, using two examples; discuss how female body image can be influenced by participation.
- Discuss the validity of a statement that showcasing lean women and muscular men promotes participation (Higher).
- Describe two ways media representation of the body may affect young people's participation; discuss how media representation of the body may affect young men's participation.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "'By showcasing lean women and muscular men, the media helps to promote physical activity among young people.' Discuss the validity of this statement." Model: both sides (D1, D2) and a reasoned conclusion.
