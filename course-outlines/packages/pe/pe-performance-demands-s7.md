## 1. Must cover

Confidence
- [ ] C1 Confidence: a performer's belief in their ability to perform successfully. Self-efficacy: confidence in a specific situation.
- [ ] C2 Sources of self-efficacy (Bandura): performance accomplishments (past success), vicarious experiences (seeing similar others succeed), verbal persuasion (encouragement), emotional/physiological arousal (how the performer interprets their body's state).
- [ ] C3 Effects: high confidence increases effort, persistence and willingness to attempt difficult skills; low confidence leads to avoidance, hesitation and anxiety; over-confidence can cause complacency.
- [ ] C4 Strategies: set achievable goals, recall past successes, positive self-talk, imagery of success, demonstrations by similar performers, specific praise.

Anxiety and arousal
- [ ] A1 Arousal: the level of physical and mental activation or readiness, from deep sleep to extreme excitement.
- [ ] A2 Inverted-U hypothesis: performance improves as arousal rises to an optimal level, then declines; the optimal level is lower for complex or fine skills and beginners, higher for simple or gross skills and experts.
- [ ] A3 Anxiety: a negative emotional state of worry and apprehension linked to high arousal. Cognitive anxiety (worry, negative thoughts, self-doubt) versus somatic anxiety (physical symptoms: raised heart rate, sweating, muscle tension, "butterflies"). State anxiety (in a particular situation) versus trait anxiety (a general tendency to be anxious).
- [ ] A4 Effects on performance: narrowed or distracted attention, muscle tension, poor co-ordination and decisions, "choking"; some anxiety can be facilitative if interpreted positively.
- [ ] A5 Strategies: breathing control, progressive muscular relaxation, positive self-talk and reframing, imagery, pre-performance routines, simulation of competition in practice.

Motivation
- [ ] M1 Motivation: the drive to begin, direct and persist with an activity. Intrinsic (from within: enjoyment, mastery, satisfaction) versus extrinsic (from outside: rewards, trophies, praise, selection).
- [ ] M2 Effects on practice (effort, attendance, quality of repetitions when no one is watching) and on performance (persistence when losing).
- [ ] M3 Strategies: SMART goal setting, variety in training, recording progress, positive feedback and praise, giving the performer ownership and choice, team targets, appropriate rewards; over-reliance on extrinsic rewards can reduce intrinsic motivation.

Concentration
- [ ] F1 Concentration: the ability to focus attention on the relevant cues and ignore distractions, and to keep that focus over time.
- [ ] F2 Types of attentional focus (Nideffer): broad or narrow, internal or external (e.g. broad-external to read a game; narrow-external to watch the ball; narrow-internal for a pre-shot routine).
- [ ] F3 Effects: lapses cause errors and missed cues, especially late in a contest or during long waits between efforts.
- [ ] F4 Strategies: pre-performance routines, cue words, process goals, thought stopping (recognise a negative thought and replace it), simulation training with distractions, imagery, breaking a long event into segments.

Feedback
- [ ] B1 Feedback: information a performer receives about a performance.
- [ ] B2 Intrinsic (from within, through the senses and "feel", kinaesthetic) versus extrinsic (from outside: coach, video, scoreboard).
- [ ] B3 Knowledge of results (KR: about the outcome) versus knowledge of performance (KP: about the quality of the movement or technique).
- [ ] B4 Positive (what went well, motivates) versus negative (what went wrong, corrects); concurrent (during the performance) versus terminal (after it).
- [ ] B5 Importance and use: beginners need frequent, simple, positive, extrinsic feedback and KR; experienced performers use intrinsic feedback and detailed KP; too much feedback causes dependence and overload. Feedback should be specific, timely and constructive.

Mental preparation before, during and after (strategy, how it works, when it is used)
- [ ] P1 Before: goal setting, imagery/visualisation, pre-performance routine, relaxation and breathing, positive self-talk, preparation of equipment and plan.
- [ ] P2 During: cue words and self-talk, refocusing routines after errors, breathing control in breaks, process focus ("one point at a time").
- [ ] P3 After: reflection and performance review, constructive feedback, recovery and relaxation, resetting goals; dealing with a poor result by reframing.
- [ ] P4 Evaluating strategies: what each improves, its limitations (e.g. imagery needs practice and a clear image; goal setting fails if goals are unrealistic).

Personal action plan
- [ ] D1 Design: identify the psychological factors to target (from self-assessment, e.g. a questionnaire or reflective diary); set goals; choose one or two strategies for before, during and after performance; give a rationale for each; how progress will be monitored and reviewed.
- [ ] D2 A worked plan outline for one named activity (illustrative).

## 3. Traps

- Arousal and anxiety are not the same: arousal is neutral activation; anxiety is a negative emotional response.
- Knowledge of results is about the outcome; knowledge of performance is about the technique. Do not swap them.
- Feedback types are taught here only. Do not also teach them in "Skill acquisition".
- Goal-setting detail for performance goals (SMART goals for a training plan) is in `pe-planning-optimum-performance` ("Planning for optimum performance"); here goal setting is a psychological strategy, one block only.
- Strategies must be matched to the correct factor (e.g. relaxation lowers anxiety; it does not directly raise motivation).

## 4. What exams ask

- How motivation can affect practice; how confidence can affect performance; how concentration affects performance; define the factors.
- One strategy to improve concentration, motivation, confidence or mental preparedness, explained.
- Define anxiety; discuss how anxiety may affect performance; describe how another factor affects performance.
- Outline four types of feedback; explain intrinsic and extrinsic feedback; two types a coach could use; identify the type in a quotation.
- Outline three aspects of a personal action plan and their rationale.
- Two strategies to improve mental preparedness before or during performance in a named activity.
