# Package: pe-ethics-fair-play-s4 — Anti-doping rules

- **loId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 6.4
- **Syllabus:** "describe the current Irish anti-doping rules including the ways in which they
  are enforced"; "explain therapeutic use exemption in relation to the use of medicines in
  sport"; "discuss the implications of using performance-enhancing drugs for the performer and
  the activity". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: who makes and runs the
  rules; strict liability; the anti-doping rule violations; enforcement (testing, whereabouts,

## 1. Must cover

Who makes and runs the rules
- [ ] R1 The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) sets the World Anti-Doping Code and the Prohibited List.
- [ ] R2 In Ireland, Sport Ireland runs the Irish Anti-Doping Programme and the Irish Anti-Doping Rules, which apply the World Anti-Doping Code to all athletes in sports governed by NGBs that Sport Ireland recognises.
- [ ] R3 Strict liability: athletes are responsible for any prohibited substance found in their body, whether or not they intended to cheat (so they must check medicines and supplements).

Anti-doping rule violations (the rules)
- [ ] V1 Presence of a prohibited substance, its metabolites or markers in a sample.
- [ ] V2 Use or attempted use of a prohibited substance or method.
- [ ] V3 Evading, refusing or failing to submit to sample collection after being notified.
- [ ] V4 Whereabouts failures: any combination of three missed tests and/or filing failures within 12 months.
- [ ] V5 Tampering or attempted tampering with any part of doping control.
- [ ] V6 Possession of a prohibited substance or method.
- [ ] V7 Trafficking or attempted trafficking.
- [ ] V8 Administration or attempted administration to an athlete.
- [ ] V9 Complicity (assisting, covering up) and prohibited association (working with a banned coach or doctor).

Enforcement
- [ ] E1 Testing: urine and blood samples, in competition and out of competition, often with no advance notice; samples are sealed, split into A and B samples and analysed in WADA-accredited laboratories.
- [ ] E2 Whereabouts: athletes in the registered testing pool give their location in advance, including a daily 60-minute slot when they are available for testing.
- [ ] E3 Athlete biological passport: monitoring blood and steroid markers over time to detect the effects of doping.
- [ ] E4 Results management and hearings: Sport Ireland notifies the athlete; the case is heard by an independent disciplinary panel; appeals are possible.
- [ ] E5 Sanctions: disqualification of results, loss of medals and prize money, a period of ineligibility (normally four years for an intentional first violation), publication of the decision.
- [ ] E6 Education and intelligence: anti-doping education programmes for athletes and support staff; investigations using information from other sources.

Therapeutic use exemption (TUE)
- [ ] T1 A TUE is permission to use a prohibited substance or method to treat a diagnosed medical condition.
- [ ] T2 The athlete applies in advance (except in emergencies) to the relevant anti-doping organisation, with medical evidence; an independent panel of doctors decides.
- [ ] T3 Conditions for granting: the athlete's health would be significantly impaired without it; it would produce no additional performance enhancement beyond a return to normal health; there is no reasonable permitted alternative; the need is not a consequence of earlier use without a TUE.
- [ ] T4 Examples: insulin for type 1 diabetes; certain asthma medications above permitted doses; prescribed stimulant medication for diagnosed ADHD.
- [ ] T5 Misconceptions: a prescription from a doctor alone does not make a prohibited medicine allowed; a TUE is needed.

Implications (link)
- [ ] I1 One block: consequences for the performer (ban, disqualification, loss of funding, reputation, health) and for the activity (credibility, sponsorship, participation); see "Drugs and sport" for the full analysis.

## 3. Traps

- Selling a PED is a violation (trafficking); athletes cannot refuse a test without consequences; prescribed medicine is not automatically allowed. These are recurring true/false items.
- PED categories and their effects are in "Drugs and sport"; supplements in "Best practice for the use of supplements".
- Keep to "current" rules: do not describe historical rules or older sanction lengths.

## 4. What exams ask

- State two or four current Irish anti-doping rules; identify three ways the rules are enforced.
- What is a TUE; when are TUEs used; describe two circumstances where Sport Ireland might grant a TUE; under what circumstance can an athlete compete with a banned substance in their system.
- True/false on Sport Ireland's procedures (whereabouts, prescriptions, refusing tests, selling PEDs); matching terms (anti-doping rule violation, TUE, PED, code of ethics).
- State two rules an athlete may have violated when the substance is unknown (e.g. refusing a test, whereabouts failures).
