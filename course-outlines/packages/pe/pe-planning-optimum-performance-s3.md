# Package: pe-planning-optimum-performance-s3 — Aesthetic and artistic considerations

- **loId:** `pe-planning-optimum-performance-s3` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-planning-optimum-performance` · **syllabusRef:** 4.3
- **Syllabus:** "identify the artistic and/or aesthetic criteria of performance"; "compare
  different physical activities in terms of the aesthetic and/or artistic criteria"; "evaluate
  personal and/or group performance from artistic and/or aesthetic perspectives". Ordinary and
  Higher level; no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what aesthetic and
  artistic mean; the criteria; how judged activities are scored; aesthetic qualities in
  non-judged activities; comparing activities; evaluating a performance. Compositional elements
  (how routines are built) are in `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions` ("Structures,
  strategies and compositional elements"); refer to them.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] D1 Aesthetic: concerned with the beauty and quality of movement as perceived by the observer (how a movement looks and feels to watch).
- [ ] D2 Artistic: concerned with creative expression, where the performer deliberately communicates an idea, mood, story or emotion through choreographed movement.
- [ ] D3 Aesthetic and artistic criteria are judged qualitatively, by people, rather than by an objective outcome such as time, distance or score.

Criteria (each defined with an example)
- [ ] C1 Body line, shape and extension (pointed toes, straight legs, full extension).
- [ ] C2 Control, poise and balance (held positions, controlled landings).
- [ ] C3 Fluency and flow (smooth transitions between elements).
- [ ] C4 Timing, rhythm and musicality (movement matched to the music's accents).
- [ ] C5 Expression and communication (conveying mood or story; facial expression; projection to an audience).
- [ ] C6 Creativity and originality of choreography.
- [ ] C7 Use of space and variety (levels, pathways, direction).
- [ ] C8 Synchronisation and relationships in group or pair work.
- [ ] C9 Difficulty and execution (in judged sports, difficulty value plus execution score with deductions from a maximum).
- [ ] C10 Presentation (costume, props, entrance and finish) where the activity allows.

Comparing activities
- [ ] P1 Activities judged mainly on aesthetic/artistic criteria: gymnastics, dance, diving, synchronised swimming, figure skating.
- [ ] P2 Activities judged on objective outcomes (games, athletics, swimming races) in which aesthetic qualities are present but not scored (e.g. a fluent passing move, an elegant hurdling technique).
- [ ] P3 Comparing two activities, at least one artistic/aesthetic: what each is judged on, how scoring works, which criteria they share and where they differ (e.g. dance emphasises expression and musicality; gymnastics difficulty and execution).
- [ ] P4 Activities with aesthetic criteria written into the scoring (e.g. surfing judged on speed, power and flow; ski jumping style marks) as borderline cases.

Evaluating a performance
- [ ] E1 Method: video the performance; score it against agreed criteria (checklist or rating scale for each criterion); self-, peer- and teacher evaluation; compare with a model performance; identify strengths and areas to improve and how to improve them.

## 3. Traps

- Aesthetic is about the observer's perception of quality; artistic is about deliberate creative expression. Keep them distinct.
- Do not describe images from past papers (e.g. the two performances compared in 2026).
- How to build a routine (compositional elements, choreographic forms) is in "Structures, strategies and compositional elements".

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain the term "aesthetic and/or artistic criteria of performance".
- State or outline two aesthetic/artistic criteria for a named activity.
- Compare two activities (at least one artistic/aesthetic) from the perspective of their criteria (12 marks, Higher).
- Compare two pictured performances in terms of aesthetic or artistic criteria.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Compare two physical activities of your choice from the perspective of the artistic and/or aesthetic criteria of performance. At least one must be an artistic/aesthetic activity." Model: e.g. dance and diving; shared criteria, differences, how each is scored.
2. 4 marks, Higher: "Explain the term 'aesthetic and/or artistic criteria of performance'."
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Identify two artistic or aesthetic criteria of performance in a physical activity of your choice and explain each."
