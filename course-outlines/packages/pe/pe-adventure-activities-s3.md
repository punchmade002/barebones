# Package: pe-adventure-activities-s3 — Kayaking

- **loId:** `pe-adventure-activities-s3` · **chapterId:** `pe-adventure-activities` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (skills and techniques):**
  Kayaking (moving/white water): launching and landing at a low wall/bank; paddling (forwards,
  backwards, stopping, sweep and reverse sweep strokes, edging, stern rudder, ferry glide and
  reverse ferry glide); breaking in and out of a current (into eddy: bow rudder/low brace and no
  brace; from eddy: low brace, bow rudder); negotiating and use of currents and water features
  including stoppers, standing waves, whirlpools and green water; capsize drill, Eskimo roll,
  self-rescue and recovery; two advanced/group skills from capsize and rescue (self and with
  rope), e.g. combat roll, high cross, hanging draw.
  Kayaking (inland flat water/coastal): launching and landing; paddling (forwards, backwards,
  stopping, sweep and reverse sweep strokes static and moving, stern rudder, bow rudder,
  figure-of-8 course forwards and backwards, ferry glide and reverse ferry glide); draw
  strokes/sculling draw, sculling for support; capsize drill, rolls, self-rescue and recovery;
  deep-water rescues (swimmer to boat/X-rescue, rafting).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: the two variants and
  equipment; launching and landing; basic paddling strokes; steering and turning strokes;
  edging and support strokes; moving water (features, eddies, breaking in and out, ferry
  glides); capsize and rescue; safety and rules; demands. Each stroke: what it does and two or
  three key technique points.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] K1 The two variants in the specification (moving/white water; inland flat water/coastal) and the shared core skills.
- [ ] K2 Equipment: kayak, double-bladed paddle, spraydeck, buoyancy aid, helmet (moving water), appropriate clothing (wetsuit or thermal layers), throw line for groups.
- [ ] L1 Launching and landing from a low wall or bank: boat parallel to the bank, paddle across the boat and bank for support, lower in smoothly keeping weight low and central; reverse to land.
- [ ] P1 Forward paddling: upright posture, blade fully in the water near the feet, pull alongside the boat to the hip, torso rotation drives the stroke rather than the arms.
- [ ] P2 Backward paddling: look over the shoulder, place the back of the blade behind the hip, push towards the feet.
- [ ] P3 Stopping: short alternate backward strokes near the hips.
- [ ] T1 Sweep stroke: a wide arc from the feet to the stern to turn the boat away from the paddle side; reverse sweep from stern to feet to turn towards the paddle side and to slow.
- [ ] T2 Stern rudder: the blade trailed at the stern to steer while moving.
- [ ] T3 Bow rudder: the blade placed near the bow at an angle to turn the moving boat towards that side; used to break into and out of eddies.
- [ ] T4 Draw stroke and sculling draw: move the boat sideways (e.g. alongside a raft or bank) by pulling the blade towards the hip, or by sculling it back and forth.
- [ ] T5 Figure-of-8 course forwards and backwards: control of turning and steering around two markers.
- [ ] E1 Edging: tilting the boat with the hips and knees while keeping the body upright, to turn more sharply and to present the hull to current.
- [ ] E2 Low brace: the back of the blade slapped flat on the surface to recover balance; high brace as the stronger variant; sculling for support.
- [ ] W1 Water features: current, eddies (calmer or upstream-flowing water behind obstacles), eddy line, standing waves, stoppers (recirculating water below a drop; dangerous), whirlpools, green water (the smooth, unbroken water of the main current).
- [ ] W2 Breaking in (leaving the eddy into the current) and breaking out (entering the eddy): build speed, angle across the eddy line, edge downstream, use a bow rudder or low brace to turn.
- [ ] W3 Ferry glide: crossing a current without being swept downstream, with the bow angled upstream and paddling to balance the current; reverse ferry glide facing downstream.
- [ ] C1 Capsize drill: stay tucked forward, release the spraydeck by its loop, push out of the boat, keep hold of the boat and paddle, swim to the bank.
- [ ] C2 Eskimo roll: hip flick righting the boat using a paddle sweep or support while still seated, then the head comes up last.
- [ ] C3 Self-rescue and recovery: re-entering or swimming the boat to safety.
- [ ] C4 Deep-water rescues: X-rescue (rescuer lifts and empties the capsized boat across their own deck, then holds it while the swimmer re-enters); rafting (boats held together side by side for stability).
- [ ] C5 Advanced/group skills named by the specification as examples: combat roll (a roll in moving water), high cross (crossing a fast current through the top of a wave), hanging draw (a draw stroke held while moving forward). Use a throw line for rescue with rope.
- [ ] S1 Safety and rules: never paddle alone; buoyancy aid always; helmet in moving water; check conditions, levels and forecasts; know the river grade; scout rapids; group signals; qualified leader; carry a throw line, first aid and spare clothing; cold-water awareness.
- [ ] D1 Demands: muscular endurance of shoulders, arms and trunk; balance; cardio-respiratory endurance on long trips; confidence and composure after a capsize.

## 3. Traps

- A sweep turns the boat away from the paddle side; the reverse sweep turns it towards the paddle side.
- Stoppers are hazards to avoid for beginners; do not describe them as safe play features.
- The specification lists "Canoeing/Kayaking" in the activity list but gives skills only for kayaking; write kayaking.

## 4. What exams ask

- Kayaking appears as a named activity in written answers (safety equipment, risk, confidence, muscular endurance, skill analysis of a stroke).
