# Package: pe-technology-media-sport-s1 — The impact of technology on sport and physical activity

- **loId:** `pe-technology-media-sport-s1` · **chapterId:** `pe-technology-media-sport` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 8.1

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] T1 Types: equipment and materials (lighter rackets, carbon-fibre bikes, advanced footwear, swimsuits); wearable technology (GPS units, heart-rate monitors, fitness trackers); analysis technology (video, analysis software and apps, motion capture); officiating technology (goal-line technology, video referrals, Hawk-Eye); medical and recovery technology; broadcasting and digital media.

Performer
- [ ] P1 Advantages: better equipment improves performance and safety (helmets, protective padding); wearables and data individualise training and monitor load; prosthetics (e.g. running blades) enable participation and competition.
- [ ] P2 Disadvantages: cost and unequal access ("technological doping" debates); over-reliance on data; some equipment banned when it gives too great an advantage (e.g. full-body polyurethane swimsuits).

Coach/choreographer
- [ ] C1 Advantages: video and analysis software for technique and tactics; GPS and heart-rate data for workload and injury prevention; communication apps; music and editing software for choreography.
- [ ] C2 Disadvantages: cost, time to analyse, information overload, privacy of athlete data.

Official
- [ ] O1 Advantages: goal-line technology, video assistant referees (VAR), the television match official (TMO) in rugby, Hawk-Eye for line calls in tennis and for points in Gaelic games at major venues, communication headsets, electronic timing; more accurate decisions and fairness.
- [ ] O2 Disadvantages: delays that disrupt the flow of the game, controversy over interpretation, cost so only available at elite level, reduced authority of the on-field official.

Spectator
- [ ] S1 Advantages: replays and big screens, live statistics, streaming and apps, better viewing experience, access from home.
- [ ] S2 Disadvantages: cost of subscriptions, delays while decisions are reviewed, reduced attendance at live events.

Own use and evaluation
- [ ] U1 Students' own use: recording performances on phones or tablets, analysis apps, fitness trackers and heart-rate monitors for training zones, online training plans, digital portfolios for the Physical Activity Project.
- [ ] E1 Evaluating technology in analysing training and performance: objective, accurate, immediate feedback; trends over time; but cost, accuracy of consumer devices, need for expertise to interpret, risk of ignoring the "feel" and context of performance.

## 3. Traps

- Methods of analysis are taught in `pe-planning-optimum-performance` ("Methods of analysis"); here evaluate technology's role, don't re-list methods.
- Media coverage (TV, social media) is the next subsection.
- Keep each stakeholder (performer, coach, official, spectator) separate; exam questions name one.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline two ways technology has impacted coaches (or performers, or officials) in a named activity; one advantage and one disadvantage of a new technology for officials.
- Discuss two advancements in technology that have enhanced spectators' experience.
- Examine how advances in technology have impacted performers or coaches, with examples.
- Analyse the role of technology in the evaluation of sporting performance.
- Name and explain two types of technology a coach might use in training.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Examine how developments in technology have impacted officials in physical activities of your choice."
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Evaluate the role of technology in the analysis of training and the evaluation of sporting performance."
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Name two types of technology and explain how a coach might use each in training."
