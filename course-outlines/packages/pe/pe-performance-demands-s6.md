## 1. Must cover

Training methods (each: what it is, components developed, energy system, a named activity it suits)
- [ ] M1 Continuous training: steady, moderate intensity without rest for at least 20 minutes in the aerobic zone; cardio-respiratory and muscular endurance (e.g. long runs, swims, cycles).
- [ ] M2 Interval training: periods of work alternated with rest or recovery; work-to-rest ratio, number of repetitions and sets set the demand; high-intensity intervals develop anaerobic capacity and speed, longer intervals develop aerobic fitness; suits intermittent sports.
- [ ] M3 Fartlek ("speed play"): continuous running with changes of pace and terrain; trains aerobic and anaerobic systems; suits games players.
- [ ] M4 Circuit training: a series of stations worked in turn with set work and rest times; can target muscular endurance, strength, cardio-respiratory endurance or sport skills.
- [ ] M5 Resistance (weight) training: working against a load; low repetitions with heavy loads build strength, high repetitions with light loads build muscular endurance; exercises such as squats, lunges, presses, rows, curls.
- [ ] M6 Plyometric training: jumping, bounding and hopping in which a lengthening (landing) contraction is followed immediately by a forceful shortening (take-off) contraction; develops power; needs a strength base, a good surface and low volume.
- [ ] M7 Flexibility training: static stretching (held), dynamic stretching (controlled movement through range, used in warm-ups), PNF stretching (stretch, contract against resistance, relax and stretch further).
- [ ] M8 Comparing methods for the three selected activities: e.g. interval and fartlek suit a hurler better than continuous training because the game is intermittent.

Training in and outside the activity
- [ ] A1 Training in the activity: conditioned games and sport-specific drills (specific, motivating, trains skill under fatigue). One bullet; detail is in "Applying the fitness components".
- [ ] A2 Training outside the activity: gym-based resistance training, plyometrics, swimming or cycling as cross-training, yoga or Pilates for flexibility and core strength.
- [ ] A3 Benefits of training outside the activity: overloads specific components precisely, builds a base, adds variety, reduces overuse of sport-specific movements. Limitation: less specific, so it must be linked back to the activity.

Designing a programme from test data
- [ ] D1 Steps: test results identify the weakest important components; set goals; select methods; apply FITT and the principles; plan sessions per week across several weeks with progression; include warm-up, cool-down and rest days; re-test to evaluate and adjust.
- [ ] D2 A worked outline programme for one named activity over about six weeks (illustrative values labelled as such).

Recovery and adaptation
- [ ] R1 Adaptation happens during recovery after training (supercompensation: the body rebuilds slightly above its previous level if recovery is adequate).
- [ ] R2 Strategies: cool-down (light activity and stretching); rehydration; refuelling with carbohydrate and protein soon after exercise; sleep; active recovery sessions; massage and foam rolling; cold-water immersion; compression garments; planned rest days.
- [ ] R3 Each strategy with its reason (e.g. carbohydrate restores muscle glycogen; protein repairs muscle tissue; sleep supports tissue repair).
- [ ] R4 Avoiding overtraining by planning recovery (link only; signs and schedules of overtraining are in "Safe practice", `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions`).

Periodisation
- [ ] P1 Periodisation: dividing a training plan into planned phases or cycles, each with a specific aim, so that volume, intensity and recovery vary systematically to bring the performer to peak at target competitions.
- [ ] P2 Cycles: macrocycle (long-term, often a year or season), mesocycle (a block of several weeks with one focus), microcycle (usually a week of sessions).
- [ ] P3 Phases: preparation (general base then specific fitness), competition (maintenance and peaking, with tapering before the main event: reduced volume, intensity kept), transition (active rest and recovery after the season).
- [ ] P4 Why it is used: peak at the right time, avoid overtraining and injury, develop qualities in a logical order, keep motivation through variety. Peaking for a single major event (e.g. a four-year Olympic cycle) uses a long-term plan built from these cycles.

## 3. Traps

- Plyometrics needs a strength base; do not recommend it for beginners without that caution.
- Periodisation timeframes: an Ordinary question matched "a full year" to the macrocycle, "two months" to the mesocycle and "two weeks" to the microcycle. Keep the order macro > meso > micro.
- Principles of training and FITT are taught in the previous subsection; signs of overtraining are in "Safe practice". Do not repeat them.
- Nutrition detail for recovery (what to eat) belongs to "Diet, nutrition and hydration"; here give the reason in one line.

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain two methods of fitness training to prepare athletes for a named activity; outline how weight training and circuit training could improve a chosen component.
- Identify two approaches to training outside a named activity and discuss how each helps (e.g. resistance training; yoga or Pilates).
- Explain two recovery strategies that support adaptation; describe two strategies to aid recovery after training.
- Define periodisation; why coaches use it; outline two named cycles or phases; match cycles to timeframes; read a periodised plan and identify the components targeted; explain its importance for an Olympic cycle.
- Name a method of training for speed and for flexibility, with an example activity for each.

## 5. Practice questions to write

2. 8 marks, Higher: "Explain two recovery strategies athletes use after exercise to support adaptation." Model: two strategies, each with the physiological reason.
3. 8 marks, Ordinary: "Name and describe two methods of fitness training suitable for a games player." Model: e.g. interval and fartlek, each described and linked to the game's demands.
