## 1. Must cover

- [ ] P1 Purpose: to stay alive and reach safety after an unexpected fall into water, often clothed and cold; conserve energy and heat until rescued.
- [ ] E1 Entries into unknown conditions: feet first, slowly (slide-in); straddle entry into known deep water keeps the head above water.
- [ ] D1 Submerging feet first: push down with the arms, legs together, to sink vertically.
- [ ] D2 Head-first surface dive: bend at the hips, drive the legs up vertically so their weight pushes the body down.
- [ ] D3 Feet-first surface dive: rise slightly, then sweep the arms up to sink vertically feet first; useful in unknown water.
- [ ] F1 Floating: lie back, head back, ears in the water, body and limbs spread to increase surface area; breathe calmly; with or without a buoyancy aid.
- [ ] T1 Treading water: upright body; legs use an eggbeater or breaststroke-type kick; hands scull flat back and forth near the surface.
- [ ] T2 Sculling: figure-of-eight hand movements that push water down to keep the head up or move gently.
- [ ] C1 Swimming in clothing: clothes trap water and add drag, so use slow, efficient strokes (breaststroke, sidestroke, back strokes with the arms under water); keep clothing on in cold water for insulation; maintain form.
- [ ] U1 Underwater swimming: breaststroke-type arm and leg action, eyes open, used to escape surface hazards; never hyperventilate beforehand.
- [ ] N1 Turns in distance swimming: efficient open turns at the wall to keep a steady pace over distance.
- [ ] H1 HELP (Heat Escape Lessening Posture): float with a buoyancy aid, knees drawn up to the chest and arms crossed over the chest to protect the main areas of heat loss (head, armpits, groin).
- [ ] H2 HUDDLE: two or more people with buoyancy aids hold each other closely, chests together, to share heat and stay together; children placed in the middle.
- [ ] S1 Cold water shock: a sudden gasp and rapid breathing on entry; stay still and calm for the first minute; float rather than thrash.
- [ ] S2 Hypothermia risk increases with time in cold water, so movement is limited to conserve heat unless safety is very close.

## 3. Traps

- In cold water, swimming hard loses heat faster; the HELP and HUDDLE positions keep still.
- Never recommend hyperventilating before underwater swimming.
