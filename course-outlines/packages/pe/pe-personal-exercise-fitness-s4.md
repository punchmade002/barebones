# Package: pe-personal-exercise-fitness-s4 — Applying training principles to each method

- **loId:** `pe-personal-exercise-fitness-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-personal-exercise-fitness` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus:** learners are expected to demonstrate the ability to apply the following aspects
  to each method of training: principles of training (readiness, specificity, progression,
  overload, rest/recovery); training zones; thresholds; work-recovery intervals;
  warm-up/cool-down.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. This subsection applies, it does not re-teach.
  The principles, FITT and training zones are defined in "Assessing fitness and the principles
  of training" (chapter on physical and psychological demands). Write in this order: a one-line
  reminder of each aspect; then a worked application table-style block for one aerobic method
  (e.g. continuous running) and one conditioning method (e.g. weight training), showing each
  aspect applied; then warm-up and cool-down specific to each method; then monitoring with heart
  rate and perceived exertion.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] A1 Readiness: one line, then applied (e.g. check fitness base and technique before adding interval or heavy weight work).
- [ ] A2 Specificity: applied (e.g. match the method to the goal: running for a runner's endurance; squats for jumping power).
- [ ] A3 Progression: applied (e.g. add 5 minutes per week to continuous runs; add a small load when all reps are completed with good form).
- [ ] A4 Overload: applied through FITT (frequency, intensity, time, type).
- [ ] A5 Rest and recovery: applied (rest days, recovery between sets, easy weeks).
- [ ] Z1 Training zones: aerobic zone about 60–80% of maximum heart rate (220 minus age), anaerobic zone about 80–90%; applied to each aerobic method.
- [ ] Z2 Thresholds: aerobic threshold (lower limit for a training effect) and anaerobic (lactate) threshold; continuous training near but below it, intervals above it.
- [ ] Z3 Work-recovery intervals: applied to intervals and circuits (e.g. 1:1, 1:2, 1:3) and to rest between weight sets (longer for strength, shorter for endurance), as typical guidance.
- [ ] W1 Warm-up and cool-down for each method: e.g. running (easy jog, dynamic leg drills; walk and static stretches), weight training (light cardio, mobility, warm-up sets with light load; stretching of muscles used), aerobics (low-impact moves building up; slowing tempo and stretching).
- [ ] M1 Monitoring: heart-rate monitor or pulse checks, rating of perceived exertion, a training log; adjusting the programme when targets are met early or not reached.
- [ ] E1 Worked example, continuous running for a 17-year-old: maximum heart rate 220 − 17 = 203; aerobic zone about 122–162 bpm; 3 runs a week of 25–40 minutes, progressing 5 minutes a week, with one rest day between runs (illustrative).
- [ ] E2 Worked example, weight training for muscular endurance: 2–3 sets of 15 or more repetitions at a light load, 30–60 seconds' rest, adding repetitions then load; warm-up sets; rest days between sessions for the same muscles (illustrative).

## 3. Traps

- Apply, don't re-define: one line per principle, then the application.
- Keep the specification's five named principles first; reversibility and variety may be mentioned in one bullet only.

## 4. What exams ask

- Apply principles of training to a method; FITT for a component; training zones and heart rate; work-recovery intervals.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Apply the principles of readiness, specificity, progression, overload and rest/recovery to a training method of your choice."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Calculate the aerobic training zone for a 17-year-old and explain how they could use it in continuous training."
