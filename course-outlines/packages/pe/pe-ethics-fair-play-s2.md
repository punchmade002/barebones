## 1. Must cover

Codes of ethics
- [ ] C1 A code of ethics: a written set of principles and standards of behaviour expected of everyone involved in a sport, usually published by its NGB or club. Codes of conduct set out specific expected behaviours for each group.
- [ ] C2 Why they exist: protect participants (especially children), promote fair play and respect, set standards, give a basis for discipline, protect the sport's image.
- [ ] C3 Ireland: Sport Ireland's Code of Ethics and Good Practice for Children's Sport (child-centred, safe sport); NGBs adopt codes of conduct and child safeguarding policies; clubs appoint children's officers or designated liaison persons.
- [ ] C4 Participants: play by the rules, respect opponents and officials, no cheating or doping, no abuse, do your best, be a good team-mate.
- [ ] C5 Parents: encourage rather than pressure, focus on enjoyment and effort, respect coaches' and officials' decisions, model good behaviour on the sideline, support the club.
- [ ] C6 Spectators: applaud good play by both sides, no abuse of players or officials, respect the venue and rules.
- [ ] C7 Coaches: put players' welfare first, treat all fairly, be qualified and safeguarding-trained, model fair play, never encourage cheating or doping, use positive coaching.
- [ ] C8 Club officials: run the club fairly and transparently, implement safeguarding, ensure qualified coaches and safe facilities, deal with complaints and discipline consistently.
- [ ] C9 Writing code statements that comply with the principles of ethical practice (e.g. "Players will accept the referee's decision without dissent"; "Coaches will give every player a fair opportunity to take part").

Sportsmanship and gamesmanship
- [ ] S1 Sportsmanship: playing within the rules and the spirit of the game, with respect, honesty and grace in winning and losing (e.g. helping a fallen opponent, kicking the ball out when an opponent is injured, shaking hands, applauding opponents).
- [ ] S2 Gamesmanship: bending the rules or using dubious, psychological or time-wasting tactics to gain an advantage without technically cheating (e.g. time-wasting, feigning injury, diving to win a free, sledging, deliberately delaying a restart, unsettling an opponent before a serve).
- [ ] S3 Influence on engagement: sportsmanship creates a positive, safe, enjoyable environment that keeps people involved and attracts new participants and volunteers; gamesmanship can sour the experience, cause conflict, lead to drop-out (especially of young players and officials), damage the sport's image, and may win in the short term.

## 3. Traps

- Gamesmanship is not outright cheating (e.g. doping is cheating, not gamesmanship).
- "You cannot use faking injury" appears in exam questions; give several different examples of gamesmanship.
- The principles themselves are in "Principles of ethical practice"; refer to them.

## 4. What exams ask

- What is a code of ethics for sport? Explain how a code of ethics applies to one of participant, parent, spectator, coach, club official.
- List statements that comply with the principles of ethical practice for a code of ethics.
- Discuss the difference between sportsmanship and gamesmanship; explain sportsmanship and its influence on engagement; define gamesmanship and give two examples; identify which picture shows each.
- Describe the impact of sportsmanship or gamesmanship on engagement, with two examples.
