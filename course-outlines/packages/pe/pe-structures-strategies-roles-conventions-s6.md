# Package: pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions-s6 — The role of official

- **loId:** `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions-s6` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions` · **syllabusRef:** 3.6
- **Syllabus:** "explain the rules and regulations pertaining to the selected physical
  activities"; "describe the safety regulations and procedures in the selected physical
  activities"; "implement appropriate safety checks on equipment, facilities and player
  clothing"; "use the appropriate scoring/recording systems for the selected activities";
  "describe the demands of officiating in terms of personal fitness, psychological readiness,
  personal attire and suitable equipment"; "demonstrate the ability to communicate assertively
  with participants"; "identify strategies to manage conflict in a variety of situations;
  between the official and participants, and/or between participants"; "evaluate their role as
  an official using an official they admire as the reference point". Ordinary and Higher level;
  no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: types of official and
  their roles; rules and safety regulations; pre-competition safety checks; scoring and
  recording systems; demands of officiating; assertive communication; managing conflict;
  self-evaluation against an admired official. Use at least three different activities so
  students can answer "choose three physical activities" questions.

## 1. Must cover

Officials and their roles
- [ ] O1 Types: referee/umpire (in charge of the contest), linesperson/assistant referee, umpires in Gaelic games (scores), judges (gymnastics, diving, dance), timekeepers, scorers, starters (athletics, swimming), video/TMO officials.
- [ ] O2 Roles: apply and interpret the rules, keep the game flowing, ensure safety, record scores and results, manage discipline, start and finish the contest.

Rules and safety regulations
- [ ] R1 Explaining the main rules of a selected activity (scoring, fouls and sanctions, restarts).
- [ ] R2 Safety regulations by activity and who enforces them (e.g. referee checks for jewellery and studs; a lifeguard or official enforces pool depth rules and no diving in shallow water; a high-jump official checks landing mats; a hurling referee enforces helmets).

Safety checks (before competition)
- [ ] S1 Equipment: balls correctly inflated, posts and nets secure, padding on posts where required.
- [ ] S2 Facilities: playing surface free of glass, holes and debris, lines clearly marked, adequate lighting, safe run-off areas.
- [ ] S3 Player clothing: correct footwear and studs, no jewellery, mandatory protective equipment (helmets, gumshields, shin guards), kits that distinguish teams.

Scoring and recording systems (outline for at least two activities with different systems)
- [ ] K1 Gaelic football and hurling: a goal (under the crossbar into the net) is worth 3 points; a point (over the crossbar between the posts) is worth 1; scores written as goals–points (e.g. 2-10 = 16).
- [ ] K2 Basketball: field goals are worth 2 points, or 3 from beyond the three-point line; free throws 1 point each.
- [ ] K3 Tennis: points love, 15, 30, 40, game; deuce and advantage; six games (with a two-game margin) wins a set.
- [ ] K4 Athletics and swimming: times and distances recorded; scoring by placing.
- [ ] K5 Judged activities (gymnastics, dance, diving): panels of judges score difficulty and execution/artistry; a deduction-based system from a maximum for execution.

Demands of officiating
- [ ] D1 Personal fitness: cardio-respiratory endurance and speed to keep up with play (e.g. a rugby or soccer referee covers a large distance with repeated high-intensity efforts), agility, positioning.
- [ ] D2 Psychological readiness: concentration for the full contest, confidence and decisiveness, composure under pressure from players, coaches and crowds, consistency, resilience after a mistake.
- [ ] D3 Personal attire: the correct, distinguishable uniform, suitable footwear for the surface, weather-appropriate layers.
- [ ] D4 Suitable equipment: whistle (and spare), watch/stopwatch, scorecard and pen, cards (yellow, red, black where used), coin for the toss, flags, communication headsets at higher levels.
- [ ] D5 Importance of each (e.g. a coin for the toss; attire marks the official out and conveys authority).

Communication and conflict
- [ ] A1 Assertive communication: clear, confident, firm and calm; decisive signals; eye contact; strong whistle; explaining decisions briefly; not aggressive or passive.
- [ ] M1 Managing conflict between official and participants: stay calm and neutral, explain the decision and the rule, speak through the captains, use the disciplinary sanctions consistently, do not argue or debate decisions at length.
- [ ] M2 Managing conflict between participants: separate players, early intervention and warnings, call the captains to calm their team-mates, use advantage or stoppages to cool the situation, apply sanctions consistently, report serious incidents.
- [ ] E1 Evaluating own officiating against an admired official: criteria (positioning, decision accuracy, communication, fitness, game management), honest comparison, one strength, one target, an action.

## 3. Traps

- Safe practice for organising events, warm-ups and injuries is in "Safe practice"; this subsection is the official's checks and regulations.
- Do not invent scoring rules for less common activities; describe the general method if unsure.
- Assertive is not aggressive.

## 4. What exams ask

- Describe the demands of officiating under two of: personal fitness, psychological readiness, personal attire (and equipment).
- Identify and describe strategies officials use to manage conflict between participants.
- Describe appropriate attire and equipment for an official in a named activity; the importance of two pieces of attire and two pieces of equipment.
- Outline two safety checks before a competition; for three activities, one safety regulation each and which official implements it.
- Outline the scoring/recording system for two activities with different systems; outline three rules and a scoring system for a named activity.
- Describe two roles of an official; outline the roles of two competition officials; identify three demands on a coach and outline scoring systems (mixed short question).
