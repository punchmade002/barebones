# Package: pe-games-s4 — Striking/fielding games

- **loId:** `pe-games-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-games` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (skills and techniques):**
  Cricket: batting (straight/on/off-drive, forward/back); batting (cut/pull/glance); bowling
  (medium or fast or spin, line/length); fielding close; fielding deep.
  Softball/rounders: catching/receiving/throwing (various); hitting/striking/batting;
  pitching/bowling (various: medium, fast, swerve, spin, line/length); fielding on base/close;
  fielding deep/outfield.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what striking/fielding
  games share; cricket (batting stance and grip, front-foot and back-foot drives, cut, pull and
  glance; bowling action, line and length, types of delivery; close and deep fielding); softball
  and rounders (catching and throwing, batting, pitching/bowling, fielding positions); basic
  rules and scoring; tactics; safety.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] G1 Shared features: one team bats to score runs (or rounders) while the other bowls/pitches and fields to stop runs and get batters out; innings structure.
- [ ] CB1 Cricket batting stance and grip: side-on stance, feet shoulder-width, head still and eyes level; hands together at the top of the handle.
- [ ] CB2 Drives (straight, on and off): front-foot drive to a full ball (step towards the ball, head over it, high elbow, straight bat follow-through); back-foot shots to shorter balls.
- [ ] CB3 Cut (square on the off side to a short, wide ball), pull (horizontal bat to a short ball on leg side), glance (deflecting the ball fine on the leg side).
- [ ] CW1 Bowling action: run-up, gather, side-on or front-on delivery stride, straight bowling arm (no throwing), release, follow-through.
- [ ] CW2 Line and length: aiming at or just outside off stump; good length makes the batter unsure whether to go forward or back.
- [ ] CW3 Types: medium and fast pace (seam and swing), spin (off-spin and leg-spin turning the ball off the pitch).
- [ ] CF1 Close fielding (slips, short leg): low, ready, soft hands; deep fielding (boundary): long barrier to stop the ball, attacking the ball, flat throw to the keeper.
- [ ] SR1 Softball/rounders catching and throwing: two-handed catch, hands forming a cup or basket, giving with the ball; overarm throw with a side-on stance and step towards the target.
- [ ] SR2 Hitting/batting: side-on stance, eyes on the ball, swing level through the ball, follow through, drop the bat (do not throw it) and run.
- [ ] SR3 Pitching/bowling (various): underarm action; in rounders the ball must pass between the batter's head and knee on the batting side; variations in pace, swerve and spin; line and length.
- [ ] SR4 Fielding positions: backstop, bowler, first to fourth post fielders (rounders) or basemen (softball), deep fielders covering the outfield; fielding on base (stretching to catch with a foot on the base) and deep (backing up).
- [ ] R1 Basic rules and scoring: cricket runs and boundaries (4 and 6), dismissals (bowled, caught, run out, LBW, stumped); rounders scores a rounder for reaching fourth post, a batter is out if caught, if a post is stumped before they reach it, or if they overtake another batter; softball scores runs for reaching home and three outs end the innings.
- [ ] T1 Tactics: batting into gaps, running between wickets or posts with calls ("yes", "no", "wait"); fielding placement for the batter and bowler, backing up throws; bowling to the field set.
- [ ] S1 Safety: protective equipment in cricket (helmet, pads, gloves, box), a hard ball's danger zone, batters drop not throw the bat, fielders call for catches.

## 3. Traps

- In cricket the bowling arm must not straighten during delivery (throwing is illegal).
- Rounders bowling is underarm.

## 4. What exams ask

- Striking/fielding games are less common contexts; use them for skill analysis, decision-making, levers (batting) and teamwork.
