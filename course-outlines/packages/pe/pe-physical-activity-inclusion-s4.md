- **Syllabus:** "examine if and how, participants with a disability could participate in the

  opportunities in their school and/or in the community"; "examine the provision of pathways
  towards excellence for participants in adapted physical activities". Ordinary and Higher

- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what adapted physical

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] A1 Adapted physical activity: physical activity modified (rules, equipment, environment, instruction) so that people with a disability can take part meaningfully and safely.
- [ ] A2 Integration (placing a participant in an existing activity and expecting them to fit in) versus inclusion (changing the provision so everyone can take part with appropriate challenge).
- [ ] A3 The inclusion spectrum: open activity (everyone does the same), modified activity (changes so all can take part together), parallel activity (groups by ability doing the same activity at different levels), separate activity (a group practises separately for a time), disability sport/reverse integration (non-disabled participants join a disability sport such as wheelchair basketball or boccia).
- [ ] S1 STEP framework: adapt Space (smaller or larger area, zones), Task (simplified rules, more time, fewer touches), Equipment (larger, lighter, softer, audible or brightly coloured balls; lower nets; ramps), People (smaller groups, matched opponents, buddies, roles).
- [ ] S2 Adaptations must keep the core challenge and the central purpose of the activity.
- [ ] P1 Applying STEP to the student's three selected activities: e.g. a team game for a wheelchair user (zones, extra time in possession, a lighter ball); athletics for a visually impaired runner (guide runner, tether); badminton for a player with limited mobility (smaller court, seated play).
- [ ] P2 Equipment adaptations in a named activity and two other adaptations (an Ordinary pattern): e.g. racquet size and weight, ball type, court size, rules on bounces.
- [ ] E1 Evaluating provision in school: accessible facilities, adapted equipment, trained staff, inclusive PE planning, extra-curricular opportunities, links with disability sport clubs; ways to enhance it (specialist equipment, staff training, inclusive sports days, a disability sport club).
- [ ] E2 Evaluating provision in the community: accessible venues and transport, inclusive clubs, disability sport clubs, Local Sports Partnership programmes and inclusion officers; cost and awareness as gaps.
- [ ] X1 Pathways to excellence: inclusive club → disability sport club and NGB programmes → national squads → Paralympic or Special Olympics competition; classification systems in Paralympic sport group athletes with similar levels of impairment so competition is fair; funding, coaching and carding for elite para-athletes.
- [ ] X2 Barriers on the pathway (few specialist coaches, cost of adapted equipment such as racing wheelchairs or running blades, travel to specialist centres) and how they could be improved.

## 3. Traps

- Special Olympics (intellectual disability) and the Paralympics (mainly physical, visual and some intellectual impairment classes) are different; do not merge them.
- Adaptations should not remove the challenge; avoid answers that turn the activity into something else.
- Supports and barriers for people with disabilities in general are in "Supports and barriers for selected groups".

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain "adapted physical activity"; outline two ways a school or community can provide for it; suggest three ways a school could enhance provision.
- How equipment can be adapted for an athlete with a disability in a named activity; two other adaptations.
- Examine ways participants in adapted physical activities can be given opportunities to achieve excellence.
- Explain two ways coaches can adapt activities to be more inclusive (Ordinary).
- Using the STEP framework, adapt a named team game for a wheelchair user (in the app's test questions).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Using the STEP framework, examine how a participant with a physical disability could take part in a physical activity of your choice."
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Examine ways in which participants in adapted physical activities can be given opportunities to achieve excellence."
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Explain 'adapted physical activity' and outline two ways a school can provide for it."
