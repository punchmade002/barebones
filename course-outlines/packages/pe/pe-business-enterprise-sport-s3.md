# Package: pe-business-enterprise-sport-s3 — Mass participation in sport

- **loId:** `pe-business-enterprise-sport-s3` · **chapterId:** `pe-business-enterprise-sport` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 10.3
- **Prescribed for June 2027** (SEC circular S51/25). Write in full Higher Level depth.
- **Syllabus:** "examine the growth of mass participation sporting events and the opportunities
  for business and enterprise they provide". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only
  material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what mass-participation
  events are, with Irish examples; reasons for their growth; business and enterprise
  opportunities (one block, each opportunity with who benefits); benefits to participants and
  communities (short); costs and problems.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] M1 Mass-participation sporting events: organised events in which large numbers of people of mixed ability take part at the same time, usually for personal challenge, health or charity rather than to win (e.g. city marathons, fun runs, parkrun, cycling sportives, open-water swims, obstacle and colour runs, triathlons).
- [ ] M2 Irish examples (checked): the Dublin Marathon; the Women's Mini Marathon in Dublin; parkrun; the Great Limerick Run.

Reasons for growth
- [ ] G1 Health and fitness awareness and national promotion of activity.
- [ ] G2 Charity fundraising and personal challenge goals ("bucket list" events).
- [ ] G3 Social media, apps and wearable trackers that make training and sharing results easy.
- [ ] G4 Inclusive formats: walk, jog or run; shorter distances; women-only events; free weekly events.
- [ ] G5 Sponsorship and professional event organisers who market events widely.
- [ ] G6 Running and cycling clubs, couch-to-5k style programmes and workplace challenges.

Business and enterprise opportunities (each: what it is, who benefits)
- [ ] B1 Entry fees: income for the organiser and charities.
- [ ] B2 Title and partner sponsorship: brands gain exposure to thousands of participants and spectators.
- [ ] B3 Merchandise and event products: branded T-shirts, medals, photos.
- [ ] B4 Retail: running shoes, clothing, watches and trackers, nutrition products bought for training.
- [ ] B5 Services: coaching, training plans and apps, physiotherapy and sports massage, timing companies.
- [ ] B6 Local economy: hotels, restaurants, pubs, shops and transport busy on event weekends; visitors from outside the area.
- [ ] B7 Media and advertising: coverage and online content.
- [ ] B8 Charities raise large sums through participants' fundraising.
- [ ] B9 Enterprise: new businesses set up to organise events, sell event photography, provide timing chips or run training groups.

Benefits and costs
- [ ] S1 Benefits to participants and communities: health, goal-setting, community spirit, inclusivity, charity income (short; full benefits are in the notes on promoting physical activity).
- [ ] C1 Costs and problems: road closures and disruption to residents, policing and stewarding costs, litter and environmental impact, rising entry fees that exclude lower-income groups, injury risk for unprepared participants, events dominated by commercial interests.

## 3. Traps

- Do not copy the past-paper description of the Great Limerick Run's popularity.
- Media coverage of mass participation is in "Media in sport" (Topic 8); refer to it in one bullet at most.
- Tourism from events in general is the next subsection; here focus on the event's own business opportunities and local spending.

## 4. What exams ask

- Describe two opportunities that mass-participation events provide for business and enterprise (Higher).
- Define mass-participation sporting events; give two examples; what opportunities they provide for businesses (Ordinary).
- Discuss two ways the growth of mass-participation events has provided opportunities for business in recent years.
- Mass-participation events such as the Olympics provide opportunities for business: analyse (case study).
