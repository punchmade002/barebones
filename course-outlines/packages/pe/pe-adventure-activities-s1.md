# Package: pe-adventure-activities-s1 — The adventure activities area

- **loId:** `pe-adventure-activities-s1` · **chapterId:** `pe-adventure-activities` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6 (Physical activity areas)
- **Syllabus:** "Individual and co-operative activities that take place in a challenging
  environment, usually outdoors, in which elements that cannot be fully controlled, influence
  the selection and use of skills and strategies to attain set goals. There is a particular
  emphasis on interpersonal skills and reflection on learning." Learners may choose one of
  orienteering, canoeing/kayaking, rock-climbing, sailing, rowing/sculling. Section 6 also says
  that in each activity learners aim to develop the standard of their performance, be creative
  as an individual and/or team member, and be consistent in the quality of their performance.
  Section 7: the performance assessment (30%, common level, assessed by the SEC from video)
  requires the listed skills, tactical/compositional knowledge, and rules and safe practice.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. A short overview subsection. Write in this
  order: what the area is; the five activities and the choice rule; features common to
  adventure activities (environment, risk, interpersonal skills, reflection); safety
  management common to all; what the performance assessment asks for. Each activity has its own
  subsection; do not teach activity skills here.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] A1 The specification's definition of the area (paraphrase): individual and co-operative activities in a challenging, usually outdoor environment, where uncontrollable elements influence the choice and use of skills and strategies.
- [ ] A2 The choice: learners study three activities, each from a different area; from this area they choose one of orienteering, canoeing/kayaking, rock-climbing, sailing or rowing/sculling.
- [ ] A3 Uncontrollable elements: weather, wind, water conditions, terrain, visibility, daylight; performers must read conditions and adapt their plan.
- [ ] A4 Interpersonal skills: communication, trust, teamwork, leadership and decision-making in a group; co-operation with partners (belayer and climber, crew in a boat).
- [ ] A5 Reflection on learning: reviewing decisions and route or plan choices after each session.
- [ ] A6 Managed risk: the challenge comes from real but controlled risk.
- [ ] S1 Safety common to all: risk assessment of the site and conditions; qualified instruction and supervision; checking weather and tide forecasts; appropriate equipment checked before use (helmets, buoyancy aids, harnesses); telling someone the plan and return time; emergency and communication plans; first aid; group size and ratios; respecting the environment (Leave No Trace style behaviour: take litter home, stay on paths, avoid disturbing wildlife).
- [ ] S2 Respect for the environment is part of the orienteering skill list and good practice in all adventure activities.
- [ ] P1 The performance assessment: one of the student's three activities; the listed skills and techniques, applied in a conditioned practice, competitive version or personal performance; tactics/strategies; rules, regulations, codes of practice and safe practice before, during and after.
- [ ] P2 Using adventure activities as the "named activity" in written answers: e.g. confidence and anxiety management, decision-making under uncertainty, fitness demands (muscular endurance, cardio-respiratory endurance, balance), safe practice.
