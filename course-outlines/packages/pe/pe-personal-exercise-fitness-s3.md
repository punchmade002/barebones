# Package: pe-personal-exercise-fitness-s3 — Conditioning and resistance

- **loId:** `pe-personal-exercise-fitness-s3` · **chapterId:** `pe-personal-exercise-fitness` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (conditioning and resistance and what each requires):** weight training (upper
  extremity pushing: pressing, extensions; upper extremity pulling: rows, curls; lower extremity
  exercises: squats, lunges); core stability (relaxation and breathing; control; routine;
  alignment; flow); circuit training (exercises specific to themes, equipment, work-recovery
  intervals).
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: weight training (safety
  principles; each listed exercise group with one or two exercises and key technique points;
  sets, repetitions and load for strength versus muscular endurance); core stability (what the
  core is; each listed element; example exercises); circuit training (designing by theme and
  equipment; work-recovery intervals; an example circuit).

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] W1 Weight-training safety: warm-up, correct technique before load, controlled movement, breathing out on the effort, a spotter for heavy pressing, checking collars on bars, progressive loading.
- [ ] W2 Upper-body pushing, pressing: bench press or shoulder press (grip just wider than shoulders, lower under control, press to full extension without locking aggressively, back flat on the bench).
- [ ] W3 Upper-body pushing, extensions: triceps extension (elbows still, extend at the elbow only).
- [ ] W4 Upper-body pulling, rows: bent-over or seated row (flat back, pull the elbows back, squeeze the shoulder blades).
- [ ] W5 Upper-body pulling, curls: biceps curl (elbows fixed at the sides, no swinging).
- [ ] W6 Lower body, squats: feet shoulder-width, chest up, knees track over toes, sit back to thighs parallel, drive up through the heels.
- [ ] W7 Lower body, lunges: long step, front knee over the ankle, back knee towards the floor, upright trunk.
- [ ] W8 Loading: strength uses heavy loads and few repetitions (about 3–6) with longer rests; muscular endurance uses light loads and many repetitions (about 15 or more) with short rests (typical guidance).
- [ ] K1 Core stability: the core is the muscles of the trunk (abdominals, back and pelvis) that stabilise the spine; benefits include posture, balance, transfer of force and injury prevention.
- [ ] K2 Relaxation and breathing: controlled breathing, breathing out on effort, no breath-holding.
- [ ] K3 Control: slow, precise movements; holding positions without shaking or sagging.
- [ ] K4 Routine: a planned sequence of exercises (e.g. plank, side plank, bird-dog, dead bug, glute bridge).
- [ ] K5 Alignment: neutral spine, head in line, hips level.
- [ ] K6 Flow: smooth transitions between exercises.
- [ ] C1 Circuit training: a series of stations worked in turn; themes (e.g. muscular endurance, cardio, sport-specific skills), equipment (body weight, medicine balls, kettlebells, steps), work-recovery intervals (e.g. 30 seconds work, 30 seconds rest, as typical guidance).
- [ ] C2 Designing a circuit: alternate muscle groups (upper, lower, core, cardio) so each recovers; 8–10 stations; set the number of circuits; progress by more work time, less rest or more circuits.
- [ ] C3 Example circuit for a games player (illustrative).

## 3. Traps

- Do not teach muscle anatomy beyond naming the main muscle worked in one phrase; that belongs to the 2026 specification, not this course.
- Strength and muscular endurance loading are opposites; do not mix them up.

## 4. What exams ask

- Weight and circuit training to improve a named component; the difference between training for strength and for muscular endurance; principles of training applied.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Describe how weight training and circuit training could be used to improve muscular endurance in a physical activity of your choice."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Give two key technique points for a squat and explain why each matters."
