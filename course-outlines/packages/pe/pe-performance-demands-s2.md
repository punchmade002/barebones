- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. One block per component: definition, the forms

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] H1 Cardio-respiratory endurance (aerobic fitness, stamina): the ability of the heart, lungs and blood vessels to take in, transport and use oxygen to sustain whole-body activity over a long period. Important in distance running, Gaelic football, soccer, swimming over distance. Linked to the aerobic energy system.
- [ ] H2 Muscular endurance: the ability of a muscle or muscle group to perform repeated contractions over a period of time without fatigue (e.g. the legs of a rower or cyclist, the arms in repeated tackles). Distinguish from cardio-respiratory endurance (local to a muscle group versus whole body).
- [ ] H3 Strength: the maximum force a muscle or muscle group can exert against a resistance in a single contraction. Forms: maximal strength (one maximal effort, e.g. a rugby scrum), explosive or elastic strength (force applied quickly; leads into power), strength endurance (repeated sub-maximal force); static strength (no movement, e.g. holding a position) and dynamic strength (with movement).
- [ ] H4 Flexibility: the range of movement possible at a joint or series of joints. Static flexibility (range held at rest) and dynamic flexibility (range used during movement). Important in gymnastics, dance, hurdles, goalkeeping; reduces injury risk.
- [ ] H5 Body composition: the relative proportions of fat mass and lean (fat-free) mass (muscle, bone, organs) in the body, usually expressed as body-fat percentage. Different activities favour different compositions (low body fat for distance running; more lean mass for rugby forwards).
- [ ] H6 Somatotypes as context for body composition (assumed, one bullet): ectomorph (lean, linear), mesomorph (muscular), endomorph (rounder, higher fat); most people are a mix.
- [ ] H7 For each component, one health benefit (e.g. cardio-respiratory endurance lowers the risk of heart disease; flexibility helps everyday movement and reduces injury) as well as the performance benefit.
- [ ] H8 Muscular endurance versus strength training from the perspective of intensity: endurance uses lower loads and more repetitions; strength uses heavy loads and few repetitions (a 2022 Higher dance-fitness part). One bullet; load and repetition ranges are in "Designing a fitness plan".

## 3. Traps

- Cardio-respiratory endurance is whole-body; muscular endurance is local to a muscle group. Do not use one to define the other.
- Power is performance-related fitness (next subsection), not a form of strength in the specification's list. Explosive strength may be mentioned as the link.
- Body composition is not the same as body weight or BMI.
- Fitness tests belong in "Assessing fitness and the principles of training". Training methods belong in "Designing a fitness plan".

## 4. What exams ask

- Define five components of health-related fitness (Higher, 40-mark part).
- Define cardio-respiratory endurance, body composition and flexibility, then apply FITT to one of them (the FITT part is in "Assessing fitness").
- Define flexibility; why flexibility matters for a tennis player.
- Name the most important HRF component for a chosen activity (the test part is in "Assessing fitness").
- Describe the difference between training for muscular endurance and muscular strength in terms of intensity.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Define the five components of health-related fitness." Model: five precise definitions as in H1–H5, each with a one-line example.
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Define flexibility and explain why it is important in a physical activity of your choice." Model: definition; two reasons tied to the activity (range for technique, injury prevention).
