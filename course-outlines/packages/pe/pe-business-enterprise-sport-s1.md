## 1. Must cover

Definitions
- [ ] D1 Sponsorship: a business provides money, products or services to a sport, team, event or performer in return for association, exposure and commercial benefit (e.g. logo on jerseys, naming rights, title sponsorship of a competition).
- [ ] D2 Endorsement: a performer is paid to publicly promote and use a product or brand.
- [ ] D3 Merchandising: selling branded goods (jerseys, scarves, equipment, souvenirs) connected with a sport, team, event or performer.
- [ ] D4 Sport-related advertising: advertising that uses sport, sports settings or athletes to sell products, including products unrelated to sport.
- [ ] D5 What a sponsor gains: brand awareness and exposure to a large audience, a positive image by association with success and health, access to target markets, hospitality, sales.

Impact on sport and performers
- [ ] I1 Sponsorship: funds grassroots programmes, facilities, coaching, events and broadcasting; allows elite athletes to train full-time.
- [ ] I2 Endorsements: large income for star performers; raise their profile; obligations (appearances, filming) take time; the athlete's image is tied to the brand and vice versa.
- [ ] I3 Merchandising: major income for clubs and NGBs; builds fan identity; replica kits change often and are expensive for families.

Advantages and disadvantages of sponsorship
- [ ] N1 For the NGB: advantages (income for development and high performance, facilities, coach education, marketing expertise, profile); disadvantages (dependence on one sponsor, loss of income if the sponsor withdraws, sponsor influence over scheduling and decisions, reputational damage from an unsuitable sponsor, ethical conflicts such as alcohol or gambling brands).
- [ ] P1 For the performer: advantages (income to train full-time, free equipment and travel, profile and career after sport); disadvantages (contractual obligations and time demands, pressure to perform and win, loss of the deal after injury or poor form, loss of privacy, must not damage the brand, conflicts with team sponsors, being linked to a product they would not choose).
- [ ] S1 For the sport/activity: advantages (higher profile and media coverage, better events and facilities, more participants); disadvantages (sponsors favour popular sports and men's and elite sport, so others struggle; commercialisation and changes to suit sponsors and TV; the sport's image linked to unhealthy products).

Sport-related advertising and its messages to young people
- [ ] A1 Forms: television and online adverts with athletes; social media and influencer content; shirt and stadium advertising; product placement; branded events and competitions.
- [ ] A2 Positive messages: being active is enjoyable and achievable; effort and perseverance pay off; role models of diverse backgrounds and abilities; healthy lifestyles.
- [ ] A3 Negative messages: linking sport with unhealthy products (sugary drinks, fast food, alcohol, gambling); unrealistic body ideals; success depends on expensive gear; winning at all costs; gambling normalised through betting adverts during matches.
- [ ] A4 Analysing an advert: who is shown, what product, what message about sport, bodies and success, and its likely effect on young viewers.

Code of practice for youth sport sponsorship
- [ ] C1 No sponsors whose products are inappropriate for young people: alcohol, gambling, tobacco and vaping, high-fat/high-sugar food and drink brands.
- [ ] C2 The sponsor's values and conduct must align with the club's code of ethics, child safeguarding, inclusion and fair play.
- [ ] C3 No use of children's images, names or data in marketing without parental consent.
- [ ] C4 Sponsorship benefits the whole club fairly (girls' and boys' teams, all ages), not only elite teams.
- [ ] C5 The sponsor has no say over team selection, coaching or club decisions.
- [ ] C6 Transparent written agreement approved by the committee, with a fixed term and a review and exit clause.
- [ ] C7 Each statement justified (e.g. a logo on a child's jersey is a constant endorsement to the child and everyone watching, so an unhealthy brand contradicts the club's health message).

## 3. Traps

- Sponsorship is a business relationship with a sport, team or event; endorsement is a performer promoting a product. Keep them distinct.
- Give advantages and disadvantages for each of the three stakeholders separately; exam questions name one.
- The business dimension of a named company is the next subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define sponsorship and endorsement; explain sponsorship, merchandising and sport-related advertising; outline the difference between merchandising and endorsement with examples.
- Advantages and disadvantages of sponsorship for performers; advantages for the NGB; impacts on a named NGB; positive impact of sponsorship deals for an athlete; disadvantages for an athlete.
- Discuss the impact of merchandising on sport.
- Analyse two forms of sport-related advertising in terms of their messages to young people; positive and negative messages.
- State and justify three criteria or statements for a code of practice for selecting commercial sponsorship for a youth team.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of sponsorship for a national governing body, the performers and the sport."
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Write three statements you would include in a code of practice for selecting commercial sponsorship for a youth sports team, and justify each."
3. 8 marks, Higher: "Analyse two different forms of sport-related advertising in terms of the messages they give to young people."
