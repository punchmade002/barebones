  **syllabusRef:** 2.4
- **Syllabus:** "examine the extent to which different components of health- and

- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: analysing the fitness

## 1. Must cover

Analysing the demands of an activity
- [ ] N1 Needs analysis (assumed): working out which components an activity, position or role demands by looking at its duration, intensity, work-to-rest pattern, the movements involved and the energy systems used; then comparing the performer's current fitness with those demands.
- [ ] N2 "Examine the extent": rank components as essential, important or less important for the activity and justify each with a specific moment of play or performance.
- [ ] N3 Positional and role differences within one activity (e.g. a soccer goalkeeper needs reaction time, power and agility more than cardio-respiratory endurance; a midfielder the reverse).

Worked profiles (one block each; essential components with reasons)
- [ ] P1 An invasion game (e.g. Gaelic football or soccer): cardio-respiratory endurance, speed, agility, power, muscular endurance.
- [ ] P2 An individual closed or aesthetic activity (e.g. gymnastics): flexibility, strength, balance, power, co-ordination; cardio-respiratory endurance less important.
- [ ] P3 An endurance activity (e.g. distance running or swimming): cardio-respiratory endurance, muscular endurance, body composition; reaction time less important.

Developing fitness in a demanding practice context
- [ ] D1 Training fitness within the activity: conditioned and small-sided games, game-speed drills and skill circuits, so that fitness is developed while the skill is practised under fatigue and pressure.
- [ ] D2 Benefits: specificity (same movements, energy systems and decisions as competition); motivation; time-efficient; trains skill execution under fatigue.
- [ ] D3 Limitations: harder to control intensity and overload precisely; may not develop maximal strength or flexibility; some players work less than others. So it is combined with training outside the activity (in "Designing a fitness plan").
- [ ] D4 Examples: a small-sided possession game with restricted touches (cardio-respiratory endurance, agility, speed); a dribbling relay through cones (speed, agility, co-ordination).

Combined approach
- [ ] C1 A combined approach develops a health-related and a performance-related component in the same activity or session (e.g. flexibility and balance together; muscular endurance and agility in a circuit).
- [ ] C2 Designing one: name the two components, describe the activity (what the performer does, sets/repetitions or time, progression), explain how each component is trained, and link it to the activity's demands.
- [ ] C3 Example: a single-leg balance-and-reach routine on an unstable surface, extended through a full range, trains balance (PRF) and flexibility (HRF); a circuit alternating ladder agility drills with body-weight exercises trains agility (PRF) and muscular endurance (HRF).

## 3. Traps

- Do not name or describe fitness tests here (they are in "Assessing fitness and the principles of training"), except to say a needs analysis is followed by testing.
- Training methods (continuous, interval, fartlek, weights, plyometrics) are taught in "Designing a fitness plan". Mention a method only as part of a combined-approach example.
- A combined approach must name one HRF and one PRF component and train both together.

## 4. What exams ask

- Choose one HRF and one PRF component from a list and outline one activity that trains both as a combined approach (8 marks, Higher).
- Discuss the extent to which named components (e.g. reaction time, muscular endurance, power) are required in a chosen activity.
- Identify the main component of fitness for two contrasting events (e.g. 10,000 m and shot-put) and explain.
- Identify fitness components required in a chosen activity (Ordinary, often in a table).

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Discuss the extent to which reaction time, muscular endurance and power are required in a physical activity of your choice." Model: one activity; each component rated and justified with specific moments; a reasoned conclusion on which matters most.
2. 8 marks, Higher: "Outline one activity that could be used as a combined approach to training flexibility and balance in a physical activity of your choice." Model: a described activity with progression, explaining how each component is trained.
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Identify the main component of fitness needed by a marathon runner and by a shot-putter and explain each choice."
