# Package: pe-performance-demands-s5 — Assessing fitness and the principles of training

## 1. Must cover

Fitness testing
- [ ] T1 Purposes: identify strengths and weaknesses against the demands of the activity, set realistic goals, design and monitor a training programme, motivate, compare with norms (normative data).
- [ ] T2 Good testing practice: tests must be valid (measure what they claim to measure) and reliable (give consistent results when repeated); standardised protocol each time (same warm-up, surface, time of day, equipment, tester); safe; test before and after a programme to show progress.
- [ ] T3 Field tests (practical, cheap, many people at once) versus laboratory tests (more accurate, expensive). Keep to one bullet.

A test for each component (name, how it is carried out in outline, what is recorded)
- [ ] C1 Cardio-respiratory endurance: multistage fitness test (bleep test, 20 m shuttles at increasing pace until the performer cannot keep up; level reached recorded, VO2 max estimated); Cooper 12-minute run (distance covered); Yo-Yo intermittent recovery test for intermittent team sports.
- [ ] C2 Muscular endurance: press-up test and sit-up (curl-up) test, maximum repetitions in one minute or to exhaustion; plank hold time.
- [ ] C3 Strength: hand-grip dynamometer; one-repetition maximum (1RM) on a lift.
- [ ] C4 Flexibility: sit-and-reach test (hamstrings and lower back).
- [ ] C5 Body composition: skinfold callipers at set sites; bioelectrical impedance; BMI (with its limitation that muscular people can score as overweight).
- [ ] C6 Speed: 30 m sprint (from a standing start, timed).
- [ ] C7 Agility: Illinois agility run; T-test.
- [ ] C8 Power: vertical (sergeant) jump; standing broad jump.
- [ ] C9 Balance: stork stand (time balanced on one foot).
- [ ] C10 Co-ordination: alternate-hand wall-toss test (catches in 30 seconds).
- [ ] C11 Reaction time: ruler drop test (distance the ruler falls before it is caught).

Designing a test battery
- [ ] B1 A test battery is a set of tests chosen to measure the components a specific activity demands, based on a needs analysis.
- [ ] B2 Steps: analyse the activity's demands; select the key components; choose a valid, reliable, specific test for each; order the tests so fatigue does not distort results (non-fatiguing tests such as flexibility, power and agility first; maximal endurance test last); standardise conditions; record and compare with norms or previous results.
- [ ] B3 Worked battery for one named team game (e.g. soccer: 30 m sprint, Illinois agility, vertical jump, Yo-Yo test), each test justified by the activity's demands.

Principles of training (each: meaning, and its value from a performance perspective)
- [ ] P1 Specificity: training must match the demands of the activity (energy systems, muscles, movements, speeds).
- [ ] P2 Progression: training load is increased gradually as the body adapts.
- [ ] P3 Overload: the body must work harder than normal to adapt; achieved by raising frequency, intensity or time (FITT).
- [ ] P4 Rest and recovery: adaptation happens during recovery; too little causes overtraining.
- [ ] P5 Readiness: the performer must be physically and mentally ready (age, maturity, fitness base, skill) for the training.
- [ ] P6 Also accepted in exam answers: reversibility (fitness gains are lost if training stops), individual differences (programmes suit the individual), variety/avoiding tedium (vary training to keep motivation and avoid overuse).
- [ ] P7 Evaluating the principles from a performance perspective: e.g. specificity transfers training to competition but can neglect general fitness; overload drives improvement but raises injury risk if progression is too fast.

FITT formula
- [ ] F1 Frequency (how often), Intensity (how hard), Time (how long), Type (what kind of exercise).
- [ ] F2 FITT applied to each of the eleven components (one line each, e.g. flexibility: 3–5 times a week, stretch to mild tension, hold each stretch about 30 seconds, static or dynamic stretching). Values are typical guidance; label them as such.

Training zones and thresholds
- [ ] Z1 Maximum heart rate estimated as 220 minus age.
- [ ] Z2 Aerobic training zone about 60–80% of maximum heart rate; anaerobic training zone about 80–90%.
- [ ] Z3 Thresholds: the aerobic threshold (lower edge of the aerobic zone) and the anaerobic (lactate) threshold (the intensity above which lactic acid builds up faster than it can be removed).
- [ ] Z4 Monitoring intensity by heart rate or by the rating of perceived exertion (RPE).

## 3. Traps

- Validity and reliability are different; don't merge them.
- Do not put the endurance test first in a battery.
- The 2026 specification names extra principles and "validity and reliability" as outcomes; here they are applied to testing only, not taught as a separate outcome. Do not refer to the 2026 specification.
- Training methods and periodisation belong to "Designing a fitness plan". Recovery strategies are there too.
- Do not copy test norms or results tables from past papers.

## 4. What exams ask

- Design a performance-related fitness test battery for a named activity (table: component, test, justification).
- Name and justify tests for a combine-style trial in a named sport.
- Name and describe a test for muscular endurance or for a named component.
- Define three principles of training; name another and outline its importance; examine the impact of two principles on performance; explain how a coach uses two principles in programme design.
- Identify the principle described in a case study (e.g. a gradual increase in load for young players: progression or readiness).
- Describe the FITT formula; apply it to a named component.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Design a fitness test battery for a physical activity of your choice, justifying each test." Model: needs analysis in one sentence; four tests for four components; ordering and standardisation.
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Examine the impact that two principles of training can have on the performance of an athlete." Model: two principles, each defined, applied and evaluated.
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Apply the FITT formula to cardio-respiratory endurance." Model: one line for each letter, with typical values labelled as guidance.
