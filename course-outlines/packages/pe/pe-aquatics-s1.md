# Package: pe-aquatics-s1 — The aquatics area

- **loId:** `pe-aquatics-s1` · **chapterId:** `pe-aquatics` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus:** "Water-based activities that utilise swimming and/or diving skills and
  techniques for performance, water safety, or recreational purposes." Learners may choose one
  of lifesaving, survival swim, two swimming strokes, water-polo, synchronised swimming.
  Section 6: in each activity learners aim to develop the standard of their performance, be
  creative, and be consistent. Section 7: the performance assessment (30%, common level, SEC
  assessed from video) covers the listed skills, tactics/compositional knowledge, and rules and
  safe practice.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. A short overview. Write in this order: what the
  area is; the five options and the choice rule; water safety common to all aquatic activities;
  what the performance assessment asks for; using aquatics as the named activity in written
  answers. Activity skills belong in their own subsections.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] A1 The specification's definition of the area (paraphrase): water-based activities using swimming and/or diving skills for performance, water safety or recreation.
- [ ] A2 The choice: one of lifesaving, survival swimming, two swimming strokes, water polo or synchronised swimming; lifesaving is the specification's own example aquatics activity.
- [ ] A3 Purposes: performance (racing strokes, water polo, synchronised swimming), water safety (lifesaving, survival), recreation.
- [ ] S1 Pool safety: lifeguard supervision, knowing depths, no diving in shallow water, walking not running on poolside, entering water feet-first when the depth is unknown, pool rules, hygiene (showering).
- [ ] S2 Open-water safety: cold water shock, currents and tides, never swim alone, bright swim caps, check conditions, swim parallel to the shore.
- [ ] P1 The performance assessment: one of the student's three activities; the listed skills and techniques applied in conditioned, competitive or personal performance; tactics or compositional knowledge; rules and safe practice before, during and after.
- [ ] W1 Aquatics as a named activity in written answers: e.g. energy systems in sprint and distance swimming, technique analysis of a stroke, Newton's third law in propulsion, streamlining, the role of a lifeguard as an official.

## 3. Traps

- Do not teach strokes or rescue techniques here; they have their own notes.
- Do not give coursework instructions.

## 4. What exams ask

- Written questions using aquatics as context: energy demands of two swimming events, technology helping a swimmer, NGB support (Swim Ireland), safety regulations at a pool.

## 5. Practice questions to write
