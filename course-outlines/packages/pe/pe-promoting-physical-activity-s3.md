# Package: pe-promoting-physical-activity-s3 — Physical activity promotion

- **loId:** `pe-promoting-physical-activity-s3` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-promoting-physical-activity` · **syllabusRef:** 5.3
- **Syllabus:** "analyse the role of national and local policies in the promotion of physical
  activity"; "examine the role of the national governing bodies of their selected physical
  activities, including how the activity is provided for at local, national and international
  level"; "design a physical activity promotion and adherence tip sheet for a specific target
  group"; "discuss the characteristics of significant people who promote and support others'
  participation in physical activity". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only
  material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: national policies and
  bodies; local structures; national governing bodies (role at local, national, international
  level, with named examples); designing a promotion and adherence tip sheet; significant
  people.

## 1. Must cover

National and local policy
- [ ] N1 National Physical Activity Plan for Ireland: the government's plan to increase physical activity levels across the population, with actions across health, education, transport, sport and communities.
- [ ] N2 National Sports Policy 2018–2027: the government's ten-year policy to increase sport participation, develop high performance and improve sports facilities.
- [ ] N3 Healthy Ireland: the government framework to improve the health and wellbeing of the population, including physical activity.
- [ ] N4 Sport Ireland: the statutory body responsible for developing sport in Ireland; funds NGBs and Local Sports Partnerships, runs participation programmes and anti-doping, and supports high performance.
- [ ] N5 Local Sports Partnerships (LSPs): Sport Ireland-funded local bodies in counties and cities that increase participation locally, especially among under-represented groups, through local programmes, coach education, community events and information.
- [ ] N6 Local policies and actions: county council recreation and active-travel plans (greenways, parks, cycle lanes), school physical activity policies, club development plans.
- [ ] N7 Analysing the role of a named policy: its aims, the actions it funds, who it targets, strengths (coordination, funding) and limitations (implementation and funding gaps, reliance on volunteers).

National governing bodies
- [ ] G1 An NGB is the organisation that governs a sport or activity in a country: sets and enforces rules, organises competitions, develops coaches and officials, supports clubs, selects national teams, promotes participation, and links to the international federation.
- [ ] G2 Named NGBs (at least the ones for common activities): GAA (Gaelic football and hurling), Camogie Association, Ladies Gaelic Football Association (LGFA), Football Association of Ireland (FAI), Irish Rugby Football Union (IRFU), Basketball Ireland, Hockey Ireland, Athletics Ireland, Swim Ireland, Badminton Ireland, Tennis Ireland, Gymnastics Ireland, Rowing Ireland.
- [ ] G3 Local level: affiliating clubs and giving them governance, insurance, safeguarding policies and codes of conduct; coach, official and first-aid education; development officers working with schools and clubs; local leagues and blitzes; grants for facilities and equipment.
- [ ] G4 National level: national competitions and leagues, rules and disciplinary systems, national coaching structures, talent pathways and development squads, participation programmes and campaigns, safeguarding standards.
- [ ] G5 International level: membership of international federations, selecting and preparing national teams for international competition, hosting international events, implementing international rules and anti-doping.
- [ ] G6 How an NGB can promote participation at local level (Ordinary: three ways) and use extra funding (development officers, programmes, facilities, coach education, campaigns).

Promotion and adherence tip sheet
- [ ] T1 Choose a specific target group (e.g. teenage girls, older adults, office workers, beginners returning after a long absence).
- [ ] T2 Promotion tips: start small; choose enjoyable activities; exercise with friends or a group; schedule activity like an appointment; use free local options (parkrun, walking routes, greenways); set goals.
- [ ] T3 Adherence tips: SMART goals; track progress with an app or diary; vary activities; reward milestones; plan for barriers (bad weather, time); build gradually to avoid injury and soreness; join a club or class for social support.
- [ ] T4 Each tip justified (why it helps that target group keep going).

Significant people
- [ ] P1 Who they are: parents and family, PE teachers, coaches, peers, local club volunteers, sports stars and role models.
- [ ] P2 Characteristics: enthusiastic and passionate about activity; lead by example (active themselves); positive and encouraging; approachable and supportive; knowledgeable; inclusive and fair to all abilities; reliable and committed; good communicators; visible and relatable to the target group.
- [ ] P3 How role models increase participation: inspire people to try an activity, show it is possible "for people like me", raise the profile of a sport.

## 3. Traps

- Healthy Ireland, the National Physical Activity Plan and the National Sports Policy are acceptable answers for "a national policy"; do not invent others.
- Pathways to excellence and high-performance support are the next subsection; here, NGBs' international role is limited to G5.
- Do not describe specific sponsorship deals (that is `pe-business-enterprise-sport`).

## 4. What exams ask

- Name a national policy that promotes physical activity and analyse how it does so.
- Name an NGB and examine how it supports local participation; name two other NGBs and their sports; suggest three ways an NGB can promote activity locally; describe how an NGB can promote activity at local and national levels with extra funding.
- Two ways to promote participation from your tip sheet, and two adherence tips for someone returning after a long absence, justified; design a tip sheet for young adults.
- Explain two characteristics of significant people such as role models; describe two characteristics of people who support others' participation; three characteristics from a case study.
