# Package: pe-aquatics-s4 — Two swimming strokes

- **loId:** `pe-aquatics-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-aquatics` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (skills and techniques for two strokes):** body position/alignment; hand
  entry/hand glide position/hand exit position; kick; stroke co-ordination; head action,
  breathing action; starts, turns, finishes; sprint and distance swimming. The outline asks for
  all four competitive strokes so any pair can be chosen.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: principles common to all
  strokes (streamlining, drag, propulsion); then one block per stroke (front crawl, backstroke,
  breaststroke, butterfly) using the same headings: body position, arm action (entry, catch and
  pull, exit, recovery), kick, breathing and head, co-ordination; then starts, turns and
  finishes; then sprint versus distance swimming.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] G1 Streamlining and drag: a flat, horizontal, straight body reduces resistance; propulsion comes from pushing water backwards (Newton's third law).
- [ ] FC Front crawl: flat body, slight body roll; hand enters in front of the shoulder, fingers first, extends, catch with a high elbow, pull in an S or straight line under the body to the thigh, exit at the thigh, relaxed elbow-high recovery; flutter kick from the hip with relaxed ankles; breathing by rotating the head to the side with the body roll (bilateral every three strokes or unilateral); usually six kicks per arm cycle.
- [ ] BK Backstroke: on the back, head still, eyes up, hips high; little-finger-first entry in line with the shoulder; bent-arm pull to the thigh; straight-arm recovery over the top; flutter kick; regular breathing; body roll; backstroke flags warn of the wall.
- [ ] BR Breaststroke: flat body with slight undulation; arms pull out and round in a heart shape and recover forward together under the water; whip kick with feet turned out and drawn up then driven round and back; breathe as the arms pull; timing is pull, breathe, kick, glide.
- [ ] FL Butterfly: undulating body (dolphin) movement; both arms enter together in front of the shoulders, pull under the body in a keyhole shape and recover over the water together; two dolphin kicks per arm cycle; breathe forward as the arms finish the pull.
- [ ] ST Starts: dive start from blocks for front crawl, breaststroke and butterfly (crouch, drive, streamlined entry through one point, glide, then stroke); backstroke start in the water holding the block, driving back over the water.
- [ ] TU Turns: tumble turn for front crawl and backstroke (somersault at the wall, plant feet, push off streamlined); open (touch) turn with a two-handed touch for breaststroke and butterfly.
- [ ] FI Finishes: touch the wall at full stretch without slowing; two hands together for breaststroke and butterfly.
- [ ] SP Sprint swimming: maximal effort, faster stroke rate, more kicking, fewer breaths; ATP-PC and lactic acid systems.
- [ ] DI Distance swimming: even pacing, longer and more efficient strokes, two-beat or lighter kick to save energy, regular breathing; aerobic system.
- [ ] AN Common faults to analyse: sinking legs (head too high), crossing over at entry, dropping the elbow in the pull, mistimed breathing.

## 3. Traps

- Breaststroke and butterfly require a simultaneous two-handed touch at turns and the finish; front crawl and backstroke do not.
- The breaststroke kick is a whip kick with feet turned out (not a flutter kick).
- Energy systems are taught in "Energy systems" in the chapter on physical and psychological demands; one bullet each for sprint and distance.

## 4. What exams ask

- Swimming appears as a named activity in written answers (economy of movement in front crawl, Newton's laws, energy systems for sprint and distance events, technology).
