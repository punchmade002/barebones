# Package: pe-adventure-activities-s5 — Sailing

- **loId:** `pe-adventure-activities-s5` · **chapterId:** `pe-adventure-activities` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (skills and techniques):** leaving/returning to windward shore; leaving/returning
  to a leeward shore; tacking/going about (upwind); gybing; capsize drill and recovery; sailing
  course up/downwind.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: boat parts and terms;
  points of sailing and the no-go zone; the five essentials; leaving and returning to windward
  and leeward shores; tacking; gybing; sailing upwind and downwind; capsize recovery; safety and
  rules of the road; demands.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] B1 Boat parts and terms (dinghy): hull, bow, stern, mast, boom, mainsail, jib, rudder and tiller, centreboard/daggerboard, sheets (ropes controlling sails); windward (the side the wind blows from) and leeward (the side away from the wind).
- [ ] B2 Points of sailing: close-hauled (as close to the wind as possible), close reach, beam reach (wind across the boat), broad reach, run (wind from behind).
- [ ] B3 No-go zone: about 45 degrees either side of the wind direction, in which a sail cannot drive the boat.
- [ ] B4 The five essentials: sail trim, boat balance, boat trim (fore and aft), centreboard position, course made good.
- [ ] W1 Windward shore (wind blowing off the shore): leaving is easy as the wind blows the boat away; returning means sailing upwind, so the boat approaches close-hauled and the sails are let out to stop.
- [ ] W2 Leeward shore (wind blowing onto the shore): harder and more dangerous; leaving requires pushing off and sailing close-hauled to get away; returning means the boat is blown in fast, so lower or depower the mainsail early and approach slowly under jib.
- [ ] T1 Tacking (going about): turning the bow through the wind to change direction when sailing upwind. Sequence: "Ready about", "Lee-oh", push the tiller away, cross the boat under the boom, change hands, sheet in on the new side.
- [ ] G1 Gybing: turning the stern through the wind when sailing downwind, so the boom swings across. Sequence: "Stand by to gybe", "Gybe-oh", pull the tiller towards you, duck, control the boom across, straighten up. Gybing is more dangerous because the boom swings quickly.
- [ ] C1 Upwind course: zig-zag (tacking) close-hauled because the boat cannot sail directly into the wind.
- [ ] C2 Downwind course: run or broad reach, centreboard mostly up, beware of an accidental gybe.
- [ ] R1 Capsize drill: stay with the boat; check crew is safe; swim to the centreboard; one person stands on the centreboard and leans back to right the boat while the other is scooped in; bail out; never swim away from the boat.
- [ ] S1 Safety: buoyancy aid always, suitable clothing for cold water, check weather and tides, a safety boat, tell someone your plan.
- [ ] S2 Rules of the road (basic): a boat on port tack gives way to a boat on starboard tack; a windward boat keeps clear of a leeward boat; an overtaking boat keeps clear.
- [ ] D1 Demands: balance, co-ordination and agility moving across the boat; muscular endurance for hiking; decision-making in changing wind; teamwork and communication between helm and crew.

## 3. Traps

- Tacking turns the bow through the wind; gybing turns the stern through the wind. Do not swap them.
- Windward shore: wind blowing off the land; leeward shore: wind blowing onto the land.

## 4. What exams ask

- Sailing is used as a named activity in written answers (teamwork and roles, communication, safe practice, decision-making).
