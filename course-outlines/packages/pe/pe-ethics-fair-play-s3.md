# Package: pe-ethics-fair-play-s3 — Drugs and sport

- **loId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play-s3` · **chapterId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 6.3
- **Syllabus:** "describe the different categories of performance-enhancing drugs, giving
  examples of how they affect performance"; "analyse the implications for the performer and
  the sport of using performance-enhancing drugs". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level
  only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what a PED is and the WADA
  Prohibited List; one block per category (examples, performance effect, sports where it is
  misused, health risks); prohibited methods; why athletes use PEDs; implications for the
  performer; implications for the sport. The rules and their enforcement are the next
  subsection.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] D1 A performance-enhancing drug (PED): a substance taken to improve performance artificially, giving an unfair advantage. The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) publishes the Prohibited List each year: substances and methods banned at all times, in competition only, or in particular sports.

Categories (each: examples, effect on performance, risks)
- [ ] C1 Anabolic agents (e.g. anabolic steroids such as nandrolone, testosterone, stanozolol): increase protein synthesis and muscle mass, strength and power, allow harder training and faster recovery. Risks: liver damage, heart disease, acne, aggression, hormonal effects (infertility; masculinisation in women).
- [ ] C2 Peptide hormones and growth factors (e.g. erythropoietin (EPO), human growth hormone (HGH)): EPO raises red blood cell production and oxygen-carrying capacity (endurance); HGH increases muscle and aids recovery. Risks: thickened blood, clots, stroke and heart attack (EPO); abnormal growth, heart problems (HGH).
- [ ] C3 Stimulants (e.g. amphetamines, cocaine, ephedrine): increase alertness and aggression, mask fatigue and pain. Risks: high blood pressure, heart problems, overheating, addiction, poor judgement.
- [ ] C4 Diuretics and masking agents: increase urine output for rapid weight loss (weight-category sports) and to dilute or hide other drugs. Risks: dehydration, cramps, kidney problems.
- [ ] C5 Narcotic analgesics (e.g. morphine): mask pain so the athlete can compete injured. Risks: worsening injury, addiction.
- [ ] C6 Beta-blockers (prohibited in particular precision sports such as archery and shooting): lower heart rate and steady tremor. Risks: fatigue, low blood pressure.
- [ ] C7 Other classes on the list (one line): beta-2 agonists, hormone and metabolic modulators, cannabinoids, glucocorticoids.
- [ ] M1 Prohibited methods: blood doping (transfusion of blood to raise red cell count), tampering with samples, gene doping.

Why athletes use them
- [ ] W1 Pressure to win, financial rewards and sponsorship, national pressure, belief that rivals are doping, recovering from injury faster, making a weight category, ageing athletes trying to stay competitive.

Implications for the performer
- [ ] I1 Health: short- and long-term risks (as above), including death in extreme cases.
- [ ] I2 Sanctions: bans (normally up to four years for a first serious violation), disqualification of results, loss of medals and records.
- [ ] I3 Financial and career: loss of funding, prize money and sponsorship; career ended.
- [ ] I4 Reputation and personal: public disgrace, damage to family, mental-health effects, loss of self-respect.

Implications for the sport
- [ ] J1 Loss of credibility and public trust; every outstanding performance is doubted.
- [ ] J2 Loss of sponsors, broadcasters and spectators; reduced funding.
- [ ] J3 Clean athletes cheated of medals, places and earnings; records reallocated years later.
- [ ] J4 Participation and role-model damage: young people and parents turn away; pressure on others to dope.
- [ ] J5 Cost of testing and education; possible exclusion of a country or team from competition.

## 3. Traps

- Creatine and caffeine (at normal doses) are not prohibited; do not list them as PEDs.
- Do not confuse anti-doping rules (next subsection) with drug categories.
- Do not invent statistics on numbers of positive tests.

## 4. What exams ask

- Identify two categories of PEDs and describe how one affects performance; complete a table of three categories with an example and effect each.
- Give an example of a PED in named categories (peptide hormones, anabolic agents, stimulants).
- Explain why an athlete might use a PED; discuss two negative implications; implications for the performer (with examples); examine the notion that doping damages the sport; consequences for the athlete and for the activity when rules are broken.
- Suggest two effects of anabolic steroids on performance.
