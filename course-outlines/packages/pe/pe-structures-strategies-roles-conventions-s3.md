## 1. Must cover

Safe practice and organising events
- [ ] S1 Safe practice: the procedures and behaviours that reduce the risk of injury or harm before, during and after physical activity.
- [ ] S2 In training and performance: appropriate warm-up and cool-down, correct technique, suitable progression and loads, supervision, rules followed, matching participants by size/age/ability where relevant.
- [ ] S3 Organising an event: a risk assessment of the venue and activity; first aid cover and an emergency action plan; trained stewards and officials; checking facilities and equipment; clear rules and briefings; crowd and traffic management; weather contingency; hydration points and shelter; communication systems.
- [ ] S4 Intrinsic risk factors (from the performer: fitness, previous injury, technique, fatigue) and extrinsic risk factors (from outside: equipment, surface, weather, opponents, coaching).

Warm-up
- [ ] W1 Phases: (1) pulse raiser: light, gradually increasing cardiovascular activity; (2) mobility and dynamic stretching through the ranges the activity uses; (3) activity-specific drills and skill rehearsal building to game intensity. Mental preparation can be included.
- [ ] W2 Benefits: raises heart rate and body and muscle temperature, increases blood flow and oxygen to muscles, improves muscle elasticity and joint mobility, reduces injury risk, rehearses skills, prepares mentally.
- [ ] W3 A warm-up for one named activity, with timings (illustrative).

Cool-down
- [ ] C1 Content: light activity gradually reducing in intensity, then static stretching.
- [ ] C2 Benefits: gradually returns heart rate and breathing to rest, prevents blood pooling and dizziness, speeds removal of lactic acid, may reduce muscle soreness (DOMS), helps flexibility, time to reflect.

Attire, equipment and facilities
- [ ] A1 Attire: footwear suited to the surface and activity (studs for grass, court shoes for indoor), clothing for the conditions, removal of jewellery.
- [ ] A2 Protective equipment: helmets with faceguards in hurling (compulsory) and camogie, gumshields in rugby, shin guards in soccer and hockey, buoyancy aids in water activities.
- [ ] A3 Selection: correct size and fit, approved standards, suited to the performer's level (e.g. racket grip size and head size by ability).
- [ ] A4 Maintenance and its importance: regular inspection and repair of equipment and facilities (nets, posts, goals secured, surfaces free of hazards, pool water quality), proper storage; prevents injury, keeps equipment effective, extends its life. Give an example of maintaining a facility and why.

Injuries and first aid
- [ ] I1 Types: acute (sudden, e.g. sprain, strain, fracture, dislocation, concussion, cuts) versus chronic/overuse (develop over time, e.g. tendinitis, shin splints, stress fracture, tennis elbow).
- [ ] I2 Sprain (ligament damage, e.g. ankle sprain from landing awkwardly) versus strain (muscle or tendon damage, e.g. hamstring strain from sprinting without a warm-up).
- [ ] I3 Two common injuries in each of two named activities, with causes.
- [ ] I4 First aid for soft-tissue injuries: PRICE (Protect, Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) in the first 48–72 hours; refer for medical assessment if severe.
- [ ] I5 Assessing an injury on the field: SALTAPS (See, Ask, Look, Touch, Active movement, Passive movement, Strength) to decide whether the player can continue.
- [ ] I6 Concussion: signs (confusion, headache, dizziness, loss of consciousness, balance problems); remove from play immediately and do not return that day ("if in doubt, sit them out"); medical clearance and a graduated return to play.
- [ ] I7 Cuts and bleeding: apply pressure with a clean dressing, use gloves, the player leaves the field until bleeding stops (blood rule).

Overtraining and fatigue
- [ ] O1 Overtraining: training too hard or too often without enough recovery, so performance declines.
- [ ] O2 Symptoms: persistent fatigue, raised resting heart rate, poor sleep, frequent illness and injury, loss of motivation, irritability, falling performance, muscle soreness that does not go away.
- [ ] O3 Designing schedules to avoid it: plan progression gradually (about 10% a week is a common rule of thumb), rest days and easy weeks, periodisation, vary intensity, monitor load (session RPE × duration, heart rate, wellness questionnaires), adequate sleep and nutrition, listen to the athlete.
- [ ] O4 Evaluating a training schedule: identify where load jumps too quickly or recovery is missing, and propose changes.

## 3. Traps

- A sprain is a ligament injury; a strain is a muscle or tendon injury.
- Do not recommend heat on a fresh soft-tissue injury.
- Do not give return-to-play timelines for concussion in days; say "medical clearance and a graduated return".
- Officials' pre-competition safety checks are in "The role of official"; event organisation is here.
- Periodisation itself is taught in `pe-performance-demands` ("Designing a fitness plan"); here only use it as one way to avoid overtraining.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define safe practice and give examples; describe two things organisers should do to keep participants safe; examine two approaches to safe practice at a major event.
- Identify two benefits of warming up; outline the phases of a warm-up; describe two warm-up activities; describe a warm-up structure; outline two reasons for a cool-down.
- Importance of selecting suitable attire and equipment (sometimes excluding running); how to maintain a facility and why.
- Identify two common injuries in a named activity, their causes and first aid treatment.
- Define overtraining; symptoms; explain two ways a schedule can be designed to prevent it.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Describe a warm-up structure athletes can use to prepare for performance, and outline two reasons for including a cool-down." Model: three phases with content and purpose; two cool-down reasons.
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Identify two injuries common in a named physical activity, their causes, and the first aid treatment for one." Model: two injuries with specific causes; PRICE applied step by step.
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Define overtraining and outline two ways a training schedule can be designed to prevent it."
