# Package: pe-promoting-physical-activity-s2 — Physical activity participation

- **loId:** `pe-promoting-physical-activity-s2` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-promoting-physical-activity` · **syllabusRef:** 5.2
- **Syllabus:** "present a physical activity biography for themselves and another with an
  activity profile different from their own"; "discuss the supports and barriers to physical
  activity participation in school and in the community"; "use different methods of collecting
  data about physical activity participation, e.g. pedometers, activity diaries"; "analyse
  physical activity participation patterns in a specific group in their school community";
  "evaluate different approaches to physical activity promotion". Ordinary and Higher level; no
  Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: physical activity
  biography; supports and barriers (school, community; personal, social, environmental,
  economic); methods of collecting participation data; analysing participation patterns;
  approaches to promotion and their evaluation. National policies and governing bodies are in
  "Physical activity promotion".

## 1. Must cover

Physical activity biography
- [ ] B1 What it is: a record of a person's physical activity history across their life so far (activities, ages, settings, levels, influences, high and low points, reasons for starting and stopping), and their current activity profile.
- [ ] B2 How to present one: a timeline by life stage, with influences (family, school, friends, coaches, facilities) and turning points; then compare with someone whose profile differs (e.g. a non-participant, an older adult, someone with a disability) and explain the differences.

Supports and barriers
- [ ] S1 In school: supports such as PE classes, school teams and clubs, extra-curricular activities, qualified teachers and coaches, facilities and equipment, lunchtime leagues, Active School Flag, positive peer culture; barriers such as limited PE time, exam pressure, a narrow range of competitive sports, lack of facilities, changing-room and kit concerns, low confidence.
- [ ] S2 In the community: supports such as local clubs, sports partnerships, parks, greenways and walking routes, free events, family involvement, accessible and affordable facilities; barriers such as cost, transport, lack of local facilities, safety, opening hours, weather, lack of programmes for some groups.
- [ ] S3 Grouped by type: personal (time, motivation, confidence, skill level, injury, body image), social (family, friends, culture), environmental (facilities, transport, safety, weather), economic (membership fees, equipment, travel).

Collecting participation data
- [ ] D1 Pedometers (steps per day; cheap, objective; don't record cycling, swimming or intensity).
- [ ] D2 Activity diaries or logs (type, duration, intensity of activities; detailed but rely on honesty and memory).
- [ ] D3 Questionnaires and surveys (many people quickly; self-report bias); interviews and focus groups (reasons and attitudes in depth).
- [ ] D4 Wearable trackers and smartphone apps (steps, heart rate, distance; cost and accuracy limits); heart-rate monitors (intensity).
- [ ] D5 Observation and attendance records (e.g. numbers using a facility or club).
- [ ] D6 For each method, the type of information collected and one strength and limitation.

Analysing participation patterns
- [ ] A1 Analysing a specific group in the school (e.g. a year group, girls in senior cycle): collect data, present it (tables, bar charts), describe patterns (which activities, how often, drop-off with age), explain likely reasons (exams, interests, cost, facilities, gender), suggest actions.
- [ ] A2 Common patterns: participation falls through adolescence, especially in senior cycle; team-sport participation is often higher in boys and drop-out higher in girls; popular activities reflect local facilities and traditions (e.g. strong Gaelic games participation where clubs are local; low swimming where there is no pool).

Approaches to promotion and their evaluation
- [ ] P1 Approaches: national media campaigns; school-based programmes (Active School Flag, daily activity breaks); community events (parkrun, fun runs); targeted programmes for low-participation groups; making environments active (greenways, cycle lanes); incentives and free access; using role models; wearable technology and challenges.
- [ ] P2 Evaluating them: reach (how many and who), cost, sustainability (does activity continue once the programme ends), whether they reach inactive groups, how success is measured (participation records, questionnaires, trackers).
- [ ] P3 Ways a school can increase participation (Ordinary): wider range of activities, non-competitive options, lunchtime and after-school clubs, student input into choices, links with local clubs, positive role models.

## 4. What exams ask

- Outline two supports and two barriers to participation in the community; discuss two barriers in the community; describe three supports in schools (excluding PE); identify supports in the community and how each helps.
- Name methods of collecting participation data and the information each collects; identify three methods; suggest methods used for a survey.
- Interpret participation data from a school survey and explain differences between activities and year groups; ways a school can increase participation.
- Discuss two approaches to physical activity promotion; evaluate measures of a programme's success (in the app's test questions).
