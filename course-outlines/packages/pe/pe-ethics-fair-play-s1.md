# Package: pe-ethics-fair-play-s1 — Principles of ethical practice

- **loId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play-s1` · **chapterId:** `pe-ethics-fair-play` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 6.1
- **Syllabus:** "explain the principles of ethical practice in sport; the importance of
  integrity, respect, fairness and equity in the context of the selected activities". The
  topic introduction says responsibility lies with participants, officials, leaders/coaches,
  national governing bodies and groups responsible for sport. Ordinary and Higher level; no
  Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: what ethics and fair play
  are; the four principles (one block each: definition, what it looks like in practice, a
  breach, why it matters, applied to a named activity); who is responsible; why upholding them

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] E1 Ethics in sport: the moral principles about what is right and wrong that guide behaviour in sport. Fair play: playing within the rules and the spirit of the game, with respect for others.
- [ ] E2 Integrity: honesty and strong moral principles; doing the right thing when no one is watching (e.g. owning up to touching the ball last, not diving or feigning injury, not doping). Importance: trust in results and in the sport.
- [ ] E3 Respect: for opponents, team-mates, officials, coaches, spectators, the rules and oneself (e.g. accepting decisions, shaking hands, no abuse or sledging). Importance: safe, positive environment; retention of officials and players.
- [ ] E4 Fairness: everyone competes under the same rules and conditions and no one seeks an unfair advantage (e.g. no cheating, consistent refereeing, fair team selection). Importance: the result reflects ability and effort.
- [ ] E5 Equity: giving everyone access and opportunity according to their needs, so that differences in gender, ability, disability, ethnicity or income do not exclude people (e.g. equal facilities and coaching for girls' teams, adapted equipment). Equity differs from equality: equality treats everyone the same; equity gives people what they need for a fair chance.
- [ ] E6 Responsibility: participants, coaches and leaders (model behaviour, teach fair play), officials (apply rules impartially), parents and spectators, NGBs and sports bodies (codes, education, sanctions).
- [ ] E7 Importance of upholding the principles: protects participants' safety and welfare, keeps sport enjoyable, maintains the credibility of results, encourages participation, protects sponsorship and the sport's image, teaches values for life.
- [ ] E8 Breaches and their effects: cheating, doping, match-fixing, violence, abuse of officials, discrimination, win-at-all-costs coaching.

## 3. Traps

- Equity is not the same as equality; define the difference once.
- Sportsmanship and gamesmanship are taught in "Codes of ethics"; here mention them only as examples.
- Drugs and anti-doping have their own subsections.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define integrity and outline its importance to an activity; identify a second principle.
- Identify three principles of ethical practice; list two and outline the importance of upholding them.
- Explain the importance of equity in a named activity.
- Explain how a doping athlete did not adhere to at least two principles of ethical practice (case study).
- Describe two principles of ethical practice from a photograph of a fair-play act.
