# Package: pe-business-enterprise-sport-s4 — Tourism and sport

- **loId:** `pe-business-enterprise-sport-s4` · **chapterId:** `pe-business-enterprise-sport` ·
  **syllabusRef:** 10.4
- **Prescribed for June 2027** (SEC circular S51/25). Write in full Higher Level depth.
- **Syllabus:** "discuss the potential of sports marketing on the development of tourism in
  Ireland". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: sports marketing and sports
  tourism (definitions, types of sports tourist); ways sports marketing develops tourism in
  Ireland (each with a checked example); benefits for the economy and communities; costs and
  limitations; evaluating the potential.

## 1. Must cover

Definitions
- [ ] D1 Sports marketing: using sport to promote and sell products, services or places, and promoting sport itself to attract participants, spectators, sponsors and broadcasters.
- [ ] D2 Sports tourism: travel to take part in or watch sport, or to visit sporting attractions.
- [ ] D3 Types of sports tourist: active (travel to take part, e.g. golf, surfing, cycling, walking, running events); event (travel to watch, e.g. international matches, golf tournaments, horse racing); nostalgia (visiting stadiums, museums and halls of fame).

How sports marketing develops tourism in Ireland
- [ ] W1 Marketing Ireland as a destination for an activity: golf; surfing and water sports on the Wild Atlantic Way; walking and cycling on greenways. Visitors come specifically for the activity, stay longer and spend more.
- [ ] W2 Bidding for and hosting major international events, which bring visiting teams, media and spectators and show Ireland to a worldwide television audience (e.g. the Ryder Cup at the K Club in 2006 and at Adare Manor in 2027; Six Nations matches at the Aviva Stadium; the Tour de France starting in Dublin in 1998; American college football games at the Aviva Stadium).
- [ ] W3 Annual events that attract visitors every year (e.g. the Galway Races; the Irish Open golf championship).
- [ ] W4 Spectacle events in scenic locations that market the landscape (e.g. the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series held at Inis Mór).
- [ ] W5 Sporting heritage attractions: stadium tours and museums (e.g. the GAA Museum and tours at Croke Park).
- [ ] W6 Using famous athletes and teams in tourism campaigns.

Benefits
- [ ] B1 Economic: spending on accommodation, food, transport and retail; jobs; taxes; benefits spread to rural and coastal areas outside the main tourist season.
- [ ] B2 Image and legacy: positive international image; improved facilities and infrastructure used by local people afterwards; repeat visits.
- [ ] B3 Community: pride, volunteering, increased local participation inspired by events.

Costs and limitations
- [ ] C1 Public cost of bidding, hosting and security; money may leave the area through international companies.
- [ ] C2 Congestion, overcrowding, rising prices and disruption for residents; environmental damage from visitors.
- [ ] C3 Facilities built for one event may be under-used afterwards.
- [ ] C4 Ireland's weather, limited large venues and transport links limit some events.
- [ ] E1 Evaluating the potential: high for activity tourism and heritage, given the landscape and Irish sporting culture; depends on investment in facilities and transport, and on spreading the benefits locally.

## 3. Traps

- Do not quote any figure for the value of sports tourism, including the past-paper one.
- The 2027 Ryder Cup is scheduled; describe it as "scheduled for" or "to be held at", not as having happened.
- Mass-participation events' own business opportunities are in the previous subsection.

## 4. What exams ask

- Discuss how sports marketing can affect the development of tourism in Ireland (10 marks, Higher; also from a golf-tourism stimulus).
- Explain sports marketing; describe the impact of hosting a sporting event on tourism in Ireland (Ordinary).
- Give two examples of sports events that could attract tourists to Ireland; describe two ways sports marketing could develop tourism.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 10 marks, Higher: "Discuss the potential of sports marketing for the development of tourism in Ireland." Model: three or four distinct ways with checked examples; benefits and limitations; judgement.
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Explain sports marketing and describe one way it could help develop tourism in Ireland."
