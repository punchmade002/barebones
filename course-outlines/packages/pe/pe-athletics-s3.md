# Package: pe-athletics-s3 — Throwing events

- **loId:** `pe-athletics-s3` · **chapterId:** `pe-athletics` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus:** throwing events shot-put, discus, javelin, hammer; skills and techniques: grip,
  stance and preparation; movement into throwing action; release and follow-through; overall
  efficiency.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: principles common to all
  throws (summation of forces, release speed, angle and height, the kinetic chain); then one
  block per event with the same headings (grip, stance and preparation, movement into the
  throw, release and follow-through, key rules, safety); overall efficiency and common faults.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] P1 Distance depends mainly on release speed, then release angle and release height.
- [ ] P2 Summation of forces (kinetic chain): force generated from the legs, through the hips and trunk, to the shoulder, arm and hand in sequence, each segment adding speed.
- [ ] P3 Lever link: a long, straight throwing arm at release lengthens the lever and increases release speed.
- [ ] SP Shot-put: grip at the base of the fingers, shot tucked into the neck under the jaw, elbow high; stance at the back of the circle facing away (glide) or side-on; glide or rotational movement across the circle; drive from the legs, hip turn, then push (not throw) the shot from the neck at about 40 degrees, wrist flick; follow-through with a reverse (switching feet) to stay in the circle; leave from the back half of the circle.
- [ ] DI Discus: grip with the fingertips spread over the rim, palm down; stance at the back of the circle; preliminary swings; a turn (one and a half rotations) across the circle in the transverse plane; long, straight arm sweeping round at shoulder height; release off the index finger with the discus spinning clockwise (for a right-hander); reverse to stay in the circle.
- [ ] JA Javelin: grip behind the cord (e.g. the Finnish or American grip); approach run with the javelin carried above the shoulder; withdrawal of the javelin back with a straight arm over the last strides; cross-over (impulse) steps; plant of the front leg; hips then shoulders rotate; elbow leads over the shoulder, throwing over a braced front leg; release at about 30–35 degrees along the line of the shaft; must land tip first in the sector.
- [ ] HA Hammer: two-handed grip on the handle; preliminary winds (swings) around the head; three or four accelerating turns on the heels and toes in the transverse plane; long straight arms; low point and high point of the orbit; release over the left shoulder (for a right-hander); throw from a cage.
- [ ] R1 Rules common to the circle throws: start from a stationary position in the circle, the implement must land within the sector, do not touch the top of the stop board or the outside of the circle, leave from the back half after the implement lands.
- [ ] S1 Safety: throw only on command, retrieve on command and carry implements back (never throw them back), stay behind throwers, cages for discus and hammer, check the sector is clear.
- [ ] O1 Overall efficiency: smooth rhythm, balance in the circle, sequencing of the kinetic chain, a blocked or braced front side.
- [ ] O2 Common faults: throwing with the arm only (no leg drive), opening the shoulders too early, low release angle, falling out of the circle (a foul).

## 3. Traps

- The shot is pushed, not thrown; a throwing action from behind the shoulder is a foul.
- Discus and hammer rotation is in the transverse plane about the longitudinal axis (link to "Analysing skill: the biomechanical perspective").
- The javelin must land tip first.

## 4. What exams ask

- Throws are frequent contexts: the plane and axis of discus and hammer turns, the javelin as a third-class lever, practice methods for a javelin thrower, training with light and heavy implements, component of fitness for shot-put.
