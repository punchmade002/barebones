# Package: pe-physical-activity-inclusion-s1 — Supports and barriers for selected groups

- **loId:** `pe-physical-activity-inclusion-s1` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-physical-activity-inclusion` · **syllabusRef:** 7.1
- **Not prescribed for June 2027.** Topic 7 is not one of the two topics the SEC prescribed
  for the 2027 examination (circular S51/25). Write it after the prescribed chapters.
- **Syllabus:** "analyse the supports and barriers to physical activity participation for at
  least two of the following groups: women; older adults; people with physical disability;
  people with intellectual disability; different ethnic groups; different socio-economic
  groups"; "discuss examples of discrimination, stereotyping, inclusiveness and/or prejudice in
  physical activity provision for these groups"; "investigate the coverage of sport in the
  media from the perspective of these groups". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only
  material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: the four key terms; then
  one block per group (all six) with supports, barriers, examples of discrimination or
  stereotyping, and media coverage; then a short block comparing groups.

## 1. Must cover

Key terms (each defined with a sporting example)
- [ ] K1 Inclusion/inclusiveness: providing opportunities so that everyone can take part meaningfully, with dignity and appropriate challenge, whatever their background or ability.
- [ ] K2 Discrimination: treating a person or group unfairly because of a characteristic such as gender, age, disability, race or income (e.g. fewer facilities or worse training times for women's teams).
- [ ] K3 Stereotyping: a fixed, oversimplified belief that all members of a group share certain characteristics (e.g. assuming older adults are too frail for strength training; generalisations about players from a particular country).
- [ ] K4 Prejudice: a pre-judgement or negative attitude towards a group, not based on reason or experience.

Groups (each block: supports, barriers, examples of discrimination/stereotyping, media coverage)
- [ ] G1 Women: barriers such as fewer role models and less media coverage, body image and self-consciousness, kit and changing facilities, caring responsibilities and time, gender stereotypes about "suitable" sports, unequal pay and resources; supports such as women's leagues and programmes, female coaches and leaders, family-friendly timetabling, recent increases in coverage.
- [ ] G2 Older adults: barriers such as health conditions, fear of injury or falling, lack of confidence, stereotypes about ageing, transport, few suitable programmes; supports such as walking groups, age-friendly classes, Go for Life programmes, social benefits, GP referral.
- [ ] G3 People with physical disability: barriers such as inaccessible facilities and transport, lack of adapted equipment, untrained coaches, cost, attitudes; supports such as accessible venues, adapted sports (wheelchair basketball, para-swimming), inclusion officers, disability sport organisations.
- [ ] G4 People with intellectual disability: barriers such as complex instructions, lack of trained coaches, limited programmes, transport and supervision needs, low expectations; supports such as Special Olympics clubs, simplified instructions, buddy systems, inclusive club sessions.
- [ ] G5 Different ethnic groups: barriers such as language, cultural or religious factors (dress codes, prayer times, mixed-gender sessions), racism, lack of role models, unfamiliar sports; supports such as anti-racism programmes, inclusive dress rules, multicultural teams, clubs welcoming newcomers.
- [ ] G6 Different socio-economic groups: barriers such as cost of fees, kit and travel, lack of local facilities, time pressures from shift work; supports such as free or subsidised programmes, school sport, Local Sports Partnership initiatives in disadvantaged areas, free public spaces.
- [ ] G7 Media coverage from these groups' perspective: under-representation of women's, older and disability sport; focus on appearance or personal life rather than performance; stereotyped commentary; positive developments in coverage of major women's and Paralympic events.
- [ ] G8 Comparing barriers for two groups (a 2025 Higher pattern): similarities (cost, facilities) and differences.

- Define discrimination, stereotyping, inclusiveness or prejudice; identify which a given statement is an example of and explain.
- Compare the barriers for two groups; describe a barrier and a support for one chosen group.

- Fill-in-the-blanks on inclusion vocabulary.
