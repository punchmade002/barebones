- **Syllabus:** "discuss the demands of non-playing roles including desirable interpersonal
  skills"; "guide the performer in identifying and planning for short- and long-term goals in
  pursuit of optimum performance"; "design a physical fitness and skills programme for a

  practice/performance as the need arises"; "use strategies designed to support a performer's

## 1. Must cover

Demands and qualities
- [ ] D1 Demands on a coach/choreographer: technical knowledge of the activity (skills, tactics, rules, choreography), knowledge of fitness, psychology and safety; planning and organisation; time commitment; managing a group; making selection decisions; duty of care and safeguarding (Garda vetting and child-safeguarding training for those working with under-18s in Ireland).
- [ ] D2 Desirable interpersonal skills: clear communication, active listening, empathy, patience, approachability, enthusiasm, fairness and consistency, ability to give constructive feedback, leadership, emotional control.
- [ ] D3 Personal qualities and technical qualities for successful coaching (a 2025 Higher pattern).

Goals
- [ ] G1 Short-term goals (days to weeks) as steps towards long-term goals (a season or more).
- [ ] G2 SMART goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound (SMARTER adds Evaluate and Review).
- [ ] G3 Outcome, performance and process goals: outcome (result, e.g. win), performance (personal standard, e.g. 80% first serves in), process (technique focus, e.g. high ball toss). Performance and process goals are more within the performer's control.
- [ ] G4 Guiding the performer: agree goals together from performance analysis, write them down, review them regularly.

Designing a fitness and skills programme for a specific performer
- [ ] P1 Steps: analyse the performer (fitness tests, skill analysis); set goals; choose fitness methods and skill practices; apply FITT and the principles of training and of effective practice; plan weekly sessions with progression; monitor and adjust. (Methods and principles are taught in `pe-performance-demands` and "Skill acquisition"; apply them.)

Communication, modification, motivation, reflection, time
- [ ] C1 Effective communication: short, clear instructions and key teaching points; demonstrations; questioning to check understanding; positive body language; feedback that is specific, timely and constructive (e.g. praise, correction, praise); adjusting language to the performer's age and level.
- [ ] M1 Modifying practice or performance as needed: simplify or make harder, change the drill, add or remove opposition, adapt for injury, fatigue or weather, change tactics or choreography at half-time.
- [ ] V1 Motivation strategies: goal setting, variety, recording progress, praise and recognition, giving the performer ownership and choice, a positive training environment, avoiding punishment as a training tool.
- [ ] F1 Guiding reflection: training diaries, video review with the performer, questioning ("what went well, what would you change?"), linking reflection to the next session's goals.
- [ ] T1 Managing practice/training time: a session plan with timings; equipment set up in advance; minimal waiting and maximum activity time; clear transitions; keeping to the plan while allowing flexibility; prioritising the session's main objective.
- [ ] A1 Analysing own coaching against an admired coach/choreographer: choose criteria (communication, planning, motivation, technical knowledge, results), compare honestly, identify one strength and one area to develop, and an action to improve.

## 3. Traps

- Feedback types (intrinsic, extrinsic, KR, KP) are taught in `pe-performance-demands` ("Psychological preparation"); here describe how a coach gives feedback.
- Goal setting for the planning cycle (four performance goals in the Physical Activity Project) is in `pe-planning-optimum-performance`; here it is the coach guiding a performer.
- The basketball coach's quotation on interpersonal skills (2022 Higher) and other past-paper quotations must not be reused.

## 4. What exams ask

- Identify three demands on a coach/choreographer; describe two desirable interpersonal skills (often from a scenario where a coach's instruction is misunderstood).
- Discuss personal and technical qualities needed for successful coaching.
- Explain why a coach needs to manage practice/training time effectively.
- Discuss the importance of feedback types a coach could use (overlap with "Psychological preparation").

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks, Higher: "Discuss the personal and technical qualities needed for successful sports coaching." Model: at least three of each, explained with examples.
2. 8 marks, Higher: "Describe two desirable interpersonal skills of a coach or choreographer and explain how each supports the performer."
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Explain why a coach needs to manage practice time effectively and give two ways of doing so."
