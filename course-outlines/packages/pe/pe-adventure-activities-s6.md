# Package: pe-adventure-activities-s6 — Rowing and sculling

- **loId:** `pe-adventure-activities-s6` · **chapterId:** `pe-adventure-activities` ·
  **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus (skills and techniques):** sculling strokes; rowing on stroke side; rowing on bow
  side; paddling with square blades; single stroke to hands away.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: sculling versus sweep
  rowing; boat and equipment terms; the phases of the stroke (catch, drive, finish/extraction,
  recovery) with key points; square blades and feathering; stroke side and bow side; single
  stroke to hands away (a drill); paddling; safety and commands; demands.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] T1 Sculling: each rower uses two oars (sculls), one in each hand. Sweep rowing: each rower uses one oar, on one side of the boat.
- [ ] T2 Stroke side and bow side (sweep rowing): stroke side is the side of the rower's oar on their right as they sit facing the stern (the port side of the boat); bow side is the other side (starboard). Rowers learn both.
- [ ] T3 Equipment: boat (shell), seat on a slide, foot stretcher, rigger, gate (swivel holding the oar), blade, handle. Rowers face the stern.
- [ ] S1 Catch: at the front of the slide, shins near vertical, arms long, blade placed quickly into the water.
- [ ] S2 Drive: legs push first, then the back swings, then the arms draw the handle to the body; the blade stays buried.
- [ ] S3 Finish (extraction): handle drawn to the lower ribs, blade tapped down and out cleanly.
- [ ] S4 Recovery: "hands away" first (arms extend), then body swings forward, then the slide comes forward slowly and controlled; ratio of slow recovery to fast drive.
- [ ] S5 Sequence summary: drive is legs–body–arms; recovery is arms–body–legs.
- [ ] Q1 Square blades: the blade stays vertical (square) throughout, including on the recovery; easier for beginners and used in drills. Feathering: turning the blade flat on the recovery to reduce air resistance.
- [ ] Q2 Paddling with square blades: light, controlled continuous rowing at low pressure with square blades, to practise timing and blade height.
- [ ] H1 Single stroke to hands away: a drill in which the rower completes one stroke and stops at the hands-away position (arms extended, body still upright) to check balance and sequence before continuing.
- [ ] C1 Crew timing and balance: blades enter and leave the water together; the stroke sets the rhythm; the cox steers and gives commands in coxed boats.
- [ ] R1 Safety: capsize and swim drills before going out, staying with the boat, checking weather and water conditions, lights and high-visibility clothing in poor light, launch supervision, knowing the circulation pattern on the water.
- [ ] D1 Demands: cardio-respiratory endurance and muscular endurance (aerobic system dominates in a 2,000 m race), power in the drive, flexibility at the catch, co-ordination and timing within the crew.

## 3. Traps

- Drive sequence is legs, body, arms; recovery is arms, body, legs. Do not reverse.
- Rowers face the stern, so the rower's right is the boat's port side.

## 4. What exams ask

- Rowing is used as a named activity in written answers (energy systems, muscular endurance, technique analysis of the stroke, teamwork).
