## 1. Must cover

Definitions
- [ ] D1 Structure: the organised arrangement of players or performers, e.g. a team formation, positional roles, set-piece arrangements, a relay order.
- [ ] D2 Strategy: an overall plan to achieve a goal, made before the performance, using the team's or performer's strengths and the opponent's weaknesses. Tactics: the specific actions used during the performance to carry out the strategy.
- [ ] D3 Compositional elements: the design elements used to build a dance or gymnastics routine: action (what moves), space (levels, pathways, direction, formations), dynamics (how it moves: speed, force, flow), relationships (to other performers, props, music), and choreographic form (e.g. motif and development, canon, unison, binary, rondo).

Structures (examples)
- [ ] S1 Team formations (e.g. 4-4-2 or 4-3-3 in soccer; zonal versus man-to-man marking; a basketball 2-3 zone).
- [ ] S2 Set plays and restarts (kick-outs in Gaelic football, line-outs in rugby, short corners in hockey).
- [ ] S3 Individual sport structures (e.g. lane and race order in a relay; doubles formations in badminton: side-by-side in defence, front-and-back in attack).

Strategies and tactics (examples, each with how it uses strengths or exploits weaknesses)
- [ ] T1 Attacking strategies: width and depth, overlap runs, fast break, pick and roll in basketball, playing to the space behind a high defensive line.
- [ ] T2 Defensive strategies: pressing high, a deep compact block, double-teaming a key player, zonal versus man-to-man defence.
- [ ] T3 Net/wall strategies: serve-and-volley, targeting an opponent's backhand, moving the opponent around the court.
- [ ] T4 Individual strategies: pacing (even pace versus a fast finish), race tactics (sitting in behind then kicking).

Compositional elements in practice
- [ ] C1 Applying action, space, dynamics and relationships to a short group dance or gymnastics sequence.
- [ ] C2 Choreographic approaches: motif and variation, canon and unison, contrast, climax, transitions; use of music and props.

Evaluating use
- [ ] E1 Methods of analysis: video review, notational analysis (tallying passes, turnovers, scores from each strategy), checklists against key principles, coach or peer observation. Details of analysis methods are in `pe-planning-optimum-performance` ("Methods of analysis"); here apply them.
- [ ] E2 Criteria for evaluation: did the structure or strategy achieve its aim, were roles carried out, how did opponents respond.

Modifying structures and strategies
- [ ] M1 Why modify: the score and time remaining, opponents' changes, weather and pitch conditions, injuries or dismissals, fatigue, a strategy that is not working.
- [ ] M2 How: change formation (e.g. adding an extra defender when protecting a lead), switch marking system, change the point of attack, modify a routine for a smaller space or different music. Use the "pick and roll becomes pick and pop" kind of example: name the adaptation and why it works.

## 3. Traps

- Strategy is the overall plan; tactics are the in-game actions. Do not use them as identical words without saying so once.
- Compositional elements belong with dance and gymnastics; for games use structures and strategies.
- Aesthetic and artistic criteria (how a performance is judged) are taught in `pe-planning-optimum-performance` ("Aesthetic and artistic considerations"); here teach how routines are built.
- Identifying opponents' weaknesses is in "Roles and relationships"; mention it only as the reason for a strategy.

## 4. What exams ask

- Explain two structures or two strategies that help an athlete or team succeed, with examples (8 marks, Higher).
- Describe a strategy or compositional element for a game/performance plan; outline an adaptation if it is not working.
- Explain, using an example, a strategy for a mixed relay running order and why the strategy might change.
- Identify weaknesses in an opposition team and a strategy to exploit one (Ordinary; see also "Roles and relationships").

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 8 marks, Higher: "Explain two strategies that can help a team succeed in a physical activity of your choice." Model: one activity; two named strategies with how each uses strengths or exploits weaknesses.
2. 10 marks, Higher: "Describe a strategy or compositional element suitable for a performance plan and outline how you would adapt it if it were not working." Model: a specific strategy described step by step; a named adaptation with the reason.
3. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Name three compositional elements used in a dance or gymnastics routine and give an example of each."
