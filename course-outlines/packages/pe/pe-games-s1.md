# Package: pe-games-s1 — The games area

- **loId:** `pe-games-s1` · **chapterId:** `pe-games` · **syllabusRef:** S6
- **Syllabus:** "Individual, pair or team games that require the use of activity-specific
  skills, game appreciation, decision-making, tactical knowledge and strategic thinking in
  pursuit of a defined goal." Learners study one of three categories (invasion,
  striking/fielding, net/wall); the performance assessment focuses on one activity in the
  category (e.g. Gaelic football). Basketball is part of the specification's own example trio.
  Section 7: the performance assessment covers the listed skills, the ability to apply and
  adapt tactics and strategies in conditioned or competitive play, and rules and safe practice.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. A short overview. Write in this order: what the
  area is; the three categories with their defining features and the games in each; game
  appreciation and principles of play common to each category; the choice rule and the
  performance assessment (conditioned practice and competitive play); safety common to games;
  using games as the named activity.

## 1. Must cover

- [ ] A1 Definition (paraphrase): individual, pair or team games needing activity-specific skills, game appreciation, decision-making, tactical knowledge and strategic thinking to achieve a defined goal.
- [ ] C1 Invasion games: teams invade the opponents' territory to score in a goal or zone (Gaelic football, hurling/camogie, rugby union, soccer, basketball, hockey, netball, Olympic handball).
- [ ] C2 Net/wall games: players send an object over a net or against a wall so the opponent cannot return it (badminton, tennis, volleyball, table tennis, handball, squash).
- [ ] C3 Striking/fielding games: one team strikes an object and runs to score while the other fields to limit runs and get batters out (cricket, softball, rounders).
- [ ] P1 Principles of play in invasion games: attack (possession, width, depth, penetration, support, mobility) and defence (pressure, delay, cover, balance, compactness), and transition between them.
- [ ] P2 Principles in net/wall games: create space on the opponent's side, move the opponent, return to a base position, vary pace, height and direction.
- [ ] P3 Principles in striking/fielding games: batting (place the ball into gaps, run sensibly), fielding (positions to cover gaps, backing up, quick returns).
- [ ] D1 Game appreciation and decision-making: reading the game, choosing the right option (pass, shoot, dribble), anticipating opponents.
- [ ] A2 Choice rule and assessment: one category and one game; performance in conditioned practices (small-sided, rule-modified games) and in competitive play, showing skills, tactics and rule knowledge.
- [ ] S1 Safety common to games: warm-up, protective equipment for the game, suitable footwear, checking the playing surface and goals, rules on dangerous play, officials enforcing safety.
- [ ] W1 Games as the named activity in written answers: structures and strategies, roles, officials and scoring systems, fitness profiles, energy systems in intermittent sport, ethics and fair play.

## 3. Traps

- Do not teach individual game skills here; each category has its own notes.
- Structures and strategies in general are taught in "Structures, strategies and compositional elements"; apply them briefly.

## 4. What exams ask

- Games are the most common context in written answers: weaknesses in an opposition team, strategies, roles, officials, scoring systems, fitness components.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Explain two principles of play in an invasion game of your choice."
2. 6 marks, Ordinary: "Name the three categories of games and give two examples of each."
