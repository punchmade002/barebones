# Package: pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions-s4 — Rules, rituals and conventions

- **loId:** `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions-s4` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-structures-strategies-roles-conventions` · **syllabusRef:** 3.4
- **Syllabus:** "organise an event/performance in which the particular rules, roles, rituals
  and conventions of the activity are respected"; "demonstrate the ability to communicate
  respectfully with performers/players and officials". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher
  Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: definitions of rules,
  rituals and conventions with examples; why each matters; organising an event that respects
  them; respectful communication. Officials' knowledge of rules and scoring systems is in "The
  role of official"; event safety is in "Safe practice".

## 1. Must cover

Definitions with examples
- [ ] R1 Rules: the formal, written regulations of an activity, set by its governing body and enforced by officials: scoring, permitted actions, playing area and equipment, time, sanctions (e.g. the offside rule in soccer; the four-step rule in Gaelic football; a foot fault in tennis).
- [ ] R2 Rituals: customary, often symbolic actions repeated before, during or after an activity (e.g. the national anthem before an international match; the haka performed by the New Zealand rugby team; a team huddle; a minute's silence).
- [ ] R3 Conventions: unwritten codes of behaviour that participants are expected to follow, not enforced by rules (e.g. shaking hands with opponents and officials after a match; kicking the ball out of play when an opponent is injured and returning possession; a tennis player raising a hand to apologise for a net cord winner; applauding opponents off the field).
- [ ] R4 Why they matter: rules make competition fair and safe; rituals build identity, tradition and focus; conventions express respect and fair play and maintain the spirit of the game.

Organising an event that respects them
- [ ] O1 Planning: fixture and format, venue, equipment, officials appointed and briefed, rules clarified to teams in advance, scoring and recording system in place, roles assigned (organiser, officials, timekeeper, first aid, stewards).
- [ ] O2 Building in rituals and conventions: team introductions, handshakes, presentation of awards, acknowledging officials and opponents.
- [ ] O3 Evaluating the event: did it run on time, were rules applied consistently, did participants behave respectfully, what would be changed.

Respectful communication
- [ ] C1 With players/performers: clear, calm instructions; listening; encouraging rather than criticising; appropriate language.
- [ ] C2 With officials: accept decisions, ask questions through the captain at an appropriate time, no dissent, thank officials.
- [ ] C3 Non-verbal communication: body language, eye contact, gestures, hand signals (e.g. a referee's signals); tone of voice.
- [ ] C4 Effective communication skills (an Ordinary pattern: list three other than hand signals): clear voice, short precise instructions, positive body language, active listening, use of a whistle, consistent signals, eye contact.

## 3. Traps

- A convention is unwritten; if a rule-book requires it (e.g. kit colours), it is a rule.
- Do not invent rule details for specific sports beyond those named here; describe rules generally if unsure.
- Officials' scoring/recording systems and safety checks belong to "The role of official".

## 4. What exams ask

- Define rules, rituals and conventions as they relate to physical activity (8 marks, Higher).
- Outline three rules of a named activity (Ordinary, sometimes paired with a scoring system).
- Outline three effective communication skills other than hand signals.
