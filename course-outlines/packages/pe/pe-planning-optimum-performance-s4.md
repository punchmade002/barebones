# Package: pe-planning-optimum-performance-s4 — Planning for optimum performance

- **loId:** `pe-planning-optimum-performance-s4` · **chapterId:**
  `pe-planning-optimum-performance` · **syllabusRef:** 4.4
- **Syllabus:** "explain how information from their performance analysis was used to inform
  planning to achieve performance goals"; "present performance goals to address areas for
  improvement"; "design a practice/training plan to improve personal performance in relation to
  performance goals"; "provide evidence on the effectiveness of the programme design in
  achieving the performance goals". Ordinary and Higher level; no Higher Level only material.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write in this order: from analysis to goals;
  types of goal and SMART goals; designing the practice/training plan; monitoring, reflection
  and adapting; concluding analysis and evidence of effectiveness; a short closing block on how
  this cycle is assessed in the Physical Activity Project (see the outline's Appendix B), with no
  coursework instructions.

## 1. Must cover

From analysis to goals
- [ ] G1 Using analysis data to choose priorities: the four areas identified in "Methods of analysis" become four performance goals; explain the link between each piece of evidence and each goal.
- [ ] G2 Short-term goals (a few days to weeks; steps) versus long-term goals (a season or more).
- [ ] G3 Outcome goals (results, e.g. win the league), performance goals (a personal standard independent of others, e.g. raise free-throw success from 60% to 75%), process goals (technique or behaviour, e.g. keep the elbow under the ball).
- [ ] G4 SMART goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound; writing a short-term performance goal in SMART form.

Designing the practice/training plan
- [ ] P1 Plan contents: the goals; the methods and drills chosen for each goal (skill practice methods, fitness methods, psychological strategies); sessions per week, duration and progression across the weeks; FITT and the principles of training and of effective practice applied; warm-up and cool-down; rest; how progress will be recorded.
- [ ] P2 Justify the plan's design from theory (e.g. distributed practice for a beginner, progressive overload for strength).

Monitoring, reflection and adapting
- [ ] M1 Ongoing evidence: training logs or diaries, video at intervals, mid-programme retests, heart-rate data, coach feedback.
- [ ] M2 Reflection: what went well, what didn't, why, what to change next; adapting the plan (e.g. increasing difficulty when a target is reached early; changing a drill that is not working; adjusting for injury).

Concluding analysis and evidence of effectiveness
- [ ] E1 Repeat the same methods of analysis used at the start, under the same conditions, so the results can be compared fairly.
- [ ] E2 Evidence: before-and-after test scores, success rates, video comparison, statistics; was each goal achieved, partly achieved or not achieved, and why.
- [ ] E3 Next steps: new goals for continued improvement.

Link to assessment
- [ ] A1 The Physical Activity Project (20% of the overall mark) follows this cycle over eight to ten weeks in the role of performer or coach/choreographer, in four sections: performance analysis; identification of four performance goals; evidence of ongoing training/practice and reflection; concluding analysis. Written-exam questions may ask "as part of your studies you designed…".

## 3. Traps

- Do not give coursework instructions (word counts, file formats, templates); the SEC sets those.
- Goal setting as a psychological strategy is in `pe-performance-demands` ("Psychological preparation"); the coach guiding goals is in "The role of coach/choreographer". Here goals are part of the planning cycle.
- Methods of analysis themselves are in "Methods of analysis"; refer to them.

## 4. What exams ask

- Define a short-term goal and a performance goal; write a short-term performance goal.
- Read a periodised or training plan and interpret it (overlap with `pe-performance-demands`).
- Explain how analysis informed planning; describe how progress was evidenced (often framed as "As part of your studies you…").

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks, Higher: "Define a short-term goal and a performance goal, and write a short-term performance goal for a physical activity of your choice."
2. 12 marks, Higher: "Explain how information from a performance analysis can be used to plan for optimum performance, and how the effectiveness of the plan can be evidenced." Model: analysis → four goals → plan → monitoring → concluding analysis with the same methods.
