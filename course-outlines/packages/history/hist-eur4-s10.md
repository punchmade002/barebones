## 3. Traps

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay (2006, 2016, 2018, 2024, 2025 style): Solzhenitsyn and de Beauvoir as writers and social critics; the controversy each caused; their influence. Marks go to a clear thesis, a balanced profile of each (who, what they wrote with dates, what they challenged, how contemporaries and historians judged them), and a comparison (East: official repression, exile; West: freedom to publish, public debate, conservative attack).
- Strong answers connect the work to events: the Khrushchev thaw and the 1962 novel; censorship, 1968 publication abroad, the 1970 Nobel and the 1973–74 sequence of The Gulag Archipelago, arrest, expulsion; for Beauvoir, Les Temps modernes, Algeria and the 1954 Goncourt.
- Higher essay on religious practice (2009, 2012, 2015 style, often paired with youth culture, the media or women): decline of observance in the West with causes and national contrasts, against persecution or control in the East, with the ecumenical movement and Islam as further changes. Credit for a structure that compares West and East and for named examples (Mindszenty, Wyszyński, Leipzig, the Netherlands, the World Council of Churches).
- Ordinary Level 30/40-mark paragraph on Solzhenitsyn: arrest in 1945 for criticising Stalin in letters, eight years in camps then exile, Ivan Denisovich (1962, Novy Mir, Khrushchev), the Gulag Archipelago (Paris, 1973), Nobel (1970), expulsion (1974). Each correct, dated point earns marks.
- Ordinary Level paragraph on de Beauvoir: Les Temps modernes (1945), The Mandarins (Goncourt 1954), memoirs (1958), political causes; The Second Sex appears in other questions.
- Short ordinary answers: define "dissident writer" (a writer who opposed the state's policy and faced punishment; examiners accept "disagreed with the system" only if tied to censorship, camp or exile); name two reasons church attendance fell; say what the ecumenical movement aimed at.
- Exam tip material: always give the year of each work, separate the novel from the non-fiction book, and link each event (Writers' Union expulsion, KGB seizure) to its consequence.
