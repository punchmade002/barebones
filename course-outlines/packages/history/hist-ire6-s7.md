# Package: hist-ire6-s7 — The impact of EEC membership; case study: the impact of the EEC on fisheries

- **loId:** `hist-ire6-s7` · **chapterId:** `hist-ire6`
- **Syllabus:** Later Modern Ireland, Topic 6 (Government, economy and society in the Republic of Ireland, 1949–1989), Society and economy: "economic change and its social consequences; the impact of EEC membership." Case study: the impact of the EEC on fisheries. Outline scope: the Common Agricultural Policy and farm incomes; regional and social funds; foreign investment and new industry; the decline of protected industries; equality directives and their effect on women's rights; changes in attitudes and sovereignty; then, in depth, Irish fishing before 1973, the accession terms and the Common Fisheries Policy, Bord Iascaigh Mhara and fleet development, ports (Killybegs, Castletownbere), foreign fleets in Irish waters, benefits and costs for coastal communities, and the conflicting views of fishermen and government. Key concept: common market. No key personality is assigned. Write in full Higher Level detail.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Before 1958 Ireland was protectionist and stagnant; the First Programme and Lemass moved it towards free trade, including the Anglo-Irish Free Trade Agreement (1965), so protected industries already faced competition before 1973.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Ireland applied to join in 1961 and 1967 and was blocked both times by de Gaulle's veto; Britain's entry and Ireland's followed; the 1972 referendum approved entry (detail belongs to the notes on the UN and the EEC).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Ireland depended heavily on the British market for exports, above all farm produce.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) The economic problems of the 1970s and 1980s (oil crises, inflation, unemployment, debt, emigration) are the setting for the later gains and costs.

Key concept and terms
- [ ] K1 Common market: free trade among members, a common external tariff, free movement of goods, workers and capital.
- [ ] K2 Terms defined as used: Common Agricultural Policy, intervention, quota, derogation, total allowable catch, relative stability, Bord Iascaigh Mhara, European Regional Development Fund, directive.

What membership changed
- [ ] E1 Entry on 1 January 1973 (with Britain and Denmark); tariffs on trade with members phased out over a transition period; common external tariff adopted.
- [ ] E2 EEC law took precedence in the areas covered by the treaties; the Third Amendment (1972) made this possible; Irish courts apply EEC law and the European Court of Justice.
- [ ] E3 Irish participation: ministers in the Council, members in the European Parliament, Patrick Hillery as first Irish Commissioner, the first Irish presidency of the Council (1975).
- [ ] E4 Less dependence on Britain: exports and trade diversified towards the continent.
- [ ] E5 Net transfers from the EEC budget (farm support, funds): a large source of income by the 1980s.
- [ ] E6 The European Monetary System (March 1979) and the end of the sterling link.

Agriculture and the CAP
- [ ] C1 How the CAP worked: guaranteed minimum prices, intervention buying of surplus.
- [ ] C2 Farm prices rose towards EEC levels; farm incomes rose sharply in the early years (beef and dairy).
- [ ] C3 Land values and investment (machinery, buildings, drainage) rose, helped by grants.
- [ ] C4 Uneven gains: larger farms in the east and south gained most; small western farms least.
- [ ] C5 Overproduction and milk quotas (1984).
- [ ] C6 Falling numbers on the land and falling share of employment and output from agriculture.

Regional and social funds
- [ ] R1 The European Regional Development Fund (1975) and Ireland's share.
- [ ] R2 The European Social Fund: training and employment schemes (including state training).
- [ ] R3 What the money was spent on: roads, water and sewerage, telecommunications, industrial estates, under Irish government direction.
- [ ] R4 The 1988 reform of the structural funds and the 1989–93 programmes.

Foreign investment, industry and the decline of protected firms
- [ ] I1 Foreign firms came to sell into the EEC, drawn by IDA grants, an English-speaking young workforce and low taxes.
- [ ] I2 The 10 per cent tax rate on manufacturing profits (1981).
- [ ] I3 New leading sectors: electronics, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, engineering (example: Digital in Galway, opened 1971, before entry).
- [ ] I4 Decline of protected firms (clothing, textiles, footwear): lost protection, closures, job losses.
- [ ] I5 Unemployment of the late 1970s and 1980s: membership did not solve all weaknesses.
- [ ] I6 Exports diversified; manufactured goods became the larger share of exports.

Social effects, equality and sovereignty
- [ ] W1 Equal pay (1975) and equal treatment (1976) directives; reinforcement of the Anti-Discrimination (Pay) Act 1974 and Employment Equality Act 1977.
- [ ] W2 The marriage bar (lifted 1973) and the 1978 social welfare directive (changes in the 1980s).
- [ ] W3 Free movement: easier emigration and more contact with continental Europe; changed attitudes and a wider outlook.
- [ ] W4 Sovereignty: critics' view of lost independence; the debate returned in the Crotty case and later referendums (pointer only).

Case study: Irish fishing before 1973
- [ ] F1 A small, under-developed industry: old, small inshore boats; limited range; a few thousand full-time fishermen in coastal counties, many part-time.
- [ ] F2 Small home market (little fish eaten outside Fridays); little exported.
- [ ] F3 Foreign trawlers fished close to the coast under historic rights because the exclusive Irish limit was narrow.
- [ ] F4 Bord Iascaigh Mhara set up in 1952: grants and loans for boats, training, marketing; fleet modernisation in the 1960s and early 1970s.
- [ ] F5 Fishing was a tiny share of output and exports, so it was a minor concern in the negotiations.

Case study: accession terms and the Common Fisheries Policy
- [ ] F6 The 1970 EEC principle of equal access to all members' waters.
- [ ] F7 Ireland's derogation: exclusive six-mile zone, 12 miles in parts of the coast, to the end of 1982.
- [ ] F8 Criticism: the government gave fishing away to secure the farming terms.
- [ ] F9 The Hague resolution (1976): 200-mile limits from 1977; recognition of Ireland's special needs (the "Hague preferences").
- [ ] F10 The Common Fisheries Policy (January 1983): total allowable catches, national quotas by relative stability, a 12-mile coastal belt, review after ten years.
- [ ] F11 Irish complaint: quotas small compared with the value of the fish in Irish waters.

Case study: development, ports and foreign fleets
- [ ] F12 BIM and grant-financed new, larger vessels; fleet growth in the late 1970s and 1980s.
- [ ] F13 Killybegs (leading port; pelagic fleet; processing) and Castletownbere (whitefish).
- [ ] F14 Investment in other ports (Dunmore East, Howth, Rossaveal, Kilmore Quay).
- [ ] F15 Foreign fleets in Irish waters; the Naval Service as fishery protection; disputes over illegal fishing and quota shares.

Case study: benefits, costs and conflicting views
- [ ] F16 Benefits: investment, jobs in some coastal towns, processing and exports, a large area of sea.
- [ ] F17 Costs: quotas, small inshore fishermen and smaller harbours losing out, young people leaving.
- [ ] F18 Fishermen's view (small share of their own waters; fisheries treated as secondary to farming) against the government's view (the best terms available).
- [ ] F19 Opponents of entry (Common Market Defence Campaign) against supporters; fishing as a lasting example in debates on the costs of membership.

Assessment (paired judgement points, final block)
- [ ] S1 Achievements: better-priced markets, transfers, foreign investment, equality law, wider outlook.
- [ ] S2 Limits: closures and unemployment, uneven farm gains, the west and small farms, fishing gaining less than farming.
- [ ] S3 Contemporary view: supporters (modernisation) against opponents (sovereignty, traditional industries).
- [ ] S4 Later view: membership underpinned transformation, but the full benefits came later (1990s) and not evenly.

## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "Intel at Leixlip" as an example of foreign investment, because Intel's Leixlip plant dates from 1989 at the very end of the period and its start year is not certain. Digital in Galway is usable only as an example of foreign investment drawn by IDA grants and sectors, dated 1971.
- Do not use: Digital in Galway as an example of firms drawn by EEC membership or market access after entry. It opened in 1971, before entry in 1973; say "opened 1971, before entry".
- Do not use: the marriage bar's removal in 1973 as a result of EEC directives. It followed the Commission on the Status of Women (1970–72) and fell in the year of entry; say "lifted in 1973, the year of entry", not that the EEC forced it.
- Do not use: the Common Market Defence Campaign as the source of the fishing complaint. It opposed entry on sovereignty grounds in general; the fishing grievance came from fishermen's organisations and critics of the terms.
- Do not use: "the government replied that Ireland could not manage the waters alone" as a reported government statement, because it is not well sourced. Give the government's side as: it got the best terms it could (the 12-mile zone and the Hague preferences) and fishing mattered little beside farming.
- Do not use: Spanish fleets in Irish waters before 1986 as legal EEC fishing; Spain was not a member until 1986.
- Do not use: any figure for the size of Irish fishing catches, fleets, jobs, quotas as a percentage, farm income rises, or EEC transfers in pounds. Past papers give figures; they are not checked here.
- Do not use: ERDF "created partly because Ireland pressed" as a firm claim; say it was set up in 1975 and Ireland was a leading beneficiary.

Confusable people, terms and dates
- Irish entry on 1 January 1973 vs the May 1972 referendum vs the 1972 accession treaty.
- The 1970 equal-access principle vs the 1976 Hague resolution (200 miles, preferences) vs the 1983 Common Fisheries Policy (quotas). The 1983 CFP replaced the temporary 1973 derogation, which ended at the close of 1982.
- The six-mile exclusive zone (with 12 miles in parts) of 1973 vs the 12-mile belt of 1983.
- Total allowable catch (the whole limit for a species) vs quota (a state's share) vs relative stability (the rule fixing the shares).
- The 1976 Hague fisheries resolution is not the 1969 Hague summit of the EEC leaders.
- Equal pay directive (1975) vs the Irish Anti-Discrimination (Pay) Act (1974); equal treatment directive (1976) vs the Employment Equality Act (1977).
- Milk quotas (1984) vs fish quotas (1983 CFP).
- ERDF (1975, regions) vs the Social Fund (training) vs the 1988 reform (both doubled).
- BIM (1952, pre-EEC state board) vs the EEC's own policy.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, the commonest form, set almost every year: the impact of EEC membership on Ireland to 1989 (economy and society), sometimes paired with the UN (which mattered more), the First Programme, Vatican II or television. Marks go to a clear judgement at the start, then paired points with dates: CAP and farm incomes, transfers and funds, foreign investment, equality law, against closures of protected firms, unemployment, uneven gains and sovereignty. Name the date of entry and at least two dated measures.
- Strong answers divide winners from losers (large farmers and exporters against small western farmers, textile workers and fishermen), and separate the immediate effects of the 1970s from the longer view of the 1990s.
- Higher essay on the impact of the EEC on fisheries (set with television): a mixed verdict is expected, with new investment and a large sea area against small quotas, foreign fleets and grievance. A narrative with no judgement scores poorly.
- Ordinary Level 30/40-mark answer on fishing, set very often: the four or five facts that earn most marks are the pre-1973 state of the industry (small, old inshore boats, foreign trawlers under historic rights), the accession derogation (six and 12 miles to 1982), the Hague 200-mile limit, the 1983 CFP (catches and quotas), and BIM with named ports. Each correct, dated, relevant point is rewarded; students must name the derogation's terms and years.
- Documents-based questions on fishing (Ordinary): expect to identify who vetoed the first application, what access to fish stocks membership gave and which farm subsidy system helped farmers. Know the CAP and the 1973 entry with Britain and Denmark.
- Short answers: the date of entry, who joined with Ireland, what the CAP was, what the ERDF did, which directive gave equal pay.
- Equality: a short paragraph linking the 1975 and 1976 directives to the 1974 and 1977 Irish Acts earns credit only if the dates are right.
- Sovereignty and attitudes: examiners reward a point that membership changed outlook and law as well as income, and that opponents warned of lost independence.
