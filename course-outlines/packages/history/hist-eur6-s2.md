# Package: hist-eur6-s2 — US foreign policy, 1945–1972: Berlin, Korea, Cuba and Vietnam

- **loId:** `hist-eur6-s2` · **chapterId:** `hist-eur6`
- **Syllabus:** Later Modern, The United States and the world, 1945–1989 (Politics): "US foreign
  policy, 1945–1972: Berlin, Korea, Cuba, Vietnam." Outline scope: containment, the Truman
  Doctrine, the Marshall Plan and NATO; Berlin (the airlift 1948–49, the Wall 1961, Kennedy's
  1963 visit); Korea (1950–53: causes, the UN, Inchon, Chinese intervention, MacArthur's
  dismissal, the armistice); Cuba (the revolution 1959, the Bay of Pigs 1961, the missile crisis
  of October 1962 and its resolution); Vietnam to 1972 (aid to France, Eisenhower's advisers,
  Kennedy, Nixon's Vietnamisation and the expansion into Cambodia; Johnson's escalation is a
  separate case study); how successful US leaders were. Key personality: Harry Truman (part 2:
  foreign policy and overall evaluation). Key concepts: internationalism, imperialism. Write in
  full Higher Level detail.

## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The Second World War left the USA the richest industrial power and, until 1949, the only atomic power; the wartime alliance with the USSR broke down as Soviet forces held Eastern Europe (Yalta and Potsdam conferences; Churchill's "iron curtain" speech, 1946).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Pre-war US isolationism, ended by Pearl Harbor and the war.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Germany and Berlin were divided into four occupation zones/sectors (US, British, French, Soviet), with Berlin inside the Soviet zone.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) Communism vs capitalism as the Cold War's basis; the Chinese civil war between Mao's Communists and Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalists.

Key concepts (also in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Internationalism: active US involvement in world affairs after 1945 instead of isolation.
- [ ] K2 Imperialism: domination of one country by another through political, military or economic control; applied to US influence in Cuba and to Soviet control of Eastern Europe.
- [ ] K3 Containment: checking Soviet/communist expansion by firm resistance.
- [ ] K4 Domino theory (essential term).
- [ ] K5 Massive retaliation and flexible response (essential terms).

## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: US troops "about 540,000 in early 1969", because the peak came in 1969 (spring), so say "at the 1969 peak".
- Do not use: "Seoul recaptured on 28 September" as a fixed date, because sources vary by a day or two; say late September 1950.
- Do not use: "the Soviet missiles were removed only in exchange for a secret US concession" without the public pledge; give both terms, and note the Turkey withdrawal came in 1963.
- Do not use: a precise Korean War US death figure other than "about 36,000 in all" (this counts non-battle deaths; battle deaths were lower).
- Do not use: Acheson's speech as the sure cause of the invasion, because historians dispute it; say "may have encouraged".
- Do not use: "Kennedy backed ... the quarantine" as a blockade without noting the term: a blockade is an act of war in law, which is why Kennedy said "quarantine".
- Do not use: refugees "well over two million" with an exact total; say "more than two million".
- Do not use: Johnson-era details (Tonkin Gulf, Rolling Thunder, Westmoreland, Tet) in this subsection.

Confusable people, terms and dates
- Containment (idea, Kennan) vs Truman Doctrine (political and military aid, Greece and Turkey) vs Marshall Plan (economic aid to Western Europe) vs NATO (alliance).
- Berlin blockade (1948–49) vs Berlin Wall (1961) vs Khrushchev's 1958 ultimatum.
- Korea: UN Security Council resolutions came because the USSR was boycotting it. MacArthur led UN forces; the war was never formally declared.
- Cuba: Bay of Pigs (April 1961, Kennedy) vs missile crisis (October 1962). The quarantine (22 October) vs the Soviet ships turning back (24 October) vs Black Saturday (27 October) vs the settlement (28 October).
- Viet Minh (against the French) vs Viet Cong/NLF (in the South) vs North Vietnamese army.
- Eisenhower sent advisers (not combat troops); Kennedy raised the numbers; Diem's fall (2 November 1963) came three weeks before Kennedy's assassination.
- Vietnamisation (Nixon, 1969 on, withdrawal) vs escalation (Johnson, 1965–68).
- Massive retaliation (Eisenhower/Dulles) vs flexible response (Kennedy).

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, the commonest form: Berlin, Korea and Cuba together (a recurring question), or Korea and Cuba, or Korea and Vietnam, or Truman's foreign policy. Marks go to a judgement stated early (how successful, or which was more important) and sustained with dated evidence from each episode. Narrative with no verdict scores poorly.
- Strong answers rank the episodes: for example Berlin as a clear Western success, Korea as a limited success or stalemate, Cuba as a success with a hidden concession, Vietnam as failure. They link them through containment.
- Truman's foreign policy as a judgement: the Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO, airlift and Korea as achievements; China and Korea's stalemate as limits; a named historian's view adds weight. Both foreign and domestic record may be asked, so know where each is covered.
- Why Korea and Vietnam happened: both from containment and the domino theory; differences in method (UN force and a declared limit in Korea; a gradual commitment in Vietnam). Comparing the two impacts (cost, politics, outcome) is a repeat topic.
- Cuba: why the crisis began (Castro, Bay of Pigs, Khrushchev's motives), how Kennedy handled it (ExComm options, the quarantine) and how it ended (both public and secret terms). Marks fall if only the public terms are given.
- Berlin: cause of each crisis (blockade, ultimatum, Wall), the Western response and its result. The airlift as a success without firing a shot is the usual point.
- Ordinary Level 30/40-mark paragraphs on Cuba, Berlin or Korea reward four or five correct dated statements in order (cause, events, result). Cuba is the commonest; the sequence from 1 January 1959 to 28 October 1962 earns marks.
- Ordinary Level short answers on documents (a Kennedy television address, a diplomat's account of the crisis, a family's shelter memoir): who made the statement, what Soviet action it opposed, what ended the crisis. Short factual answers earn the marks.
- Vietnam before Johnson: the domino theory, the 1954 US share of French costs, Diem and the advisers are asked as the origins of the war.
- Nixon's Vietnamisation: its aims, troop figures falling, Cambodia and the protests, and the 1973 end, asked as short and paragraph questions.
- Each of the points above can become an exam tip, for example: state the verdict in the first sentence; give both terms of the Cuban settlement; name the Eisenhower, Kennedy and Nixon steps separately in Vietnam.
