## 3. Traps

- Do not use: "the Dáil cabinet rejected the offer on 10 August", because the exact date and body are uncertain; say August 1921.
- Do not use: "the Free State's parliament and government were to be responsible to the Dáil", because the Treaty put the executive responsible to the Irish parliament (under Canadian practice); say parliament.
- Do not use: "the Treaty said nothing of land annuities" and "Griffith was the first president of the Dáil to head a pro-Treaty government", because both are doubtful or confusing.
- Do not use: "the pro-Treaty side won about three-quarters of the votes in the June 1922 election", because the figure depends on how parties are counted; say pro-Treaty candidates won a clear majority (detail belongs to the Civil War notes).
- Do not use: "Collins, widely seen as most honest..." or any ranking of the delegates' honesty; Griffith's character is a judgement, attribute it.
- Do not use: Laffan as a named historian of Griffith (attribution not checked).
- Confusables: external association is not dominion status; Document No. 2 is de Valera's text, not a Treaty draft; the Provisional Government (Collins, from the Southern Ireland parliament) is different from the Dáil government (Griffith); Cadogan Gardens (Collins) v Hans Place (delegation); Dáil cabinet vote (4–3) v Dáil vote (64–57).
- The delegates did not agree the whole text in advance with Dublin, but they did consult the cabinet on 3 December; do not say they never consulted.
- Neighbouring subsections: the War of Independence and the 1918 election (before); the Civil War, Collins's death, the 1922 election, the Boundary Commission's collapse in 1925 (after); Northern Ireland's government under Craig (partition notes).

## 4. What exams ask

- Why the negotiations and terms were controversial: give three groups of reasons (who negotiated and with what authority; the pressure and the signing without final reference to Dublin; the terms short of the republic: Crown, oath, ports, partition). Each needs a dated fact.
- Treaty and Civil War: link the Dáil split over the Crown, oath and partition, but add other causes (army loyalties, personalities, the way the Treaty was negotiated).
- Key personalities: role of Griffith, Collins and de Valera (what each did, why, with dates), often as a short 20-mark paragraph; marks for a specific fact and a short judgement.
- Document-based questions on the delegation's journey, Hans Place or Cadogan Gardens, and crowds: use the facts in the table to place documents.
- Terms questions: list the terms accurately with the Dominion model, oath, ports, Boundary Commission; marks for the exact detail, not for general descriptions.
- Judgement answers: balance Achievements v Limits; pro-Treaty v anti-Treaty; contemporary v later views.
