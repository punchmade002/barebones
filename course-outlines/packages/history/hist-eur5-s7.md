## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essays on the social and economic effects of decolonisation on Africa (frequent). Marks go to a stated judgement (largely damaging or mixed) and paired points: colonial structures, debt, dependence, famine against some health, education and trade gains.
- Essays on Nyerere and ujamaa, often with Lomé or with another leader. Strong answers state a balanced verdict on ujamaa: gains in education, health and national unity against forced villagisation and economic decline; they date the Arusha Declaration (1967), villagisation (1973–74) and the 1986 reversal.
- Lomé questions: expect who signed (EEC and ACP), when (1975), what was new (non-reciprocal access), STABEX, and a limit (commodity dependence). Each correct dated point earns marks.
- Development worker questions: the problems they faced and the work they did. Use concrete problems (roads, supplies, cultural misunderstanding, war, projects collapsing after the donor left) and a named example.
- Ordinary Level short answers: define NGO, ujamaa, Lomé; name an Irish agency and its founding date; state what the Arusha Declaration committed Tanzania to.
- Famine questions: link causes (war, drought, policy) to outcomes (relief, Band Aid, Live Aid); better answers add criticisms of relief.
- Evaluating aid: examiners reward both sides (relief, schools, health; tied aid, dependence, support for corrupt regimes).
