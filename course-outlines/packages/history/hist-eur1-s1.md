## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) The French Revolution and Napoleon spread liberal and national ideas; Napoleon dissolved the Holy Roman Empire (1806) and reorganised Germany (Confederation of the Rhine).
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Napoleon's defeat: Leipzig (1813), the first Treaty of Paris (1814), the Hundred Days, Waterloo and the second Treaty of Paris (1815).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) The Habsburg (Austrian) Empire was a dynastic, multinational state, so nationalism threatened to break it apart.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) The great powers of 1815: Austria, Britain, Prussia and Russia, with defeated France soon readmitted.

Key concepts (each defined as used in this topic; also in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Liberalism: constitutions, elected parliaments, civil liberties (free press, free speech), limits on rulers; mainly supported by the educated middle class and students.
- [ ] K2 Nationalism: a people sharing language, culture and history should have its own state; meant unity for Germans and Italians, independence for Greeks, Belgians and Poles.
- [ ] K3 Autocracy: rule by one person with unlimited power, no constitution or elected body (Austria, Russia, Prussia in practice).
- [ ] K4 Conservatism: Metternich's belief in monarchy, aristocracy, the Church, tradition and hierarchy as the basis of order (an essential term, not an outline key concept).

The Congress of Vienna, 1814–15
- [ ] V1 Dates, the leading delegates and the "Big Four" plus Talleyrand.
- [ ] V2 Aims: contain France, restore legitimate rulers, balance of power, reward the victors, prevent revolution.
- [ ] V3 Legitimacy: restored dynasties (Bourbons in France, Spain, Naples; the Pope).
- [ ] V4 Balance of power and the barrier of buffer states around France.
- [ ] V5 Austria's gains: Lombardy-Venetia and Habsburg relatives in central Italy; gave up the Austrian Netherlands.
- [ ] V6 Prussia's gains: Rhineland, Westphalia, part of Saxony, Swedish Pomerania; the "watch on the Rhine".
- [ ] V7 Russia's gains: Congress Poland.
- [ ] V8 Britain's gains: naval bases and colonies; a moderate peace for France.
- [ ] V9 Italy after 1815: divided, Austrian dominance, Papal States restored, Piedmont strengthened by Genoa.
- [ ] V10 Nationalities ignored; judgement of the settlement (it kept the peace among the great powers, but ignored liberal and national feeling).

The German Confederation (Bund)
- [ ] B1 Created by the Federal Act (June 1815) to replace the Holy Roman Empire: 39 sovereign states.
- [ ] B2 The Diet (Bundestag) at Frankfurt: delegates of governments, Austria presiding, no elected parliament, unanimity or large majorities for key decisions.
- [ ] B3 Purpose: security against France and revolution, not German unity.
- [ ] B4 Article 13 (constitutions promised); south German constitutions; Austria and Prussia granted none.
- [ ] B5 Austro-Prussian dualism: the Bund worked only when Austria and Prussia agreed.

The Concert of Europe and the Congress System
- [ ] C1 Quadruple Alliance (November 1815): uphold the settlement, contain France, meet periodically.
- [ ] C2 Holy Alliance (September 1815): Alexander I's idea, Christian principles; Britain stayed out; it came to stand for the eastern autocracies.
- [ ] C3 Aix-la-Chapelle (1818): occupation of France ended, France readmitted.
- [ ] C4 Troppau (1820) and the Troppau Protocol: the right of intervention; Britain's objection.
- [ ] C5 Laibach (1821): Austrian troops into Naples; Piedmont rising crushed.
- [ ] C6 Verona (1822): French intervention in Spain (1823) approved; Britain refused.
- [ ] C7 Britain's withdrawal (Castlereagh to Canning) and the end of the Congress System; what remained was the alliance of the three eastern powers.

Metternich's aims and methods
- [ ] M1 Aims: preserve the 1815 settlement and the Habsburg Empire; resist liberalism and nationalism; Austria's leadership in Germany and Italy.
- [ ] M2 Austria at home: autocracy under Francis I and Ferdinand I; police, informers, opening of letters, censorship; Metternich's rival Kolowrat.
- [ ] M3 The Carlsbad Decrees (1819): the cause (the murder of Kotzebue).
- [ ] M4 The Carlsbad Decrees: terms (Burschenschaften banned, university supervisors, dismissals, press censorship) and the Mainz Central Investigating Commission.
- [ ] M5 The Six Articles (June 1832) and the further July 1832 measures (Ten Articles) after Hambach.
- [ ] M6 Intervention abroad: Austrian troops in Italy (1821, 1831); use of the congresses.
- [ ] M7 Dependence on Prussia (in the Bund) and Russia (in Europe).

Challenges to the system, 1815–1834
- [ ] H1 The Burschenschaften (Jena, 1815): student liberal nationalism; black, red and gold.
- [ ] H2 The Wartburg Festival (October 1817).
- [ ] H3 The revolutions of 1820–21 in Spain, Portugal, Naples and Piedmont, and their suppression.
- [ ] H4 Greece (from 1821): Byron and philhellenism; Navarino (1827); independence (1830–32) against Metternich's wishes.
- [ ] H5 France 1830: the July Revolution replaced Charles X with Louis-Philippe (brief; detail belongs to the France notes).
- [ ] H6 Belgium 1830–31: independence from the Netherlands, Leopold I; neutrality guaranteed 1839. A breach of the 1815 settlement.
- [ ] H7 Poland 1830–31: the November Uprising crushed by Russia.
- [ ] H8 Central Italy 1831: risings in Modena, Parma and the Papal States crushed by Austria.
- [ ] H9 Germany 1830–33: several states (Brunswick, Saxony, Hesse-Kassel, Hanover) gained constitutions after unrest.
- [ ] H10 The Hambach Festival (May 1832): unity, a free press and popular sovereignty.

The Zollverein as Prussia's challenge
- [ ] Z1 The Prussian Customs Law (1818).
- [ ] Z2 The Zollverein in force from 1 January 1834 with 18 states.
- [ ] Z3 How it worked: free trade inside, common external tariff, shared revenue.
- [ ] Z4 Austria outside; Metternich's fear; it gave Prussia economic leadership of Germany (a lead taken further in later notes).

The fall of Metternich, March 1848
- [ ] F1 The trigger: the February 1848 revolution in Paris; economic distress of the 1840s.
- [ ] F2 13 March 1848 in Vienna: demonstrations, shooting, resignation that evening, flight to England.
- [ ] F3 Consequence, briefly: revolution spread across the Habsburg lands and Germany (point to the 1848 notes; no detail here).

Key personality: Clemens Metternich (one block, every point)
- [ ] P1 Who he was: Rhineland noble, born Koblenz 1773; the French revolutionary armies took his family estates.
- [ ] P2 Career: ambassador (Dresden, Berlin, Paris), Austrian foreign minister from 1809, state chancellor from 1821; host and dominant figure at Vienna.
- [ ] P3 What he did, as a direct participant: the settlement, the congresses, the Carlsbad Decrees and 1832 measures, intervention in Italy.
- [ ] P4 Shaped by events: Greece, 1830 and the Zollverein showed the limits of his power; 1848 ended his career.
- [ ] P5 His ideas in his own words: denied there was a "system" (he saw it as defending the natural order); Italy "a geographical expression".
- [ ] P6 Contemporaries: conservatives (rulers, Tsar) saw a guardian of peace; liberals and nationalists a symbol of reaction and police rule.
- [ ] P7 Historians: favourable (Srbik, Kissinger; Sked's later defence) vs critical (Schroeder; German nationalist historians such as Treitschke).
- [ ] P8 Later life: exile 1848, return to Vienna 1851, death 1859; memoirs published from 1880.

Assessment (paired judgement points, final block)
- [ ] S1 Success: no general war among the great powers, 1815–1853.
- [ ] S2 Success: revolutions in Italy and liberal movements in Germany crushed; Habsburg Empire intact; Austrian presidency of the Bund kept.
- [ ] S3 Limit: the Congress System broke up in the 1820s; Greek and Belgian independence changed the 1815 map.
- [ ] S4 Limit: the Zollverein built Prussian leadership in Germany without Austria.
- [ ] S5 Limit: repression without reform (no Austrian constitution, nationalities unsatisfied); peace also owed to war-weariness and British sea power.
- [ ] S6 Overall: change delayed for over thirty years, causes not removed; the system collapsed within days in March 1848. Contemporary vs later views.

## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: Ceylon in the list of Britain's 1815 gains, because Britain already held it by the Treaty of Amiens (1802). Use Malta, the Cape Colony, Heligoland and the Ionian Islands.
- Do not use: the Greek war "broke the principle of non-intervention", because the Congress System's principle was the right to intervene against revolution; the point is that Britain, France and Russia intervened on the rebels' side, against Metternich's wishes.
- Do not use: Charles X as "the last Bourbon king", because Louis-Philippe was also a Bourbon (the Orléans branch). Say Charles X was the last king of the senior Bourbon line, or just name him.
- Do not use: Srbik and Kissinger alone with "critics argue" for the other side; name Schroeder for the critical view and note Sked's later defence, as in section 2.

Doubtful quotations: do not use
- "When France sneezes, Europe catches cold" and "Error has never approached my spirit" are often attributed to Metternich but are not well attested.
- Do not put any version of "there is no Metternich system" in quotation marks; give it as reported thought only.

Confusable people, terms and dates
- Holy Alliance (September 1815, Christian principles, Britain out) vs Quadruple Alliance (November 1815, the working treaty that set up the congresses, Britain in).
- Congress of Vienna (1814–15) vs the Congress System (the later meetings, 1818–1822).
- Troppau = Opava; Laibach = Ljubljana. Troppau set the principle; Laibach applied it to Naples.
- Castlereagh (to 1822, cooperated but rejected general intervention) vs Canning (from 1822, broke with the system).
- Francis I of Austria is the same man as Francis II, the last Holy Roman Emperor. Ferdinand I of Austria (1835–48) is not Ferdinand of Naples or Ferdinand VII of Spain.
- Carlsbad Decrees (1819, after Kotzebue) vs Six Articles and Ten Articles (1832, after Hambach). Wartburg (1817) vs Hambach (1832).
- Metternich was foreign minister (from 1809) at the Congress; he became state chancellor only in 1821.
- Zollverein: the Prussian Customs Law is 1818, treaties 1833, in force 1834. Austria was never a member.
- German Confederation (1815, 39 states, Austria presiding) vs North German Confederation (1867, Prussia).
- The Diet of the German Confederation was not elected; do not confuse it with the Frankfurt Parliament of 1848.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, the commonest form: how successful/effective was Metternich's system or leadership of European affairs, 1815–1848, or what was his contribution. Marks go to a clear judgement stated at the start and sustained, supported by balanced success-vs-limit points with dates (congresses, Carlsbad, 1830, 1832, Zollverein, 1848). A narrative of events without judgement scores poorly.
- Strong answers separate the short term (1815–1830 largely successful) from the long term (1830 breaches; 1848 collapse), and separate international peace (success) from domestic repression (limit).
- Explaining "the system": students should define it as both international (Congress System, intervention) and internal (censorship, police, Carlsbad, the Bund). Linking the two earns credit.
- Cause and result: why the Carlsbad Decrees were passed (Kotzebue) and what they did; why 1830 weakened the settlement; why the Zollverein threatened Austria; why Metternich fell in March 1848.
- Comparing ideas (2025 style): Metternich's conservative view of society (monarchy, aristocracy, religion, hierarchy, order) set against another figure's; students need Metternich's beliefs stated explicitly, not only his actions.
- Ordinary Level short answers on a document (for example an eyewitness account of March 1848 in Vienna, or a historian's view of his methods): who he was, his title, what method he used (the Congress System, the German Diet, Austria's position in Lombardy-Venetia), when and why his system ended; what the Congress of Vienna aimed at; the 1830 changes in France and Belgium.
- Ordinary Level 30/40-mark paragraph on Metternich: career dates, aims, three or four methods with dates, challenges, fall in 1848. Each correct, relevant, dated statement earns marks.
- Achievements vs limits and contemporary vs later views: rewarded as paired points; naming a historian (Kissinger, Srbik, Schroeder) with their view adds weight.
