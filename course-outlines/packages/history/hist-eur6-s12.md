## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "2.6 million in 1950 to more than 8 million by 1970", because the exact 1950 figure is unreliable and sources differ; use "about" with the rounded range in section 2 (under three million to about eight million, 1950-1970).
- Do not use: enrolment "tripled since 1945" or "from 1945 to 1970", because the war-depressed 1945 level was about 1.7 million; date the tripling from about 1950.
- Do not use: Graham "declined to join Falwell's Moral Majority in 1979", because there is no clear record of an invitation; say he kept his distance from it and warned against mixing religion with party politics.
- Do not use: the Houston speech claim that Kennedy "declared his religion was his private affair" as a quotation; give the separation of church and state point only.
- Do not use: "Church membership ... reaching a high point by the end of the decade" with a precise percentage; use "roughly two-thirds".
- Do not use: Cronkite's broadcast as the cause of Johnson's decision not to stand again, because the evidence for Johnson's remark is weak; say it was one report among several that influenced opinion.
- Do not use: "the university conceded to most demands soon after" as an exact claim; say the rules on political activity were relaxed.
- Do not use: a Bakker prison term, because the sentence was reduced on appeal; say he was convicted of fraud and jailed.
- Do not use: Roe v. Wade or the ERA as detailed content; they belong to the women's rights notes. Mention only as issues that mobilised the religious right.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay on religion as a growing force 1945-1989 (or on religion and the media together): marks go to two stages (1950s revival and civil religion, then the politicised religious right from the 1960s-70s), a clear judgement and dated evidence (1954, 1956, 1962-63, 1979, 1980).
- Higher essay with Graham paired with another personality: Graham's career with dates (1949, 1950, 1956, 1957), his use of media, his links to presidents, and a stated judgement on impact. Name a limit (closeness to Nixon, caution on civil rights) as well as achievements.
- Ordinary Level 30/40-mark paragraph on Billy Graham: birth and training, the 1949 Los Angeles crusade and why it succeeded (press coverage), the Association and radio (1950), Christianity Today (1956), the crusades and presidents. Each dated fact earns marks.
- Ordinary Level paragraph on the revival: church membership, the Pledge (1954), the motto (1956), anti-communism, suburban churches.
- Role of the mass media: Kennedy-Nixon debate, Vietnam coverage, Watergate and the press; students must link the medium to a political effect, not only describe it.
- Moral Majority and the religious right: who founded it and when, its aims, its role in 1980, and why it faded.
- Higher education: why numbers grew (GI Bill, affluence, federal aid), then the Free Speech Movement (cause, leader, protest, outcome) and wider campus protest.
- Short-answer and document questions: define fundamentalism or the Moral Majority in one or two precise sentences; identify an item from its date; use one source detail then add own knowledge.
- Paired-point judgements (achievements vs limits, contemporary vs later views) are rewarded, with a historian's or contemporary's view only where the checked facts allow.
