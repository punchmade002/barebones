## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Northern Ireland was governed by a Unionist (mainly Protestant) government at Stormont; Catholics and nationalists were a large minority and Derry (Londonderry) had a Catholic nationalist majority.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Derry's local government was controlled by unionists despite that majority (gerrymandered ward boundaries); Derry suffered high unemployment and emigration.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Terence O'Neill was Prime Minister from 1963; his government's modernisation programme included expansion of higher education, new towns and economic planning.

Key concepts (each defined as used in this case study; also in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Discrimination: unequal treatment because of religion or background; here the claim that the location decision favoured the unionist east over the nationalist west.
- [ ] K2 Propaganda: material used to shape opinion; used by the Derry campaign (meetings, pamphlets, motorcade, slogans) and by the government (presenting Coleraine as a sound planning choice).
- [ ] K3 "West of the Bann" (essential term): the grievance that investment, industry and public bodies were placed east of the River Bann.
- [ ] K4 Lockwood Committee, University for Derry Committee, New University of Ulster, Magee College, Robbins Report, Benson Report (essential terms, also in keyTerms).

Background and causes
- [ ] B1 Magee College's position: founded in Derry in 1865 by the Presbyterian Church; taught Trinity College Dublin degrees; not a university; Derry's existing higher education.
- [ ] B2 Higher education in Northern Ireland before the 1960s: only one university, Queen's University Belfast.
- [ ] B3 The Robbins Report (1963) and the UK-wide expansion of higher education: the context for planning a second university.
- [ ] B4 Why Derry expected the university: second city, existing college, high unemployment, long neglect; the site as a test of Stormont's fairness.
- [ ] B5 Derry's economic decline and its traditional industries (shirt-making; mainly female employment, high male unemployment).

The Lockwood Committee and the Derry campaign, 1963–65
- [ ] L1 Lockwood Committee: appointed 1963; chairman Sir John Lockwood; its task (advise on higher education in Northern Ireland, including a second university and its site).
- [ ] L2 The University for Derry Committee: formed to campaign for the city; John Hume as chair.
- [ ] L3 Cross-community support: nationalists and some unionists and business people in Derry backed the campaign.
- [ ] L4 The arguments for Derry: population, existing Magee buildings, need for jobs, fairness to the west.
- [ ] L5 Other towns competed (Coleraine, others); the committee took evidence.

The decision, February 1965
- [ ] D1 The Lockwood report (February 1965) recommended Coleraine, County Londonderry, not Derry.
- [ ] D2 The reasons given for Coleraine: central site, access, room for expansion, a planned new university town (the government's case).
- [ ] D3 The motorcade to Stormont (February 1965): Derry's protest against the decision.
- [ ] D4 The "faceless men" allegation: that unseen people, not an open process, had decided the matter; no proof offered.
- [ ] D5 The Stormont debate and vote: the government accepted the report and won the vote; nationalists and some Derry unionists opposed.
- [ ] D6 The campaign continued after the vote and the grievance lasted for years.

Outcome
- [ ] O1 The New University of Ulster opened at Coleraine in 1968.
- [ ] O2 Derry was left without a new university; Magee remained in Derry (later part of the University of Ulster, formed 1984 by the merger of the New University of Ulster and Ulster Polytechnic).

Related "west of the Bann" grievances
- [ ] W1 The Benson Report (1963) and railway closures: the Derry Road line (Portadown–Omagh–Derry) closed in 1965.
- [ ] W2 Craigavon: the new city between Lurgan and Portadown, designated 1965 (following the Matthew Report, 1963), seen in Derry as drawing investment east.
- [ ] W3 Derry's economic decline: unemployment, emigration, few new industries.
- [ ] W4 The link to local government: unionist control of Londonderry Corporation meant Derry's own representatives could not influence the decisions.
- [ ] W5 Link to housing: overcrowding in Derry (the direct action of 1968 belongs to the civil rights notes).

Key personality: John Hume, part 1 (one block, every point)
- [ ] H1 Who he was: born Derry 1937; educated in Derry and at St Patrick's College, Maynooth; a teacher; helped found a credit union in Derry (about 1960).
- [ ] H2 What he did in this case study: chaired the University for Derry Committee (1964–65); organised across the community; led public meetings and the protest.
- [ ] H3 Participant: he was a leader of the campaign, not an observer.
- [ ] H4 Shaped by events: defeat taught him that protest needed organisation and cross-community support; the campaign made him a public figure.
- [ ] H5 Contemporaries: seen by supporters as moderate and effective; some unionists saw him as a nationalist agitator.
- [ ] H6 Historians: see the campaign as the start of his career and of his constitutional, cross-community approach.
- [ ] H7 Pointer only: later roles (Derry Citizens' Action Committee in 1968, Stormont MP for Foyle in 1969, founder of the SDLP in 1970, MP for Foyle from 1983) belong to later notes.

Significance and assessment (final block, paired judgement points)
- [ ] S1 For Derry: confirmed a sense of neglect; a clear example of the discrimination grievance; fed the civil rights movement in Derry.
- [ ] S2 For Hume: experience of organising a broad campaign; reputation.
- [ ] S3 For the civil rights movement: showed that public investment could be a political issue; added to housing and voting grievances.
- [ ] S4 Nationalist case strong (size, Magee, unemployment); government case strong (central site, formal committee, room to grow).
- [ ] S5 Nationalist case limited (no proof of a plot); government case limited (a small town; wider pattern of investment in the east).
- [ ] S6 Contemporary view (nationalist: discrimination; unionist: planning decision) against later historians (a symbol of wider inequality rather than proof of one plot).
- [ ] S7 Both sides agreed Derry deserved higher education; the dispute was about site, not principle.

## 3. Traps

Do not use (from the current notes)
- Do not use: "The Robbins Report ... Northern Ireland began planning a second university" as a simple cause, because the Lockwood Committee was appointed in the same year and the timing is close. Say Robbins and Lockwood belong to the same wave of expansion.
- Do not use: the Derry Road closure "left Derry without a direct rail link to Belfast", because the Derry–Coleraine–Belfast line stayed open. Say it ended the line through Omagh to Portadown and weakened the city's links with the south-west and Dublin direction.
- Do not use: "faceless men" as "a popular slogan" with a named originator. Describe it as an allegation made by Derry supporters.
- Do not use: Hume "led deputations and public protests in 1964" or any date more exact than 1964–65 for his campaign work.
- Do not use: "Hume was the founder of the credit union movement in Derry" as a sweeping claim; say he helped found a credit union in Derry.
- Do not use: "first students arrived that autumn" or any exact date within 1968 for the opening.
- Do not use: the claim that Magee "became part of the University of Ulster formed in 1984" with a year for Magee's joining; say it later became a campus of the University of Ulster.
- Do not use: "Derry housing protesters in 1968" in detail; it belongs to the civil rights notes.

Confusable people, terms and dates
- Derry / Londonderry; Londonderry Corporation vs County Londonderry (Coleraine was in the county, not the city).
- Lockwood Committee (1963–65, this case) vs the Lockwood report (February 1965).

- New University of Ulster (Coleraine, 1968) vs University of Ulster (1984 merger).
- Queen's University Belfast vs Magee College vs Trinity College Dublin (whose degrees Magee taught).

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay (2008, 2009, 2012, 2016, 2019, 2024 style): why the Coleraine decision was controversial, and how it contributed to tension in Derry or to the civil rights movement. Marks go to a clear thesis, the facts in order (Lockwood 1963, report February 1965, motorcade, Stormont vote, 1968 opening) and links to the wider "west of the Bann" pattern. Essays that only list the decision score poorly.
- Comparative essay: this controversy against the Apprentice Boys, or against civil rights activity. Students need the key facts of each and a stated judgement of which divided Derry more; the controversy is usually presented as a political dispute within a formal process.
- DBQ (2022 Higher, 40 marks): document-based questions on the University for Derry campaign; marks for comparing two documents on cross-community opposition, judging a source's reliability (eyewitness, later account, strong opinions) and a closing question on how Coleraine and other issues raised tension in Derry. Students must place the document in context (Lockwood, 1965, Derry) and name at least two other grievances (rail closure, Craigavon, housing, local government).
- Ordinary Level 40-mark paragraph (2006 to 2020, 2024 style): the case for Coleraine, the case for Derry, the role of Lockwood, the part played by Hume, and the opening of the university. Each correct dated statement earns marks; "central site, access, room for expansion" is the standard government case.
- Ordinary Level short answers on a document: where the university was to be built, why Coleraine (central location between Belfast and Derry) and why Derry people reacted.
- Hume as part-1 personality: his role as chair, the cross-community base, and what the campaign meant for his career; an answer should not narrate his later SDLP career.
- Paired judgement points (the final block) are rewarded: strength and limit of each side's case, contemporary versus later views, and the conclusion that it was a symbol of inequality rather than proof of a plot.
