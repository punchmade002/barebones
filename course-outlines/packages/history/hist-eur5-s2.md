## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "Indonesia abolished the Netherlands-Indonesian Union unilaterally in 1954", because the unilateral abrogation came in 1956. Say only that it was a weak link that did not last.
- Do not use: "Dutch refused to accept it for four years" as a bare statement; say the Dutch refused to recognise the proclamation until the 1949 transfer of sovereignty.
- Do not use: "the Geneva Accords, signed on 21 July 1954", as a single exact date; the ceasefire agreements are dated 20 July and the final declaration 21 July. Say "July 1954". Likewise the United States did not sign.
- Do not use: "recognised ... as a free state within the French Union" without the Indochinese Federation, because the March 1946 text named both.
- Do not use: "more than 10,000" French captured, because counts vary; say about 10,000.
- Do not use: "Dien Bien Phu ... news reached Geneva the day after the surrender"; say the Indochina talks opened on 8 May 1954, the day after the surrender, but give no more.
- Do not use: "the Netherlands recognised the Republic's authority over Java, Madura and Sumatra" without "de facto"; Dutch sovereignty was retained in the interim, and that ambiguity caused the later dispute over meaning.
- Do not use: "the UN Security Council condemned the Dutch action"; the resolutions of December 1948 and January 1949 called for a ceasefire, release of the leaders and restoration of the Republic's government, but did not formally condemn.
- Do not use: "Linggadjati Agreement of 15 November 1946" without noting that this was when it was initialled; formal signing followed in 1947.
- Do not use: the Haiphong death toll or any figure for Indonesian killings other than the stated range.
- Do not use: the claim that Ho's Viet Minh "fought the Japanese from the hills" as a large campaign; their wartime fighting was small-scale guerrilla activity.

- Democratic Republic of Vietnam (Ho, North) vs State of Vietnam (Bảo Đại, 1949, French-backed) vs Republic of Vietnam (the South from 1955, not in this subsection).
- First Indochina War (1946–54, France) vs the later American war in Vietnam.

- Sukarno vs Suharto; Hatta was vice-president.
- French Union (1946) vs Indochinese Federation (1887, Indochina's own unit).
- PKI (Indonesian communists) is not the Viet Minh.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay: Ho Chi Minh and Sukarno, one or both, with judgement on how effective each was in winning independence and in what they failed to achieve. Marks go to a stated judgement, then achievements (independence, defeat of colonial restoration) against limits (division of Vietnam; authoritarian drift and 1965 in Indonesia), with dates.
- Comparison essays (Gandhi with Ho, Gandhi with Sukarno, Ho with Sukarno): contrast aims and methods (armed struggle and a communist party-state vs negotiation, diplomacy and balancing), and how colonial powers responded (France fought, the Dutch fought then yielded to UN and US pressure).
- Higher question on Sukarno's Indonesia: list internal problems (instability, regional rebellion, army-communist rivalry, economic decline) and external problems (the Dutch and West New Guinea, Malaysia, relations with the UN and China). Each needs a named event with a date.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark profile of Ho Chi Minh: birth, years abroad, Communist Party, Viet Minh 1941, proclamation 2 September 1945, war with France, Dien Bien Phu, Geneva, death 1969. Each correct, dated statement earns a mark.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark profile of Sukarno: birth 1901, engineer, PNI 1927, imprisonment 1929, Japanese occupation, proclamation with Hatta, first president, Guided Democracy, fall.
- Ordinary Level 40-mark questions: one strength and one weakness of Ho, or Sukarno's career in four or five points, or the Dutch police actions and how independence came; a named event, a date and a consequence for each point.
- Document questions (Ordinary Level) on the French Union and the Vietnam war: identify the French Union, what the United States was doing, why the region mattered (rubber, tin, trade); read the document for the answer, not memory.
- Weak answers narrate Dien Bien Phu without saying why it ended the war; strong ones link it to Geneva and the 1954 division. In the Indonesian case, strong answers show diplomatic pressure (UN, US) mattered as much as fighting.
