## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Emigration from 1840 created a large Irish community in the USA, Britain, Canada and Australia, so "Ireland" was imagined abroad as much as lived at home.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Irish America was mainly Catholic and had faced discrimination; its leaders sought acceptance (Al Smith's defeat in 1928 is the marker used for JFK).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Ireland in the 1950s: heavy emigration and economic stagnation, then the turn to economic expansion under Seán Lemass and T. K. Whitaker's programme (1958).

Key concepts and terms (each in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Stage Irishman: 19th-century theatrical stereotype (drunken, quick-tempered, sentimental, comic) that passed into film.
- [ ] K2 Céilí band: group playing traditional dance music for dancers.
- [ ] K3 Bord Fáilte: the Irish Tourist Board, set up 1952.
- [ ] K4 An Tóstal: national festival of Irish culture and heritage, from 1953.
- [ ] K5 Heritage tourism: travel to places of historical and cultural interest.
- [ ] K6 Roots tourism: people of Irish descent visiting ancestral places.
- [ ] K7 Folk revival: popular interest in traditional and ballad music in the late 1950s and 1960s.
- [ ] K8 Film censor: official under the Censorship of Films Act 1923.
- [ ] K9 Assimilation vs keeping an Irish identity (chapter concept): how images and heritage links sustained Irishness among people who were assimilating.

Hollywood's Irish

- [ ] H11 (context) Film in Ireland: Censorship of Films Act 1923, Ardmore Studios 1958; Irish audiences saw mainly Hollywood and British films.

Key personality: Maureen O'Hara (one block, every point)
- [ ] P1 Who she was: born Maureen FitzSimons, Ranelagh, Dublin, 1920; Abbey Theatre school; film career began in Britain, then to Hollywood (1939).

## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: Jamaica Inn (1939) as "her first Hollywood film", because it was a British production made in Britain. It was her breakthrough film under Hitchcock; her first Hollywood film was The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1939).
- Do not use: Danny Boy "written in 1910 ... to an Irish air" or as a product of the Irish-American song industry, because the lyrics were by an Englishman (1910) and were fitted to the Londonderry Air only in 1913. Use the wording in section 2.
- Do not use: "Irish critics objected" to The Quiet Man as if Irish opinion was united, because contemporary Irish reception was mixed and the film was popular; criticism of its stereotypes is strongest in later commentary. Say "some critics".
- Do not use: Gael-Linn recordings "reaching emigrant audiences", An Coimisiún le Rincí Gaelacha, the Irish Hospitals' Sweepstake and the 1939 New York World's Fair pavilion; they are not in the outline's scope, and the emigrant-audience claim is unchecked.
- Do not use: "genealogy services" or "church records" as a developed industry in the 1950s–60s, because no checked example exists. Describe roots tourism generally, name no institution.
- Do not use: "his portrait hung in many Irish homes" as a fact; say instead that his death was widely mourned in Ireland.
- Do not use: a claim that Kennedy's visit "caused" confidence or a policy change, because historians see it as fitting Lemass's Ireland.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay on film and music and/or tourism and heritage: marks go for explaining how images were created (stage Irishman, Ford, The Quiet Man, Irish-American song, McCormack, the folk revival) and how they turned into links with the diaspora (Bord Fáilte, An Tóstal, Shannon, roots tourism). Name a film, a song or performer, and a tourism body with dates for each part.
- Strong answers judge the images: powerful and widely known, but sentimental, rural and stereotyped; they pair achievement with limit and show that the images served Irish tourism interests and the emigrant's longing for home.
- Higher questions on Kennedy: two sides, the USA (first Catholic president, acceptance of Irish Catholics after Al Smith) and Ireland (the 1963 visit, pride, self-image). Give dates (1960, 26–29 June 1963, 22 November 1963), his ancestry (Dunganstown, 1849) and one historian's view that the visit fitted Lemass's confidence. Often paired with Joe Kennedy: keep John's part distinct.
- Questions that group three figures (for example Croker, O'Hara, Kennedy): say what image of the Irish each projected, with one dated fact each; O'Hara earns marks as spirited, glamorous Irish heroine of Hollywood.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark profile of Maureen O'Hara: birth name and birthplace (Ranelagh, 1920), Abbey Theatre training, Jamaica Inn 1939 under Hitchcock, a later film or two with dates, and her work with Ford and John Wayne in The Quiet Man (1952). Each correct dated statement earns marks.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark Kennedy question: ancestry (Patrick Kennedy, Dunganstown, 1849), first Catholic president 1960, why that mattered to Irish Americans, the 1963 visit. A "why" or "significance" part wants a reason, not just the fact.
- Common mistakes to avoid: confusing the filming and release years of The Quiet Man; mixing John F. and Joe Kennedy; describing An Tóstal and Bord Fáilte as the same thing; giving no dates for tourism developments.
- Document-style or short-answer prompts: expect to explain what an image shows about Ireland (rural, comic, romantic) and to say who it was made for (American audiences and tourists).
