## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "the raids ... strengthened the case for Canadian confederation" as a result of the 1870 raid, because confederation came in 1867; if used at all, attach it to the 1866 raids and say "helped".
- Do not use: "Devoy ... sentenced ... in 1866", because he was arrested in 1866 and the sentence followed at his trial (some sources date the trial to 1867). Write "arrested in 1866 and sentenced to 15 years".
- Do not use: "about half a million Ulster Unionists" signed the Covenant, because the Covenant (men) and the women's declaration are different counts. Say "nearly half a million men and women".
- Do not use: "the campaign faded after 1951", because the date is not firm and the cause is vague; say only that it achieved no change.
- Do not use: any dollar figure for Land League or Clan na Gael funds, because no checked figure is available.
- Do not use: "Ulster will fight and Ulster will be right"; it is Lord Randolph Churchill's phrase (1886), not Bonar Law's.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, the commonest form: nationalists and/or unionists seeking support abroad; how effective was Irish nationalism as an international force, or how successful were Ulster Unionist efforts compared with the nationalists. Marks go to a clear judgement stated at the start, balanced achievements vs limits with named organisations and dates, and a direct comparison of nationalist and unionist networks.
- Higher essay on Devoy and/or Bonar Law: their careers, what each did to influence Ireland from outside it, and a verdict. Show at least three dated actions each (Catalpa 1876, New Departure 1878, Gaelic American 1903, the Rising 1916; Conservative leadership 1911, Blenheim 1912, Prime Minister 1922) and name how contemporaries and historians judged them.
- Strong answers separate money, organisation and publicity from results: diaspora support sustained movements but rarely decided outcomes; the decisive events happened in Ireland and Britain.
- Ordinary Level 40-mark paragraph on John Devoy: birth, Fenian membership, arrest and exile, Catalpa, New Departure, Gaelic American, 1916 support. Each correct dated statement earns marks, so the notes need a compact, checkable chain of dated facts.
- Ordinary Level and Higher on Ulster Unionist lobbying: Ulster-Scots identity, Orange lodges in Canada and Australia, the Covenant, Bonar Law; credit for noting that influence was moral rather than decisive.
- Cause and result questions: why Clan na Gael became the main American body, why American money mattered to the Land League, why the anti-partition campaign failed (no US government action, Britain's Ireland Act, Irish government weakness abroad).
- Achievements vs limits and contemporary vs later views are rewarded as paired points; naming a historian (Golway on Devoy, Blake on Bonar Law) adds weight.
