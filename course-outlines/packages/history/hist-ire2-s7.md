Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Ireland's Industrial Revolution was confined mainly to Belfast and the north-east; Dublin was a port, administrative and commercial city with little large-scale industry, so work for the unskilled was casual.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Ireland was part of the United Kingdom; Dublin Castle, Dublin Corporation and the Dublin Metropolitan Police (DMP) ran the city.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Trade union basics: a union bargains collectively with employers over pay and conditions; a strike is a withdrawal of labour, a lockout is the employers shutting workers out.

Key concept
- [ ] K1 Socialism: common ownership of the means of production and the end of the class system; Connolly's version tied it to Irish national independence. Also in keyTerms.

Dublin's urban poverty before 1913
- [ ] D1 Tenements: old Georgian houses on the north side and in the Liberties divided into rooms; overcrowding, no running water in rooms, shared yard toilet; the 1911 census figure for one-room families.
- [ ] D2 Landlords and rents; some owned slum property while sitting on Dublin Corporation; the Local Government Board housing inquiry of 1913–14.
- [ ] D3 High death rate, tuberculosis, infant mortality.
- [ ] D4 Unskilled casual labour at the docks, carting and building: hired by the day, no security, low pay; women's work in laundries, factories and domestic service.
- [ ] D5 The Church Street collapse, September 1913.
