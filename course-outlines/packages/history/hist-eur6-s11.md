## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "the Hollywood Ten were sentenced to prison" with no qualification of timing as if they went straight to jail, because they were sentenced in 1948 but appeals delayed their imprisonment until 1950.
- Do not use: "The Paramount decree required the studios to sell their cinemas" as an instant event, because the 1948 ruling began a process of divorcement completed in the following decade.
- Do not use: the "silent majority" that Nixon appealed to "in 1968 and 1972", because the phrase comes from his November 1969 speech.
- Do not use: "the National Farm Workers Association merged ... to become the United Farm Workers in 1966", because the 1966 merger created the United Farm Workers Organizing Committee; the name United Farm Workers dates from 1972.
- Do not use: "Roots drew about 100 million viewers" unqualified, because that figure is for the final episode.
- Do not use: "Stonewall began the modern gay rights movement" as flat fact; say it is widely seen as a turning point, as organisations existed before 1969.
- Do not use: Woodstock's crowd as a single exact figure; say about 400,000, estimates vary.
- Do not use: a cause of Monroe's death beyond "barbiturate overdose, ruled probable suicide"; no conspiracy claim is supported.
- Do not use: a full date for Ali's change of name; say March 1964.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essays pair Monroe, Mailer or Ali with another personality (Friedan, Graham) and ask for their contribution to American society or culture. Marks go to a clear judgement on what each contributed, supported by dated facts (works, fights, rulings) and by both contemporary and later views; a list of biography scores poorly.
- A Hollywood question asks what the studios showed and how it reflected or shaped values: the American Dream on screen, Cold War messages, the red scare and blacklist, and television's challenge. Name specific films with years.
- For Ali, strong answers link sport, religion and politics: the name change, the draft refusal on grounds of faith, the penalty, the 1971 reversal, and why he became a symbol of black pride and resistance to Vietnam.
- For Mailer, examiners expect the three works with dates, the idea behind each (war, hipster rebellion, anti-war protest) and a view on his public persona and critics.
- For Monroe, expect the rise to stardom (1953 films), the glamorous image tied to advertising and the media, studio difficulties and the 1962 death with a careful cause.
- Ordinary Level short questions ask for two or three dated facts about one personality (a film, a title, a date of death or a ruling); each correct dated statement earns a mark, so notes should open with the dated headline facts.
- Judgement questions on consensus ask whether the 1950s were united and whether the 1960s broke it; reward answers that use both counter-culture and the red scare as evidence, and that note that most Americans were not hippies.
- Where a question asks for causes or results, give the red scare's effects (blacklist, ruined careers) and the counter-culture's effects (backlash, multicultural claims) in separate, dated points.
