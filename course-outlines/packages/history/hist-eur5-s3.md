Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) Palestine was part of the Ottoman Empire until Britain captured it in the First World War; Arabs were the large majority, with a small Jewish minority.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) Britain's wartime promises conflicted: the McMahon–Hussein letters (1915–16) to the Arabs, the Sykes–Picot agreement with France (1916), and the Balfour Declaration (1917).
- [ ] A3 (assumed) The League of Nations Mandate system: Britain administered Palestine on the League's behalf until 1948.
- [ ] A4 (assumed) The Cold War context of the 1950s (US and Soviet rivalry, Soviet arms deals, Western oil interests) and the Suez Canal as the sea route between Europe and Asia.

Key concepts
- [ ] K1 Zionism: the movement for a Jewish national homeland in Palestine, founded in its political form by Herzl (first Congress, Basel, 1897).
- [ ] K2 Terrorism: violence against civilians or political targets to create fear and advance a political aim, used by Jewish and Arab groups in Palestine (the outline's key concept; define it neutrally and give the King David Hotel and Deir Yassin as examples).
- [ ] K3 Arab nationalism: the movement for Arab independence and unity, opposed to Western control and to Israel (essential term).
- [ ] K4 Nationalisation (essential term, Suez).

| W7 | Resolution 181, 29 November 1947, UN General Assembly: 33 votes for, 13 against, 10 abstentions (Britain abstained). Two states, with Jerusalem under international control. About 55 per cent of the land for the Jewish state, when Jews were about one-third of the population. The US and the Soviet Union both voted in favour. |
| W8 | Arab Higher Committee and Arab states rejected partition; fighting began in December 1947. Britain refused to enforce partition. Mandate ended 14 May 1948. Plan Dalet: Jewish forces took Tiberias, Haifa and Jaffa in April–May 1948; most of their Arab inhabitants fled. |

| I5 | Armistice agreements 1949: Jordan held the West Bank (annexed in 1950, recognised by few states); Egypt administered the Gaza Strip. |
| I6 | Law of Return, 1950: Jews may immigrate to Israel. Many Jews also left or were pushed out of Arab states after 1948. |
| N1 | Free Officers overthrew King Farouk on 23 July 1952. Nasser became prime minister in April 1954 and was elected president by referendum in June 1956. |

| N4 | Soviet-bloc arms deal via Czechoslovakia, 1955. Egypt recognised communist China in May 1956. |

| S2 | Nationalisation of the Suez Canal Company announced by Nasser on 26 July 1956. Britain's Prime Minister: Anthony Eden. Anglo-French plan was called Operation Musketeer. |
| S4 | Protocol of Sèvres, signed secretly near Paris on 24 October 1956 by Britain, France and Israel (Ben-Gurion attended). Israel would attack Sinai; Britain and France would then demand both sides withdraw from the canal and occupy it to "separate" the combatants. |

## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "serving from 1948 to 1953" for Ben-Gurion's first term (factCard), because it contradicts the notes' own "1948–54"; he left office at the start of 1954. Use 1948–54 and 1955–63.
- Do not use: "from 1955" for Egypt's ban on the Straits of Tiran, because Egypt restricted Israeli shipping earlier; say only that Egypt barred Israeli ships from the canal and the straits.
- Do not use: "a British-French force had advanced about 23 miles south of Port Said", because the distance is not well checked. Say the force was advancing down the canal at the ceasefire.
- Do not use: "about fifty ships" sunk in the canal, because the figure varies; say "a number of ships".
- Do not use: "about 100,000 troops" as a flat figure for the British garrison; say "tens of thousands of troops" if needed.
- Do not use: the flat claim that Ben-Gurion "backed the expulsions from Lydda and Ramle" as established fact; give it as a point historians debate (see BG6).
- Do not use: "withdrew its forces by 15 May 1948" as a precise withdrawal date; British forces left in stages and the last did not leave until later. Say the Mandate ended on 14 May 1948.
- Do not use: Palestinian refugees as a single exact figure; give "about 700,000" with the range.
- Do not use: Haganah "linked to" Ben-Gurion as if he commanded it directly; say the Haganah served the Jewish Agency which he led.
- Do not use: "the Arab Higher Committee and Arab states rejected" the Peel partition as if all Arabs and no Zionists agreed; say Arab leaders rejected it and the British soon dropped it.

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, the commonest form: how far Britain contributed to tensions in the Middle East, 1945–1956 (asked repeatedly). Marks go to a clear judgement with points on both sides: for Britain (contradictory wartime promises, the Mandate's handling of immigration, the White Paper, the sudden withdrawal in 1948, the Suez collusion) and against (Arab and Jewish nationalism, the Holocaust, the US and UN, Arab rejection of partition, Nasser's own actions).
- Higher essay on British withdrawal from Palestine and/or the Suez Crisis, or on the origins of the Arab–Israeli conflict: strong answers give causes in order with dates, then results, then a judgement; weak answers narrate the 1948 war without explaining origins.
- Higher comparison of Suez with another international event (for example Katanga): the international reaction, the part of the superpowers and the UN; students must give facts for each, not only for Suez.
- Higher essay on Europe's role in the Arab–Israeli conflict: Britain's Mandate, partition, Suez, then the decline of European influence; link to the US replacing Britain.
- Higher essay on a personality (Ben-Gurion and/or Nasser): career dates, key actions, how contemporaries and historians judge each. A balanced view with both achievements and failings earns the top marks; a list of biography does not.
- Ordinary Level short answers on a document (a cartoon, a speech by Nasser, an account of the Suez landing): who Nasser was, why he nationalised the canal, what Britain and France wanted, why the invasion failed, what the UN did.
- Ordinary Level 30/40-mark paragraph on Ben-Gurion or Nasser: birth, key offices, three or four specific actions with dates (for Ben-Gurion the Jewish Agency in 1935, partition acceptance, 14 May 1948, prime minister; for Nasser the 1952 coup, 1954 and 1956 offices, the nationalisation on 26 July 1956, the survival of 1956).
- Ordinary Level paragraph on the Suez Crisis: causes (the Aswan dam withdrawal, nationalisation), the Sèvres collusion, the invasion dates, US and Soviet pressure and the result for Britain.
- Ordinary Level paragraph on British withdrawal from Palestine: the Balfour Declaration, immigration and conflict, the UN partition plan (November 1947), the end of the Mandate and the birth of Israel, the Arab refusal.
- Key concepts (Zionism, terrorism) are asked as short definitions; give a clear one-line definition plus one example.
