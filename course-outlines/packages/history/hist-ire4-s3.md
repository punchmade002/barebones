## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay on anti-Irish sentiment in both the USA and Britain, 19th century (asked repeatedly): marks go to naming the causes (religion, poverty and disease, competition for work, stereotypes) and illustrating each with a dated example from each country (Philadelphia 1844, Know-Nothings 1854, Nast, Stockport 1852, Murphy 1867, Clerkenwell 1867). A list of causes without examples from both countries scores badly.
- Strong answers compare the two countries: in the USA nativism became a political party; in Britain hostility was mixed with sectarian rivalry and flared at Fenian threats. They also note the "NINA" debate rather than asserting the signs as fact.
- Higher essay on Irish participation in politics in the USA and Britain: structure by country; USA = Democratic Party, ward politics, Tammany, Irish mayors, Kennedys; Britain = Irish National League of Great Britain, 1885 and 1886 voting, T. P. O'Connor, Labour. The best answers explain why the forms differed (machines in the USA; Parliamentary Party and Labour in Britain) and end with a judgement on how much influence the Irish gained.
- Higher essays on challenges and opportunities in North America, or on Irish-American identity: use politics and the police as the opportunity side, and discrimination and the Molly Maguires as the challenge side.
- Key-personality questions come as paired or three-way profiles (Molly Maguires, Croker, Kennedy, O'Dwyer, often with a Devoy or a Mannix). Each profile needs dated facts: for Croker, birth, rise, Tammany leadership, Van Wyck, Lexow and Mazet, retirement and Orby; for Kennedy, the SEC, Maritime Commission and ambassadorship with the resignation; for O'Dwyer, Mayo birth, emigration in 1925, law, the 1968 Senate run, brother William. A good Higher answer closes with a judgement on what the person shows about the Irish in America.
- Ordinary Level paragraph (30 marks) on the Molly Maguires: who they were and where, the conditions, Gowen and McParland, the trials, the hangings, and one interpretation. Each correct, relevant, dated statement earns marks.
- Ordinary Level paragraph on Croker, Kennedy or O'Dwyer: birth, career steps with dates, what they achieved, one criticism or controversy.
- Ordinary Level paragraph on anti-Irish feeling or Irish voting: three or four causes or features, each with a short example (a riot, a cartoon, ward organisers giving jobs for votes).
- Short document questions: define discrimination; give a reason why Irish emigrants were disliked; read a cartoon or advertisement for hostility; explain why Irish Americans voted Democratic.
- Judgement points are rewarded as pairs: achievement vs limit (power vs corruption), contemporary vs later view (reformers vs communities; historians on assimilation), and the Mollies as victims vs guilty. Naming Kevin Kenny with his view adds weight.
