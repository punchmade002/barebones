## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: "She resigned as Prime Minister on 22 November 1990", because 22 November is the day she announced her resignation; she left office on 28 November 1990 when Major took over. The notes' factCard "Thatcher's resignation: date" must say "announced".
- Do not use: "She was a participant in the Falklands War", because she was not a combatant; she was the Prime Minister who sent the task force.
- Do not use: "Governments abandoned the post-war commitment to full employment", as a general claim, because it is too broad; say rather that fighting inflation replaced full employment as the priority of the Thatcher government.
- Do not use: "In December the OPEC states agreed to double the posted price", because the December 1973 increase took the price to roughly four times the pre-war level by early 1974 along with the October rise; say "prices roughly quadrupled".
- Do not use: "fuel rationing in some countries" without naming one; name the Netherlands only, and do not say Britain rationed petrol.
- Do not use: "The oil crisis caused the end of the boom" as a flat statement, because inflation, the collapse of Bretton Woods and falling productivity had already weakened it.
- Do not use: "Heath's defeats in 1974" as the reason for her election without noting both 1974 elections.
- Do not use: "she defeated Edward Heath" as if one ballot decided the leadership, because Heath was knocked out in the first ballot and she won the second ballot against Willie Whitelaw.

Doubtful quotations: do not use
- "The lady's not for turning" (1980) is real, but not needed; do not use unless attributed to her 1980 conference speech.
- "There is no such thing as society" (1987) is often misquoted without its context; do not use.
- "There is no alternative" (TINA); do not use.
- Do not put any other words in quotation marks as Thatcher's.

Confusable people, terms and dates
- OPEC (1960; all producers) vs OAPEC (1968; Arab members only); the October 1973 price rise (by the Gulf members of OPEC) vs the output cuts and embargo (by OAPEC).
- Embargo (stopped supply to particular countries) vs price rise (affected everyone): the Netherlands was embargoed but all importers faced the price rise.
- First oil shock (1973–74, Yom Kippur War) vs second oil shock (1979, Iranian revolution).
- Stagflation vs ordinary recession vs inflation alone.
- Monetarism (controlling money supply) vs Keynesianism (managing demand).
- Privatisation (selling state firms) vs deregulation (removing rules, as in the City in 1986).
- Heath's government (1970–74; three-day week, miners' dispute) vs Thatcher's (1979–90; miners' strike 1984–85); the Labour governments of Wilson and Callaghan (1974–79) came between.
- Thatcher the leader (1975) vs Prime Minister (1979).
- Single European Act (1986, supported) vs Maastricht (1992, after her fall); Fontainebleau (1984, rebate) vs Bruges (1988, speech).
- Greenham Common (cruise missiles) vs the Libya raid (April 1986); both support for Reagan.
- Orgreave (June 1984 clash) vs Cortonwood (the pit whose threatened closure started the strike).

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay on Thatcher (most years): how effective or successful was her leadership, at home and abroad, or her impact on Britain. Marks go to a stated judgement, balanced between achievements (inflation, union reform, privatisation, Falklands, Britain's standing, rebate) and costs (unemployment, industrial decline, social division, poll tax, fall in 1990). Each point needs a date and a named policy.
- Higher essay pairing Thatcher with other personalities (Monnet, Delors, de Beauvoir) on their attitudes to Europe or their influence: state her attitude explicitly (supporter of the single market, opponent of federalism) with Fontainebleau, the Single European Act and Bruges.
- Higher essay on the impact of the 1973 oil crisis: causes and trigger, immediate effects (price, embargo, rationing, inflation), longer-term effects (unemployment, industrial change, energy policy), and a verdict on how lasting the impact was. Strong answers separate the oil shock from other causes of the end of the boom.
- Higher essay combining boom and recession (growth to 1973 then crisis): link the two halves; the oil crisis as the end of the boom.
- Ordinary Level 40-mark question on the oil crisis: origin in the Arab–Israeli conflict (6 October 1973), the price rises and embargo (dates), effects on Western economies (inflation, unemployment, balance of payments, energy saving). One correct dated statement per mark.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark paragraph on Thatcher: leader 1975 and Prime Minister 1979, monetarism, union laws, privatisation (British Telecom), Falklands, miners' strike, resignation in 1990. Aim for four or five correct dated points.
- Ordinary Level short answers on a document (an Arab oil minister's statement, a photograph of queues, a graph of unemployment): what the oil embargo was, who was targeted, one effect.
- Comparative questions on the 1970s and 1980s as a whole: recession, unemployment, deindustrialisation and the response of governments.
- Cause and result: why the miners lost the strike; why Thatcher fell; why unemployment rose after 1979.
