## 1. Must cover (one bullet or block for every item; tick each off)

Background (assumed)
- [ ] A1 (assumed) After 1945 European empires lost the power and the will to hold colonies: war-weariness, the weakness of Britain and France, US and Soviet hostility to empire, the UN, and growing nationalist movements.
- [ ] A2 (assumed) France's wartime defeat (1940), Vichy and the Free French under de Gaulle; the Fourth Republic (1946–58) was unstable, with weak governments.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Britain's withdrawal from India (1947) and France's defeat in Indochina (1954) showed that empires could be ended; these belong to earlier subsections and are only a pointer here.

Key concepts (each defined as used in this topic; also in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Assimilation: the French aim of making colonial subjects French in language, law and culture; Algeria was claimed as part of France, but most Muslims never got full equality.
- [ ] K2 (French) Union and Community: the French Union (1946) kept the empire under Paris; the French Community (1958 constitution) gave territories internal self-government, with foreign affairs, defence and currency kept by France.
- [ ] K3 Terrorism: violence against civilians to spread fear and win a political aim; used in Algeria by the FLN and by the OAS (and see torture and reprisals by French forces).
- [ ] K4 Self-determination, Fifth Republic, pieds-noirs, harkis, FLN, OAS, Évian Accords, Loi-cadre, "winds of change" (essential terms for keyTerms).

The wave of African independence
- [ ] W1 Ghana, 6 March 1957, the first sub-Saharan colony to become independent (Nkrumah).
- [ ] W2 Tunisia and Morocco independent in 1956 (the Algerian war's neighbours and FLN bases).
- [ ] W3 The Loi-cadre (Defferre, 1956): elected assemblies and governments in French African territories.
- [ ] W4 De Gaulle's 1958 offer at Brazzaville (24 August) of Community or independence; the earlier Brazzaville Conference (1944) promised reform but not self-government.
- [ ] W5 Guinea's "No" in the September 1958 referendum: independence on 2 October 1958; the first French colony to leave.
- [ ] W6 1960, the "Year of Africa": 17 states independent; most French territories (and Madagascar) left the Community by negotiation and signed co-operation accords.
- [ ] W7 Macmillan's Cape Town speech, 3 February 1960: the "wind of change"; British transfers of power mostly negotiated, unlike France in Algeria.

Algeria under French rule, 1830–1954
- [ ] R1 Conquest from 1830; three départements treated as part of France (1848).
- [ ] R2 The pieds-noirs: about one million of about nine million people by 1954; they held land, wealth and local power.
- [ ] R3 Muslims mostly subjects not citizens; citizenship required giving up Islamic personal status; the 1947 Statute and its two equal colleges favoured the settlers.
- [ ] R4 Assimilation as policy and its failure; nationalist movements (Messali Hadj's MTLD, 1946).
- [ ] R5 Sétif and Guelma, 8 May 1945: settler deaths, then repression; disputed death toll.

The FLN rising and the war, 1954–1958
- [ ] L1 FLN rising, 1 November 1954.
- [ ] L2 French response: conscripts, about 400,000 troops at the peak, wide army powers.
- [ ] L3 Soummam Valley Congress (August 1956): political leadership organised.
- [ ] L4 Morice Line (from 1957).
- [ ] L5 Battle of Algiers (1957): Massu's paratroopers, torture, the FLN network broken.
- [ ] L6 Torture: Alleg's *La Question* (banned 1958); effect on opinion in France.
- [ ] L7 FLN terrorism and violence against rival Algerians; French reprisals and killing of civilians.
- [ ] L8 Regroupement camps for rural Algerians.
- [ ] L9 Casualties and their disputed figures.
- [ ] L10 International dimension: Tunisia, Morocco, Egypt; Sakiet bombing (February 1958); UN debates.

The May 1958 crisis and the Fifth Republic
- [ ] M1 13 May 1958 in Algiers: settlers and army seize buildings, Committee of Public Safety.
- [ ] M2 The Fourth Republic's collapse: de Gaulle invested on 1 June 1958 (as prime minister).
- [ ] M3 "Je vous ai compris" (Algiers, 4 June) and "Vive l'Algérie française" (Mostaganem, 6 June): the settlers' false hopes.
- [ ] M4 The constitution (referendum 28 September 1958): a strong presidency, the Fifth Republic, the Community.
- [ ] M5 De Gaulle elected president in December 1958; took office January 1959.
- [ ] M6 The Fourth Republic's instability (more than twenty governments, 1946–58) as a cause of its fall.

From "Algérie française" to self-determination, 1959–1961
- [ ] G1 16 September 1959: offer of self-determination with three options.
- [ ] G2 Barricades Week, January 1960.
- [ ] G3 Melun talks with the FLN (June 1960) fail; November 1960 talk of an Algerian Republic.
- [ ] G4 Referendum of 8 January 1961.
- [ ] G5 Algiers putsch, April 1961: the generals fail.
- [ ] G6 The OAS (1961): bombings and assassinations in Algeria and France.

Independence, 1962
- [ ] E1 Évian Accords, 18 March 1962; ceasefire 19 March.
- [ ] E2 Terms: rights of the French minority, Mers-el-Kébir base, Saharan oil and gas.
- [ ] E3 Referendums: France 8 April, Algeria 1 July 1962; independence 5 July 1962.
- [ ] E4 Ben Bella; the later coup of Boumediene (1965).
- [ ] E5 Last OAS violence: rue d'Isly (26 March), Salan's arrest, Petit-Clamart (22 August 1962), Bastien-Thiry's execution (1963).
- [ ] E6 Post-independence relations: French bases and Mers-el-Kébir handed over (1968); oil and gas nationalised (1971); continuing French influence in sub-Saharan Africa (co-operation accords, Foccart).

The exodus of the pieds-noirs and the harkis
- [ ] X1 The settlers' flight in 1962; the strain on France.
- [ ] X2 The Oran violence of 5 July 1962.
- [ ] X3 The harkis: who they were, French refusal of organised rescue, reprisals, camps in France.
- [ ] X4 Later recognition of the harkis (2001, 2021).
- [ ] X5 End of the French presence on the land; farms taken over.

Key personality: Charles de Gaulle (one block, every point)
- [ ] P1 Who he was: born Lille 1890, army officer; 18 June 1940 appeal; Free French.
- [ ] P2 What he did, with dates: provisional government 1944–46; RPF 1947; return 1958; the Fifth Republic; the path to Évian; resignation 1969; death 1970.
- [ ] P3 Shaped by events or shaping them: the settlers' welcome in 1958 against his 1959 turn; the army, cost and international pressure.
- [ ] P4 Participant: he made the decisions at each stage (1958 return, 1959 offer, Barricades Week, putsch, Évian).
- [ ] P5 Contemporaries: settlers, the army and the OAS called him a traitor; many French and Algerians saw him as the man who ended the war.
- [ ] P6 Historians: planned withdrawal from the start vs drift towards independence.
- [ ] P7 African policy: the Community, co-operation accords, Foccart.

Assessment (paired judgement points, final block)
- [ ] S1 Achievement: ended a war the Fourth Republic could not win; offered self-determination; gave France a stable constitution.
- [ ] S2 Limit: the war's cost, the exodus, the divided army, delayed and ambiguous policy.
- [ ] S3 Achievement and limit: the Community failed, but influence in Africa continued through accords.
- [ ] S4 Why Europe withdrew: cost, unpopularity, world opinion, UN and US pressure, African nationalism; Britain negotiated, France fought first.
- [ ] S5 Contemporary view (settlers, OAS, generals) vs later view (ended the war without the Republic falling).

## 3. Traps

Do not use (wrong or doubtful facts in the current notes):
- Do not use: the Soummam Congress "confirmed the primacy of the armed struggle", because the Soummam platform stressed the primacy of politics over the military; say only that it set up the FLN's political leadership.
- Do not use: "about nine million Muslims" voting in the 1947 second college, because the Muslim population figure is uncertain and the first college included some Muslims; say the colleges were equal in size.
- Do not use: "Algérie algérienne" for 4 November 1960, because the exact phrase and date are not secure; say he spoke of a future Algerian Republic.
- Do not use: "Brazzaville Conference of 1944 that he had convened"; he opened it but it was a Free French gathering.
- Do not use: "French government gave orders to prevent their organised transfer" without qualification; say the government refused or obstructed organised rescue and officers who helped were punished (only this level of detail).
- Do not use: "Algeria became independent on 5 July 1962" without noting the 1 July vote; 5 July is the date celebrated, after the referendum result, and 3 July was when France recognised it.
- Do not use: "de Gaulle was invested as president on 1 June 1958"; he became prime minister then.

Doubtful quotations: do not use "Algérie algérienne", "I have understood you" attributed to any other place or date, or any quotation of Macmillan beyond the phrase above.

## 4. What exams ask

- Ordinary Level 30-mark key personality on de Gaulle: born Lille 1890, 18 June 1940 appeal, Free French, provisional government 1944–46, RPF, return in 1958, the Algerian policy, resignation 1969. Each dated, relevant statement earns marks; name at least four stages.
- Ordinary Level 40-mark paragraph on Algeria and de Gaulle's return: the May 1958 crisis (13 May, investiture 1 June), "Je vous ai compris", the change in 1959 to self-determination, Barricades Week or the putsch, the OAS, the Évian Accords and independence in 1962. Marks go to the sequence with dates, and to explaining why settlers felt betrayed.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark paragraph on the winds of change: Ghana 1957, Guinea 1958, Macmillan 1960, the Year of Africa; give the speaker, place and year of the Cape Town speech.
- Ordinary Level 40-mark paragraph on the Algerian war itself: 1954 rising, troop numbers, the Battle of Algiers, Massu and torture, with one clear sentence on the results.
- Higher 100-mark essay on de Gaulle and French colonialism (or why European countries retreated): state a judgement at the start (effective but costly and not entirely by design) and balance achievements against limits, with dated evidence from Algeria, the Community and Africa. Strong answers also compare the French and British experience and consider both views of whether he planned withdrawal.
- Short document-style questions test one fact each: who the pieds-noirs were, why the FLN rose, why the government could not give in quickly (the million settlers and their supporters in France), what the OAS did, what the harkis were.
- Terms often tested: pieds-noirs, FLN, OAS, harkis, Évian Accords, self-determination; give a definition with the date and who used it.
- Common weak answers: mixing up 1958 prime minister and 1959 president, merging the Barricades Week with the putsch, and giving the Évian Accords as the end of the war with no mention of the referendums or the exodus.
