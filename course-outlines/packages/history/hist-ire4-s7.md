## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, the commonest form: how successful or important were Irish missionaries in Africa and Asia, or the impact and success of the Holy Ghost mission. Marks go to a clear judgement stated at the start, with balanced achievement and limit points, and dated detail (1885, 1902, 1945, 1950, 1960, 1967).
- Strong answers split achievements (schools, hospitals, local clergy, institutions that outlasted colonial rule) from limits (cultural effects, paternalism, ties to the colonial state, expulsion from China, Biafra), and separate the contemporary Irish view from later historians' views.
- Questions on the 1945–1966 case study reward the specific expansion (parishes, schools, hospitals, training colleges), the education emphasis, the grant-in-aid, independence and localisation. Name Nigerians (Arinze, Tansi) to show local leadership.
- Personality questions on Galvin or Martin: the usual marking points are birth, training, the founding date, offices or hospitals, the 1936 instruction (Martin), expulsion (Galvin), and a judgement of significance. Give each point a date.
- A spiritual empire question wants the phrase defined, why Ireland supplied missionaries, and a critical view (an Irish self-image, not an empire in the political sense).
- Ordinary Level paragraphs (30 or 40 marks) on the mission: aims (convert the Igbo, use schools), what it did after 1945, who ran it, problems (rivalry, grant-in-aid, Biafra). Each correct dated statement earns marks.
- Ordinary Level questions on Martin: the 1936 ban and its lifting is a standard point, with Nigeria and Drogheda.
