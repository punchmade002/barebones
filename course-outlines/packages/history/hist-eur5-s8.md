Key concepts (each defined as used in this topic; also in keyTerms)
- [ ] K1 Racism: treating people as inferior or different because of race or colour; shown in prejudice, discrimination and violence.

- [ ] K3 Identity: how migrants and their children saw themselves, caught between the culture of origin and the host society.
- [ ] K4 Assimilation: the expectation that migrants adopt the majority culture (France), against multiculturalism or integration with diversity (Britain; an essential term, not an outline key concept).

## 4. What exams ask

- Higher 100-mark essay, common form: race relations in Britain and/or France, 1945–1990 (problems, similarities and differences, which country managed better). Marks go to a stated judgement at the start and a balance of dated points from each country. Treating only Britain scores poorly.
- Britain: causes of migration (labour shortage, Nationality Act, recruitment); where migrants settled; racism and riots (1958, 1981); legislation both restricting entry (1962, 1968, 1971) and outlawing discrimination (1965, 1968, 1976). Strong answers separate the two kinds of law.
- Multicultural Britain: students need three areas of impact (music and food, religion, politics) and at least one problem (Rushdie).
- France: why migrants came, where they lived, the 1974 halt, family reunification, the second generation. Link to the colonial past (Algeria) for extra credit.
- Compare and contrast: models of integration (multiculturalism against assimilation), legal status of migrants, ethnic statistics, riots and far-right politics.
- Ordinary Level short answers: Nationality Act 1948 and Windrush, what happened in Notting Hill in 1958, why Britain recruited workers, what the 1965 Act banned, riots of 1981 (where, who, why), causes of French immigration, bidonvilles.
- Ordinary Level 30-mark paragraph: Commonwealth migration to Britain (cause, 1948 Act, Windrush, recruitment, settlement, discrimination). Each correct, dated statement earns marks.
- Document questions (Notting Hill 1958 report, Brixton 1981): who, where, when, what caused it, what followed.
- Achievements vs limits and contemporary vs later views: rewarded as paired points; the Scarman Report and the Race Relations Acts are the best evidence.
