## 1. Must cover

The method (Higher Level only; one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 [HL] Step 1: Write the balanced equation and the Kc expression.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Step 2: Convert any masses to moles (n = mass ÷ M).
- [ ] M3 [HL] Step 3: Set up a table with rows Initial (moles at the start), Change (moles reacting or forming, in the ratio of the coefficients) and Equilibrium (initial + change) for every species.
- [ ] M4 [HL] Step 4: Divide each equilibrium amount by the volume of the container in litres to get concentrations in mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] M5 [HL] Step 5: Substitute into the Kc expression and calculate; give the answer to 3 significant figures (with units if asked).
- [ ] M6 [HL] When the total number of moles is the same on each side of the equation (e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, esterification), the volume cancels and moles can be used directly in the expression.
- [ ] M7 [HL] To find equilibrium amounts from Kc: let x be the amount that reacts, write each equilibrium amount in terms of x, substitute into the Kc expression and solve for x; if both sides are perfect squares, take the square root; otherwise rearrange into a quadratic and use x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a.
- [ ] M8 [HL] Choose the root that makes physical sense: no equilibrium amount can be negative, so x cannot exceed the starting amount of a reactant.

Problem 1: Kc from equilibrium amounts (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] A1 [HL] Data: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) in a 2.00 L flask at a fixed temperature; at equilibrium there are 0.100 mol H₂, 0.150 mol I₂ and 0.840 mol HI.
- [ ] A2 [HL] Concentrations: [H₂] = 0.100 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0500; [I₂] = 0.150 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0750; [HI] = 0.840 ÷ 2.00 = 0.420 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] A3 [HL] Kc = [HI]² / [H₂][I₂] = (0.420)² ÷ (0.0500 × 0.0750) = 0.1764 ÷ 0.00375 = 47.0 (no units).

Problem 2: Kc from initial amounts and one equilibrium amount (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] B1 [HL] Data: 2.00 mol N₂ and 6.00 mol H₂ are sealed in a 4.00 L vessel; at equilibrium 1.20 mol NH₃ is present. N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).
- [ ] B2 [HL] Change: forming 1.20 mol NH₃ uses 0.600 mol N₂ and 1.80 mol H₂ (ratio 1 : 3 : 2).
- [ ] B3 [HL] Equilibrium amounts: N₂ = 2.00 − 0.600 = 1.40 mol; H₂ = 6.00 − 1.80 = 4.20 mol; NH₃ = 1.20 mol.
- [ ] B4 [HL] Concentrations (÷ 4.00 L): [N₂] = 0.350, [H₂] = 1.05, [NH₃] = 0.300 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] B5 [HL] Kc = [NH₃]² / [N₂][H₂]³ = (0.300)² ÷ (0.350 × 1.05³) = 0.0900 ÷ 0.405 = 0.222 L² mol⁻².

Problem 3: Kc from percentage dissociation (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] C1 [HL] Data: 41.7 g of PCl₅ is heated in a sealed 5.00 L vessel; at equilibrium 40.0% has dissociated. PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g).
- [ ] C2 [HL] M(PCl₅) = 31 + 5 × 35.5 = 208.5 g mol⁻¹; moles PCl₅ = 41.7 ÷ 208.5 = 0.200 mol.
- [ ] C3 [HL] Moles dissociated = 40.0% of 0.200 = 0.0800 mol; at equilibrium PCl₅ = 0.120 mol, PCl₃ = 0.0800 mol, Cl₂ = 0.0800 mol.
- [ ] C4 [HL] Concentrations (÷ 5.00 L): [PCl₅] = 0.0240, [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = 0.0160 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] C5 [HL] Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] / [PCl₅] = (0.0160 × 0.0160) ÷ 0.0240 = 0.0107 mol L⁻¹.

Problem 4: equilibrium amounts from Kc, perfect square (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] D1 [HL] Data: 1.00 mol ethanoic acid and 1.00 mol ethanol are mixed and reach equilibrium; Kc = 4.00. CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
- [ ] D2 [HL] Let x mol of ester form: at equilibrium acid = ethanol = 1.00 − x; ester = water = x. The volume cancels.
- [ ] D3 [HL] Kc = x² ÷ (1.00 − x)² = 4.00; square root of both sides: x ÷ (1.00 − x) = 2.00.
- [ ] D4 [HL] x = 2.00 − 2.00x, so 3.00x = 2.00 and x = 0.667 mol of ester (and of water); acid and ethanol = 0.333 mol each.
- [ ] D5 [HL] Mass of ester = 0.667 × 88 = 58.7 g (M of CH₃COOC₂H₅ = 88 g mol⁻¹).

Problem 5: equilibrium concentrations from Kc, quadratic (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] E1 [HL] Data: 1.00 mol H₂ and 2.00 mol I₂ are placed in a 1.00 L flask; Kc = 49.0 at this temperature. H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).
- [ ] E2 [HL] Let x mol of H₂ react: at equilibrium H₂ = 1.00 − x, I₂ = 2.00 − x, HI = 2x.
- [ ] E3 [HL] 49.0 = (2x)² ÷ [(1.00 − x)(2.00 − x)], so 4x² = 49.0(2.00 − 3.00x + x²), giving 45.0x² − 147x + 98.0 = 0.
- [ ] E4 [HL] x = (147 ± √(147² − 4 × 45.0 × 98.0)) ÷ (2 × 45.0) = (147 ± 63.0) ÷ 90.0, so x = 2.33 or 0.933.
- [ ] E5 [HL] x = 2.33 is impossible (more than the 1.00 mol of H₂ present), so x = 0.933.
- [ ] E6 [HL] Equilibrium concentrations: [H₂] = 0.0667, [I₂] = 1.07, [HI] = 1.87 mol L⁻¹. Check: 1.87² ÷ (0.0667 × 1.07) ≈ 49.

## 3. Traps

- Use equilibrium amounts, not initial amounts, in the Kc expression.
- Divide moles by the volume unless the volume cancels (M6); for Problem 2 and 3 it does not.
- Use the mole ratio in the Change row (Problem 2: 1 : 3 : 2).
- Raise each concentration to its power (cube [H₂] in the Haber expression).
- Reject a root that gives a negative equilibrium amount.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Calculate Kc from equilibrium amounts in a stated volume.
- Calculate Kc from initial amounts and one equilibrium amount (Haber; CO + H₂O; CO + Cl₂; SO₂ + O₂).
- Calculate Kc from a mass and a percentage dissociated (PCl₅, NOBr).
- Given Kc, find an equilibrium amount, mass or percentage decomposition (esterification; BrCl or HI decomposition).
- Show that adding more of a reactant gives a new equilibrium with the same Kc.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "1.00 mol of carbon monoxide and 1.00 mol of steam were placed in a 10.0 L vessel and heated: CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO₂(g) + H₂(g). At equilibrium 0.600 mol of CO₂ was present. Write the Kc expression and calculate Kc (sample data)." Model answer: Kc = [CO₂][H₂] / [CO][H₂O]; equilibrium CO = H₂O = 0.400 mol, CO₂ = H₂ = 0.600 mol; volume cancels (or concentrations 0.0400 and 0.0600 mol L⁻¹); Kc = 0.360 ÷ 0.160 = 2.25.
2. 9 marks: "1.00 mol of hydrogen iodide was sealed in a 1.00 L flask and heated: 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g), Kc = 0.0200. Calculate the equilibrium concentrations of H₂ and HI, and the percentage of HI decomposed (sample data)." Model answer: let x = mol H₂ formed; HI = 1.00 − 2x; x² ÷ (1.00 − 2x)² = 0.0200; x ÷ (1.00 − 2x) = 0.141; x = 0.110 mol L⁻¹ H₂ (and I₂); [HI] = 0.780 mol L⁻¹; 22.0% decomposed.
3. 6 marks: "Explain why, in the reaction H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, the value of Kc can be calculated from the numbers of moles without knowing the volume of the container." Model answer: M6, with the expression showing the volume cancelling: (n(HI)/V)² ÷ [(n(H₂)/V)(n(I₂)/V)] = n(HI)² ÷ n(H₂)n(I₂).
