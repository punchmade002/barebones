## 1. Must cover

Intramolecular and intermolecular (one block)
- [ ] D1 Intramolecular bonding: the strong bonds within a molecule (covalent bonds), holding its atoms together.
- [ ] D2 Intermolecular forces: the weaker forces of attraction between separate molecules.
- [ ] D3 Intermolecular forces are much weaker than covalent bonds: boiling water overcomes the forces between H₂O molecules but does not break the O–H bonds.
- [ ] D4 Van der Waals forces is the collective name for the intermolecular forces: London dispersion forces and permanent dipole–dipole forces (including hydrogen bonding).
- [ ] D5 Diagram: two water molecules with the O–H covalent bonds labelled "intramolecular" and the attraction between an H of one molecule and the O of the other labelled "intermolecular (hydrogen bond)".

London dispersion forces (one block)
- [ ] L1 They act between all molecules, and are the only intermolecular forces between non-polar molecules (e.g. H₂, Cl₂, CH₄, noble gas atoms).
- [ ] L2 They arise because electrons are always moving: at any instant the electron cloud may be unevenly spread, giving a temporary (instantaneous) dipole, which induces a dipole in a neighbouring molecule; the two attract.
- [ ] L3 They are the weakest type of intermolecular force for small molecules, but get stronger as molecules get larger and have more electrons.

Permanent dipole–dipole forces (one block)
- [ ] P1 A polar molecule has a permanent dipole: a δ+ end and a δ− end (see "Electronegativity and bond type").
- [ ] P2 Dipole–dipole forces are the attractions between the δ+ end of one polar molecule and the δ− end of a neighbouring one, e.g. between HCl molecules (Hδ+–Clδ−).
- [ ] P3 They are stronger than London dispersion forces between molecules of similar size.

Hydrogen bonding (one block)
- [ ] H1 A hydrogen bond is a particularly strong dipole–dipole attraction between a hydrogen atom bonded to a small, very electronegative atom (fluorine, oxygen or nitrogen) in one molecule and a lone pair on an F, O or N atom of a neighbouring molecule.
- [ ] H2 It is strong because the H–F, O–H and N–H bonds are very polar, leaving the small hydrogen atom strongly δ+.
- [ ] H3 Examples: water (H₂O), ammonia (NH₃), hydrogen fluoride (HF), alcohols such as ethanol, carboxylic acids.
- [ ] H4 Hydrogen bonds are the strongest intermolecular forces, but still much weaker than covalent bonds.
- [ ] H5 Diagram: water molecules with δ+ and δ− labelled and hydrogen bonds drawn as dashed lines from H to the lone pair on O of the next molecule.

Ion–dipole forces (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] X1 [HL] An ion–dipole force is the attraction between an ion and the oppositely charged end of a polar molecule.
- [ ] X2 [HL] When an ionic compound such as sodium chloride dissolves in water, the δ− oxygen ends of water molecules are attracted to Na⁺ ions and the δ+ hydrogen ends to Cl⁻ ions; the ions become surrounded by water molecules (hydrated).
- [ ] X3 [HL] Ion–dipole forces are stronger than dipole–dipole forces; they explain why ionic compounds dissolve in polar solvents such as water.
- [ ] X4 [HL] Diagram: a Na⁺ ion surrounded by water molecules with their O atoms pointing towards it, and a Cl⁻ ion surrounded by water molecules with their H atoms pointing towards it.

Order of strength (one block)
- [ ] S1 For molecules of similar size: London dispersion < dipole–dipole < hydrogen bonding, all much weaker than covalent and ionic bonds.

## 3. Traps

- Dipole moments are not required: don't define or calculate them.
- A hydrogen bond is not a covalent bond to hydrogen; it is between molecules.
- Hydrogen bonding needs H bonded directly to N, O or F: there is no hydrogen bonding between CH₄ molecules or in HCl.
- The specification writes "van der Waal's forces"; write "van der Waals forces".
- Keep ion–dipole in its own Higher Level block.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Distinguish intramolecular bonding from intermolecular forces.
- Explain how London dispersion forces arise; explain hydrogen bonding with water as an example.
- Name the strongest intermolecular force in a named substance.
