# Package: chem8-s4 — Relative molecular mass of a volatile liquid

- **loId:** `chem8-s4` · **chapterId:** `chem8` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.4
- **Syllabus:** 1.4.4 "conduct an experiment to determine the relative molecular mass of a gas
  derived from a liquid". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Write as one full investigation (NOTES_STANDARD
  "Investigation" block type) using the standard school method: a volatile liquid (propanone)
  vaporised in a conical flask in a boiling water bath, the vapour's mass, volume, temperature
  and pressure measured, and PV = nRT used to find n and then Mr. The ideal gas equation is
  taught in "The ideal gas equation"; use it without re-teaching. Use R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## 1. Must cover

Principle (one block)
- [ ] P1 A volatile liquid is one that evaporates easily (has a low boiling point), so it can be turned completely into vapour at 100 °C.
- [ ] P2 If the mass (m), volume (V), temperature (T) and pressure (P) of the vapour are measured, the number of moles is n = PV ÷ RT, and Mr = m ÷ n (molar mass in g mol⁻¹).
- [ ] P3 The vapour is assumed to behave as an ideal gas.

Equipment (one block)
- [ ] E1 A clean, dry conical flask (about 250 cm³), a small square of aluminium foil with a rubber band, a pin, a large beaker of boiling water (water bath) on a hotplate, a thermometer, a top-pan balance (0.01 g), a barometer, a graduated (measuring) cylinder, and a dropper of the volatile liquid (propanone).

Method (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 Weigh the dry conical flask with the foil cap and rubber band.
- [ ] M2 Add about 2–3 cm³ of propanone, replace the foil cap and pierce one small pinhole in it.
- [ ] M3 Clamp the flask in the boiling water bath so that as much of it as possible is immersed.
- [ ] M4 Leave it until all the liquid has vaporised; the vapour pushes the air out of the flask through the pinhole, and excess vapour escapes, so the flask is left full of propanone vapour at the temperature of the boiling water.
- [ ] M5 Record the temperature of the water bath and the atmospheric pressure from the barometer.
- [ ] M6 Remove the flask, cool it (the vapour condenses), dry the outside thoroughly and reweigh it. The increase in mass is the mass of vapour that filled the flask.
- [ ] M7 Find the volume of the flask by filling it completely with water and measuring the water with a graduated cylinder.
- [ ] M8 Diagram: the conical flask with a pierced foil cap, clamped in a beaker of boiling water on a hotplate, with a thermometer in the water.

Calculation (one block, worked sample data)
- [ ] C1 Sample data: mass of vapour 0.51 g; volume of flask 270 cm³ = 2.70 × 10⁻⁴ m³; temperature 100 °C = 373 K; pressure 101 000 Pa.
- [ ] C2 n = PV ÷ RT = (101 000 × 2.70 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.314 × 373) = 27.27 ÷ 3101 = 8.79 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] C3 Mr = m ÷ n = 0.51 ÷ 8.79 × 10⁻³ = 58.0; the accepted Mr of propanone (CH₃COCH₃) is 58.
- [ ] C4 Units: P in Pa, V in m³, T in K; 1 cm³ = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³.

Safety (one block)
- [ ] S1 Propanone is highly flammable: no naked flames; heat the water bath with a hotplate or electric heater.
- [ ] S2 Propanone vapour is an irritant: work in a well-ventilated room; take care with boiling water and hot glassware.

Sources of error and improvements (one block)
- [ ] R1 Not all the liquid vaporises, or the flask is removed too early: mass too high, Mr too high; wait until no liquid is visible.
- [ ] R2 Water on the outside of the flask when reweighed: mass too high; dry carefully.
- [ ] R3 The pinhole is too large and air re-enters as the flask cools, or vapour escapes before weighing: affects the measured mass.
- [ ] R4 Part of the flask is above the water, so the vapour there is cooler than 373 K.
- [ ] R5 The vapour is not a perfect ideal gas, especially close to its boiling point.
- [ ] R6 The mass of the vapour is small compared with the flask, so a balance reading to 0.01 g (or better) is needed; repeat and average.

## 3. Traps

- The mass measured after cooling is the mass of the vapour that filled the flask at 373 K (it has condensed); the air in the flask at the start doesn't affect it because the vapour displaced it.
- Use the temperature of the boiling water, not room temperature, in the calculation.
- Convert cm³ to m³ (× 10⁻⁶) when P is in pascals.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Describe how the mass and volume of the vapour were found.
- Why is a pinhole made; why is a volatile liquid used; why must no flame be used?
- Calculate Mr from given data.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks: "Describe how you would determine the relative molecular mass of propanone by vaporising it in a conical flask, stating the measurements that must be made." Model answer: M1–M7.
2. 9 marks: "In an experiment, 0.44 g of the vapour of a volatile liquid filled a flask of volume 250 cm³ at 373 K and 101 000 Pa. Calculate the relative molecular mass of the liquid (sample data)." Model answer: n = (101 000 × 2.50 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.314 × 373) = 25.25 ÷ 3101 = 8.14 × 10⁻³ mol; Mr = 0.44 ÷ 8.14 × 10⁻³ = 54.
