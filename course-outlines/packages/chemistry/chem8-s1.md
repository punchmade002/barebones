## 1. Must cover

The mole and the Avogadro constant (one block)
- [ ] M1 The mole (symbol mol) is the SI unit of amount of substance.
- [ ] M2 One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions or electrons); this number is the Avogadro constant, NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
- [ ] M3 This is the number of atoms in 12 g of carbon-12 (the definition used before 2019).
- [ ] M4 Always state the particle: one mole of oxygen atoms (O) contains 6.02 × 10²³ atoms; one mole of oxygen molecules (O₂) contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules and 1.204 × 10²⁴ atoms.
- [ ] M5 Significance and scale: atoms are far too small to count, so chemists count them by weighing; the mole links a mass you can weigh to a number of particles. One mole of water is only 18 g (about 18 cm³) but contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules.

Relative molecular mass and molar mass (one block)
- [ ] R1 Relative molecular mass (Mr): the sum of the relative atomic masses of all the atoms in a molecule (or formula unit), e.g. Mr(H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18; Mr(H₂SO₄) = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98; Mr(CaCO₃) = 40 + 12 + 3(16) = 100. Mr has no units.
- [ ] R2 Molar mass (M): the mass of one mole of a substance, in grams per mole (g mol⁻¹); numerically equal to Ar or Mr, e.g. the molar mass of water is 18 g mol⁻¹.
- [ ] R3 Density: mass per unit volume, ρ = m ÷ V, in g cm⁻³ or kg m⁻³ (e.g. water about 1.00 g cm⁻³).

Interconverting quantities (one block, skills; one bullet per relationship with a worked example)
- [ ] C1 Moles from mass: n = m ÷ M. Example: 8.8 g of CO₂ (M = 44 g mol⁻¹) = 8.8 ÷ 44 = 0.20 mol.
- [ ] C2 Mass from moles: m = n × M. Example: 0.25 mol of NaCl (M = 58.5 g mol⁻¹) = 0.25 × 58.5 = 14.6 g.
- [ ] C3 Number of particles from moles: N = n × NA. Example: 0.20 mol of CO₂ = 0.20 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.20 × 10²³ molecules.
- [ ] C4 Moles from number of particles: n = N ÷ NA.
- [ ] C5 Number of atoms in a molecule: multiply by the atoms per molecule, e.g. 0.20 mol CO₂ contains 0.60 mol of atoms = 3.61 × 10²³ atoms.
- [ ] C6 Gas volume from moles: V = n × molar volume (22.4 L mol⁻¹ at STP; see "Molar volume and Avogadro's law").
- [ ] C7 Mass from volume and density: m = ρ × V, then convert to moles, e.g. 10.0 cm³ of ethanol (density 0.789 g cm⁻³, M = 46 g mol⁻¹) = 7.89 g = 0.172 mol.
- [ ] C8 Summary as a route: mass ⇄ moles ⇄ number of particles, and moles ⇄ gas volume; always go through moles.

## 3. Traps

- Mr has no units; molar mass has units g mol⁻¹.
- For diatomic gases, the number of atoms is twice the number of molecules.
- Don't teach molar volume or STP in detail here.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define the mole and the Avogadro constant.
- Calculate moles, masses and numbers of particles; numbers of atoms in a given mass.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "Define the mole and calculate the number of molecules in 4.4 g of carbon dioxide." Model answer: M1, M2; 4.4 ÷ 44 = 0.10 mol; × 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.02 × 10²² molecules.
2. 9 marks: "Calculate (i) the mass of 0.15 mol of calcium carbonate, (ii) the number of moles in 9.8 g of sulfuric acid, (iii) the number of oxygen atoms in 9.8 g of sulfuric acid." Model answer: 15 g; 0.10 mol; 0.40 mol O atoms = 2.41 × 10²³ atoms.
