# Package: chem10-s3 — Empirical and molecular formulae

- **loId:** `chem10-s3` · **chapterId:** `chem10` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.7
- **Syllabus:** 1.4.7 "use the concept of a mole to determine empirical and molecular formulae".
  Learn about: "the concept of a mole as applicable to stoichiometry and to the analysis of
  quantitative problems, including: … percentage composition …". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Percentage composition, empirical formula (from
  masses and from percentages), molecular formula, and water of crystallisation, each with a
  worked example. Use Ar: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Mg 24, S 32, Cu 63.5.

## 1. Must cover

Definitions (one block)
- [ ] D1 Empirical formula: the simplest whole-number ratio of the atoms of each element in a compound, e.g. CH₂O.
- [ ] D2 Molecular formula: the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule, e.g. glucose C₆H₁₂O₆; it is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.
- [ ] D3 For ionic compounds the formula is always the empirical formula (e.g. NaCl, MgO).

Percentage composition (one block, skills)
- [ ] P1 Percentage of an element by mass = (number of atoms of the element × Ar ÷ Mr) × 100.
- [ ] P2 Example: nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃ (Mr = 80): (2 × 14 ÷ 80) × 100 = 35.0%.
- [ ] P3 Example: water in hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O (Mr = 249.5): (5 × 18 ÷ 249.5) × 100 = 36.1%.

Finding an empirical formula (one block, skills; one step per bullet)
- [ ] E1 Step 1: Write the mass (or percentage) of each element.
- [ ] E2 Step 2: Divide each by its relative atomic mass to get moles.
- [ ] E3 Step 3: Divide each by the smallest number of moles to get the ratio.
- [ ] E4 Step 4: If the ratio isn't whole (e.g. 1.5 or 1.33), multiply all values by 2 or 3 to get whole numbers.
- [ ] E5 Example from percentages: 40.0% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O: C 40.0 ÷ 12 = 3.33; H 6.7 ÷ 1 = 6.7; O 53.3 ÷ 16 = 3.33; ratio 1 : 2 : 1, empirical formula CH₂O.
- [ ] E6 Example from masses (sample data): 2.40 g of magnesium burns to give 4.00 g of magnesium oxide, so 1.60 g of oxygen combined: Mg 2.40 ÷ 24 = 0.100 mol; O 1.60 ÷ 16 = 0.100 mol; ratio 1 : 1, formula MgO.

Finding the molecular formula (one block, skills)
- [ ] M1 Divide the relative molecular mass by the mass of the empirical formula unit, then multiply the empirical formula by the result.
- [ ] M2 Example: empirical formula CH₂O (mass 30) and Mr = 180: 180 ÷ 30 = 6, so the molecular formula is C₆H₁₂O₆.
- [ ] M3 Example: a hydrocarbon of 85.7% C and 14.3% H with Mr 56: C 7.14, H 14.3; ratio 1 : 2, CH₂ (mass 14); 56 ÷ 14 = 4; C₄H₈.

Experiment: the formula of magnesium oxide (one block, investigation)
- [ ] X1 Step 1: Weigh a clean crucible and lid; add a coiled length of clean magnesium ribbon and reweigh.
- [ ] X2 Step 2: Heat strongly, lifting the lid slightly from time to time to let air in while stopping white smoke (magnesium oxide) escaping.
- [ ] X3 Step 3: When the magnesium no longer glows, remove the lid and heat strongly for a few more minutes; cool and reweigh; heat, cool and reweigh again until the mass is constant.
- [ ] X4 Step 4: Calculate the masses of magnesium and of oxygen that combined, then their moles and ratio (as in E6).
- [ ] X5 Safety: don't look directly at burning magnesium (very bright); the crucible is very hot (tongs, heat-proof mat).
- [ ] X6 Diagram: crucible with lid on a pipeclay triangle on a tripod over a Bunsen burner.

Water of crystallisation (one block, skills)
- [ ] W1 Water of crystallisation is water chemically held in a fixed ratio within the crystals of some salts, shown after a dot in the formula, e.g. CuSO₄·5H₂O, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
- [ ] W2 Heating to constant mass drives it off; e.g. blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate turns white (anhydrous).
- [ ] W3 Example (sample data): 2.50 g of CuSO₄·xH₂O gives 1.60 g of anhydrous CuSO₄ (M = 159.5): water 0.90 g = 0.050 mol; CuSO₄ = 1.60 ÷ 159.5 = 0.0100 mol; ratio 1 : 5, x = 5.

## 3. Traps

- Don't round ratios such as 1.5 to 2; multiply up instead.
- Mass of oxygen = mass of oxide − mass of metal.
- Gravimetric analysis and yields are taught in the chapter "Stoichiometry: Limiting Reactants and Percentage Yield".

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Calculate an empirical formula from percentage composition; find the molecular formula from Mr.
- Calculate percentage composition of an element in a compound.
- Find x in a hydrated salt from heating data.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "A compound contains 52.2% carbon, 13.0% hydrogen and 34.8% oxygen by mass, and its Mr is 46. Find its empirical and molecular formulae." Model answer: C 4.35, H 13.0, O 2.175; ratio 2 : 6 : 1; C₂H₆O (mass 46), so molecular formula C₂H₆O.
2. 6 marks: "Calculate the percentage by mass of water in washing soda, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (Na = 23)." Model answer: Mr = 286; 180 ÷ 286 × 100 = 62.9%.
