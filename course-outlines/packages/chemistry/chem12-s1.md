## 1. Must cover

Volumetric analysis (one block)
- [ ] V1 Volumetric analysis (titration) finds the concentration of a solution (the analyte) by measuring the volume that reacts exactly with a known volume of another solution of known concentration.
- [ ] V2 The calculation depends on knowing one concentration exactly, so at least one solution must be a standard solution.
- [ ] V3 (assumed) Key terms: titre (the volume added from the burette); end point (when the indicator changes colour); equivalence point (when the reactants have reacted in exactly the ratio of the balanced equation); a good indicator's end point coincides with the equivalence point.

Primary standards (one block)
- [ ] P1 Recap: a primary standard is a very pure, stable substance of known formula that can be weighed accurately and dissolved to give a standard solution directly (properties and method in "Solutions and concentration").
- [ ] P2 Acid–base primary standard: anhydrous sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃.
- [ ] P3 Redox primary standard: ammonium iron(II) sulfate, (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂·6H₂O.
- [ ] P4 Importance: every result in volumetric analysis is only as accurate as the standard; an impure or unstable standard gives a wrong concentration and so a wrong answer for the analyte.

Standardising solutions (one block)
- [ ] S1 Many reagents can't be made up directly as standard solutions: sodium hydroxide absorbs water and carbon dioxide from the air; hydrochloric acid is volatile; potassium manganate(VII) solution slowly decomposes; sodium thiosulfate crystals have uncertain water content.
- [ ] S2 Standardisation is finding the exact concentration of such a solution by titrating it against a primary standard (or a solution already standardised against one).
- [ ] S3 Example: hydrochloric acid is standardised by titration against a standard solution of sodium carbonate: Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂.
- [ ] S4 Example: sodium hydroxide is then standardised against the standardised hydrochloric acid; potassium manganate(VII) is standardised against ammonium iron(II) sulfate.
- [ ] S5 A solution standardised in this way is sometimes called a secondary standard.
