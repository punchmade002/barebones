# Package: chem9-s2 — Evidence for the kinetic theory

- **loId:** `chem9-s2` · **chapterId:** `chem9` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.1
- **Syllabus:** 1.1.1 "understand experimental evidence for the kinetic theory of matter".
  Learn about: "Brownian motion and diffusion of gases as evidence for the kinetic theory of
  matter (calculations not required)". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Statement of the theory, then two investigations:
  Brownian motion (smoke cell) and diffusion (ammonia and hydrogen chloride; bromine). No
  calculations (Graham's law not required).

## 1. Must cover

The kinetic theory (one block)
- [ ] K1 The kinetic theory of matter states that all matter is made of particles that are in constant, random motion.
- [ ] K2 The particles have kinetic energy; the higher the temperature, the faster they move (their average kinetic energy is proportional to the temperature in kelvin).
- [ ] K3 The evidence comes from effects we can see that are caused by particles we cannot see.

Brownian motion (one block, investigation)
- [ ] B1 Brownian motion is the continuous, random, zig-zag movement of small visible particles (such as smoke or pollen grains) suspended in a gas or liquid.
- [ ] B2 It was first observed by the botanist Robert Brown in 1827, with pollen grains in water.
- [ ] B3 Method (smoke cell): fill a small glass cell with smoke and cover it; light it from the side; view it through a microscope.
- [ ] B4 Observation: the smoke particles appear as bright specks that jiggle about randomly and never stop.
- [ ] B5 Explanation: the smoke particles are being bombarded unevenly from all sides by fast-moving, invisible air molecules; at any instant more hit one side than the other, so the particle moves.
- [ ] B6 Conclusion: the air is made of particles in constant, random motion. The motion becomes more vigorous when the temperature is raised.
- [ ] B7 Diagram: a smoke cell lit from the side under a microscope, and the zig-zag path of one smoke particle.

Diffusion of gases (one block, investigation)
- [ ] D1 Diffusion is the spreading out of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration, until evenly mixed, as a result of their random motion.
- [ ] D2 Bromine: a few drops of liquid bromine in a gas jar give brown vapour, which spreads upwards through a second jar of air placed on top until both jars are evenly coloured. Safety: bromine is toxic and corrosive: fume cupboard, gloves.
- [ ] D3 Ammonia and hydrogen chloride: cotton-wool plugs soaked in concentrated ammonia and concentrated hydrochloric acid are placed at opposite ends of a long glass tube, which is sealed with stoppers.
- [ ] D4 After a few minutes a white ring of ammonium chloride forms inside the tube where the gases meet: NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s).
- [ ] D5 The ring forms nearer the hydrochloric acid end, showing that ammonia molecules (Mr 17) diffuse faster than the heavier hydrogen chloride molecules (Mr 36.5): lighter particles move faster at the same temperature.
- [ ] D6 Safety: concentrated ammonia and hydrochloric acid give off irritant, corrosive fumes: fume cupboard, goggles, gloves, tongs to handle the plugs.
- [ ] D7 Diagram: the long tube with the ammonia plug at one end, the hydrochloric acid plug at the other, and the white ring closer to the hydrochloric acid end.
- [ ] D8 Diffusion also happens in liquids (e.g. a crystal of potassium manganate(VII) slowly colours still water purple) but much more slowly than in gases, because the particles are closer together and collide more often.

## 3. Traps

- In Brownian motion the visible particles move because of collisions with invisible molecules; the smoke particles are not themselves molecules.
- Calculations (Graham's law of diffusion) are not required; don't give the equation.
- Diffusion does not need stirring or currents.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Describe an experiment to demonstrate diffusion in gases, with observations and explanation.
- What is Brownian motion; what does it show?
- Why does the ammonium chloride ring form nearer the HCl end?
