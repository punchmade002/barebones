- **Syllabus:** 1.1.5 "verify, using primary data, the law of conservation of mass and explain

  conservation of mass and of energy that underpin all physical and chemical changes". Normal
  type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. The two laws, then one full investigation (a

## 1. Must cover

The laws (one block)
- [ ] L1 Law of conservation of mass: in a chemical reaction (or physical change), matter is neither created nor destroyed, so the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants.
- [ ] L2 Antoine Lavoisier established the law in the late 1700s by careful weighing of reactions in sealed vessels.
- [ ] L3 Law of conservation of energy: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another (e.g. chemical energy to heat and light when a fuel burns); the total energy of a reaction and its surroundings stays the same.
- [ ] L4 Both laws underpin all physical and chemical changes, and are why equations must be balanced and why energy changes in reactions can be measured.

Investigation: a precipitation in a sealed flask (one block, investigation)
- [ ] I1 Aim: to verify, using primary data, that mass is conserved in a chemical reaction.
- [ ] I2 Hypothesis: the total mass before and after the reaction will be the same, because atoms are only rearranged.
- [ ] I3 Equipment: conical flask, small test tube, stopper, electronic balance (0.01 g), sodium chloride solution, silver nitrate solution.
- [ ] I4 Step 1: Pour some sodium chloride solution into the conical flask.
- [ ] I5 Step 2: Half-fill the small test tube with silver nitrate solution and lower it carefully, upright, into the flask so the solutions don't mix.
- [ ] I6 Step 3: Stopper the flask and weigh the whole apparatus.
- [ ] I7 Step 4: Tilt the flask so the solutions mix; a white precipitate of silver chloride forms: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq).
- [ ] I8 Step 5: Reweigh the stoppered flask; repeat the investigation three times.
- [ ] I9 Results: the mass before and after is the same (within the precision of the balance), so mass is conserved.
- [ ] I10 Safety: silver nitrate stains the skin and is an irritant (gloves, goggles).
- [ ] I11 Diagram: a stoppered conical flask on a balance, containing sodium chloride solution with a small test tube of silver nitrate solution standing inside.

Open and closed systems (one block)
- [ ] O1 When marble chips (calcium carbonate) react with hydrochloric acid in an open flask, the mass on the balance decreases: CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g); the carbon dioxide escapes.
- [ ] O2 If the same reaction is done in a sealed flask (or with a balloon over the neck to trap the gas), the mass stays the same, showing that the apparent loss was gas leaving, not mass being destroyed.
- [ ] O3 When magnesium burns in air, the product is heavier than the magnesium, because oxygen from the air has combined with it: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.

Explaining with models (one block)
- [ ] M1 Particle model: in a reaction, the atoms in the reactants are rearranged to form the products; no atoms are created or destroyed, so the total mass stays the same.
- [ ] M2 A balanced equation is a symbolic model of conservation: there are the same number of each kind of atom on both sides (e.g. 1 Ag, 1 N, 3 O, 1 Na and 1 Cl on each side of the silver nitrate equation).
- [ ] M3 A physical model (e.g. molecular model kits): building the reactant molecules, taking them apart and rebuilding the product molecules uses exactly the same pieces.
- [ ] M4 Diagram: particle diagrams of 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O showing four H atoms and two O atoms before and after.

## 3. Traps

- A mass decrease in an open flask is not a failure of the law; gas escaped.
- Mass is conserved; the number of moles of molecules need not be.
- Don't use lead nitrate in the method (toxic).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- State the law of conservation of mass; describe an experiment to verify it.
- Explain why mass appears to decrease when a carbonate reacts with acid in an open flask.
