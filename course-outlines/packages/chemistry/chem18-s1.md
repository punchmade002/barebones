## Part: Dynamic equilibrium (formerly `chem18-s1`)

- **loId:** `chem18-s1` · **chapterId:** `chem18` · **syllabusRef:** 3.3.1
- **Syllabus:** 3.3.1 "appreciate that some reactions tend to be reversible and explain the
  concept of dynamic chemical equilibrium". Learn about: "the concept of reaching a state of
  dynamic chemical equilibrium including: concentrations of reactants and products being
  constant (closed system); rate of forward and reverse reactions are equal; the state of
  equilibrium can be achieved from either direction of the reaction". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Reversible reactions, the meaning of dynamic
  equilibrium and its features, with graphs. Kc is in "The equilibrium constant"; disturbing an
  equilibrium is in "Le Châtelier's principle". Weak-acid and indicator equilibria are in
  "Acid–base theories and strength" and "Titration curves, indicators and vinegar"; name them only as

### 1. Must cover

Reversible reactions (one block)
- [ ] R1 Some reactions go to completion: the reactants are converted completely into products and the reaction cannot easily be reversed, e.g. burning magnesium, 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s).
- [ ] R2 A reversible reaction is one in which the products can react to form the reactants again; both the forward and the reverse reactions take place. It is shown by the sign ⇌.
- [ ] R3 Examples: the Haber process, N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g); hydrogen and iodine, H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g); esterification, CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O; nitrogen dioxide and dinitrogen tetroxide, 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g) (brown ⇌ colourless).
- [ ] R4 Other examples elsewhere in the course: a weak acid in water, CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ CH₃COO⁻(aq) + H⁺(aq); an acid–base indicator, HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻.
- [ ] R5 The "forward" reaction is the one from left to right as written; the "reverse" (back) reaction is from right to left.

Dynamic equilibrium (one block)
- [ ] D1 Chemical equilibrium is a state of dynamic balance in a reversible reaction in which the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.
- [ ] D2 It is called dynamic because both reactions are still happening at equilibrium: reactant particles are still forming products and product particles are still forming reactants, at the same rate.
- [ ] D3 Because they are formed and used up at the same rate, the concentrations of reactants and products stay constant at equilibrium, so the observable properties (colour, pressure, density) stop changing.
- [ ] D4 The concentrations are constant, but they are not equal; at equilibrium there may be much more product than reactant, or the reverse.
- [ ] D5 Static equilibrium is different: nothing is moving (e.g. a metre stick balanced at its centre). An analogy for dynamic equilibrium: a person walking up a down-escalator (or running on a treadmill) at the same speed as it moves stays in the same place although both movements continue.

The closed system (one block)
- [ ] S1 Equilibrium can be reached only in a closed system: no reactants or products can enter or leave (energy, such as heat, can still be exchanged with the surroundings).
- [ ] S2 Example: heating calcium carbonate, CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g). In a sealed container the CO₂ builds up and equilibrium is reached; in an open container the CO₂ escapes, the reverse reaction cannot happen, and the calcium carbonate decomposes completely.
- [ ] S3 A fizzy drink in a closed bottle: CO₂(g) ⇌ CO₂(aq) is at equilibrium; once the bottle is opened the gas escapes and the drink goes flat.

Reaching equilibrium (one block)
- [ ] G1 At the start (only reactants present), the forward rate is high and the reverse rate is zero.
- [ ] G2 As reactants are used up the forward rate falls; as products build up the reverse rate rises.
- [ ] G3 Equilibrium is reached when the two rates become equal; from then on all concentrations are constant.
- [ ] G4 The same equilibrium can be reached from either direction: starting with 1 mol of H₂ and 1 mol of I₂, or with 2 mol of HI only, in the same sealed flask at the same temperature, gives the same equilibrium mixture.
- [ ] G5 Concentration–time graph (e.g. for N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ starting with N₂ and H₂): the reactant curves fall and the product curve rises from zero, each changing quickly at first; all three level off (become horizontal) at the same time, when equilibrium is reached. H₂ falls three times as fast as N₂, and NH₃ rises twice as fast as N₂ falls.
- [ ] G6 Rate–time graph: the forward-rate curve falls and the reverse-rate curve rises from zero until they meet and continue as one horizontal line at equilibrium.
- [ ] G7 Diagram: (a) concentration against time with the reactant and product curves levelling off at the moment equilibrium is reached (marked); (b) forward and reverse rates against time meeting at equilibrium.

### 3. Traps

- At equilibrium the concentrations are constant, not equal, and the reactions have not stopped.
- Equal rates, not equal amounts.
- An open container is not a closed system; a gas escaping lets a reaction go to completion.
- Don't introduce Kc here.

### 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- What is meant by a reversible reaction; by chemical equilibrium; why it is dynamic.
- Explain equilibrium in terms of rates and in terms of concentrations; why concentrations stay constant.
- When the colour of an equilibrium mixture stops changing, has the reaction stopped? Explain.
- Why equilibrium is reached some time after mixing the reactants.

- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Writing Kc expressions, what affects its value,
  and (HL) using its size and the reaction quotient to predict how a reaction proceeds.
  Numerical problems are in "Kc calculations"; Le Châtelier's principle and the shifts it
  predicts are in "Le Châtelier's principle". Ka and Kw (expressions of the same kind) are in
  "pH calculations and dissociation constants" and "Self-ionisation of water and pH".

### 1. Must cover

The equilibrium law and Kc (one block)
- [ ] K1 The equilibrium law: for a reaction at equilibrium at a fixed temperature, the concentrations of products and reactants are related by a constant, the equilibrium constant Kc.
- [ ] K2 For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD: Kc = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b.
- [ ] K3 Square brackets mean concentration in mol L⁻¹, at equilibrium.
- [ ] K4 Products go on top (numerator) and reactants on the bottom (denominator); each concentration is raised to the power of its coefficient in the balanced equation; the concentrations are multiplied, not added.
- [ ] K5 Example: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g): Kc = [NH₃]² / [N₂][H₂]³.
- [ ] K6 Example: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g): Kc = [HI]² / [H₂][I₂].
- [ ] K7 Example: PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g): Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] / [PCl₅].
- [ ] K8 Example: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O: Kc = [CH₃COOC₂H₅][H₂O] / [CH₃COOH][C₂H₅OH]; water is included here because it is a product, not the solvent.
- [ ] K9 Example: Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq): Kc = [FeSCN²⁺] / [Fe³⁺][SCN⁻].

Units of Kc (one block)
- [ ] U1 The units of Kc depend on the expression: substitute mol L⁻¹ for each concentration and cancel.
- [ ] U2 When the total of the powers is the same top and bottom (e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, or esterification), the units cancel and Kc has no units.
- [ ] U3 For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃: (mol L⁻¹)² / (mol L⁻¹)⁴ = (mol L⁻¹)⁻², i.e. L² mol⁻²; for PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂: mol L⁻¹.

What affects the value of Kc (one block)
- [ ] F1 For a given reaction, the value of Kc changes only with temperature.
- [ ] F2 Changing a concentration does not change Kc: the system shifts until the ratio in the Kc expression has its original value again.
- [ ] F3 Changing the pressure of a gas mixture does not change Kc: the concentrations change, but the new equilibrium mixture gives the same value of Kc.
- [ ] F4 A catalyst does not change Kc: it speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner with the same composition.
- [ ] F5 Forward reaction exothermic (ΔH negative): raising the temperature decreases Kc (less product at equilibrium); lowering it increases Kc. Example: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹.
- [ ] F6 Forward reaction endothermic (ΔH positive): raising the temperature increases Kc; lowering it decreases Kc. Example: PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂ (ΔH positive); PCl₅ decomposes further on heating.
- [ ] F7 So a value of Kc must always be quoted with its temperature; and if you are told how Kc changes with temperature you can deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic.

Using Kc to describe and predict (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] P1 [HL] A large Kc (much greater than 1) means the equilibrium mixture is mainly products: the equilibrium lies to the right and the reaction goes almost to completion.
- [ ] P2 [HL] A small Kc (much less than 1) means the mixture is mainly reactants: the equilibrium lies to the left and little product forms.
- [ ] P3 [HL] Kc near 1 means appreciable amounts of both reactants and products are present at equilibrium; e.g. esterification, Kc ≈ 4, so starting with 1 mol each of ethanoic acid and ethanol about two-thirds of a mole of ester forms.
- [ ] P4 [HL] Kc tells how far a reaction goes, not how fast: a reaction with a large Kc may be very slow (e.g. without a catalyst).
- [ ] P5 [HL] Reaction quotient, Q: the same expression as Kc but using the concentrations at any moment, not necessarily at equilibrium.
- [ ] P6 [HL] If Q < Kc, the mixture has too little product: the reaction proceeds forward (to the right) until Q = Kc. If Q > Kc, it proceeds in reverse (to the left). If Q = Kc, the mixture is at equilibrium.
- [ ] P7 [HL] Worked prediction (sample data): for esterification with Kc = 4.0, a mixture with 0.50 mol each of acid, ethanol, ester and water has Q = (0.50 × 0.50) ÷ (0.50 × 0.50) = 1.0; Q < Kc, so more ester forms.
- [ ] P8 [HL] This explains Le Châtelier's principle mathematically: adding a reactant makes the denominator larger, Q falls below Kc, and the reaction proceeds forward until Q = Kc again.
- [ ] P9 [HL] Writing the equation the other way round inverts Kc: for 2HI ⇌ H₂ + I₂, Kc = [H₂][I₂] / [HI]², which is 1 ÷ (Kc for H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI) at the same temperature.

### 3. Traps

- Products over reactants, never the reverse; powers are the coefficients; don't add concentrations.
- Use concentrations (mol L⁻¹), not moles, unless the volume cancels.
- Only temperature changes Kc; concentration, pressure and a catalyst change the composition or the rate, not Kc.
- A large Kc does not mean a fast reaction.
- Examples here are homogeneous (all gases or all in solution); don't set up expressions with solids.

- Effect on Kc of raising temperature, increasing pressure, adding a reactant, adding a catalyst; justify.
- Deduce whether a reaction is exothermic or endothermic from how Kc changes with temperature.
- (HL) What the size of Kc says about the position of equilibrium.

## Part: Kc calculations (formerly `chem18-s3`)

- **loId:** `chem18-s3` · **chapterId:** `chem18` · **syllabusRef:** 3.3.3
- **Syllabus:** 3.3.3 "**solve problems involving the mathematical model for the equilibrium
  constant KC**". Learn about: "**how to calculate KC given equilibrium concentrations; how to
  calculate equilibrium concentration given the value of KC**". All bold: the whole subsection
  is Higher Level only. Mark every block heading " (Higher Level only)".
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. The method (including the initial–change–
  equilibrium table), then five worked problems, one block each, every step its own bullet. All
  numbers are sample data with arithmetic checked. Writing Kc expressions and their units is in
  "The equilibrium constant"; use them without re-teaching. Ar values: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16,
  P 31, Cl 35.5, I 127.

### 1. Must cover

The method (Higher Level only; one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 [HL] Step 1: Write the balanced equation and the Kc expression.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Step 2: Convert any masses to moles (n = mass ÷ M).
- [ ] M3 [HL] Step 3: Set up a table with rows Initial (moles at the start), Change (moles reacting or forming, in the ratio of the coefficients) and Equilibrium (initial + change) for every species.
- [ ] M4 [HL] Step 4: Divide each equilibrium amount by the volume of the container in litres to get concentrations in mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] M5 [HL] Step 5: Substitute into the Kc expression and calculate; give the answer to 3 significant figures (with units if asked).
- [ ] M6 [HL] When the total number of moles is the same on each side of the equation (e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, esterification), the volume cancels and moles can be used directly in the expression.
- [ ] M7 [HL] To find equilibrium amounts from Kc: let x be the amount that reacts, write each equilibrium amount in terms of x, substitute into the Kc expression and solve for x; if both sides are perfect squares, take the square root; otherwise rearrange into a quadratic and use x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a.
- [ ] M8 [HL] Choose the root that makes physical sense: no equilibrium amount can be negative, so x cannot exceed the starting amount of a reactant.

Problem 1: Kc from equilibrium amounts (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] A1 [HL] Data: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) in a 2.00 L flask at a fixed temperature; at equilibrium there are 0.100 mol H₂, 0.150 mol I₂ and 0.840 mol HI.
- [ ] A2 [HL] Concentrations: [H₂] = 0.100 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0500; [I₂] = 0.150 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0750; [HI] = 0.840 ÷ 2.00 = 0.420 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] A3 [HL] Kc = [HI]² / [H₂][I₂] = (0.420)² ÷ (0.0500 × 0.0750) = 0.1764 ÷ 0.00375 = 47.0 (no units).

Problem 2: Kc from initial amounts and one equilibrium amount (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] B1 [HL] Data: 2.00 mol N₂ and 6.00 mol H₂ are sealed in a 4.00 L vessel; at equilibrium 1.20 mol NH₃ is present. N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).
- [ ] B2 [HL] Change: forming 1.20 mol NH₃ uses 0.600 mol N₂ and 1.80 mol H₂ (ratio 1 : 3 : 2).
- [ ] B3 [HL] Equilibrium amounts: N₂ = 2.00 − 0.600 = 1.40 mol; H₂ = 6.00 − 1.80 = 4.20 mol; NH₃ = 1.20 mol.
- [ ] B4 [HL] Concentrations (÷ 4.00 L): [N₂] = 0.350, [H₂] = 1.05, [NH₃] = 0.300 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] B5 [HL] Kc = [NH₃]² / [N₂][H₂]³ = (0.300)² ÷ (0.350 × 1.05³) = 0.0900 ÷ 0.405 = 0.222 L² mol⁻².

Problem 3: Kc from percentage dissociation (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] C1 [HL] Data: 41.7 g of PCl₅ is heated in a sealed 5.00 L vessel; at equilibrium 40.0% has dissociated. PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g).
- [ ] C2 [HL] M(PCl₅) = 31 + 5 × 35.5 = 208.5 g mol⁻¹; moles PCl₅ = 41.7 ÷ 208.5 = 0.200 mol.
- [ ] C3 [HL] Moles dissociated = 40.0% of 0.200 = 0.0800 mol; at equilibrium PCl₅ = 0.120 mol, PCl₃ = 0.0800 mol, Cl₂ = 0.0800 mol.
- [ ] C4 [HL] Concentrations (÷ 5.00 L): [PCl₅] = 0.0240, [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = 0.0160 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] C5 [HL] Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] / [PCl₅] = (0.0160 × 0.0160) ÷ 0.0240 = 0.0107 mol L⁻¹.

Problem 4: equilibrium amounts from Kc, perfect square (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] D1 [HL] Data: 1.00 mol ethanoic acid and 1.00 mol ethanol are mixed and reach equilibrium; Kc = 4.00. CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
- [ ] D2 [HL] Let x mol of ester form: at equilibrium acid = ethanol = 1.00 − x; ester = water = x. The volume cancels.
- [ ] D3 [HL] Kc = x² ÷ (1.00 − x)² = 4.00; square root of both sides: x ÷ (1.00 − x) = 2.00.
- [ ] D4 [HL] x = 2.00 − 2.00x, so 3.00x = 2.00 and x = 0.667 mol of ester (and of water); acid and ethanol = 0.333 mol each.
- [ ] D5 [HL] Mass of ester = 0.667 × 88 = 58.7 g (M of CH₃COOC₂H₅ = 88 g mol⁻¹).

Problem 5: equilibrium concentrations from Kc, quadratic (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] E1 [HL] Data: 1.00 mol H₂ and 2.00 mol I₂ are placed in a 1.00 L flask; Kc = 49.0 at this temperature. H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).
- [ ] E2 [HL] Let x mol of H₂ react: at equilibrium H₂ = 1.00 − x, I₂ = 2.00 − x, HI = 2x.
- [ ] E3 [HL] 49.0 = (2x)² ÷ [(1.00 − x)(2.00 − x)], so 4x² = 49.0(2.00 − 3.00x + x²), giving 45.0x² − 147x + 98.0 = 0.
- [ ] E4 [HL] x = (147 ± √(147² − 4 × 45.0 × 98.0)) ÷ (2 × 45.0) = (147 ± 63.0) ÷ 90.0, so x = 2.33 or 0.933.
- [ ] E5 [HL] x = 2.33 is impossible (more than the 1.00 mol of H₂ present), so x = 0.933.
- [ ] E6 [HL] Equilibrium concentrations: [H₂] = 0.0667, [I₂] = 1.07, [HI] = 1.87 mol L⁻¹. Check: 1.87² ÷ (0.0667 × 1.07) ≈ 49.

### 3. Traps

- Use equilibrium amounts, not initial amounts, in the Kc expression.
- Divide moles by the volume unless the volume cancels (M6); for Problem 2 and 3 it does not.

- Reject a root that gives a negative equilibrium amount.

### 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "1.00 mol of carbon monoxide and 1.00 mol of steam were placed in a 10.0 L vessel and heated: CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO₂(g) + H₂(g). At equilibrium 0.600 mol of CO₂ was present.
