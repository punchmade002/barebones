## 1. Must cover

Conjugate pairs (one block)
- [ ] C1 [HL] When an acid donates a proton, what is left can accept a proton back: it is the conjugate base of that acid.
- [ ] C2 [HL] When a base accepts a proton, it forms the conjugate acid of that base.
- [ ] C3 [HL] A conjugate acid–base pair is two species that differ by one proton (H⁺), e.g. HCl and Cl⁻; H₃O⁺ and H₂O; NH₄⁺ and NH₃.
- [ ] C4 [HL] Every Brønsted–Lowry reaction has two conjugate pairs: acid₁ + base₂ ⇌ acid₂ + base₁.

Identifying the species (one block, skills)
- [ ] M1 [HL] Step 1: For each reactant, see whether it loses or gains a proton in going to the products.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Step 2: The reactant that loses a proton is the acid; the one that gains a proton is the base.
- [ ] M3 [HL] Step 3: On the product side, the species formed from the base is the conjugate acid, and the species formed from the acid is the conjugate base; in a reversible reaction they act as an acid and a base in the reverse reaction.

Worked examples (one block; one bullet per equation)
- [ ] W1 [HL] HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻: acids HCl and H₃O⁺; bases H₂O and Cl⁻; pairs HCl/Cl⁻ and H₃O⁺/H₂O.
- [ ] W2 [HL] NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻: acids H₂O and NH₄⁺; bases NH₃ and OH⁻; pairs NH₄⁺/NH₃ and H₂O/OH⁻.
- [ ] W3 [HL] CH₃COOH + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H₃O⁺: pairs CH₃COOH/CH₃COO⁻ and H₃O⁺/H₂O.
- [ ] W4 [HL] HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CO₃²⁻ + H₃O⁺ (HCO₃⁻ as an acid) and HCO₃⁻ + H₃O⁺ ⇌ H₂CO₃ + H₂O (HCO₃⁻ as a base).
- [ ] W5 [HL] NH₃ + HCl → NH₄⁺ + Cl⁻ (no water involved): pairs NH₄⁺/NH₃ and HCl/Cl⁻.

Strength of conjugates and amphoteric species (one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] The stronger an acid, the weaker its conjugate base: HCl is a strong acid, so Cl⁻ is a very weak base that barely accepts protons.
- [ ] S2 [HL] A weak acid has a relatively strong conjugate base: ethanoic acid is weak, and the ethanoate ion (CH₃COO⁻) readily accepts a proton.
- [ ] S3 [HL] An amphoteric (amphiprotic) species can act as either an acid or a base: water (W1, W2) and the hydrogencarbonate ion (W4).

## 2. Checked facts and examples

All equations above are correct.

## 3. Traps

- Conjugate pairs differ by exactly one H⁺: H₂O and OH⁻ are a pair; H₃O⁺ and OH⁻ are not.
- The conjugate base of an acid is on the product side, not the other reactant.
- Ka and pH calculations are in the pH chapter.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Identify the acids, bases and conjugate pairs in a given equation.
- Explain the term conjugate pair; why water is amphoteric.
