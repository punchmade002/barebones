# Package: chem9-s1 — The particulate nature of matter

- **loId:** `chem9-s1` · **chapterId:** `chem9` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1 LA
- **Syllabus:** 1.1 learn about: "the particulate nature of matter: pure substances (elements,
  compounds) or mixtures of substances". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Classification of matter with particle-level
  descriptions. States of matter and changes of state are "Using the kinetic theory";
  separating mixtures is "Separation techniques".

## 1. Must cover

Matter is made of particles (one block)
- [ ] M1 All matter is made of tiny particles (atoms, molecules or ions) that are constantly moving, with spaces between them.
- [ ] M2 (assumed) The three states: in a solid the particles are packed closely in a fixed, regular arrangement and vibrate about fixed positions; in a liquid they are close together but can move past each other; in a gas they are far apart and move rapidly and randomly in all directions.
- [ ] M3 Diagram: three boxes showing the particle arrangement in a solid, a liquid and a gas.

Pure substances (one block)
- [ ] P1 A pure substance contains only one kind of particle (one element or one compound) and has fixed properties, such as a sharp melting point and boiling point.
- [ ] P2 An element is a substance made of only one kind of atom and cannot be split into simpler substances by chemical means, e.g. iron (Fe), oxygen (O₂).
- [ ] P3 A compound is a substance made of two or more different elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio, e.g. water (H₂O), sodium chloride (NaCl); its properties differ from those of its elements, and it can be separated into them only by a chemical reaction.
- [ ] P4 Molecules can be of elements (O₂, N₂, Cl₂) or of compounds (H₂O, CO₂).

Mixtures (one block)
- [ ] X1 A mixture contains two or more substances that are not chemically combined; its composition can vary, and its components keep their own properties.
- [ ] X2 A mixture can be separated by physical methods (see "Separation techniques") and has no sharp melting or boiling point: it melts or boils over a range of temperatures.
- [ ] X3 Homogeneous mixture: uniform composition throughout, with a single phase, e.g. salt solution, air, an alloy.
- [ ] X4 Heterogeneous mixture: non-uniform, with visibly different parts or phases, e.g. sand and water, oil and water, granite.
- [ ] X5 Testing purity: a pure substance has a sharp melting point that matches the data-book value; impurities lower the melting point and make it melt over a range.

Comparing (one block)
- [ ] K1 Compound vs mixture, paired points: fixed composition vs variable; chemically combined vs not; new properties vs components keep their properties; separated chemically vs physically; energy usually taken in or given out when formed vs little energy change on mixing.
- [ ] K2 Diagram: particle diagrams of an element (single atoms and diatomic molecules), a compound (identical molecules each of two kinds of atom) and a mixture (two kinds of particle mixed).

## 2. Checked facts and examples

Only the examples above.

## 3. Traps

- An alloy is a homogeneous mixture; don't teach alloys further (not on the specification).
- Air is a mixture, not a compound.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Distinguish between an element, a compound and a mixture.
- Classify substances; distinguish homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "Distinguish between an element, a compound and a mixture, giving one example of each." Model answer: P2, P3, X1.
2. 6 marks: "Give two differences between a compound and a mixture, and explain how melting point can be used to test whether a substance is pure." Model answer: K1 (two), X5.
