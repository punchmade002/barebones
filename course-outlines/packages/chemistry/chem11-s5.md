# Package: chem11-s5 — Strong vs weak, concentrated vs dilute

- **loId:** `chem11-s5` · **chapterId:** `chem11` · **syllabusRef:** 3.4.7
- **Syllabus:** 3.4.7 "distinguish between: weak and strong acids (and bases); concentrated and
  dilute acids (and bases)". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. The two distinctions, examples, experimental
  evidence, and the trap of confusing them. Ka, Kb and pH calculations are in the pH chapter
  (Higher Level).

## 1. Must cover

Strong and weak acids (one block)
- [ ] A1 A strong acid is completely dissociated (ionised) into ions in water: HCl(aq) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) (single arrow).
- [ ] A2 Strong acids: hydrochloric (HCl), nitric (HNO₃), sulfuric (H₂SO₄).
- [ ] A3 A weak acid is only slightly dissociated in water; most molecules stay undissociated and an equilibrium is set up: CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ CH₃COO⁻(aq) + H⁺(aq).
- [ ] A4 Weak acids: ethanoic acid (vinegar), citric acid, carbonic acid (CO₂ in water), most organic acids.

Strong and weak bases (one block)
- [ ] B1 A strong base is completely dissociated in water, e.g. sodium hydroxide and potassium hydroxide: NaOH(aq) → Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq).
- [ ] B2 A weak base reacts only slightly with water to produce hydroxide ions, e.g. ammonia: NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq).

Concentrated and dilute (one block)
- [ ] C1 Concentration is the amount of acid (or base) dissolved per litre of solution (mol L⁻¹).
- [ ] C2 A concentrated acid has a large amount of acid per litre (e.g. 10 M HCl); a dilute acid has a small amount (e.g. 0.1 M HCl).
- [ ] C3 Strength and concentration are independent: a dilute strong acid (0.01 M HCl) is still fully dissociated; a concentrated weak acid (glacial ethanoic acid) is still only slightly dissociated.

Evidence: comparing acids of the same concentration (one block, investigation)
- [ ] E1 pH: 0.1 M hydrochloric acid has a pH of 1.0; 0.1 M ethanoic acid has a pH of about 2.9, because it produces far fewer H⁺ ions.
- [ ] E2 Rate of reaction: magnesium ribbon fizzes much faster in 0.1 M HCl than in 0.1 M ethanoic acid (higher concentration of H⁺ ions).
- [ ] E3 Conductivity: 0.1 M HCl conducts electricity much better than 0.1 M ethanoic acid (more ions).
- [ ] E4 The same volumes of the two acids at the same concentration still neutralise the same amount of sodium hydroxide and give the same total volume of hydrogen with excess magnesium, because the weak acid's equilibrium shifts to release more H⁺ as H⁺ is used up.
- [ ] E5 Safety: concentrated acids and alkalis are corrosive: goggles, gloves; dilute by adding acid to water.

## 2. Checked facts and examples

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| E1 | pH of 0.1 M HCl = 1.0; pH of 0.1 M ethanoic acid ≈ 2.9 (Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵). |

## 3. Traps

- "Strong" doesn't mean "concentrated"; "weak" doesn't mean "dilute".
- E4 is a common exam trap: same concentration, same volume means the same amount of base is needed.
- Don't do Ka or pH calculations here (Higher Level, in the pH chapter).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Distinguish strong and weak acids, concentrated and dilute acids, with examples.
- Describe evidence that ethanoic acid is weaker than hydrochloric acid.

## 5. Practice questions to write

2. 9 marks: "Describe two experiments that would show that 0.1 M ethanoic acid is a weaker acid than 0.1 M hydrochloric acid, and explain why equal volumes of the two acids need the same volume of sodium hydroxide to neutralise them." Model answer: two of E1–E3; E4.
