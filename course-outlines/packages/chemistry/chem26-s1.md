## 1. Must cover

Stoichiometry and the method (one block, skills; one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 Stoichiometry is the study of the quantitative relationships between the amounts of reactants and products in a chemical reaction, given by the balanced equation.
- [ ] M2 The coefficients in a balanced equation give the ratio of moles, not of masses; masses must always be converted to moles before the ratio is used.
- [ ] M3 Step 1: Write the balanced equation (see "Balancing equations").
- [ ] M4 Step 2: Convert the quantity you are given into moles: n = m ÷ M for a mass; n = V ÷ 22.4 for a gas volume in litres at STP; n = c × V (V in litres) for a solution.
- [ ] M5 Step 3: Use the mole ratio from the equation to find the moles of the substance asked for: moles wanted = moles known × (coefficient of wanted ÷ coefficient of known).
- [ ] M6 Step 4: Convert those moles into the quantity asked for: m = n × M; V = n × 22.4 L at STP; number of particles = n × 6.02 × 10²³.
- [ ] M7 Step 5: Give the answer with units and to a sensible number of significant figures (usually 3); check that it is reasonable.

Example 1: mass to mass (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] A1 Question: what masses of carbon dioxide and calcium oxide are produced when 25.0 g of calcium carbonate is heated strongly until it decomposes completely?
- [ ] A2 Equation: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g); ratio CaCO₃ : CaO : CO₂ = 1 : 1 : 1.
- [ ] A3 Moles of CaCO₃ = 25.0 ÷ 100 = 0.250 mol (M = 100 g mol⁻¹).
- [ ] A4 Moles of CO₂ = moles of CaO = 0.250 mol.
- [ ] A5 Mass of CO₂ = 0.250 × 44 = 11.0 g; mass of CaO = 0.250 × 56 = 14.0 g.
- [ ] A6 Check by conservation of mass: 11.0 + 14.0 = 25.0 g, the mass of calcium carbonate.

Example 2: mass to gas volume (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] B1 Question: what volume of hydrogen, measured at STP, is produced when 1.20 g of magnesium reacts with excess hydrochloric acid?
- [ ] B2 Equation: Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g); ratio Mg : H₂ = 1 : 1.
- [ ] B3 Moles of Mg = 1.20 ÷ 24 = 0.0500 mol, so moles of H₂ = 0.0500 mol.
- [ ] B4 Volume of H₂ = 0.0500 × 22.4 = 1.12 L (1120 cm³) at STP.
- [ ] B5 "Excess" acid means there is more than enough acid, so all the magnesium reacts and the magnesium decides the amount of hydrogen.

Example 3: gas volume to mass (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] C1 Question: what mass of hydrogen peroxide must decompose to give 560 cm³ of oxygen at STP?
- [ ] C2 Equation: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g); ratio H₂O₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1.
- [ ] C3 Moles of O₂ = 0.560 L ÷ 22.4 L mol⁻¹ = 0.0250 mol (convert cm³ to L first: 560 cm³ = 0.560 L).
- [ ] C4 Moles of H₂O₂ = 2 × 0.0250 = 0.0500 mol.
- [ ] C5 Mass of H₂O₂ = 0.0500 × 34 = 1.70 g.

Example 4: a reactant needed and two products (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] D1 Question: 8.80 g of propane burns completely in oxygen. Calculate the mass of oxygen needed and the volume of carbon dioxide formed at STP.
- [ ] D2 Equation: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l).
- [ ] D3 Moles of C₃H₈ = 8.80 ÷ 44 = 0.200 mol.
- [ ] D4 Moles of O₂ = 5 × 0.200 = 1.00 mol; mass = 1.00 × 32 = 32.0 g.
- [ ] D5 Moles of CO₂ = 3 × 0.200 = 0.600 mol; volume = 0.600 × 22.4 = 13.4 L at STP.
- [ ] D6 Reacting gas volumes alone (all measured at the same temperature and pressure) can be found directly from the mole ratio, without converting to moles (see "Molar volume and Avogadro's law").

Example 5: liquids and thermal decomposition (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] E1 For a liquid, find the mass from its volume and density first: m = ρ × V.
- [ ] E2 Question: 5.00 cm³ of ethanol (density 0.789 g cm⁻³) burns completely. What mass of carbon dioxide forms? Mass of ethanol = 5.00 × 0.789 = 3.945 g; moles = 3.945 ÷ 46 = 0.08576 mol.
- [ ] E3 C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l), so moles of CO₂ = 2 × 0.08576 = 0.1715 mol; mass = 0.1715 × 44 = 7.55 g (keep an extra figure in intermediate values and round only at the end).
- [ ] E4 Question: 8.40 g of sodium hydrogencarbonate is heated until no more gas is given off. 2NaHCO₃(s) → Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(g) + CO₂(g).
- [ ] E5 Moles of NaHCO₃ = 8.40 ÷ 84 = 0.100 mol; moles of Na₂CO₃ = 0.100 ÷ 2 = 0.0500 mol; mass of solid residue = 0.0500 × 106 = 5.30 g.
- [ ] E6 Moles of CO₂ = 0.0500 mol; volume = 0.0500 × 22.4 = 1.12 L at STP.

Simple unit analysis (one block)
- [ ] U1 Unit analysis (dimensional analysis): write the units with every number and multiply by conversion factors so that unwanted units cancel, leaving the unit asked for; if the units don't come out right, the method is wrong.
- [ ] U2 Example chain: 3.945 g × (1 mol ÷ 46 g) = 0.08576 mol of ethanol; 0.08576 mol ethanol × (2 mol CO₂ ÷ 1 mol ethanol) × (44 g ÷ 1 mol CO₂) = 7.55 g of CO₂.
- [ ] U3 Volume conversions: 1 L = 1000 cm³ = 1 dm³; 1 m³ = 1000 L. Mass conversions: 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 tonne = 1000 kg = 10⁶ g.
- [ ] U4 Because the mole ratio is a ratio, masses can be scaled in any consistent unit: 100 g of CaCO₃ gives 56 g of CaO, so 1.00 tonne of CaCO₃ gives 0.560 tonnes of CaO.
- [ ] U5 Common slips: using cm³ in n = V ÷ 22.4 (the volume must be in litres); using Mr of the wrong substance; forgetting the mole ratio (step 3).

Gravimetric analysis (one block)
- [ ] G1 Gravimetric analysis is quantitative analysis by mass: the substance being measured is converted into a pure solid (usually an insoluble precipitate) of known formula, which is separated, dried and weighed.
- [ ] G2 Method step 1: Weigh the sample accurately and dissolve it in deionised water.
- [ ] G3 Method step 2: Add an excess of the precipitating reagent (e.g. silver nitrate solution for chloride ions; barium chloride solution for sulfate ions), so that all of the ion is precipitated: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s), white; Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s), white.
- [ ] G4 Method step 3: Check that precipitation is complete by adding a drop more reagent to the clear liquid above the settled precipitate; no further cloudiness should appear.
- [ ] G5 Method step 4: Filter through a weighed filter paper (or weighed sintered-glass crucible) and wash the precipitate with deionised water to remove soluble impurities, which would otherwise add to the mass.
- [ ] G6 Method step 5: Dry the precipitate in an oven, cool and weigh; repeat drying and weighing until the mass is constant (all water removed).
- [ ] G7 Calculation: moles of precipitate → moles of the ion (from the formula) → mass of the ion or of the original compound → percentage of the sample.
- [ ] G8 Safety and errors: silver nitrate stains skin and barium compounds are toxic (gloves, goggles); silver chloride darkens in bright light, so keep it out of direct sunlight; losing precipitate through the filter paper gives a low result; not washing or not drying to constant mass gives a high result.
- [ ] G9 Diagram: filter funnel with folded filter paper in a conical flask, the precipitate on the paper and a wash bottle of deionised water beside it.

Gravimetric worked examples (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] H1 Chloride: 0.500 g of impure sodium chloride was dissolved and treated with excess silver nitrate solution; the dry silver chloride weighed 1.148 g (M of AgCl = 143.5 g mol⁻¹).
- [ ] H2 Moles of AgCl = 1.148 ÷ 143.5 = 0.00800 mol = moles of Cl⁻ = moles of NaCl (AgCl and NaCl each contain one Cl).
- [ ] H3 Mass of chlorine = 0.00800 × 35.5 = 0.284 g, so the sample is (0.284 ÷ 0.500) × 100 = 56.8% chlorine by mass.
- [ ] H4 Mass of NaCl = 0.00800 × 58.5 = 0.468 g; purity = (0.468 ÷ 0.500) × 100 = 93.6%.
- [ ] H5 Sulfate: 1.00 g of a fertiliser containing ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄, gave 1.60 g of dry barium sulfate (M of BaSO₄ = 233 g mol⁻¹). Moles of BaSO₄ = 1.60 ÷ 233 = 6.867 × 10⁻³ mol = moles of (NH₄)₂SO₄.
- [ ] H6 Mass of (NH₄)₂SO₄ = 6.867 × 10⁻³ × 132 = 0.906 g; percentage in the fertiliser = 90.6%.

Combustion analysis (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] K1 When an organic compound of C, H and O burns completely, all its carbon ends up in CO₂ and all its hydrogen in H₂O; the masses of these products give the masses of C and H in the sample, and the oxygen is found by difference. The empirical formula is then found as in "Empirical and molecular formulae".
- [ ] K2 Data: 0.290 g of a compound containing only C, H and O burned completely to give 0.660 g of CO₂ and 0.270 g of H₂O; its Mr is 58.
- [ ] K3 Moles of C = moles of CO₂ = 0.660 ÷ 44 = 0.0150 mol; mass of C = 0.0150 × 12 = 0.180 g.
- [ ] K4 Moles of H = 2 × moles of H₂O = 2 × (0.270 ÷ 18) = 0.0300 mol; mass of H = 0.0300 g.
- [ ] K5 Mass of O = 0.290 − 0.180 − 0.0300 = 0.0800 g; moles of O = 0.0800 ÷ 16 = 0.00500 mol.
- [ ] K6 Ratio C : H : O = 0.0150 : 0.0300 : 0.00500 = 3 : 6 : 1, so the empirical formula is C₃H₆O (mass 58); since Mr = 58, the molecular formula is also C₃H₆O (e.g. propanone).

## 3. Traps

- Mole ratios apply to moles, never directly to masses (except when scaling one substance's mass in U4).
- Volume must be in litres when dividing by 22.4 L mol⁻¹; 22.4 L applies only to gases at STP.
- H₂O contains two H atoms: moles of H = 2 × moles of H₂O.
- In gravimetric analysis the precipitating reagent is added in excess; the substance being analysed decides the mass of precipitate.
- Identifying which reactant runs out when amounts of both are given is Higher Level and taught in "Limiting reagents"; in this subsection one reactant is always "in excess" as stated.
- Don't reteach empirical formula steps or balancing; point to "Empirical and molecular formulae" and "Balancing equations".

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Calculate the mass of a product or reactant from a given mass, using a balanced equation.
- Calculate the volume of a gas at STP produced or used in a reaction; the number of molecules or atoms formed.
- Multi-step problems: products of one reaction used in the next; quantities in decomposition reactions.
- Find an empirical formula from the masses of CO₂ and H₂O formed on combustion.
- Gravimetric analysis: percentage of an ion or compound in a sample from the mass of precipitate.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "12.0 g of magnesium carbonate is heated until it decomposes completely: MgCO₃(s) → MgO(s) + CO₂(g). Calculate (i) the moles of magnesium carbonate, (ii) the mass of magnesium oxide formed, (iii) the volume of carbon dioxide formed at STP. (MgCO₃ = 84)" Model answer: 12.0 ÷ 84 = 0.1429 mol (0.143 to 3 s.f.); 0.14286 × 40 = 5.71 g MgO; 0.14286 × 22.4 = 3.20 L CO₂.
2. 9 marks: "0.750 g of impure potassium chloride was dissolved in water and excess silver nitrate solution was added. The dry precipitate of silver chloride weighed 1.35 g. Name this method of analysis, state why excess silver nitrate was used, and calculate the percentage purity of the potassium chloride. (AgCl = 143.5, KCl = 74.5)" Model answer: gravimetric analysis (G1); excess so that all the chloride is precipitated (G3); moles AgCl = 1.35 ÷ 143.5 = 9.408 × 10⁻³ = moles KCl; mass KCl = 9.408 × 10⁻³ × 74.5 = 0.701 g; purity = 0.701 ÷ 0.750 × 100 = 93.4%.
3. 9 marks: "1.16 g of a compound containing only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen burned completely to give 2.64 g of carbon dioxide and 1.08 g of water. Its relative molecular mass is 58. Find its empirical and molecular formulae." Model answer: C 2.64 ÷ 44 = 0.0600 mol (0.720 g); H 2 × 1.08 ÷ 18 = 0.120 mol (0.120 g); O = 1.16 − 0.720 − 0.120 = 0.320 g = 0.0200 mol; ratio 3 : 6 : 1; C₃H₆O; Mr 58, so molecular formula C₃H₆O.
