# Package: chem10-s4 — Balancing equations

- **loId:** `chem10-s4` · **chapterId:** `chem10` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.7
- **Syllabus:** 1.4.7 "use the concept of a mole to … balance equations for reactions where
  reactants and products are specified". Learn about: "applying the concept of a mole and
  balancing chemical equations is underpinned by the law of conservation of mass". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. What an equation shows, the balancing method,
  worked examples of increasing difficulty, state symbols, ionic equations (assumed), and the
  mole meaning of the coefficients. Calculations from equations are in the chapter "Stoichiometry: Limiting Reactants and Percentage Yield".

## 1. Must cover

What an equation shows (one block)
- [ ] Q1 A chemical equation shows the reactants (left) and products (right) with their formulae; a balanced equation has the same number of each kind of atom on both sides, because atoms are conserved (law of conservation of mass).
- [ ] Q2 State symbols: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) aqueous (dissolved in water).
- [ ] Q3 The coefficients (large numbers in front) give the ratio of moles (or molecules) that react; e.g. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O means 2 mol of hydrogen react with 1 mol of oxygen to give 2 mol of water.

Method (one block, skills; one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 Step 1: Write the correct formula of every reactant and product; never change a formula to balance an equation.
- [ ] M2 Step 2: Count the atoms of each element on each side.
- [ ] M3 Step 3: Balance by putting coefficients in front of formulae, starting with an element that appears in only one substance on each side, and leaving hydrogen and oxygen (or an element on its own) until last.
- [ ] M4 Step 4: Check every element again; make sure the coefficients are the smallest whole numbers; add state symbols.

Worked examples (one block; one bullet per equation)
- [ ] W1 Magnesium burning: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s).
- [ ] W2 Methane burning: CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l).
- [ ] W3 Propane burning: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l).
- [ ] W4 Ethane burning (needs fractions doubled): 2C₂H₆(g) + 7O₂(g) → 4CO₂(g) + 6H₂O(l).
- [ ] W5 Aluminium and hydrochloric acid: 2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g).
- [ ] W6 Neutralisation: 2NaOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l).
- [ ] W7 Decomposition of hydrogen peroxide: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g).
- [ ] W8 Calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid: CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g).
- [ ] W9 Sodium and water: 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g).

Ionic equations (one block, assumed)
- [ ] I1 (assumed) An ionic equation shows only the ions that take part, leaving out spectator ions (ions that are unchanged in solution).
- [ ] I2 (assumed) Example: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq) becomes Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s); Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ are spectator ions.
- [ ] I3 (assumed) Neutralisation of any strong acid by a strong base: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
- [ ] I4 (assumed) An ionic equation must balance in charge as well as atoms.

Mole ratios from equations (one block)
- [ ] R1 The balanced equation gives the mole ratio of any two substances, e.g. in W3, 1 mol of propane needs 5 mol of oxygen and produces 3 mol of carbon dioxide.
- [ ] R2 This ratio is the starting point for calculating masses of reactants and products (taught in the chapter "Stoichiometry: Limiting Reactants and Percentage Yield").

## 2. Checked facts and examples

All equations above are balanced and correct.

## 3. Traps

- Never change subscripts in a formula to balance; change only coefficients.
- Diatomic elements: H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂.
- Don't do mass calculations here.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Balance given equations (combustion, neutralisation, metal–acid, decomposition).
- Write a balanced equation from a word description; write an ionic equation.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "Balance the following equations: (i) C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O, (ii) Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₃, (iii) Ca(OH)₂ + HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O." Model answer: 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O; 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃; Ca(OH)₂ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O.
2. 6 marks: "Explain why a chemical equation must be balanced, and state what the coefficients in 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O tell you." Model answer: Q1, Q3.
