## Part: Solving redox volumetric problems (formerly `chem16-s1`)

- **loId:** `chem16-s1` · **chapterId:** `chem16` · **syllabusRef:** 4.1.7
- **Syllabus:** 4.1.7 "solve volumetric problems". Learn about: "problem solving using varied
  units (see quantifying matter section); volumetric calculations in familiar and **unfamiliar
  contexts** for acid-base and redox titrations". Bold = Higher Level only. This chapter covers
  redox titrations; acid–base problems are in the acid–base volumetric chapters.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. What is different about redox problems: getting
  the mole ratio from the balanced redox equation (or electron count), two-step (indirect)
  titrations through iodine, and the units used for tablets and products. The general method,
  the titration formula and dilution factors are taught in "Solving acid–base volumetric
  problems": recap each in one bullet and refer there. Units are in "Solutions and concentration".
  Full worked problems are in "Worked redox volumetric problems".

### 1. Must cover

The method (one block)
- [ ] M1 Recap: the same steps as in "Acid–base volumetric problems": balanced equation and mole ratio; moles of the known solution (n = c × V, V in litres); mole ratio to moles of the other; divide by its volume; convert units and allow for dilution.
- [ ] M2 The difference: the mole ratio comes from the balanced redox equation, which may need to be balanced first (see "Balancing redox equations and the electrochemical series").
- [ ] M3 The ratio can be checked from electrons: the moles of electrons gained by the oxidising agent equal the moles lost by the reducing agent (e.g. MnO₄⁻ gains 5 e⁻ and Fe²⁺ loses 1 e⁻, so 1 MnO₄⁻ reacts with 5 Fe²⁺).
- [ ] M4 Titration formula for redox: (V_ox × M_ox) ÷ n_ox = (V_red × M_red) ÷ n_red, where n_ox and n_red are the coefficients of the oxidising and reducing agents in the balanced equation; volumes can stay in cm³ because the units cancel (as in "Acid–base volumetric problems").
- [ ] M5 Example of the formula: 23.8 cm³ of KMnO₄ reacts with 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 M Fe²⁺ (n_ox = 1, n_red = 5): M_ox = (25.0 × 0.100) ÷ (5 × 23.8) = 0.0210 mol L⁻¹ (sample data).

Mole ratios to know (one block)
- [ ] R1 MnO₄⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 5 (MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O).
- [ ] R2 I₂ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 2 (I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻).
- [ ] R3 MnO₄⁻ : I₂ = 2 : 5 (2MnO₄⁻ + 10I⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5I₂ + 8H₂O).
- [ ] R4 MnO₄⁻ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 5 when iodine made from permanganate is titrated with thiosulfate (2 : 5 : 10).
- [ ] R5 Ammonium iron(II) sulfate supplies one Fe²⁺ per formula unit: moles (NH₄)₂SO₄·FeSO₄·6H₂O = moles Fe²⁺.
- [ ] R6 Each Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O supplies one S₂O₃²⁻; each I₂ is one mole whether written as I₂ or I₃⁻.

Two-step (indirect) titrations (one block)
- [ ] I1 In an indirect titration, the substance being analysed reacts first with an excess of a reagent to release a product in proportion to it, and that product is titrated; e.g. an oxidising agent added to excess iodide releases iodine, which is then titrated with thiosulfate.
- [ ] I2 Work backwards through each equation in turn: moles of thiosulfate → moles of iodine (÷ 2) → moles of the oxidising agent (using the first equation).
- [ ] I3 The reagent added in excess (e.g. KI) doesn't need to be measured accurately, because it is not part of the calculation; only the analyte and the titrant must be measured exactly.

Scaling and varied units (one block)
- [ ] U1 Scale from the portion titrated to the whole solution (e.g. × 10 for 25.0 cm³ out of 250 cm³), then divide by the number of tablets if several were dissolved.
- [ ] U2 Mass = moles × molar mass; mg = g × 1000; use Ar(Fe) = 56 for iron, M(FeSO₄) = 152 for iron(II) sulfate, M(KMnO₄) = 158, M(Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O) = 248, M of ammonium iron(II) sulfate = 392.
- [ ] U3 Percentage by mass of a tablet = mass of the substance ÷ mass of the tablet × 100; percentage of the label value = measured mass ÷ stated mass × 100.
- [ ] U4 Concentration in g L⁻¹ = mol L⁻¹ × M; %w/v = g L⁻¹ ÷ 10 (see "Solutions and concentration").
- [ ] U5 A concentration in g L⁻¹ of a hydrated salt uses the M of the hydrate, e.g. thiosulfate solution in g L⁻¹ of Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O uses 248.

Good practice (one block)
- [ ] G1 Use the mean of concordant titres; give answers to three significant figures with units.
- [ ] G2 Check the answer is sensible: a tablet usually contains tens of milligrams of iron, not grams; a percentage can't exceed 100%.
- [ ] G3 Redox-specific errors and their direction: taking deep purple as the end point with KMnO₄ (titre too high, systematic); oxidation of Fe²⁺ by air before titration (iron result too low). Random and systematic error are defined in "Measurement, error and data".

Unfamiliar contexts (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] H1 [HL] An unfamiliar redox problem may involve a reagent or product not studied (e.g. hydrogen peroxide, hypochlorite in bleach, vitamin C, sodium oxalate); the question gives or asks for the balanced equation, and the same method applies.
- [ ] H2 [HL] Steps: identify the oxidising and reducing agents; write or balance the equation (using oxidation numbers if needed); list the data with units; plan the route through moles, including any two-step reaction and dilution, before calculating.
- [ ] H3 [HL] Example ratios from unfamiliar reactions: MnO₄⁻ : H₂O₂ = 2 : 5 (2MnO₄⁻ + 5H₂O₂ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5O₂ + 8H₂O); MnO₄⁻ : C₂O₄²⁻ = 2 : 5 (2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O); ClO⁻ : I₂ = 1 : 1 (ClO⁻ + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cl⁻ + I₂ + H₂O); vitamin C : I₂ = 1 : 1 (C₆H₈O₆ + I₂ → C₆H₆O₆ + 2H⁺ + 2I⁻).
- [ ] H4 [HL] Sodium oxalate (sodium ethanedioate, Na₂C₂O₄) is an alternative primary standard for KMnO₄; the titration is done warm (about 60 °C) because the reaction is slow at room temperature.

### 3. Traps

- The mole ratio is rarely 1 : 1 in redox problems: always take it from the balanced equation.
- In iodine–thiosulfate problems, divide moles of thiosulfate by 2 to get moles of iodine.
- Don't forget the dilution or portion factor (× 10), and divide by the number of tablets.
- Use whole-number relative atomic masses (as in the SEC *Formulae and Tables*), e.g. Fe 56, not 55.85.
- Don't redefine the units or repeat the general acid–base method; refer to "Solutions and concentration" and "Acid–base volumetric problems".

### 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Moles of MnO₄⁻ in a titre; moles of Fe²⁺ in a portion and in the whole flask; mass of iron per tablet; percentage of iron by mass.
- Concentration of KMnO₄ from a standardisation against ammonium iron(II) sulfate, in mol L⁻¹ and g L⁻¹.
- Moles of iodine in a portion from its mass; concentration of thiosulfate or of iodine.
- Higher Level: problems in unfamiliar contexts.

### 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "25.0 cm³ of an iron(II) solution needed 18.0 cm³ of 0.0200 M KMnO₄. Write the balanced equation and calculate the concentration of Fe²⁺ in (i) mol L⁻¹, (ii) g L⁻¹ (sample data)." Model answer: R1; moles MnO₄⁻ = 3.60 × 10⁻⁴; Fe²⁺ = 1.80 × 10⁻³; ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0720 mol L⁻¹; × 56 = 4.03 g L⁻¹.
2. 9 marks (Higher Level): "Sodium oxalate is a primary standard for potassium manganate(VII): 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O. 25.0 cm³ of 0.0500 M sodium oxalate, warmed and acidified, needed 24.8 cm³ of KMnO₄. Calculate the concentration of the KMnO₄ (sample data)." Model answer: moles C₂O₄²⁻ = 1.25 × 10⁻³; MnO₄⁻ = 1.25 × 10⁻³ × 2 ÷ 5 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴; ÷ 0.0248 = 0.0202 mol L⁻¹.
3. 6 marks: "Explain why, in an iodine–thiosulfate titration used to find the concentration of an oxidising agent, the potassium iodide added does not need to be measured accurately." Model answer: I1, I3.

### 1. Must cover

Problem 1: standardising potassium manganate(VII) (one block)
- [ ] P1 Data: 9.80 g of ammonium iron(II) sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄·FeSO₄·6H₂O (M = 392), was dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and made up to 250 cm³; 25.0 cm³ portions needed a mean titre of 24.0 cm³ of KMnO₄.
- [ ] P2 Equation: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O (1 : 5).
- [ ] P3 Moles of the salt = 9.80 ÷ 392 = 0.0250 mol in 250 cm³; concentration = 0.0250 ÷ 0.250 = 0.100 mol L⁻¹ of Fe²⁺.
- [ ] P4 Moles Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol; moles MnO₄⁻ = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 5 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol.
- [ ] P5 [KMnO₄] = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0240 = 0.0208 mol L⁻¹; in g L⁻¹: 0.0208 × 158 = 3.29 g L⁻¹.

Problem 2: iron(II) sulfate in tablets compared with the label (one block)
- [ ] Q1 Data: five tablets, each labelled "200 mg iron(II) sulfate", were dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and made up to 250 cm³; 25.0 cm³ portions needed a mean titre of 12.9 cm³ of 0.0100 M KMnO₄.
- [ ] Q2 Moles MnO₄⁻ = 0.0100 × 0.0129 = 1.29 × 10⁻⁴ mol; moles Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 1.29 × 10⁻⁴ = 6.45 × 10⁻⁴ mol.
- [ ] Q3 Moles Fe²⁺ in 250 cm³ = 6.45 × 10⁻³ mol; per tablet = 6.45 × 10⁻³ ÷ 5 = 1.29 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] Q4 Mass of FeSO₄ per tablet = 1.29 × 10⁻³ × 152 = 0.196 g = 196 mg, which is 196 ÷ 200 × 100 = 98.0% of the label value.
- [ ] Q5 Mass of elemental iron per tablet = 1.29 × 10⁻³ × 56 = 0.0722 g = 72.2 mg.

Problem 3: standardising sodium thiosulfate through iodine (one block)
- [ ] R1 Data: 25.0 cm³ of 0.0200 M KMnO₄ was acidified and excess KI added; the iodine liberated needed a mean titre of 25.0 cm³ of sodium thiosulfate solution, using starch near the end point.
- [ ] R2 Equations: 2MnO₄⁻ + 10I⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5I₂ + 8H₂O; I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻.
- [ ] R3 Moles MnO₄⁻ = 0.0200 × 0.0250 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; moles I₂ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5 ÷ 2 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] R4 Moles S₂O₃²⁻ = 2 × 1.25 × 10⁻³ = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol (the same as 5 × moles MnO₄⁻).
- [ ] R5 [S₂O₃²⁻] = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.100 mol L⁻¹; in g L⁻¹ of Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O: 0.100 × 248 = 24.8 g L⁻¹.

Problem 4: hypochlorite in household bleach (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Data: 25.0 cm³ of bleach was diluted to 250 cm³; 25.0 cm³ of the diluted bleach was added to excess KI and dilute sulfuric acid, and the iodine liberated needed a mean titre of 18.4 cm³ of 0.100 M sodium thiosulfate.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Equations: ClO⁻ + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cl⁻ + I₂ + H₂O (ClO⁻ : I₂ = 1 : 1); I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Moles S₂O₃²⁻ = 0.100 × 0.0184 = 1.84 × 10⁻³ mol; moles I₂ = 9.20 × 10⁻⁴ mol = moles ClO⁻ in 25.0 cm³ of diluted bleach.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Diluted concentration = 9.20 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0368 mol L⁻¹; original bleach = 0.0368 × 10 = 0.368 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] S5 [HL] As sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl, M = 74.5): 0.368 × 74.5 = 27.4 g L⁻¹ = 2.74% w/v.

Problem 5: hydrogen peroxide solution (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Data: 10.0 cm³ of a hydrogen peroxide solution was diluted to 250 cm³; 25.0 cm³ portions, acidified with dilute sulfuric acid, needed a mean titre of 20.0 cm³ of 0.0200 M KMnO₄.
- [ ] T2 [HL] Equation: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5H₂O₂ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5O₂ + 8H₂O (MnO₄⁻ : H₂O₂ = 2 : 5); here H₂O₂ is the reducing agent (O −1 → 0).
- [ ] T3 [HL] Moles MnO₄⁻ = 0.0200 × 0.0200 = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; moles H₂O₂ = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5 ÷ 2 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol in 25.0 cm³.
- [ ] T4 [HL] Diluted concentration = 1.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0400 mol L⁻¹; dilution factor = 250 ÷ 10.0 = 25, so the original = 0.0400 × 25 = 1.00 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] T5 [HL] In g L⁻¹: 1.00 × 34 = 34.0 g L⁻¹ = 3.40% w/v.

### 3. Traps

- Problem 2: elemental iron uses 56; iron(II) sulfate uses 152.
- Problem 3: the ratio MnO₄⁻ : S₂O₃²⁻ is 1 : 5, through 2 : 5 : 10.
- Problems 4 and 5: the dilution factors are 10 and 25 (250 ÷ 10.0), not always 10.
- In Problem 5 the permanganate is still the oxidising agent; hydrogen peroxide acts as a reducing agent.

### 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Standardise KMnO₄ against ammonium iron(II) sulfate; find the mass and percentage of iron in tablets; find the concentration of iodine or thiosulfate.
- Higher Level: a redox titration in an unfamiliar context (e.g. hypochlorite in bleach, hydrogen peroxide, vitamin C).

### 5. Practice questions to write

1. 9 marks: "3.81 g of iodine was dissolved in potassium iodide solution and made up to 500 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions needed 15.0 cm³ of sodium thiosulfate solution. Calculate (i) the moles of iodine in each 25.0 cm³ portion, (ii) the concentration of the thiosulfate (I₂ = 254)." Model answer: 3.81 ÷ 254 = 0.0150 mol in 500 cm³; per 25.0 cm³ = 7.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol; S₂O₃²⁻ = 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol; ÷ 0.0150 = 0.100 mol L⁻¹.
2. 12 marks: "7.84 g of ammonium iron(II) sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄·FeSO₄·6H₂O, was dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions needed 20.4 cm³ of KMnO₄. Calculate (i) the concentration of the ammonium iron(II) sulfate solution, (ii) the moles of Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³, (iii) the concentration of the KMnO₄." Model answer: 7.84 ÷ 392 = 0.0200 mol; ÷ 0.250 = 0.0800 mol L⁻¹; Fe²⁺ = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol; MnO₄⁻ = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; ÷ 0.0204 = 0.0196 mol L⁻¹.
3. 12 marks (Higher Level): "A vitamin C tablet was dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions needed 22.8 cm³ of 0.0125 M iodine solution, using starch as indicator: C₆H₈O₆ + I₂ → C₆H₆O₆ + 2H⁺ + 2I⁻. Calculate the mass of vitamin C in the tablet in mg (C₆H₈O₆ = 176)." Model answer: moles I₂ = 0.0125 × 0.0228 = 2.85 × 10⁻⁴ mol = moles vitamin C in 25.0 cm³; × 10 = 2.85 × 10⁻³ mol in the tablet; × 176 = 0.502 g = 502 mg.
