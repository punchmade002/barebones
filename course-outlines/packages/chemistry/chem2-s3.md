## 1. Must cover

Atomic number and mass number (one block)
- [ ] A1 Atomic number (Z): the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom of an element. Each element has its own atomic number, and the periodic table is arranged in order of it.
- [ ] A2 Mass number (A): the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
- [ ] A3 Number of neutrons = A − Z.
- [ ] A4 Nuclear notation: mass number written at the top left and atomic number at the bottom left of the symbol, e.g. ²³₁₁Na has 11 protons, 12 neutrons and (as an atom) 11 electrons.

Isotopes (one block)
- [ ] I1 Isotopes: atoms of the same element (same atomic number, same number of protons) with different mass numbers, because they have different numbers of neutrons.
- [ ] I2 Examples: hydrogen-1 (protium, no neutrons), hydrogen-2 (deuterium, one neutron) and hydrogen-3 (tritium, two neutrons); chlorine-35 (18 neutrons) and chlorine-37 (20 neutrons); carbon-12, carbon-13 and carbon-14.
- [ ] I3 Isotopes of an element have the same chemical properties, because they have the same number of electrons and the same electron arrangement.
- [ ] I4 They have slightly different physical properties that depend on mass, such as density and rate of diffusion.
- [ ] I5 Writing isotopes: by name and mass number (chlorine-35) or in nuclear notation (³⁵₁₇Cl).

Relative atomic mass (one block)
- [ ] R1 Relative atomic mass (Ar): the average mass of the atoms of an element, taking account of the abundance of its isotopes, relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
- [ ] R2 Carbon-12 is the standard: an atom of carbon-12 is defined as having a mass of exactly 12 units.
- [ ] R3 Ar has no units, and is usually not a whole number because it is a weighted average of the isotopes, e.g. chlorine 35.5 (about 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37). The calculation is in "Relative atomic mass from isotopic abundance".
- [ ] R4 The mass number of a single isotope is always a whole number; the relative atomic mass of an element is an average.

## 3. Traps

- Isotopes differ in neutrons, not protons or electrons.
- Don't mention that carbon-14 is radioactive or describe radioactive decay (not on the specification).
- Don't confuse mass number (whole number, one isotope) with relative atomic mass (average for the element).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define atomic number, mass number, isotope and relative atomic mass.
- State the number of each particle in a given isotope.
