# Package: chem8-s3 — Molar volume and Avogadro's law

- **loId:** `chem8-s3` · **chapterId:** `chem8` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.3
- **Syllabus:** 1.4.3 "state Avogadro's law and deduce the molar volume of a gas". Learn about:
  "molar volume of a gas and Avogadro's law; the conditions for standard temperature (K) and
  pressure (STP)". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Use the SEC/Leaving Certificate values: STP is
  273 K and 101.3 kPa (1 atm), molar volume 22.4 L mol⁻¹; room temperature and pressure about
  24 L mol⁻¹. The ideal gas equation itself is taught in "The ideal gas equation"; use it once
  to deduce the molar volume.

## 1. Must cover

Avogadro's law (one block)
- [ ] A1 Avogadro's law: equal volumes of all gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of molecules.
- [ ] A2 So at fixed temperature and pressure, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to the number of moles: V ∝ n.
- [ ] A3 (assumed) Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes: gases react in volumes that are in simple whole-number ratios to one another and to the volumes of any gaseous products, at the same temperature and pressure, e.g. 2 volumes of hydrogen react with 1 volume of oxygen.
- [ ] A4 Avogadro's law explains Gay-Lussac's law: the volume ratio equals the mole ratio in the balanced equation, e.g. N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g): 1 volume + 3 volumes → 2 volumes.

STP and molar volume (one block)
- [ ] S1 Standard temperature and pressure (STP): 0 °C (273 K) and 101.3 kPa (1 atmosphere, 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa).
- [ ] S2 Molar volume: the volume occupied by one mole of any gas at a stated temperature and pressure; at STP it is 22.4 L (22 400 cm³).
- [ ] S3 At room temperature (about 20 °C) and pressure, the molar volume is about 24 L.
- [ ] S4 IUPAC now uses a standard pressure of 100 kPa, at which the molar volume at 273 K is 22.7 L; Leaving Certificate calculations use 22.4 L at STP unless told otherwise.

Deducing the molar volume (one block, skills)
- [ ] D1 From PV = nRT, V = nRT ÷ P.
- [ ] D2 For 1 mol at 273 K and 101 325 Pa, with R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: V = 1 × 8.314 × 273 ÷ 101 325 = 0.0224 m³ = 22.4 L.
- [ ] D3 Experimentally: measure the mass of a known volume of a gas (e.g. oxygen from a cylinder, by weighing the cylinder before and after releasing gas into a gas syringe), find the number of moles from the mass, and scale the volume to one mole, correcting to STP.

Calculations (one block, skills)
- [ ] C1 Moles from gas volume: n = V ÷ 22.4 (V in L at STP), e.g. 5.6 L of CO₂ at STP = 0.25 mol.
- [ ] C2 Volume from moles: V = n × 22.4, e.g. 0.50 mol O₂ occupies 11.2 L at STP.
- [ ] C3 Mass to volume through moles: 8.0 g of methane (M = 16 g mol⁻¹) = 0.50 mol = 11.2 L at STP.
- [ ] C4 Volumes of gases in a reaction: 50 cm³ of methane burns in 100 cm³ of oxygen (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O), giving 50 cm³ of CO₂, measured at the same temperature and pressure.

## 3. Traps

- Molar volume applies to gases only, not liquids or solids.
- Avogadro's law is about numbers of molecules, not atoms or mass.
- Temperature must be in kelvin.
- Leave the full treatment of PV = nRT, its units and its assumptions to "The ideal gas equation" and "Gas relationships and the ideal gas".

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- State Avogadro's law; state the conditions of STP; give the molar volume at STP.
- Calculate the volume of a gas at STP from its mass, or moles from volume.
- Calculate volumes of reacting gases.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "State Avogadro's law and calculate the volume, at STP, occupied by 3.2 g of oxygen gas." Model answer: A1; 3.2 ÷ 32 = 0.10 mol; × 22.4 = 2.24 L.
2. 9 marks: "Show, using the ideal gas equation, that one mole of gas occupies 22.4 L at STP, and calculate the mass of 1.12 L of carbon dioxide at STP." Model answer: D2; 1.12 ÷ 22.4 = 0.050 mol × 44 = 2.2 g.
