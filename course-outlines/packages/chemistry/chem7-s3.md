## 1. Must cover

The three factors (one block)
- [ ] F1 [HL] Effective nuclear charge: the more protons, the stronger the pull on the outer electrons.
- [ ] F2 [HL] Distance: the further the outer electrons are from the nucleus (more energy levels), the weaker the pull.
- [ ] F3 [HL] Screening (shielding): inner electrons shield the outer electrons from the full nuclear charge; electrons in the same level shield each other very little.

Atomic radius (one block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] Atomic (covalent) radius: half the distance between the nuclei of two atoms of the same element joined by a single covalent bond.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Across a period it decreases: nuclear charge increases while electrons enter the same main energy level, with little extra screening, so the outer electrons are pulled closer.
- [ ] R3 [HL] Down a group it increases: each element has an extra energy level, and the extra inner electrons screen the outer electrons, outweighing the increase in nuclear charge.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Noble gases are not given a covalent radius because they don't form bonds.

First ionisation energy (one block)
- [ ] I1 [HL] First ionisation energy: the minimum energy needed to completely remove the most loosely bound electron from a neutral gaseous atom in its ground state: X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻. Unit: kJ mol⁻¹.
- [ ] I2 [HL] Values (kJ mol⁻¹), period 2: Li 520, Be 899, B 801, C 1086, N 1402, O 1314, F 1681, Ne 2081; period 3: Na 496, Mg 738, Al 578, Si 787, P 1012, S 1000, Cl 1251, Ar 1521; group 1: Li 520, Na 496, K 419.
- [ ] I3 [HL] General trend across a period: increases, because nuclear charge increases and atomic radius decreases, so the outer electron is held more tightly. Noble gases have the highest values in each period.
- [ ] I4 [HL] Down a group: decreases, because the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more screened, despite the larger nuclear charge.
- [ ] I5 [HL] Group 1 metals have the lowest values in each period, which is why they lose electrons easily and are so reactive.

Exceptions to the trend across a period (one block)
- [ ] X1 [HL] Beryllium is higher than boron (899 vs 801): beryllium's 2s sublevel is full (1s² 2s²), a stable arrangement; boron's outer electron is alone in a 2p orbital (1s² 2s² 2p¹), slightly higher in energy and more screened, so it is removed more easily.
- [ ] X2 [HL] Nitrogen is higher than oxygen (1402 vs 1314): nitrogen's 2p sublevel is half full with one electron in each orbital (2p³), a stable arrangement; in oxygen (2p⁴) one 2p orbital holds a pair, and repulsion between the paired electrons makes one easier to remove.
- [ ] X3 [HL] The same pattern occurs in period 3: magnesium higher than aluminium; phosphorus higher than sulfur.
- [ ] X4 [HL] Diagram: a graph of first ionisation energy against atomic number for elements 1–20, showing the peaks at the noble gases, the troughs at the alkali metals and the small dips after Be, N, Mg and P.

Successive ionisation energies (one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Second ionisation energy: the energy to remove an electron from a 1+ ion: X⁺(g) → X²⁺(g) + e⁻; and so on for the third and later.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Successive ionisation energies always increase, because each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion, with the same nuclear charge holding fewer electrons more tightly.
- [ ] S3 [HL] A very large jump occurs when an electron is removed from a new, inner energy level, closer to the nucleus and less screened.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Example: magnesium 738, 1451, 7733 kJ mol⁻¹: the big jump between the second and third shows two outer electrons, so magnesium is in group 2; sodium 496 then 4562: one outer electron, group 1.
- [ ] S5 [HL] The pattern of jumps is evidence for main energy levels: plotting the log of successive ionisation energies against electron number for an element such as potassium shows groups of 2, 8, 8, 1 electrons.

## 3. Traps

- Ionisation energy refers to gaseous atoms; always include (g).
- The second ionisation energy is from the 1+ ion, not the second electron of the neutral atom.
- The exceptions are about sublevel stability and electron pairing; don't explain them with "full outer shell".

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define first ionisation energy; explain the trend across a period and down a group.
- Explain why the first ionisation energy of nitrogen is greater than that of oxygen (or Be > B, Mg > Al).
- Explain the trend in atomic radius.
- Deduce the group of an element from its successive ionisation energies.
