# Package: chem10-s1 — Physical and chemical change

- **loId:** `chem10-s1` · **chapterId:** `chem10` · **syllabusRef:** 1.1.4
- **Syllabus:** 1.1.4 "distinguish between physical change and chemical change of matter".
  Learn about: "chemical and physical properties; chemical and physical change". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30.

## 1. Must cover

Properties (one block)
- [ ] P1 A physical property can be observed or measured without changing the substance into a new substance: colour, state, density, melting and boiling points, solubility, electrical conductivity, hardness.
- [ ] P2 A chemical property describes how a substance reacts to form new substances: flammability, reactivity with water, oxygen or acids, whether it is acidic or basic, how it decomposes on heating.

Physical change (one block)
- [ ] F1 A physical change is one in which no new substance is formed; the particles are not changed, only their arrangement or spacing.
- [ ] F2 It is usually easily reversed, and involves only a small energy change.
- [ ] F3 Examples: melting ice, boiling water, dissolving sugar or salt in water, sublimation of iodine, crushing a solid, magnetising iron.
- [ ] F4 The mass is unchanged.

Chemical change (one block)
- [ ] C1 A chemical change (chemical reaction) is one in which one or more new substances are formed, with different properties; bonds are broken and new bonds are made and the atoms are rearranged.
- [ ] C2 It is usually difficult to reverse, and usually involves a noticeable energy change (heat or light given out or taken in).
- [ ] C3 Signs of a chemical change: a gas is given off (effervescence); a precipitate forms; a permanent colour change; a temperature change; light is produced; a smell.
- [ ] C4 Examples: burning magnesium (forms white magnesium oxide), rusting of iron, cooking an egg, magnesium fizzing in hydrochloric acid, decomposing copper(II) carbonate by heating (green to black), and neutralisation.
- [ ] C5 Total mass is conserved in a chemical change too, if nothing escapes (see "Investigation: conservation of mass").

Comparing (one block)
- [ ] K1 Paired points: no new substance vs new substance; easily reversed vs not easily reversed; small energy change vs usually large energy change; particles unchanged vs atoms rearranged into new particles.
- [ ] K2 Borderline case: dissolving salt in water is a physical change (the salt is recovered unchanged by evaporating the water), even though the ions are separated.

## 2. Checked facts and examples

Only the examples above. Copper(II) carbonate (green) decomposes to copper(II) oxide (black) and carbon dioxide on heating.

## 3. Traps

- A change of state is always physical.
- Colour change alone isn't proof of a chemical change (e.g. dissolving copper sulfate gives a blue solution).

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Classify given changes as physical or chemical, with reasons.
- Give signs that a chemical reaction has occurred.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "Distinguish between a physical change and a chemical change, giving one example of each." Model answer: F1, C1, with examples.
2. 6 marks: "Classify each change as physical or chemical, giving a reason: (i) iodine subliming, (ii) magnesium burning, (iii) sugar dissolving in tea." Model answer: physical (no new substance, reversible); chemical (new substance MgO, light and heat); physical (sugar recovered by evaporation).
