## 1. Must cover

Polar bonds and polar molecules (one block)
- [ ] P1 [HL] A polar bond has an uneven share of electrons (δ+ and δ− ends); a polar molecule has an overall uneven distribution of charge, with a δ+ end and a δ− end.
- [ ] P2 [HL] Whether a molecule is polar depends on both its bonds and its shape: polar bonds arranged symmetrically cancel out.

Symmetry and cancelling dipoles (one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Carbon dioxide: each C=O bond is polar, but the molecule is linear (O=C=O), so the two bond polarities point in opposite directions and cancel; CO₂ is non-polar.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Tetrachloromethane (CCl₄): four polar C–Cl bonds arranged tetrahedrally cancel; non-polar.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Boron trifluoride (BF₃): three polar B–F bonds at 120° in a trigonal plane cancel; non-polar.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Water: two polar O–H bonds in a V-shaped (bent) molecule don't cancel; water is polar with δ− on oxygen.
- [ ] S5 [HL] Ammonia: three polar N–H bonds in a pyramidal molecule don't cancel; polar.
- [ ] S6 [HL] Trichloromethane (CHCl₃): the C–H bond differs from the three C–Cl bonds, so the tetrahedral molecule is unsymmetrical and polar.
- [ ] S7 [HL] Diagram: CO₂, CCl₄, H₂O and NH₃ with arrows (or δ+/δ− labels) on each bond showing whether the polarities cancel.

Effect on intermolecular forces (one block)
- [ ] F1 [HL] Polar molecules attract each other by permanent dipole–dipole forces (or hydrogen bonds) as well as London forces, so they have stronger intermolecular forces than non-polar molecules of similar size.
- [ ] F2 [HL] Non-polar molecules, including symmetrical ones with polar bonds, have only London dispersion forces between them, e.g. CO₂ is a gas at room temperature; CCl₄ is immiscible with water.
- [ ] F3 [HL] So a symmetrical molecule usually has a lower boiling point and lower solubility in water than an unsymmetrical one of similar size (e.g. CCl₄ is immiscible with water while CHCl₃ is slightly soluble; both dissolve in non-polar solvents).

Only the molecules above.

## 3. Traps

- Polar bonds do not always make a polar molecule.
- No dipole moment values or units.
- Shapes are taught in "VSEPR theory"; state them without re-deriving.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Explain why carbon dioxide is non-polar although its bonds are polar.
- Explain why water is polar but tetrachloromethane is not.
