## 1. Must cover

Problem 1: standardising hydrochloric acid (one block)
- [ ] P1 Data: 25.0 cm³ of 0.0500 M sodium carbonate needed a mean titre of 24.2 cm³ of hydrochloric acid, using methyl orange.
- [ ] P2 Equation: Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂.
- [ ] P3 Moles Na₂CO₃ = 0.0500 × 0.0250 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] P4 Moles HCl = 2 × 1.25 × 10⁻³ = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] P5 [HCl] = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0242 = 0.103 mol L⁻¹.

Problem 2: ammonia in a household cleaner (one block)
- [ ] Q1 Data: 25.0 cm³ of a cleaner was diluted to 250 cm³; 25.0 cm³ of the diluted solution needed 21.5 cm³ of 0.100 M hydrochloric acid, using methyl orange.
- [ ] Q2 Equation: NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl (1 : 1).
- [ ] Q3 Moles HCl = 0.100 × 0.0215 = 2.15 × 10⁻³ mol = moles of NH₃ in 25.0 cm³ of diluted cleaner.
- [ ] Q4 Diluted concentration = 2.15 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0860 mol L⁻¹; original = 0.0860 × 10 = 0.860 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] Q5 In g L⁻¹: 0.860 × 17 = 14.6 g L⁻¹ of ammonia.

Problem 3: water of crystallisation in washing soda (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] Data: 3.58 g of washing soda crystals, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O, were dissolved and made up to 250 cm³; 25.0 cm³ portions needed a mean titre of 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 M hydrochloric acid.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Moles HCl = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 2.50 × 10⁻³; moles Na₂CO₃ in 25.0 cm³ = 1.25 × 10⁻³; in 250 cm³ = 1.25 × 10⁻².
- [ ] R3 [HL] Mass of Na₂CO₃ = 1.25 × 10⁻² × 106 = 1.325 g; mass of water = 3.58 − 1.325 = 2.255 g.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Moles of water = 2.255 ÷ 18 = 0.125 mol; ratio water : Na₂CO₃ = 0.125 ÷ 0.0125 = 10, so x = 10 (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O).

Problem 4: a back titration of an antacid tablet (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Data: an antacid tablet containing calcium carbonate was added to 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 M hydrochloric acid (an excess); when it had reacted, the remaining acid needed 30.0 cm³ of 0.500 M sodium hydroxide.
- [ ] S2 [HL] Moles HCl added = 1.00 × 0.0500 = 0.0500 mol.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Moles HCl in excess = moles NaOH = 0.500 × 0.0300 = 0.0150 mol.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Moles HCl that reacted with the tablet = 0.0500 − 0.0150 = 0.0350 mol.
- [ ] S5 [HL] CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂, so moles CaCO₃ = 0.0350 ÷ 2 = 0.0175 mol; mass = 0.0175 × 100 = 1.75 g of calcium carbonate per tablet.
- [ ] S6 [HL] A back titration is needed because calcium carbonate is insoluble and reacts too slowly to titrate directly.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks: "2.65 g of anhydrous sodium carbonate was dissolved and made up to 500 cm³. 25.0 cm³ of this solution needed 22.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid. Calculate (i) the concentration of the sodium carbonate solution, (ii) the concentration of the hydrochloric acid." Model answer: 2.65 ÷ 106 = 0.0250 mol in 0.500 L = 0.0500 M; moles in 25.0 cm³ = 1.25 × 10⁻³; HCl = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0220 = 0.114 M.
2. 12 marks: "An antacid tablet containing magnesium hydroxide was dissolved in 40.0 cm³ of 0.500 M hydrochloric acid (an excess). The excess acid needed 12.0 cm³ of 0.500 M sodium hydroxide. Calculate the mass of magnesium hydroxide in the tablet (Mg(OH)₂ = 58)." Model answer: HCl added 0.0200 mol; excess 6.00 × 10⁻³ mol; reacted 0.0140 mol; Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H₂O; 7.00 × 10⁻³ mol × 58 = 0.406 g.
