## Part: Redox titrations (formerly `chem15-s1`)

### 1. Must cover

Redox titrations (one block)
- [ ] V1 A redox titration finds the concentration of an oxidising or reducing agent by titrating it against a reducing or oxidising agent of known concentration; the mole ratio comes from the balanced redox equation.
- [ ] V2 The apparatus and technique are the same as in "Standard solutions and acid–base titrations" (pipette, burette, conical flask, white tile, rough then accurate titrations agreeing within 0.1 cm³).
- [ ] V3 The end point is shown either by the colour of a reagent itself (self-indicating, as with potassium manganate(VII)) or by an indicator (starch in iodine–thiosulfate titrations).
- [ ] V4 The two titrations: potassium manganate(VII) with iron(II), and iodine with sodium thiosulfate.

Potassium manganate(VII) (one block)
- [ ] K1 Potassium manganate(VII), KMnO₄ (potassium permanganate), is a dark purple solid; its solution is an intense purple and is a strong oxidising agent, usually used at about 0.01–0.02 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] K2 It is not a primary standard: it can't be obtained pure, and its solution slowly decomposes (in light, and with traces of organic matter, depositing brown MnO₂), so its concentration changes; it is standardised against ammonium iron(II) sulfate shortly before use.
- [ ] K3 In acidic solution it is reduced to the almost colourless manganese(II) ion: MnO₄⁻(aq) + 8H⁺(aq) + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺(aq) + 4H₂O(l) (purple → colourless; Mn +7 → +2).
- [ ] K4 Iron(II) is oxidised: Fe²⁺(aq) → Fe³⁺(aq) + e⁻. Overall: MnO₄⁻(aq) + 5Fe²⁺(aq) + 8H⁺(aq) → Mn²⁺(aq) + 5Fe³⁺(aq) + 4H₂O(l); mole ratio MnO₄⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 5.
- [ ] K5 Self-indicating: the KMnO₄ goes in the burette; each drop is decolourised while Fe²⁺ remains in the flask; the end point is the first permanent pale pink colour, from the first drop of excess MnO₄⁻. No indicator is needed.
- [ ] K6 Because the solution is so deeply coloured, the bottom of the meniscus can't be seen: read the top of the meniscus in the burette (consistently for both readings).
- [ ] K7 Excess dilute sulfuric acid is added to the flask to supply the H⁺ ions the reduction needs. If there is too little acid, MnO₄⁻ is reduced only to manganese(IV) oxide and a brown precipitate forms: MnO₄⁻ + 2H₂O + 3e⁻ → MnO₂(s) + 4OH⁻ (Mn +7 → +4). The ratio is then no longer 1 : 5 and the end point can't be seen.
- [ ] K8 Hydrochloric acid can't be used to acidify, because MnO₄⁻ oxidises Cl⁻ to chlorine (using up extra permanganate); nitric acid can't be used because it is itself an oxidising agent and would oxidise some of the Fe²⁺.
- [ ] K9 The first few drops of KMnO₄ are decolourised slowly; later drops are decolourised almost at once. The Mn²⁺ ions produced catalyse the reaction (autocatalysis: a product of the reaction is its catalyst).
- [ ] K10 Rinse the burette thoroughly after use: permanganate leaves brown stains of MnO₂ on glass.

Ammonium iron(II) sulfate, the primary standard (one block)
- [ ] F1 Ammonium iron(II) sulfate hexahydrate, (NH₄)₂SO₄·FeSO₄·6H₂O, also written (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂·6H₂O; M = 392 g mol⁻¹; pale green crystals.
- [ ] F2 It is a double salt: two simple salts (ammonium sulfate and iron(II) sulfate) crystallised together in a fixed ratio in one crystal.
- [ ] F3 It is a suitable primary standard: obtainable very pure; stable in air (not oxidised by air in the solid, doesn't absorb water and doesn't lose its water of crystallisation); soluble; high molar mass, so weighing errors are a small percentage.
- [ ] F4 Iron(II) sulfate itself is not used: its crystals are oxidised by air to iron(III) and their water content varies, so a weighed mass doesn't correspond to a known amount of Fe²⁺.
- [ ] F5 Its standard solution is made up as in "Solutions and concentration", but the crystals are dissolved in deionised water with some dilute sulfuric acid added: the acid stops the Fe²⁺ being oxidised to Fe³⁺ by dissolved oxygen.

Standardising potassium manganate(VII) (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] S1 Pipette 25.0 cm³ of the standard ammonium iron(II) sulfate solution into a conical flask.
- [ ] S2 Add about 20 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid (an excess) with a graduated cylinder; its volume doesn't need to be exact.
- [ ] S3 Titrate with the KMnO₄ solution from the burette, swirling, until one drop gives a permanent pale pink colour; repeat until two titres agree within 0.1 cm³.
- [ ] S4 Sample data: 25.0 cm³ of 0.0500 M ammonium iron(II) sulfate needs a mean titre of 24.4 cm³ of KMnO₄.
- [ ] S5 Moles Fe²⁺ = 0.0500 × 0.0250 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol; moles MnO₄⁻ = 1.25 × 10⁻³ ÷ 5 = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol.
- [ ] S6 [KMnO₄] = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0244 = 0.0102 mol L⁻¹.

Iodine and sodium thiosulfate (one block)
- [ ] T1 Sodium thiosulfate, Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O (M = 248 g mol⁻¹), is a colourless crystalline solid and a reducing agent. "Thio" means sulfur: the thiosulfate ion, S₂O₃²⁻, is like a sulfate ion with one oxygen replaced by sulfur.
- [ ] T2 It is not a primary standard: the crystals are efflorescent (they lose water of crystallisation to the air), so their exact formula and mass of thiosulfate are uncertain; its solution is standardised against iodine made from a standard oxidising agent.
- [ ] T3 Iodine is not a primary standard either: it sublimes slowly at room temperature (so it can't be weighed accurately) and it is almost insoluble in water.
- [ ] T4 Iodine is brought into solution with potassium iodide: I₂ + I⁻ ⇌ I₃⁻. The tri-iodide ion is charged, so it dissolves in water; the solution behaves as a solution of iodine and is red-brown.
- [ ] T5 Titration reaction: I₂(aq) + 2S₂O₃²⁻(aq) → 2I⁻(aq) + S₄O₆²⁻(aq); mole ratio I₂ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 2. Iodine is the oxidising agent; thiosulfate is the reducing agent and is oxidised to the tetrathionate ion.
- [ ] T6 The iodine solution is pipetted into the conical flask and the thiosulfate is in the burette.
- [ ] T7 Colour sequence: as thiosulfate is added, the red-brown iodine fades to yellow and then pale yellow (straw-coloured). At pale yellow, add a few drops of freshly made starch solution: it turns blue-black. Add thiosulfate drop by drop until the blue-black colour just disappears, leaving a colourless solution: the end point.
- [ ] T8 Starch is added only near the end point: with a lot of iodine present, iodine binds strongly to the starch and is released only slowly, so the colour change would be gradual and the end point inaccurate. The pale yellow colour also tells you the end point is close.
- [ ] T9 Use deionised water throughout, not tap water: tap water contains dissolved chlorine from water treatment, which oxidises iodide to extra iodine (Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂).
- [ ] T10 Uses of thiosulfate: removing excess chlorine from water (reducing it to harmless chloride ions); as the "fixer" in film photography.

Standardising sodium thiosulfate (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] U1 Pipette 25.0 cm³ of standardised KMnO₄ solution into a conical flask and add about 20 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid.
- [ ] U2 Add an excess of potassium iodide (solution or solid): the purple colour disappears and red-brown iodine forms: 2MnO₄⁻ + 10I⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5I₂ + 8H₂O.
- [ ] U3 The KI is in excess for two reasons: so that all the MnO₄⁻ reacts, producing a known amount of iodine; and so that the iodine stays in solution as I₃⁻.
- [ ] U4 Titrate the liberated iodine with the thiosulfate solution as in T7.
- [ ] U5 Combined ratio: 2MnO₄⁻ → 5I₂ → 10S₂O₃²⁻, so MnO₄⁻ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 1 : 5.
- [ ] U6 Sample data: 25.0 cm³ of 0.0200 M KMnO₄ liberates iodine that needs a mean titre of 25.3 cm³ of thiosulfate.
- [ ] U7 Moles MnO₄⁻ = 0.0200 × 0.0250 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; moles I₂ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5 ÷ 2 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol; moles S₂O₃²⁻ = 2 × 1.25 × 10⁻³ = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] U8 [S₂O₃²⁻] = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0253 = 0.0988 mol L⁻¹.

Terms about water and crystals (one block)
- [ ] W1 Efflorescent: a hydrated crystal that loses some of its water of crystallisation to the air on standing, e.g. sodium thiosulfate pentahydrate, washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O).
- [ ] W2 Hygroscopic: absorbs water vapour from the air without dissolving, e.g. concentrated sulfuric acid, silica gel.
- [ ] W3 Deliquescent: absorbs so much water from the air that it dissolves in it and forms a solution, e.g. sodium hydroxide pellets.
- [ ] W4 None of these can be a primary standard, because the mass weighed doesn't correspond to a known amount of substance.

Safety (one block)
- [ ] H1 Wear goggles; KMnO₄ is an oxidising agent, harmful, and stains skin and clothes; dilute sulfuric acid is an irritant; iodine solution stains and is harmful; use a pipette filler.

### 3. Traps

- KMnO₄ is self-indicating: don't add an indicator; the end point is permanent pale pink, not deep purple.
- Read the top of the meniscus for KMnO₄.
- Acidify with dilute sulfuric acid only (not HCl or HNO₃); a brown precipitate means too little acid.
- Add starch near the end point (pale yellow), not at the start; the end point is blue-black to colourless.
- In the standardisation of thiosulfate, the ratio is 1 MnO₄⁻ : 5 S₂O₃²⁻ (via 2 : 5 : 10), not 1 : 2.
- pH curves are not used for redox titrations.
- Dissolved-oxygen (Winkler) and hypochlorite-in-bleach experiments were set on the previous syllabus; the Winkler method is off the specification. Bleach appears only as an unfamiliar-context problem in "Redox volumetric problems".

## Part: Investigation: iron in iron tablets (formerly `chem15-s2`)

- **loId:** `chem15-s2` · **chapterId:** `chem15` · **syllabusRef:** 4.1.6
- **Syllabus:** 4.1.6 "investigate, using primary data, the percentage of elemental iron in iron
  tablets" (EI: Experimental Investigation). Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Full investigation: making a solution from
  several tablets, titration with standardised potassium manganate(VII), and a full sample
  calculation to mass and percentage of iron per tablet. The chemistry of the titration (half-
  equations, why sulfuric acid, self-indicating end point, too little acid) is in "Redox
  titrations"; the rinsing and titration technique is in "Standard solutions and acid–base titrations"; making up a
  solution in a volumetric flask is in "Solutions and concentration". Recap each in a bullet and
  refer there. Why iron tablets are taken is in "Oxidation and reduction".

### 1. Must cover

Aim and chemistry (one block)
- [ ] A1 Aim: to find the mass of iron in an iron tablet, and the percentage of elemental iron by mass in the tablet, by titration with a standard solution of potassium manganate(VII).
- [ ] A2 Iron tablets contain iron as iron(II) (e.g. iron(II) sulfate), prescribed to treat iron-deficiency anaemia; the label usually gives the mass of the iron compound or of iron per tablet.
- [ ] A3 Equation: MnO₄⁻(aq) + 5Fe²⁺(aq) + 8H⁺(aq) → Mn²⁺(aq) + 5Fe³⁺(aq) + 4H₂O(l); mole ratio MnO₄⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 5.
- [ ] A4 The KMnO₄ solution is first standardised against ammonium iron(II) sulfate, just before use.
- [ ] A5 Hypothesis (example): the tablet contains the mass of iron stated on the label, within a few percent.

Making the tablet solution (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 Weigh five tablets together accurately and record the mass (to find the mean mass of one tablet).
- [ ] M2 Grind the tablets to a fine powder with a mortar and pestle, adding a little dilute sulfuric acid.
- [ ] M3 Transfer the paste to a beaker with dilute sulfuric acid and deionised water, rinsing the mortar and pestle into the beaker, and stir to dissolve.
- [ ] M4 Transfer the solution (including any insoluble material) through a funnel into a 250 cm³ volumetric flask, rinsing the beaker, rod and funnel into the flask with deionised water.
- [ ] M5 Make up to the mark with deionised water (bottom of the meniscus on the mark at eye level, last drops with a dropper), stopper and invert about 20 times.

Titration (one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] T1 Rinse the pipette with deionised water and then with the tablet solution; pipette 25.0 cm³ into a conical flask.
- [ ] T2 Add about 20 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid (an excess) to the flask.
- [ ] T3 Rinse the burette with deionised water and then with the standard KMnO₄ solution; fill it and read the top of the meniscus.
- [ ] T4 Titrate, swirling, until one drop gives a permanent pale pink colour; no indicator is added.
- [ ] T5 Do a rough titration, then accurate titrations until two agree within 0.1 cm³; titrate soon after making the solution.

Why each choice (one block)
- [ ] W1 Dilute sulfuric acid is used to dissolve the tablets to stop Fe²⁺ being oxidised to Fe³⁺ by dissolved oxygen, which would make the result too low (Fe³⁺ doesn't react with MnO₄⁻).
- [ ] W2 More dilute sulfuric acid is added before each titration to supply the H⁺ ions needed for MnO₄⁻ to be reduced to Mn²⁺; with too little acid, brown MnO₂ forms and the 1 : 5 ratio no longer holds.
- [ ] W3 Several tablets are used: the result is an average over tablets that vary slightly in mass and iron content; a larger mass gives a smaller percentage error in weighing; and the solution is concentrated enough to give a reasonable titre.
- [ ] W4 Grinding increases the surface area so the tablets dissolve fully and all the iron(II) is extracted.
- [ ] W5 The solution may be cloudy from the insoluble coating and binder (filler); this doesn't affect the result, because those substances don't react with the permanganate.
- [ ] W6 A volumetric flask and pipette are used because the 25.0 cm³ portion must be exactly one-tenth of the total.
- [ ] W7 No indicator is needed because KMnO₄ is self-indicating: the first excess drop colours the solution pink.

Sample calculation (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] C1 Data: five tablets have a total mass of 1.80 g (mean 0.360 g per tablet); 25.0 cm³ portions of the 250 cm³ tablet solution need a mean titre of 11.6 cm³ of 0.0100 M KMnO₄.
- [ ] C2 Moles MnO₄⁻ = 0.0100 × 0.0116 = 1.16 × 10⁻⁴ mol.
- [ ] C3 Moles Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 1.16 × 10⁻⁴ = 5.80 × 10⁻⁴ mol.
- [ ] C4 Moles Fe²⁺ in 250 cm³ (five tablets) = 5.80 × 10⁻⁴ × 10 = 5.80 × 10⁻³ mol; per tablet = 5.80 × 10⁻³ ÷ 5 = 1.16 × 10⁻³ mol.
- [ ] C5 Mass of iron per tablet = 1.16 × 10⁻³ × 56 = 0.06496 g = 65.0 mg.
- [ ] C6 Percentage of iron by mass = 0.06496 ÷ 0.360 × 100 = 18.0%.
- [ ] C7 If the label gives the iron(II) sulfate content instead: mass of FeSO₄ per tablet = 1.16 × 10⁻³ × 152 = 0.176 g = 176 mg; iron is 56 ÷ 152 = 36.8% of the mass of FeSO₄.
- [ ] C8 Conclusion: compare the result with the label value (percentage difference) and comment on whether the hypothesis is supported.

Safety and sources of error (one block)
- [ ] S1 Safety: wear goggles; dilute sulfuric acid is an irritant; KMnO₄ is an oxidising agent and stains skin and clothes; iron tablets are dangerous if swallowed by young children, so keep them secure and don't eat them in the laboratory.
- [ ] S2 Errors that make the result too low: Fe²⁺ oxidised to Fe³⁺ by air before titration (solution not acidified, or left standing too long); loss of tablet material while grinding or transferring.
- [ ] S3 Errors that make the result too high: overshooting the end point (taking a deep purple instead of the first pale pink); tablets that also contain vitamin C or another reducing agent, which also reacts with MnO₄⁻.
- [ ] S4 Other errors: too little acid (brown MnO₂, unclear end point); KMnO₄ that has partly decomposed since it was standardised; burette or pipette not rinsed with its own solution (see "Standard solutions and acid–base titrations").
- [ ] S5 Diagram: mortar and pestle; a 250 cm³ volumetric flask with funnel; a burette of purple KMnO₄ above a conical flask of the tablet solution on a white tile.

### 3. Traps

- Multiply by 5 for the mole ratio and by 10 for 25.0 cm³ out of 250 cm³; then divide by the number of tablets.
- Use Ar(Fe) = 56 for elemental iron; use M(FeSO₄) = 152 only if the question asks for iron(II) sulfate.
- "Percentage of elemental iron" is mass of iron ÷ mass of tablet × 100.
- No indicator; read the top of the meniscus.
- Don't repeat the full rinsing method or the permanganate half-equations here; they are in "Standard solutions and acid–base titrations" and "Redox titrations".

- Calculate the moles of Fe²⁺, the mass of iron per tablet and the percentage of iron by mass.

### 5. Practice questions to write

2. 12 marks: "Four iron tablets with a total mass of 1.52 g were dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions needed a mean titre of 14.2 cm³ of 0.0100 M KMnO₄.
