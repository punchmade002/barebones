## 1. Must cover

Arrangements in main energy levels (one block; Ordinary and Higher)
- [ ] M1 Electrons fill the lowest available energy levels first; for elements 1–20 the levels hold up to 2, 8, 8 (then the fourth level starts at potassium).
- [ ] M2 Examples: H 1; He 2; C 2,4; O 2,6; Na 2,8,1; Mg 2,8,2; Cl 2,8,7; Ar 2,8,8; K 2,8,8,1; Ca 2,8,8,2.
- [ ] M3 Diagram: a Bohr-style shell diagram for sodium (nucleus labelled 11p, 12n; shells with 2, 8, 1 electrons).
- [ ] M4 The outer-shell (valence) electrons decide chemical properties; elements in the same group have the same number of outer electrons.

Ions and stable configurations (one block; Ordinary and Higher)
- [ ] I1 A stable electronic configuration is a full outer energy level, as in the noble gases (He 2; Ne 2,8; Ar 2,8,8); usually eight outer electrons (an octet), or two for the first level.
- [ ] I2 Metals in groups 1, 2 and 13 lose electrons to form positive ions with a noble-gas configuration: Na⁺ 2,8; Mg²⁺ 2,8; Al³⁺ 2,8; K⁺ 2,8,8; Ca²⁺ 2,8,8.
- [ ] I3 Non-metals in groups 15, 16 and 17 gain electrons to form negative ions with a noble-gas configuration: N³⁻ 2,8; O²⁻ 2,8; F⁻ 2,8; S²⁻ 2,8,8; Cl⁻ 2,8,8.
- [ ] I4 Ions with the same electron arrangement as each other (e.g. Na⁺, Mg²⁺, F⁻, O²⁻ and Ne all 2,8) are isoelectronic.

Sublevels and orbitals (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] S1 [HL] A sublevel is a subdivision of a main energy level, made of orbitals of equal energy: s, p, d (and f).
- [ ] S2 [HL] An s sublevel has 1 orbital (holds 2 electrons); p has 3 orbitals (6 electrons); d has 5 orbitals (10 electrons).
- [ ] S3 [HL] Level 1 has only 1s; level 2 has 2s and 2p; level 3 has 3s, 3p and 3d; level 4 has 4s, 4p, 4d and 4f.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Order of filling (increasing energy): 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p. The 4s sublevel is lower in energy than 3d, so it fills first.

Filling rules (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] R1 [HL] Aufbau principle: electrons occupy the lowest-energy orbitals available first.
- [ ] R2 [HL] Pauli exclusion principle: an orbital holds at most two electrons, and they must have opposite spins (shown as ↑↓).
- [ ] R3 [HL] Hund's rule: when filling orbitals of equal energy (e.g. the three 2p orbitals), electrons occupy them singly with parallel spins before any pairing occurs.
- [ ] R4 [HL] Orbital (box) diagrams: one box per orbital, arrows for electrons; e.g. nitrogen 1s ↑↓, 2s ↑↓, 2p ↑ ↑ ↑ (three unpaired electrons); oxygen 2p ↑↓ ↑ ↑.

Writing configurations to element 36 (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] W1 [HL] Notation: e.g. sodium 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹; chlorine 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵; calcium 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s².
- [ ] W2 [HL] Beyond calcium the 3d sublevel fills: e.g. iron 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s² (written in level order, though 4s fills before 3d); bromine 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁵; krypton ends 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁶.
- [ ] W3 [HL] Noble-gas shorthand: e.g. potassium [Ar] 4s¹; iron [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s².
- [ ] W4 [HL] Two exceptions: chromium is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁵ 4s¹ and copper is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s¹, because a half-full or full d sublevel is especially stable.
- [ ] W5 [HL] Ions (excluding transition metal ions): remove or add electrons from the outermost sublevel, e.g. Na⁺ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶; Cl⁻ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶; Br⁻ ends 4s² 4p⁶; Ca²⁺ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶.
- [ ] W6 [HL] Stable configurations at this level: full sublevels (s², p⁶) and the noble-gas configuration ns² np⁶.

Shapes of s and p orbitals (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] H1 [HL] An s orbital is spherical, centred on the nucleus; the 2s orbital is larger than the 1s.
- [ ] H2 [HL] A p orbital is dumbbell-shaped (two lobes either side of the nucleus).
- [ ] H3 [HL] The three p orbitals in a sublevel, px, py and pz, are at right angles to each other along the x, y and z axes, and have equal energy.
- [ ] H4 [HL] Diagram: a sphere labelled s; three dumbbells on x, y and z axes labelled px, py and pz.

## 2. Checked facts and examples

All configurations above are standard; the chromium and copper exceptions are correct as given. Element 36 is krypton.

## 3. Traps

- Transition metal ions (e.g. Fe²⁺, Cu²⁺) are excluded by the specification: don't write their configurations.
- Ordinary level uses main energy levels only, for elements 1–20; keep s, p, d notation in the Higher Level blocks.
- 4s fills before 3d; don't write the potassium configuration as 2,8,9.
- Don't introduce f-orbital shapes or d-orbital shapes.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Write the s, p, d configuration of an element (often a first-row d-block element such as vanadium, chromium or iron) or of an ion.
- How many unpaired electrons / how many p orbitals are occupied in a given atom.
- State Hund's rule, the Pauli principle; draw the shapes of s and p orbitals.
- Name an ion isoelectronic with a given atom.
