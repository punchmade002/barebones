# Package: chem7-s2 — Trends and relationships

- **loId:** `chem7-s2` · **chapterId:** `chem7` · **syllabusRef:** 1.3.4
- **Syllabus:** 1.3.4 "examine trends and relationships in the periodic table". Learn about:
  trends related to "atomic number, relative atomic mass **and atomic (covalent) radius**;
  electronic structure; electronegativity (using Pauling scale); chemical reactivity
  (including the octet rule); physical properties; **first ionisation energy and successive
  ionisation energies** (limited to the first 20 **(36)** elements)". Bold = Higher Level only.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. A survey of the trends at Ordinary level, each in

## 1. Must cover

Atomic number and relative atomic mass (one block)
- [ ] Z1 Atomic number increases by one from each element to the next.
- [ ] Z2 Relative atomic mass generally increases with atomic number, but there are a few exceptions where it falls (e.g. argon 39.9 before potassium 39.1; tellurium 127.6 before iodine 126.9), because of the mix of isotopes.

Electronic structure (one block)
- [ ] E1 Across a period, the number of outer electrons increases by one at each step, in the same main energy level.
- [ ] E2 Down a group, the number of outer electrons stays the same but a new main energy level is added each time.
- [ ] E3 The repeating pattern of outer-electron arrangements is the cause of periodicity: elements with the same outer arrangement fall in the same group.

Electronegativity (one block)
- [ ] N1 Electronegativity increases across a period and decreases down a group (Pauling scale; e.g. Na 0.93 → Cl 3.16; F 3.98 → I 2.66). The explanation is in "Electronegativity and bond type".

Chemical reactivity and the octet rule (one block)
- [ ] R1 Atoms react to reach a full outer level of eight electrons (the octet rule), by losing, gaining or sharing electrons.
- [ ] R2 Metals react by losing electrons: reactivity increases down a group (group 1 and group 2) and decreases across a period from group 1 to group 2 to group 13.
- [ ] R3 Non-metals react by gaining electrons: reactivity decreases down group 17 and generally increases across a period towards group 17.
- [ ] R4 Noble gases already have a full outer level and are unreactive.

Physical properties (one block)
- [ ] P1 Across period 3, the elements change from metals (sodium, magnesium, aluminium: shiny conductors) through a metalloid (silicon: semiconductor) to non-metals (phosphorus, sulfur, chlorine, argon: non-conductors).
- [ ] P2 Melting points across period 3 rise to a maximum at silicon (giant covalent structure) and then fall sharply for the molecular non-metals and argon.
- [ ] P3 Down group 1, melting points decrease (lithium 181 °C, sodium 98 °C, potassium 63 °C); down group 17, melting and boiling points increase (gases to liquid to solid).

Higher Level trends (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] H1 [HL] Atomic (covalent) radius decreases across a period and increases down a group.
- [ ] H2 [HL] First ionisation energy generally increases across a period and decreases down a group; successive ionisation energies of an element always increase. Both are explained, with their exceptions, in "Ionisation energies and atomic radii".
- [ ] H3 [HL] Higher Level trends extend to the first 36 elements (to krypton); Ordinary level uses the first 20 (to calcium).

## 3. Traps

- Don't explain ionisation energy or radius trends in detail here; that is "Ionisation energies and atomic radii".
- Relative atomic mass anomalies are not explained by "mistakes"; they come from isotopic composition.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Describe and explain trends in reactivity down group 1 and group 17.
- State the trend in electronegativity across a period.
