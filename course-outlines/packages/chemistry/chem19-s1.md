## 1. Must cover

Self-ionisation of water (one block)
- [ ] S1 Pure water conducts electricity very slightly, so it must contain a small number of ions.
- [ ] S2 These ions come from the self-ionisation (auto-ionisation) of water: one water molecule donates a proton to another, a Brønsted–Lowry acid–base reaction: H₂O(l) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq).
- [ ] S3 In simplified form: H₂O(l) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq).
- [ ] S4 The equilibrium lies very far to the left: at 25 °C only about 2 in every 1000 million (1.8 × 10⁻⁹) water molecules are ionised at any moment.
- [ ] S5 In pure water, each ionisation gives one H₃O⁺ and one OH⁻, so [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻] always.
- [ ] S6 A solution is neutral when [H⁺] = [OH⁻], acidic when [H⁺] > [OH⁻], and basic (alkaline) when [H⁺] < [OH⁻]; even acidic solutions contain some OH⁻ and basic solutions some H⁺.

Deducing Kw (Higher Level only; one block, one step per bullet)
- [ ] K1 [HL] Step 1: Write the equilibrium constant for H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻: Kc = [H⁺][OH⁻] ÷ [H₂O].
- [ ] K2 [HL] Step 2: So little water ionises that [H₂O] is effectively constant: 1000 g of water per litre ÷ 18 g mol⁻¹ = 55.6 mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] K3 [HL] Step 3: Combine the two constants: Kc × [H₂O] = [H⁺][OH⁻]. This new constant is the ionic product of water, Kw.
- [ ] K4 [HL] Definition: Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻], or in simplified form Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻]; the product of the hydrogen ion and hydroxide ion concentrations in water or any dilute aqueous solution.
- [ ] K5 [HL] At 25 °C, Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ (units mol² L⁻², often omitted).

Using Kw (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] U1 [HL] Pure water at 25 °C: [H⁺] = [OH⁻], so [H⁺]² = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ and [H⁺] = √(1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ mol L⁻¹; pH = −log₁₀(1.0 × 10⁻⁷) = 7.0. This is why pH 7 is neutral at 25 °C.
- [ ] U2 [HL] Kw applies to every dilute aqueous solution, not just pure water: if [H⁺] rises, [OH⁻] falls so that the product stays 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C.
- [ ] U3 [HL] Example: a solution with [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻² mol L⁻¹ (pH 2) has [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻² = 1.0 × 10⁻¹² mol L⁻¹.
- [ ] U4 [HL] Taking −log₁₀ of Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] gives pH + pOH = 14 at 25 °C (pOH = −log₁₀[OH⁻]); this is used for bases in "pH calculations".

Temperature dependence of Kw (Higher Level only; one block)
- [ ] T1 [HL] Self-ionisation is endothermic in the forward direction (ΔH about +56 kJ mol⁻¹): it is the reverse of neutralisation, H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O, which is exothermic.
- [ ] T2 [HL] By Le Châtelier's principle, raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the right (the endothermic direction), producing more H⁺ and OH⁻, so Kw increases; cooling decreases Kw.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Like every equilibrium constant, Kw changes only with temperature; that is why Kw values are quoted with a temperature.
- [ ] T4 [HL] Values: 0 °C, 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁵ (neutral pH 7.47); 25 °C, 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ (pH 7.00); 50 °C, 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ (pH 6.63); 100 °C, 5.1 × 10⁻¹³ (pH 6.14).
- [ ] T5 [HL] Hot pure water has a pH below 7 but is still neutral, because [H⁺] and [OH⁻] increase equally and stay equal; neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻], not pH = 7.
- [ ] T6 [HL] So pH 7 is the neutral point only at 25 °C; at higher temperatures the neutral pH is lower, at lower temperatures higher.

## 3. Traps

- Hot water with pH 6.6 is not acidic: neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻].
- [H₂O] does not appear in Kw: it is constant and has been absorbed into the constant.
- Kw increases with temperature because the forward reaction is endothermic; don't say "because the water evaporates" or "because the particles move faster" alone.
- Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻], not [H⁺] + [OH⁻], and not [H⁺][OH⁻] ÷ [H₂O].
- pH calculations for acids and bases belong in "pH calculations"; keep here to pure water and the [H⁺]–[OH⁻] link.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Write an equation for the self-ionisation of water.
- Define Kw; write its expression; show that the pH of pure water is 7 at 25 °C.
- Calculate [OH⁻] in a solution from its [H⁺] or pH using Kw.
- Explain why the pH of pure water falls as temperature rises, yet the water stays neutral.
