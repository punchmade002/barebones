## 1. Must cover

The theory (one block)
- [ ] T1 [HL] VSEPR stands for valence shell electron pair repulsion.
- [ ] T2 [HL] Electron pairs in the outer (valence) shell of the central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible, which fixes the shape of the molecule.
- [ ] T3 [HL] Both bonding pairs and lone pairs count.
- [ ] T4 [HL] Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs: lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair. So lone pairs squeeze the bond angles smaller.
- [ ] T5 [HL] The shape is described by the positions of the atoms only, not the lone pairs.

Method (one block, skills)
- [ ] M1 [HL] Step 1: Draw the Lewis (dot-and-cross) diagram of the molecule.
- [ ] M2 [HL] Step 2: Count the electron pairs around the central atom and how many are bonding pairs and lone pairs.
- [ ] M3 [HL] Step 3: Arrange the pairs as far apart as possible and name the shape from the positions of the atoms, adjusting the angle for lone pairs.

The five shapes (one block; one bullet per shape with example and angle)
- [ ] S1 [HL] Two bonding pairs, no lone pairs: linear, 180°, e.g. BeCl₂ (gas).
- [ ] S2 [HL] Three bonding pairs, no lone pairs: trigonal planar, 120°, e.g. BF₃.
- [ ] S3 [HL] Four bonding pairs, no lone pairs: tetrahedral, 109.5°, e.g. CH₄, CCl₄, SiH₄.
- [ ] S4 [HL] Three bonding pairs and one lone pair: pyramidal (trigonal pyramidal), about 107°, e.g. NH₃. (PH₃ is also pyramidal, but its measured angle is much smaller, about 93.5°.)
- [ ] S5 [HL] Two bonding pairs and two lone pairs: V-shaped (bent), about 104.5°, e.g. H₂O. (H₂S is also V-shaped, but its measured angle is much smaller, about 92°.)
- [ ] S6 [HL] NH₃ and H₂O have four electron pairs arranged roughly tetrahedrally like CH₄, but the lone pairs push the bonds closer together, so 109.5° → 107° → 104.5° as lone pairs increase from 0 to 2.

Visualising shapes (one block)
- [ ] V1 [HL] Diagrams: draw 3D shapes with wedges (bond towards you) and dashed lines (bond away from you), and show lone pairs on the central atom.
- [ ] V2 [HL] 3D models: ball-and-stick kits show bond angles; digital models (molecular-modelling software or apps) can be rotated.
- [ ] V3 [HL] Diagram: the five shapes, each with its example, bond angle and any lone pairs labelled.

## 3. Traps

- Single bonds only, up to four pairs (ABn): don't include double bonds (e.g. CO₂) or five or six pairs (PCl₅, SF₆).
- NH₃ is pyramidal, not trigonal planar; H₂O is V-shaped, not linear.
- Polarity and symmetry are in "Shape, polarity and properties".

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Use VSEPR theory to predict and explain the shapes of molecules such as CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, BF₃, BeCl₂, PH₃, H₂S.
- Explain why the bond angle in water is less than in methane.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 12 marks: "Use VSEPR theory to predict the shape of (i) a methane molecule, (ii) an ammonia molecule, and explain why the bond angle in ammonia is smaller than in methane." Model answer: T2, T4, S3, S4, S6.
2. 6 marks: "State the shape and bond angle of (i) BF₃, (ii) H₂S." Model answer: trigonal planar, 120°; V-shaped (bent), with two bonding pairs and two lone pairs; VSEPR predicts less than 109.5°, and the measured angle is about 92°.
