## 1. Must cover

Definitions (one block)
- [ ] D1 Yield: the amount of a product obtained from a reaction.
- [ ] D2 Theoretical yield: the maximum amount of product that could be formed from the given amounts of reactants, calculated from the balanced equation, assuming the reaction goes to completion and all of the limiting reactant becomes the desired product.
- [ ] D3 Actual yield: the amount of product actually obtained (measured) in practice, in the laboratory or in industry.
- [ ] D4 Percentage yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100; it measures how efficiently reactants are converted into the desired product.
- [ ] D5 The actual and theoretical yields must be in the same units (both grams, both moles, or both gas volumes at the same temperature and pressure).

Why the actual yield is less than the theoretical yield (one block)
- [ ] R1 The reaction may not go to completion: many organic reactions are slow or reversible and reach equilibrium (e.g. esterification), so some reactant is left unconverted.
- [ ] R2 Side reactions form other, unwanted products from the same reactants (e.g. some ethanol forms other products instead of ethene).
- [ ] R3 Product is lost during transfer, filtration, separation and purification (e.g. some product stays dissolved in the solvent after recrystallisation, or sticks to glassware and filter paper).
- [ ] R4 The reactants may be impure, or a volatile product or reactant may evaporate.
- [ ] R5 A percentage yield above 100% means the product is impure or not fully dry (e.g. still contains water or solvent); the true yield can never exceed 100%.

Why yield matters (one block)
- [ ] W1 In industry a higher percentage yield means less raw material, energy and cost per kilogram of product, and less waste to treat or dispose of; this links yield to sustainability.
- [ ] W2 Pharmaceuticals are made in many steps from simpler precursors, so a low yield at each step makes the overall yield very small (see "Organic products and pharmaceuticals").
- [ ] W3 Overall yield of a multi-step synthesis = the product of the fractional yields of the steps, e.g. two steps of 80% and 75% give 0.80 × 0.75 = 0.60, an overall yield of 60%.

Method (one block, skills; one step per bullet)
- [ ] M1 Step 1: Write the balanced equation.
- [ ] M2 Step 2: Find the moles of the reactant that decides the amount of product (the one not in excess).
- [ ] M3 Step 3: Use the mole ratio to find the moles of product, then convert to grams (or a gas volume): this is the theoretical yield.
- [ ] M4 Step 4: Percentage yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100.

Example 1: preparing ethene (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] E1 4.60 g of ethanol was dehydrated to ethene and 1.96 g of ethene was collected: C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O (the preparation is in "Investigation: preparing ethene").
- [ ] E2 Moles of ethanol = 4.60 ÷ 46 = 0.100 mol; ratio 1 : 1, so the theoretical yield is 0.100 mol of ethene.
- [ ] E3 Theoretical mass of ethene = 0.100 × 28 = 2.80 g.
- [ ] E4 Percentage yield = (1.96 ÷ 2.80) × 100 = 70.0%.
- [ ] E5 With a gas, the yield can be compared by volume: 0.100 mol of ethene is 2.24 L at STP; if 1.50 L (at STP) were collected, the percentage yield would be (1.50 ÷ 2.24) × 100 = 67.0%.

Example 2: preparing an ester (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] F1 6.00 g of ethanoic acid was refluxed with excess ethanol and an acid catalyst; 5.72 g of pure ethyl ethanoate was obtained: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
- [ ] F2 Moles of ethanoic acid = 6.00 ÷ 60 = 0.100 mol, so the theoretical yield is 0.100 mol of ethyl ethanoate.
- [ ] F3 Theoretical mass = 0.100 × 88 = 8.80 g.
- [ ] F4 Percentage yield = (5.72 ÷ 8.80) × 100 = 65.0%; the yield is well below 100% mainly because esterification is reversible and reaches equilibrium (the preparation is in "Tests, reaction schemes and preparing an ester").

Example 3: making aspirin (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] G1 Aspirin (C₉H₈O₄, M = 180) is made from salicylic acid (C₇H₆O₃, M = 138); one mole of salicylic acid gives one mole of aspirin.
- [ ] G2 2.76 g of salicylic acid = 2.76 ÷ 138 = 0.0200 mol, so the theoretical yield is 0.0200 mol of aspirin.
- [ ] G3 Theoretical mass of aspirin = 0.0200 × 180 = 3.60 g.
- [ ] G4 2.52 g of dry, recrystallised aspirin was obtained: percentage yield = (2.52 ÷ 3.60) × 100 = 70.0%.

Example 4: working back from a yield (one block, one step per bullet; sample data)
- [ ] H1 Question: what mass of ethanoic acid is needed to make 5.00 g of ethyl ethanoate if the yield is 65.0%?
- [ ] H2 Theoretical yield needed = 5.00 ÷ 0.650 = 7.69 g of ethyl ethanoate.
- [ ] H3 Moles of ethyl ethanoate = 7.692 ÷ 88 = 0.0874 mol = moles of ethanoic acid needed (1 : 1).
- [ ] H4 Mass of ethanoic acid = 0.08741 × 60 = 5.24 g.
- [ ] H5 Given a percentage yield, the actual yield = theoretical yield × (percentage ÷ 100), e.g. 15.0% of a theoretical 0.960 L of gas is 0.144 L (144 cm³).

## 3. Traps

- Percentage yield is actual ÷ theoretical, not the other way round.
- Don't mix units: compare grams with grams, or moles with moles.
- The theoretical yield is calculated from the reactant that is not in excess; working out which reactant that is when it isn't stated is Higher Level ("Limiting reagents").
- A yield over 100% is a sign of an impure or wet product, not a better reaction.
- "Atom economy" is not on the specification; don't introduce it.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define theoretical yield, actual yield and percentage yield.
- Give reasons why the actual yield is less than the theoretical yield.
- Calculate the percentage yield of an organic preparation (ethene, an ester, soap, benzoic acid) from given masses or volumes.
- Calculate the actual mass or volume of product from a given percentage yield.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "Define theoretical yield and percentage yield, and give two reasons why the actual yield of a product is usually less than the theoretical yield." Model answer: D2, D4; two of R1–R4.
2. 9 marks: "9.20 g of ethanol was dehydrated to ethene: C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O. 3.36 L of ethene, measured at STP, was collected. Calculate the theoretical volume of ethene at STP and the percentage yield." Model answer: 9.20 ÷ 46 = 0.200 mol ethanol = 0.200 mol ethene; × 22.4 = 4.48 L; yield = 3.36 ÷ 4.48 × 100 = 75.0%.
3. 9 marks: "3.00 g of ethanoic acid was heated under reflux with excess methanol and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid; 2.59 g of methyl ethanoate (M = 74) was obtained: CH₃COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₃ + H₂O. Calculate the percentage yield and suggest why it is less than 100%." Model answer: 3.00 ÷ 60 = 0.0500 mol; theoretical 0.0500 × 74 = 3.70 g; 2.59 ÷ 3.70 × 100 = 70.0%; reversible reaction reaching equilibrium, and losses during separation (R1, R3).
