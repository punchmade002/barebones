# Package: chem8-s2 — Relative atomic mass from isotopic abundance

- **loId:** `chem8-s2` · **chapterId:** `chem8` · **syllabusRef:** 1.4.2
- **Syllabus:** 1.4.2 "solve problems involving relative atomic mass and percentage abundance of
  isotopes". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Definitions of isotope and relative atomic mass
  are in "Atomic number, mass number, relative atomic mass and isotopes"; recap in one bullet
  each, then the two kinds of calculation with worked examples.

## 1. Must cover

Recap (one block)
- [ ] R1 Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different mass numbers.
- [ ] R2 Relative atomic mass is the weighted average of the isotope masses, using their percentage abundances, relative to one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

Finding Ar from abundances (one block, skills)
- [ ] A1 Method: multiply each isotope's mass number by its percentage abundance, add the products and divide by 100: Ar = Σ(mass number × % abundance) ÷ 100.
- [ ] A2 Chlorine (75% ³⁵Cl, 25% ³⁷Cl): (35 × 75 + 37 × 25) ÷ 100 = (2625 + 925) ÷ 100 = 35.5.
- [ ] A3 Boron (19.9% ¹⁰B, 80.1% ¹¹B): (10 × 19.9 + 11 × 80.1) ÷ 100 = (199 + 881.1) ÷ 100 = 10.8.
- [ ] A4 Magnesium, three isotopes (79.0% ²⁴Mg, 10.0% ²⁵Mg, 11.0% ²⁶Mg): (24 × 79.0 + 25 × 10.0 + 26 × 11.0) ÷ 100 = (1896 + 250 + 286) ÷ 100 = 24.3.
- [ ] A5 The answer must lie between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses, closer to the more abundant one: a quick check.

Finding abundances from Ar (one block, skills)
- [ ] B1 Method for two isotopes: let the abundance of one be x%; the other is (100 − x)%; set up Ar = [mass₁ × x + mass₂ × (100 − x)] ÷ 100 and solve for x.
- [ ] B2 Boron (Ar 10.8; isotopes ¹⁰B and ¹¹B): 10x + 11(100 − x) = 1080 → 1100 − x = 1080 → x = 20; so about 20% boron-10 and 80% boron-11, matching the data in A3.
- [ ] B3 Using exact whole mass numbers is an approximation; data-book isotope masses give more accurate abundances.

## 3. Traps

- Don't introduce the mass spectrometer (not on the specification); abundances are given as data.
- Divide by 100 when abundances are percentages; by the total if they are given as ratios.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Calculate the relative atomic mass of an element from given isotopic abundances.
- Calculate abundances from a given relative atomic mass.

## 5. Practice questions to write

1. 6 marks: "Gallium consists of 60.1% gallium-69 and 39.9% gallium-71. Calculate the relative atomic mass of gallium to one decimal place." Model answer: (69 × 60.1 + 71 × 39.9) ÷ 100 = (4146.9 + 2832.9) ÷ 100 = 69.8.
2. 6 marks: "Lithium has isotopes of mass numbers 6 and 7 and a relative atomic mass of 6.94. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope (sample data)." Model answer: 6x + 7(100 − x) = 694 → x = 6; 6% lithium-6 and 94% lithium-7.
