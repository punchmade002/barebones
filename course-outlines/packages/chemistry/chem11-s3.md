# Package: chem11-s3 — Arrhenius and Brønsted–Lowry theories

- **loId:** `chem11-s3` · **chapterId:** `chem11` · **syllabusRef:** 3.4.3
- **Syllabus:** 3.4.3 "compare two theories of acid-base systems and justify why Brønsted-Lowry
  theory is a more extensive model for explaining behaviour". Learn about: "two theories of
  acid-base systems: Brønsted-Lowry; Arrhenius". Normal type.
- **Prepared by:** a higher model, 2026-10-30. Definitions, examples, comparison and
  justification. Conjugate pairs are Higher Level, in "Applying Brønsted–Lowry theory";
  mention only that every acid–base reaction involves a proton transfer.

## 1. Must cover

The Arrhenius theory (one block)
- [ ] A1 Svante Arrhenius (1887): an acid is a substance that dissociates in water to produce hydrogen ions (H⁺): HCl(aq) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq).
- [ ] A2 A base is a substance that dissociates in water to produce hydroxide ions (OH⁻): NaOH(aq) → Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq).
- [ ] A3 Neutralisation is the combination of hydrogen and hydroxide ions to form water: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O.

Limitations of the Arrhenius theory (one block)
- [ ] L1 It applies only to aqueous solutions.
- [ ] L2 It can't explain substances that act as bases but contain no hydroxide, such as ammonia (NH₃) and sodium carbonate.
- [ ] L3 It can't explain acid–base reactions that happen without water, e.g. ammonia gas and hydrogen chloride gas reacting to form ammonium chloride: NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s).
- [ ] L4 It ignores the role of water: a bare H⁺ ion (a proton) doesn't exist in water; it bonds to a water molecule to form the hydronium ion, H₃O⁺.

The Brønsted–Lowry theory (one block)
- [ ] B1 Johannes Brønsted and Thomas Lowry (1923), independently: an acid is a proton (H⁺) donor; a base is a proton acceptor.
- [ ] B2 An acid–base reaction is the transfer of a proton from an acid to a base.
- [ ] B3 Example: HCl(g) + H₂O(l) → H₃O⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq): HCl donates a proton (acid) and water accepts it (base).
- [ ] B4 Example: NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq): ammonia accepts a proton (base) and water donates it (acid).
- [ ] B5 Water can act as an acid or a base depending on what it reacts with (it is amphoteric).

Why Brønsted–Lowry is more extensive (one block, comparison)
- [ ] J1 It includes every Arrhenius acid and base, and more: the hydroxide ion is itself a proton acceptor.
- [ ] J2 It explains bases with no OH⁻, such as ammonia and the carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻ + H⁺ → HCO₃⁻).
- [ ] J3 It applies to reactions without water, such as NH₃ + HCl in the gas phase.
- [ ] J4 It shows the role of water as a base (forming H₃O⁺) or as an acid.
- [ ] J5 Comparison as paired points: H⁺ producer vs proton donor; OH⁻ producer vs proton acceptor; aqueous only vs any solvent or none; doesn't explain ammonia as a base vs does.

## 3. Traps

- A proton and a hydrogen ion are the same thing (H⁺).
- Conjugate acid–base pairs are Higher Level; keep to proton donor and acceptor here.
- Lewis theory is not on the specification.

## 4. What exams ask (topics only)

- Define an acid and a base according to each theory.
- Explain why ammonia is a base according to Brønsted–Lowry but not Arrhenius.
- Justify why Brønsted–Lowry theory is more extensive.
