[hist-eur3-s7.2.b] Unemployment was concentrated in the older industrial regions of Wales, the north of England and central Scotland, while the south-east prospered.
[hist-eur3-s7.2.0] The staple industries of coal, cotton, shipbuilding, iron and steel had been the basis of the Victorian economy, and they declined sharply in the inter-war years.
[hist-eur3-s7.2.1] The coal industry lost jobs steadily from the mid-1920s, and South Wales, Durham and Northumberland were the most heavily affected coalfields.
[hist-eur3-s7.2.2] The cotton industry of Lancashire lost export markets to Japan and India and suffered a long decline, with many mills closed by the 1930s.
[hist-eur3-s7.2.3] Shipbuilding on the Tyne, Wear and Clyde was badly hit by the collapse of world trade and the lack of new orders, and many yards closed in the 1930s.
[hist-eur3-s7.2.4] Unemployment in the distressed areas could remain above 30 per cent in many towns in the early 1930s, and higher still in places such as Jarrow and Merthyr Tydfil, while the south-east and the Midlands had relatively low unemployment.
[hist-eur3-s7.2.5] Workers in the depressed regions often moved to the new industries of the south and Midlands, but many could not afford the cost of relocation or of housing.
[hist-eur3-s7.3.b] The Wall Street Crash and the collapse of exports forced the Labour government to confront a budget crisis, leading to the formation of a National Government and the abandonment of gold.

[hist-eur3-s7.3.5] The Invergordon mutiny of 15 to 16 September 1931, a few days before Britain left gold, in which sailors of the Atlantic Fleet protested against pay cuts, showed how the economy measures were resented in the armed forces.

[hist-eur3-s7.7.b] Keynes was an economist and government adviser whose ideas about public spending and demand became the basis of modern economic policy.
[hist-eur3-s7.7.0] Born in Cambridge in 1883, he was a Fellow of King's College, Cambridge, and worked in the Treasury during the First World War.

[hist-eur3-s7.7.7] He led the British delegation to the Bretton Woods conference in 1944, where he argued for an international clearing union and a more flexible monetary system, and the agreement set up the International Monetary Fund and World Bank.
[hist-eur3-s7.7.8] Created Baron Keynes in 1942, he died in April 1946 after negotiating the American loan to Britain.
