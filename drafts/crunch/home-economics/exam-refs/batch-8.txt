QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2009-HI-sectionb-appliancesequipment-B4 (LC Home Economics Higher 2009 — B4; filed under hom-appliances-equipment)
PART (a) [16 marks]
  Q: Discuss how technology has contributed to the efficient management of the home.
  A: Technology reduces workload and improves home life; completes tasks quickly, frees time and can increase effectiveness, e.g. steam cleaners; produces easier-care, low-maintenance products; uses computer chips and automated systems/timers in appliances and heating; supports budgeting/accounting software, online shopping/banking/communication and security systems.
PART (b) [26 marks]
  Q: Set out details of a study you have undertaken on one type of electrical appliance suitable for a kitchen. Refer to: type of appliance; working principle; guidelines for use; energy efficiency.
  A: Appliance studied: a microwave oven. It is a compact electrical cooking appliance suited to rapid cooking, reheating and defrosting in the kitchen.

Working principle: when the oven is switched on, a high-voltage transformer increases the domestic voltage. The magnetron converts electrical energy into electromagnetic microwaves. A waveguide directs these waves into the metal cooking cavity, where they are reflected from the walls and distributed by a mode stirrer and/or rotating turntable. Microwaves penetrate the food and cause polar molecules, especially water molecules, to oscillate rapidly. Molecular friction produces heat within the food. Heat then moves further towards the centre by conduction, so thick food may not heat completely evenly. The timer stops the magnetron at the selected time, and a door interlock prevents operation when the door is open.

Guidelines for safe and successful use: use glass, ceramic, paper or plastic labelled microwave-safe; do not use ordinary metal, foil, metallic trim or sealed containers because arcing or pressure build-up may occur. Pierce foods with skins, such as potatoes, and loosen lids. Arrange equal-sized pieces in a ring with thicker portions towards the outside. Cover food to retain moisture and prevent splashing, but leave a vent. Stir, turn or rearrange food during cooking, and allow standing time so temperature equalises. Follow manufacturer instructions and recommended power/time; take special care that poultry, minced meat and reheated high-risk foods reach a safe temperature throughout. Use oven gloves because containers can become hot, and open covers away from the face to avoid steam burns. Keep the cavity, door seals and vents clean and never operate a damaged oven or an empty cavity.

Energy efficiency: microwave cooking is efficient for small and medium portions because energy heats the food rather than first heating a large oven cavity, no preheating is normally needed, cooking time is short and less heat escapes into the room. Efficiency is improved by selecting an appropriately sized, high-energy-rated model, matching power and time to the quantity, defrosting only as necessary, keeping vents clean, using residual standing heat and switching the appliance fully off rather than leaving displays on standby. A conventional oven may be more economical when cooking several large dishes together, so the appliance should be matched to the task.
PART (c) [8 marks]
  Q: Explain how the rights of the consumer are protected by the Sale of Goods & Supply of Services Act (1980).
  A: A purchase creates a binding contract between buyer and seller. Goods/services must be fit for purpose, of merchantable quality, as described and match samples. Guarantees and shop notices are covered. Services must be supplied with care, diligence and safety by qualified people using sound materials. Redress may be a refund, repair or replacement.

PART 1(b)(i) [14 marks]
  Q: Homes can be heated by one or a combination of heating systems. Identify two heating options available and comment on the merits of each.
  A: Option 1: a full wet central-heating system, fired by gas or oil. A boiler heats water, a pump circulates it through small-bore pipes to radiators, and convection distributes heat in each room. Its main merit is that it heats the entire dwelling evenly and can also provide domestic hot water. A programmer, boiler thermostat, room thermostat, thermostatic radiator valves and separate zones allow the household to heat only required areas and times, improving comfort and efficiency. It is convenient and relatively quick, but installation and servicing are costly, heat is lost through poorly insulated pipes or fabric, and fossil-fuel price and carbon emissions are disadvantages.

Option 2: active solar water/space heating using roof-mounted solar collectors. Dark collector panels absorb solar radiation and transfer heat to fluid, which circulates through a coil in an insulated storage cylinder. The energy source is renewable, produces no fuel emissions in use and can reduce conventional fuel bills, particularly for hot water in brighter months. Grants may reduce capital cost and the system has low running costs. However, panels and specialist installation are expensive, roof orientation and unshaded area are important, output varies with Irish weather and winter demand, and a backup boiler or immersion is normally necessary.

A well-insulated house can combine the two: solar heat supplies part of demand and a controlled central system provides reliable backup.

  A: Attic: fibreglass, loose-fill, blown-fibre or foam; these poor heat conductors reduce heat loss. Windows: double/triple glazing traps insulating air/argon between panes; low-E glazing reduces conduction, while lined curtains or secondary windows add insulation and stop draughts.

PART 3(c)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Name and evaluate two leisure facilities available in your community. Refer to: cost and value for money; the range of facilities offered.
  A: Two leisure facilities in my community are the municipal sports and leisure centre and the public library/community arts centre.

The municipal sports centre charges either a pay-as-you-go admission or a monthly/annual membership. Reduced rates may be available for students, families, unemployed people and older adults. Membership is good value for a frequent user because one fee can cover the swimming pool, gym and some classes; a casual rate is better for someone attending infrequently. However, joining fees, specialist lessons and equipment or sports clothing add to the cost. Value also depends on convenient opening hours, cleanliness, qualified supervision, disability access and whether overcrowding restricts actual use.

Its range is broad: swimming lanes and lessons, children's pool, gym and fitness assessment, exercise classes such as aerobics, circuits, yoga or Pilates, indoor courts for basketball and badminton, outdoor pitches, changing rooms and sometimes a café or club rooms. This variety supports fitness, weight control, skill development and social contact for different ages.   A: Options include childminder, au pair, crèche/day care, playschool, after-school group, Montessori or naíonra. Evaluate location, hours, cost, hygiene/safety, reliability, activities, staff qualifications/ratio, child age/additional needs and convenience.
PART (b)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Analyse the reasons why adults return to education.
  A: Adults return to gain qualifications, reskill after unemployment, improve promotion/professional prospects, keep pace with technology, develop literacy/numeracy, socialise, achieve self-fulfilment or relax.
PART (b)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe how improvements in the provision of education have impacted on family life.
  A: Additional-needs resources reduce family support burden; free education and third-level assistance improve access; diverse programmes/curricula reduce stress/disengagement; online/adult education supports parents and role modelling; longer education improves income. Examples include HEAR, DARE, SUSI, Youthreach, VTOS, meals and books.
PART (c)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Discuss the causes of poverty.
In your answer refer to the cycle of poverty.
  A: The cycle of poverty persists across generations through restricted resources/opportunities. Causes include living/housing costs and social-housing shortage, family size/one-parent households, recession/unemployment, welfare reliance, addiction/social problems, low education, social policy and the poverty trap.
PART (c)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Differentiate between each of the following types of poverty.
• absolute poverty
• relative poverty.
  A: Absolute poverty is insufficient income/resources for survival/basic food, shelter and clothing, often affecting homeless people. Relative poverty is living below society’s basic norm, excluded from usual activities, commonly income below 60% of median household income.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2025-HI-sectionc-meatpreparation-Q4 (LC Home Economics Higher 2025 Paper BC — Q4; filed under hom-meat-preparation)
PART (a)(i) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the nutritional significance of meat in the diet.
  A: Meat supplies 20–30% HBV protein for growth/repair/hormones; 10–30% mostly saturated fat for energy/insulation; essentially no carbohydrate; B vitamins (and A/D in liver); haem iron, potassium, zinc, sulphur and sodium in processed meat; and 50–60% water.
PART (a)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Outline two causes of toughness in meat.
Describe four methods of tenderising meat.
  A: Toughness is caused by age, activity/cut, poor pre-slaughter rest, incorrect carcass hanging or cooking. Tenderise through correct hanging/lactic acid, mincing, pounding/piercing, marinating, curing/brining, commercial tenderiser/proteolytic enzymes and slow moist cooking.
PART (a)(iii) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe how the Bord Bia Quality Assurance Scheme ensures meat quality and safety.
  A: Bord Bia assurance tells consumers meat is quality-assured/sustainable; requires animal welfare/care, regulated feed, competent herd management, accurate livestock/medicine traceability, pest controls and farm/processing inspections; it also advises industry/government.
PART (b)(i) [16 marks]
  Q: Outline the conditions that must be met in order to qualify for mortgage approval.
  A: Meet loan-to-income limits (about 4× gross income for first-time buyers), supply at least 10% deposit, show good credit/savings and secure continuous employment/income evidence, fit age/term rules, prove identity/address, have a sound surveyed property, mortgage protection/home insurance and an Irish bank account.
PART (b)(ii) [14 marks]
  Q: Name and describe one type of mortgage available to house purchasers.
  A: Annuity/repayment mortgage: each repayment covers interest and principal, so the balance reduces over time; early payments are mostly interest; mortgage protection is required and rates may be fixed, variable or combined. Endowment, pension-linked, local-authority and other named mortgage types are also accepted with relevant details.
PART (c)(i) [15 marks]
  Q: Outline three meal planning guidelines that an older person should follow to maximise nutritional intake.
  A: Eat balanced regular meals; include easily digested protein for repair, fruit/vegetables/Vitamin C, calcium/Vitamin D, oily fish/olive oil and iron foods; choose simple/prepared/frozen foods, low-fat cooking, budget-friendly tender cuts and reduce spicy foods.
PART (c)(ii) [15 marks]
  Q: Evaluate steaming as a method of cooking for an older person.
Refer to:
• ease of use
• effect on nutritive value of food
• palatability of food cooked by this method.
  A: Ease: needs little attention/equipment, can cook a whole meal and suits small portions. Nutrition: adds no fat and retains B/C vitamins. Palatability: softens cellulose, keeps food moist and retains colour, though it may lack flavour and overcooking harms texture.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2024-HI-sectiona-proteins-Q1 (LC Home Economics Higher 2024 Paper A — Q1; filed under hom-proteins)
PART (whole) [6 marks]
  Q: In relation to protein, explain deamination.
  A: Deamination is the process by which excess protein/amino acids are used for energy and broken down in the liver. The amino group (NH₂) is converted to ammonia, then urea, and excreted by the kidneys in urine; the carboxyl group (COOH) is oxidised to produce heat and energy.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2024-HI-sectiona-vitaminsminerals-Q3 (LC Home Economics Higher 2024 Paper A — Q3; filed under hom-vitamins-minerals)
PART (whole) [6 marks]
  Q: Describe the following forms of iron and give one food source of each.
Haem iron
Non-haem iron
  A: Haem iron is ferrous, soluble and easily absorbed; sources include offal, red meat, meat products and chicken. Non-haem iron is ferric and must be changed to ferrous iron before absorption; sources include eggs, wholegrain flour, dark green vegetables, fish, legumes, nuts and seeds.
