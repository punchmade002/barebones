QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2010-HI-sectionb-foodpreservation-B3 (LC Home Economics Higher 2010 — B3; filed under hom-food-preservation)
PART (a) [26 marks]
  Q: Set out details of the findings of a comparative evaluation you have carried out on a food which can be preserved using two different methods of preservation. Refer to: name of food; suitable methods of preservation; the underlying principle of each method; risk of food spoilage.
  A: Food evaluated: strawberries preserved by freezing and by jam-making. Sound, ripe fruit from the same batch was used so differences arose from preservation rather than raw-material quality.

Freezing: strawberries were washed quickly, hulled, dried and open-frozen in a single layer so they did not stick together. They were then packed in usable portions in strong, moisture- and vapour-proof freezer bags, air was removed, and each pack was sealed, labelled and dated. They were fast-frozen at about −25°C and stored at −18°C. The principle is removal of heat: low temperature inactivates micro-organisms and greatly slows enzyme action. Water becomes ice and is therefore unavailable for microbial growth. Freezing retained fresh colour and flavour reasonably well, but ice crystals damaged cells, so thawed fruit was softer and released juice.

Jam-making: equal prepared samples were weighed and simmered to soften the fruit and release pectin. The correct proportion of sugar was added and dissolved before the mixture was boiled rapidly to setting point. It was skimmed, poured into hot sterilised jars, sealed immediately, labelled and stored cool and dry. The principles are heat destruction of micro-organisms and enzyme inactivation, together with preservation by a concentrated sugar solution. By osmosis, water leaves microbial cells; at a sufficiently high sugar concentration they cannot multiply. Acid and pectin form the gel, while the airtight seal prevents re-entry of organisms. Jam had a much sweeter taste, darker cooked colour and firmer spreadable texture, and some heat-sensitive vitamin C was lost.

Risk of spoilage: frozen fruit is not sterile. Slow freezing forms large crystals; fluctuating temperature or partial thawing makes moisture available and surviving microbes can grow. Air-permeable or loose packs cause oxidation and freezer burn—drying, toughening and discolouration. Thawed strawberries should be used promptly and never refrozen.

Jam can ferment or grow mould if too little sugar is used, setting point is not reached, jars are dirty, the seal fails or a wet/used spoon introduces organisms. Excess surface moisture also favours mould. A bulging lid, gas, mould or off-odour indicates spoilage and the product should be discarded.

Overall, freezing produced a fruit closer to fresh and useful in desserts, but required continuous energy and had a shorter quality life after thawing. Jam stored conveniently without a freezer and had a long sealed shelf life, but its sugar content was high and fresh sensory/nutritional qualities changed more.
PART (b) [16 marks]
  Q: Assess the use of plastics as a packaging material having regard to: types; suitability for purpose; environmental impact.
  A: Types include polythene bags, plastic cartons/boxes, PET bottles and polystyrene. Plastics are strong, moisture-proof, convenient, light, flexible, heat-sealable, inexpensive, printable and suitable for frozen foods, though some may contaminate food. They use non-renewable oil, are mostly non-biodegradable, burden landfill and lack recycling facilities; some PET can be recycled into polyester filling.
PART (c) [8 marks]
  Q: Outline the protection provided to the consumer by Labelling Regulations (1982, 1991 and 2002).
  A: Labels must be clear, legible and indelible, in language understood by the consumer and not obscured. They must contain essential information and not mislead or claim that food prevents/cures disease.

  A: Functions: produces collagen, forms healthy blood vessels, speeds wound healing, supports immunity, aids calcium and iron absorption, acts as an antioxidant, and maintains healthy gums and teeth. Deficiency causes impaired collagen, weak tissues, slow healing, easy bruising, increased infection and scurvy. Properties: crystalline, acidic, sweet/sour, water-soluble, affected by light and air, destroyed by alkalis and oxidase, and antioxidant.
PART (d) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the guidelines a family should follow, when doing their weekly shopping on a restricted budget, in order to provide for their nutritional needs.
  A: Buy seasonal fruit, vegetables and fish; buy in bulk where useful; plan meals and make a list; prepare foods in large quantities while considering cooking costs; look for offers and compare prices; limit expensive, less nutritious convenience/takeaway foods; choose own brands and cheaper cuts of meat with similar nutrition; consider family dietary needs and the food pyramid; choose wholegrain staples and read labels.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2023-HI-sectionb-fishseafood-Q2 (LC Home Economics Higher 2023 Paper BC — Q2; filed under hom-fish-seafood)
PART (a) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the nutritional significance of fish in the diet.
  A: Fish provides 16–18% HBV protein and essential amino acids. Fat ranges from 0–18%: oily fish supplies polyunsaturated/omega-3 fat and fat-soluble vitamins; white fish has no fat and shellfish contains cholesterol. It supplies B-group vitamins, Vitamins A and D in oily fish/white-fish liver and shellfish, non-haem iron in shellfish, calcium in canned fish, iodine, zinc, potassium and phosphorus. Water content is 65–80% and is inversely related to fat.
PART (b) [18 marks]
  Q: Give a detailed account of one process used to extend the shelf life of fish.
Refer to:
• name
• underlying principle
• effects.
  A: Freezing: at about −30°C microbial growth is inhibited, water becomes unavailable as ice and enzymes are inactivated; wrapping prevents freezer burn and vacuum packing excludes oxygen. Colour and flavour are largely unchanged, but texture may change, some Vitamin B/extractives may be lost, and poor wrapping can cause freezer burn or oxidative rancidity. Other accepted processes are salting/brining, smoking, canning and vacuum packing with their stated principles and effects.
PART (c) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe the role of the Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI) in the food industry.
  A: The FSAI coordinates enforcement of food-safety regulations and rapid-alert systems; advises the Minister; protects consumers through safe food and accurate information; works with industry on HACCP; acts against breaches through improvement notices and closure orders; and processes applications for novel foods and processing methods.

  Q: Profile an added value food you have studied.
Refer to:
• name
• stages of production
• packaging and labelling.
  A: Cheese: milk is pasteurised; lactic acid bacteria are added; lactose changes to lactic acid; milk is heated to 30°C; rennet coagulates it into curds and whey; curds are chopped and whey drained; curds are heated to 35–40°C, cut, piled (cheddaring), salted, moulded and pressed; cheese may be hot-water sprayed, date-stamped, ripened 3–12 months, graded, cut and sealed.   Q: Name and describe the role of one physical conditioning agent used in food processing.

Other accepted agents include emulsifiers, anti-caking agents, setting agents, stabilisers, anti-spattering/anti-foaming agents, firming agents, buffers and packaging gases, with their stated roles.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2023-HI-sectionb-consumerrights-Q4 (LC Home Economics Higher 2023 Paper BC — Q4; filed under hom-consumer-rights)
PART (a) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss how the following factors affect the management of the home.
• stages in the life cycle of the family
• employment patterns
• values.
  A: Family life-cycle stage affects management through children’s arrival/number/ages, teenagers’ participation, and older people’s reduced income/agility. Employment patterns include dual/single income, commuting, job sharing, shift work, home working and a parent abroad. Values influence choices such as sustainability, local shopping, environmental friendliness and organic food.
PART (b) [20 marks]
  Q: Analyse how different techniques used by retailers influence consumer spending.
  A: Retailers influence spending through store layout and aisle flow; lighting, music, aromas and samples; placing essentials at the rear or complementary goods together; eye-level shelving; multiple buys, special offers and loss leaders; online recommendations/scarcity cues; and loyalty-card coupons.
PART (c) [10 marks]
  Q: Describe how the responsibilities of a consumer can assist them in making wise choices.
  A: Consumers should know their rights and relevant laws, be informed about goods/services, balance price with quality, complain and seek redress, read labels, use resources carefully, avoid waste and consider environmental impact.
