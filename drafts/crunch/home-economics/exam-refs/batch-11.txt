passing its best-before date; do not over pack to allow air circulation; etc.

  Q: Discuss the changes that have evolved in Irish food and eating patterns in recent years.
  A: Changes include more convenience food/additives/salt/saturated fat/sugar and less fibre; irregular meals and replacement of traditional foods; more special diets and a wider international food range; new cooking methods/skills; greater nutritional knowledge and raw-food availability; focus on gut and mental health.
PART (b) [20 marks]
  Q: Outline the stages involved in the manufacture of yogurt.
In your answer refer to production, packaging and labelling.
  A: Milk is homogenised, pasteurised at 80–90°C, cooled to 37°C, inoculated with Lactobacillus bulgaricus/lactic culture, incubated 6–8 hours and cooled to 5°C. Sweeteners, nuts, fruit, colours, flavours or stabilisers may be added; yoghurt is weighed, packaged and labelled.
PART (c) [10 marks]
  Q: Describe the protection provided to the consumer by the Food Hygiene Regulations 1950-1989.
  A: The Regulations prohibit diseased, contaminated or unfit food; require contamination precautions throughout production; require hygienic registered premises/equipment/waste/water; require food-handler hygiene and HACCP; require annual licences for relevant mobile stalls; and permit closure orders and seizure/destruction of unfit food.

PART (a) [20 marks]
  Q: Analyse the changes in consumer shopping patterns over the last decade.
  A: Shopping patterns changed through new payment methods, loyalty apps, self-checkout/scan-as-you-go, changed frequency, large centres/outlets, late/24-hour opening, TV/online/social shopping, charity/vintage and resale platforms; choices are also shaped by budget, time, values, family, special diets, sustainability, culture and nutrition.
PART (b) [18 marks]
  Q: Name and evaluate two different methods of payment used by consumers to pay for in-store purchases.
  A: Cash: widely accepted, simple and has no interest, but can be lost/stolen and provides limited transaction records. Debit card: convenient/contactless and spends account funds without interest, but depends on funds/technology and can expose the user to fraud. Other accepted methods include credit cards, Revolut, phone payments, vouchers, cheques and direct debit, evaluated for availability, ease, cost and safety.

  Q: Describe the protection provided to the consumer by the Consumer Protection Act 2007.
  A: The Consumer Protection Act 2007 protects against false or misleading claims about goods, services and price, including misleading previous prices and recommended retail prices.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2024-HI-sectionb-personalfinance-Q5 (LC Home Economics Higher 2024 Paper BC — Q5; filed under hom-family-caring)
PART (a) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the role of the family in meeting the physical and psychological needs of young children so that they will become well-adjusted adults in society.
  A: Physical needs: provide nutritious food, suitable clothing, shelter and protection. Psychological needs: love/affection enables healthy relationships; a dependable caregiver creates security/trust; praise encourages self-esteem and stable development; children are protected from abuse.
PART (b) [18 marks]
  Q: Analyse the impact of social, economic and technological changes on contemporary family structures.
  A: Social changes include less extended-family living, cohabitation/urbanisation/gender equality, dual careers, fewer/later children, separation/divorce, same-sex marriage, adoption/surrogacy, multiculturalism and shared roles. Economic changes include living/housing/childcare costs, benefits, dual incomes, hybrid work and a parent abroad. Technology includes automated/smart appliances, social media/communications, educational/entertainment/security technology and home working.
PART (c) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe two supports available to older family members and state how these supports help them to maintain their independence.
  A: Supports include family/neighbours, sheltered housing, Meals on Wheels/Age Action, welfare payments, public health nurses/home-support services, medical cards and free travel. They provide care, meals, money, health help, mobility or suitable housing so older people can remain independent.
