  Q: Explain the following sociological terms:
• universality of the family
• kinship
• monogamy.
  A: Universality of the family means the family exists in virtually all known societies, though its form varies. Kinship means family ties, blood relationships, common ancestry or lineage. Monogamy is a relationship with only one partner at a time.
PART (c) [15 marks]
  Q: Describe one legal option available in Ireland to couples when their marriage has broken down.

Other accepted options are deed of separation, judicial separation and legal nullity.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2023-HI-sectionc-housing-Q1 (LC Home Economics Higher 2023 Paper BC — Q1; filed under hom-housing)

  Q: Describe the factors that influence interior design in the home.
Refer to:
• aesthetic and comfort factors
• environmental awareness.
  A: Aesthetic/comfort factors include elements and principles of design, visual appeal, taste/trends, comfortable and multipurpose spaces, accessibility, efficient/zoned heating, lighting and ergonomics. Environmental awareness considers materials’ impact during production, use and disposal, their origin, sustainability, reuse, recycling and repurposing.
PART (a)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Discuss levels of thermal comfort in relation to two different living spaces in the home.
  A: Thermal comfort depends on room function: living rooms about 17–21°C (or 19–23°C for sitting/light work); bedrooms 10–16°C; kitchens 15–19°C; bathrooms 16–19°C; at least 20°C is recommended for elderly people.
PART (a)(iii) [18 marks]
  Q: Explain the underlying principle of a thermostat and describe how it can be used in the home.
  A: A thermostat uses thermal expansion and a bimetal strip of brass and invar. Brass expands more when heated, bending the strip to break the circuit and switch the appliance off; on cooling it returns and reconnects the circuit. It is used in central heating, radiators, heaters, immersions, showers, kettles, ovens, refrigerators and similar appliances.
PART (b)(i) [20 marks]
  Q: Describe four properties of light and give an example of how each property is used in the home.
  A: Reflected light bounces from a shiny surface, e.g. mirrors/gloss paint. Diffused light is scattered through translucent material or from a non-reflective surface, e.g. frosted glass/net curtains. Absorbed light is taken in by dark/matt surfaces, e.g. dark walls/brick. Dispersed light breaks into component colours through a prism/crystal, e.g. chandelier. Refracted light bends through thick/ridged glass, e.g. glass bricks/double glazing.
PART (b)(ii) [10 marks]
  Q: Explain the underlying principle of one type of energy-efficient lighting.
  A: CFL/fluorescent: a looped glass tube coated with phosphor contains argon and mercury; current heats electrodes, vaporising mercury, and the gases react with phosphor to glow. Alternatively, an LED releases photons when electrodes combine with holes in a semiconductor (electroluminescence); colour depends on the semiconductor energy-band gap.
PART (c)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Discuss how environmental, economic and social factors influence the choice of housing styles in Ireland today.
  A: Environmental factors include energy efficiency, aspect, surroundings, building regulations, local materials, climate and sustainability. Economic factors include income, site/house/material costs, bills, investment potential and resale value. Social factors include personal preference, location, family/additional needs, availability and tradition.
PART (c)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe the impact of burning fossil fuels in the home.
Refer to:
• an emission produced by burning fossil fuels
• effects on the environment
• a strategy households could implement in order to reduce emissions.
  A: Emissions include carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxide, sulphur dioxide, smoke, water vapour and methane. Effects include rising temperature/sea levels, climate change, damaged crops, altered species distribution and disease spread. Reduce emissions through renewables, natural gas/smokeless fuel, timers and thermostats, avoiding rubbish burning or using electric cars.

Toxic Food Poisoning
