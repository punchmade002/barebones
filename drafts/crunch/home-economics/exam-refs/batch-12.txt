QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2024-HI-sectionc-housing-Q1 (LC Home Economics Higher 2024 Paper BC — Q1; filed under hom-housing)
PART (a)(i) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the factors that influence the interior design of a home:
Refer to:
• cost
• family size and circumstances
• ergonomics.
  A: Cost: work within budget and choose good-quality attractive items without overspending. Family stage/circumstances: consider number/age, play/study, income/work, preferences, additional needs, cleanability and future change. Ergonomics: allow traffic flow and access, easy cleaning, efficient work sequence/triangle and suitable surface/furniture heights.
PART (a)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Identify three design principles and describe how each design principle identified could be applied to an interior design space.
  A: Proportion: objects/colours/patterns/textures should be sized relative to each other. Emphasis: create one focal point such as fireplace or feature wall. Balance: distribute colour, pattern and texture so nothing dominates. Rhythm: link objects by repeating colour, style, shape or pattern.
PART (a)(iii) [12 marks]
  Q: Evaluate how the design and construction of a house can help lower the energy use of its occupants.
  A: Lower energy use through renewable energy, large south-facing windows, double/triple low-emissivity glazing, cavity/attic insulation, airtight construction, zoned heating, low BER/timber frame, sensor lighting and mechanical ventilation with heat recovery.
PART (b)(i) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe how each of the following contribute to the safe use of electrical appliances in the home.
• miniature circuit breaker
• earth wire.
  A: An MCB trips to the off position when a fault/overload is detected, breaking the circuit; after repair it can be reset. The green/yellow earth wire carries fault current from a live metal casing to a ground plate, preventing electrocution.
PART (b)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Identify three inefficient uses of energy in the home and assess a strategy you would recommend to improve the energy efficiency in each case identified.
  A: Heat loss: insulate walls/floors and fit efficient glazing. Inefficient heating/open fires: install a condensing boiler/stove, service it, zone and time heating. Water heating/waste: time and insulate immersion/cylinder, use solar and repair leaks. Appliances/lighting: choose A-rated appliances, full/cold washes, avoid standby and use LEDs/timers.
PART (c)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Explain the underlying principle of insulation used in the home.
Identify and describe three different methods of home insulation.
  A: Insulation uses poor conductors such as trapped air, polystyrene or fibreglass to reduce heat transfer. Attic: fibre blankets, loose fill, sprayed foam or blown cellulose. Walls: cavity air/sheets/foam/beads, internal dry lining or external insulation. Floors: fill gaps, use carpets/underlay or fibreglass. Also use draught excluders and double/triple glazing/heavy curtains.
PART (c)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Discuss the importance of having adequate ventilation in a house.
  A: Ventilation supplies oxygen-rich fresh air, removes stale air, enables combustion, controls temperature/humidity and prevents condensation, illness and structural damage.

PART (a)(ii) [15 marks]
  Q: Outline the stages involved in the production of margarine to include details of packaging and labelling.
  A: Oil is extracted/refined, then hydrogen gas is added at double bonds using nickel, converting unsaturated liquid oil to semi-solid saturated fat. Oils are blended; milk/water, salt, colour, flavour and Vitamins A/D added; lecithin emulsifies; a votator churns; portions are weighed. Package in foil/plastic tubs; label brand/type, ingredients, allergens, date, storage, weight and nutrition.
PART (a)(iii) [15 marks]
  Q: Assess the health implications for a person consuming a diet high in saturated fat.
  A: High saturated-fat intake can cause obesity when excess energy becomes adipose tissue; coronary heart disease and hypertension as LDL narrows arteries; Type 2 diabetes through reduced insulin effectiveness; gallstones; varicose veins; and infertility through hormonal or sperm effects.
