Additive-free (26%), clean-label (23%) and raw (5%) claims ranked lower, showing that respondents prioritised specific nutritional benefits over processing-related claims.
PART (c) [18 marks]
  Q: Describe two healthy snacks suitable for college students.
Outline the nutritional merits of incorporating these snacks in a person’s diet.
  A: Suitable snacks include fresh fruit or vegetables, vegetable pots, yoghurt/dairy drinks, smoothies, fresh juices, nuts, seeds, trail mixes, granola/flapjacks, dried fruit or vegetable crisps, rice/corn cakes and protein bars/balls. Nutritional merit is credited where each ingredient or food is linked to an appropriate nutrient and function.
PART (d) [18 marks]
  Q: Dairy based snacks can contribute to micro-nutrient intake.
Give an account of calcium under each of the following headings:
• sources
• biological functions
• effects of deficiency.
  A: Sources: dairy products, eggs, dark-green vegetables, oily fish, white flour, hard/bottled water, fortified juices, supplements and sesame seeds. Functions: forms and develops bones and teeth; blood clotting; muscle contraction; normal nerve function and membrane permeability; regulates cell metabolism. Deficiency: rickets, osteomalacia, osteoporosis, dental cavities, failure of blood to clot, muscular spasms and impaired nerve impulses.
PART (e) [20 marks]
  Q: Explain how the packaging on food products attracts and informs consumers.
  A: Attracts through visual appearance, natural or bold colours, natural materials, gloss/matt finish, high-quality food imagery, a short ingredient list and clearly displayed benefits. Informs through ingredients/RDA, nutrients, energy value, allergens, storage/preparation/cooking instructions and origin or production claims such as farm, free-range and organic.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2019-HI-sectionb-vitaminsminerals-Q2 (LC Home Economics Higher 2019 — Q2; filed under hom-vitamins-minerals)
PART (a) [15 marks]
  Q: Discuss the nutritional significance of vegetables in the diet.
  A: Vegetables contain 2–8% mainly LBV protein, with pulses richer and soya HBV; olives, avocados and soya supply polyunsaturated fat; carbohydrate includes fibre, starch in potatoes/root vegetables/pulses and some sugar in carrots/onions. They provide beta-carotene, vitamin C, B vitamins, folate and antioxidant vitamins A, C and E, plus calcium, iron and traces of potassium, zinc and iodine.
PART (b)(i) [10 marks]
  Q: Suggest strategies for increasing a person’s daily consumption of vegetables.
  A: Eat vegetable soups; add vegetables to salads, savoury dishes and sauces; try new vegetables and cooking methods; spiralise vegetable noodles; substitute vegetables for carbohydrate foods such as cauliflower rice; serve vegetarian main courses; add greens to smoothies.
PART (b)(ii) [10 marks]
  Q: Compile a set of guidelines for cooking vegetables in order to maintain their nutritive value.
  A: Cook quickly in minimum water with the lid on; use cooking liquid in soups/sauces; do not use alkalis or copper/brass pans; stir-fry, steam or microwave; serve al dente.
PART (c) [15 marks]
  Q: Give an account of Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) under each of the following headings:
• properties
• sources
• biological functions.
  A: Properties: white crystalline acid with a sweet/sour taste; water-soluble; unstable and lost during storage, preparation, cooking and exposure to light/air; destroyed by alkalis, oxidase and dehydration; antioxidant. Sources: blackcurrants, kiwi, citrus, strawberries, melon, peppers, tomatoes, greens, potatoes and supplements. Functions: forms connective tissue/collagen; supports immunity; aids iron and calcium absorption; heals wounds; maintains blood vessels; antioxidant and protects HDL cholesterol.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2019-HI-sectionb-foodsafety-Q3 (LC Home Economics Higher 2019 — Q3; filed under hom-food-safety)
PART (a) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the main causes of food spoilage.
  A: Bacteria cause putrefaction and odours; moulds grow visibly and may produce mycotoxins; yeasts may be visible on food surfaces; physical damage causes bruising; fats become rancid; moisture loss causes shrinkage, wrinkling or hard surfaces; enzymes over-ripen and decompose fruit, brown cut foods, deteriorate fish and hydrolyse triglycerides.
PART (b) [20 marks]
  Q: Name and give details of one common food poisoning bacteria.
Refer to:
• conditions necessary for growth
• source
• reproduction/growth of bacteria
• high risk foods.
  A: Salmonella. Growth: saprophytic or parasitic; mesophilic, 20–45°C; requires moisture and time; facultative and favours neutral pH. Sources: human, animal and bird intestines, faeces and unwashed hands. Reproduction: asexual mitosis by binary fission. High-risk foods: raw/undercooked meat, fish and eggs and unpasteurised dairy products.
PART (c) [10 marks]
  Q: Outline the role of the Health Service Executive (HSE) in relation to food safety.
  A: Registers new food premises; inspects food premises and farms; handles hygiene complaints and closure orders; ensures food safety at public gatherings; monitors food/labelling/additives/contaminants legislation; responds to rapid alerts and recalls; educates and advises businesses on compliance.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2019-HI-sectionb-personalfinance-Q4 (LC Home Economics Higher 2019 — Q4; filed under hom-personal-finance)
PART (a) [20 marks]
  Q: Analyse the factors that affect household income.
  A: Household income is affected by age and experience; gender and equality; socio-economic group and educational opportunity; number of family members employed; full-/part-time, job-sharing, flexible or seasonal hours; culture; disability and addiction.
PART (b) [20 marks]
  Q: Suggest strategies that should be followed when planning a family budget to ensure the effective management of financial resources.
  A: List all income and planned fixed, irregular and discretionary expenditure; calculate weekly/monthly spending; allow for discretionary spending and changing circumstances; include long- and short-term savings and personal expenses; keep bills/receipts; regularly review the budget and allocate income across needs.
PART (c) [10 marks]
  Q: Outline the role of the Money Advice and Budgeting Service (MABS) for families who are experiencing financial difficulties.
  A: MABS is a free, independent advice service that helps with debt, money-management skills, budgets and repayment schedules; advises on financial products and interest rates; helps with mortgage debt resolution, lender negotiation, personal insolvency and income maximisation.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2019-HI-sectionb-personalfinance-Q5 (LC Home Economics Higher 2019 — Q5; filed under hom-sociological-concepts)
PART (a) [14 marks]
  Q: Explain the following sociological terms:
• socialisation
• socio-economic group
• social mobility
• social change
• kinship.
  A: Socialisation: lifelong learning of society’s norms, values, behaviour and skills, primarily in the family and secondarily through school, work and media. Socio-economic group: classification by economic/social position into strata. Social mobility: movement between socio-economic groups, enabled by education, income and occupation. Social change: societal change caused by law reform, technology or major events. Kinship: family/blood ties, shared ancestry or lineage.
PART (b) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe the characteristics of contemporary family structures.
  A: Contemporary structures include nuclear, extended, blended/reconstituted/step, lone-parent, same-sex-parent and childless families.
PART (c) [24 marks]
  Q: Discuss the social, economic and technological changes that affect families today.
  A: Social changes include decline of the extended family, urbanisation, gender equality, dual careers, separation/divorce, same-sex marriage/adoption, changing roles, fewer/later children and addiction. Economic changes include living/housing/childcare/education costs, state supports, dual incomes and mobility for work. Technological changes include communication/social media, home working and online services, household, entertainment and security technology.
