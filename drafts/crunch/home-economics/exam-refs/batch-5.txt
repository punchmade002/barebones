  A: Architect advises on design and oversees work; engineer supervises structure and solves building problems; surveyor assesses the site/building and recommends solutions; solicitor handles contracts/title/loan drawdown; builder agrees cost/timing, prepares site, subcontracts and resolves snags. Other services include interior designer, electrician, plumber and BER assessor.
PART (a)(ii) [20 marks]
  Q: Recommend a suitable floor covering that can be used in a home office.
Refer to:
• name of floor covering
• factors to consider when selecting a floor covering
• properties.
  A: Carpet/carpet tiles. Consider cost, office function, cleaning/maintenance, aesthetics, safety and subfloor. It insulates and absorbs noise, is non-slip, soft and warm underfoot, hard-wearing and easy to maintain.
PART (a)(iii) [15 marks]
  Q: Outline how colour, pattern and texture can be used to enhance the interior design of a room.
  A: Colour schemes/tints/tones can link rooms, alter proportions, highlight features and atmosphere; bright colours reflect light/enlarge, dark absorb light/cosy, with aspect considered. Pattern adds variety, contrast and interest through scale/bold/linear motifs. Contrasting smooth/rough, cold/warm, matt/shiny textures add interest.
PART (b)(i) [12 marks]
  Q: Describe the effects of poor ventilation in the home.
  A: Stale/stuffy air, high humidity and condensation; lingering smells and poor combustion; carbon monoxide build-up, low oxygen/high CO₂; worsened asthma/bronchitis; damp, mould and structural damage.
PART (b)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Recommend one artificial method of ventilation suitable for a kitchen.
Explain the underlying principle of this method.
  A: Cooker hood: an electric motor rotates a high-speed fan, creating suction that draws warm greasy odorous air. Ducted models expel it outside; filters trap grease. Ductless models filter fumes/grease, purify and recirculate air.
PART (c)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Set out details of solar energy as an energy supply source to the home.
Refer to:
• a system used in the home to utilise solar energy
• merits of using solar energy
• sustainability.
  A: PV panels use semiconductors to convert sunlight to electricity (optionally stored in battery); passive solar design uses south/west orientation and glazing; active panels heat water. Merits: hot water/space heat, lower bills/running costs and no smoke/CO₂. Sun is renewable and can supply energy indefinitely.
PART (c)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Explain, giving an example, the underlying principle of a method of insulation used in the home.
  A: Air, polystyrene, fibreglass, wool and cellulose are poor heat conductors; they impede heat escaping/redirect it indoors. Examples: fibreglass/foam, cavity-wall air/polystyrene, wool, carpet/underlay, double glazing, draught proofing, lined curtains, cylinder jacket or pipe sleeves.

PART (a)(ii) [10 marks]
  Q: Discuss how colour can be used to flatter body size and shape.
  A: Tonal shades add depth; dark navy/black absorb light and define/slim the silhouette; one colour creates height; contrasting/complementary colours can balance shape; shades can flatter hair, eyes and skin, with blues broadly flattering.
PART (b)(i) [6 marks]
  Q: Differentiate between regenerated and synthetic fibres.
  A: Regenerated fibres are made by chemically dissolving plant cellulose and reforming it as fibre. Synthetic fibres are 100% chemical polymers derived from oil, coal or other petrochemicals.
PART (b)(ii) [9 marks]
  Q: Explain filament modification, giving two examples.
  A: Techniques that alter/improve filament or fabric properties, e.g. blending, crimping, twisting, mercerising, brushing, flame/stain resistance or water repellence.
PART (c)(i) [9 marks]
  Q: Describe one method of modifying a commercial pattern to tailor it to the specific needs of an individual.
  A: To lengthen, cut through alteration lines, insert tissue and redraw seam/fitting lines. To shorten, fold along an alteration line by half the amount to remove. Similar adjustments can alter waist, hip, sleeve or bust.
PART (c)(ii) [6 marks]
  Q: Discuss how traditional crafts have influenced Irish design and the fashion industry.
  A: Hand-weaving/knitting inspires coatigans and coats with Aran stitches; Irish linen ranges from light tops/skirts to heavy coats/jackets; lace and crochet feature in wedding dresses.
