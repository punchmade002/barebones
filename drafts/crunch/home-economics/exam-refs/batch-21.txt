QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2021-HI-sectionb-foodsafety-Q3 (LC Home Economics Higher 2021 Paper BC — Q3; filed under hom-food-safety)
PART (a) [16 marks]
  Q: Discuss the importance of temperature control during the storage and cooking/reheating of food.
  A: Store perishables at 0–5°C, cool before refrigeration, fast-freeze at −25°C and store at −18°C; never refreeze thawed food. Defrost fully in the fridge; cook meat/poultry thoroughly and high-risk foods to 100°C; serve immediately or hold above 65°C, outside the 6–63°C danger zone; consume within 90 minutes and reheat rapidly above 100°C.
PART (b) [24 marks]
  Q: Describe the stages in a Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP) system for making a hot main course dish. Refer to potential hazards and the corresponding control measures that should be implemented.
  A: Purchase: hazards include damage, contamination and pests; use reputable shops and check separation, packs, dates and temperatures. Transport/storage: risk of bacterial growth/cross-contamination; use cool bags, minimise delay, cover food, chill below 5°C and freeze at −18°C. Preparation: prevent contamination with handwashing, colour-coded boards, separate utensils/foods, covered chilled storage and fridge defrosting. Cooking/serving/reheating: cook thoroughly (poultry ≥72°C), use clean utensils, serve/hold ≥63°C, reheat covered food above 100°C once only and hold no longer than 90 minutes.
PART (c) [10 marks]
  Q: Differentiate between infectious food poisoning and toxic food poisoning.
  A: Infectious poisoning: large numbers of pathogenic bacteria are eaten; endotoxins released on death are easily destroyed by proper cooking, with symptoms about 12 hours later, e.g. Salmonella, E. coli, Campylobacter, Listeria. Toxic poisoning: preformed exotoxin is eaten; it is difficult to destroy (may need 30 minutes boiling) and symptoms can occur within two hours, e.g. Staphylococcus aureus, C. botulinum or C. perfringens.

PART (b)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Discuss three properties of light and give an example of the application of each in the home.
  A: Reflection: light bounces from shiny surfaces, e.g. mirror/glazed tile/white ceiling. Refraction: bends through thick glass, e.g. glass bricks/frosted glass. Diffusion: scatters through translucent material or from matt surfaces, e.g. opaque shade. Absorption: dark matt surfaces absorb light, making rooms seem smaller. Dispersion: prism/crystal splits light into colours, e.g. crystal chandelier.
PART (b)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Identify and evaluate two contemporary developments in lighting technology.
  A: One development is LED lighting. A light-emitting diode converts electricity directly to light and uses much less energy than a traditional filament lamp. LEDs have a very long service life, start instantly, emit little heat and are available in different lumen outputs, colour temperatures, strips and compact fittings. They reduce running and replacement costs and are suitable for general, task or accent lighting. Initial cost can be higher, some cheap lamps have poor colour rendering or flicker, and a non-compatible dimmer can cause problems; the whole electronic lamp may also become waste when it fails.

A second development is smart lighting using networked lamps, electronic controls and presence/daylight sensors. Users can zone rooms and control brightness, colour and schedules by switch, app or voice. Occupancy sensors turn lights off in empty spaces, while daylight sensors reduce artificial output when sunlight is sufficient, saving energy. Timers improve security by simulating occupancy, and remote/voice control helps people with limited mobility. However, equipment and installation cost more, controls require correct setup and compatible devices, standby/network energy is still used, and loss of internet/power, software support or security/privacy weaknesses can reduce reliability. Both systems work best when planned for room function, glare control and actual user needs.
PART (c)(i) [18 marks]
  Q: Explain how water is supplied to and stored in the home.
Refer to:
• urban or rural supply
• stopcock
• storage tank/cistern.
  A: Urban mains travel from reservoir through service pipe; external/internal stopcocks isolate supply; kitchen sink gets drinking water and a branch fills the attic cistern supplying other taps, toilets and hot-water system. Rural water may be public or a treated lined well/spring pumped and filtered. Stopcock is the shut-off valve. High attic cistern uses pressure; a ball valve controls level and it stores cold water.
PART (c)(ii) [12 marks]
  Q: Identify inefficient uses of water in the home. Suggest strategies for conserving and managing this resource.
  A: Waste occurs through toilets, appliances and running/leaking taps. Fit dual flush/rainwater harvesting; repair leaks; turn taps off and use a basin; choose efficient appliances, run full loads/economy cycles; fill kettle only as needed; shower instead of bathing.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2021-HI-sectionc-personalfinance-Q3 (LC Home Economics Higher 2021 Paper BC — Q3; filed under hom-unemployment-poverty)
PART (a)(i) [8 marks]
  Q: Define poverty.
  A: Lacking adequate food, clothing or shelter and having insufficient income/resources for an acceptable standard of living, causing exclusion from normal social participation.
PART (a)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: In relation to poverty explain each of the following:
• relative poverty
• the poverty line
• the poverty trap.
  A: Relative poverty: living below society’s accepted basic standard and unable to participate normally. Poverty line: estimated minimum income needed for basic food, clothing and shelter. Poverty trap: returning to low-paid work may remove benefits, leaving welfare more rewarding and encouraging dependence.
PART (a)(iii) [12 marks]
  Q: Discuss the extent and distribution of poverty in Ireland today.
  A: Extent fell after the 1960s/70s, rose in the 1980s recession, fell in the Celtic Tiger era and rose again with high housing/living costs and COVID-19; over 20% of households and one in four children were cited below the line. Distribution includes women over 65, home workers, older/disabled/unemployed/low-paid people, lone parents, Travellers, ethnic minorities and families in deprivation cycles.
PART (a)(iv) [12 marks]
  Q: Name and give details of one social welfare assistance/benefit available to people who are experiencing poverty.
  A: Jobseeker's Allowance is a social-assistance payment for an unemployed person who does not qualify for Jobseeker's Benefit or whose benefit has ended. It is means-tested rather than based on sufficient PRSI contributions. The applicant must normally be unemployed, capable of work, available for full-time work and genuinely seeking employment; residence and means conditions also apply. The Department assesses the claimant's own income and savings and, where relevant, a spouse's or partner's means. The weekly rate may include increases for a qualified adult and dependent children, so household circumstances affect payment. It provides a basic income for food, housing and other essential expenditure while work is sought and can therefore reduce the immediate effects of poverty. Claimants must report changes and continue to satisfy the availability and job-seeking conditions.
PART (b)(i) [15 marks]
  Q: Identify and elaborate on the factors that affect an individual’s attitude to work.
  A: Socio-economic group and education affect aspirations, qualifications, job status and advancement; extrinsic rewards such as wages/bonuses may motivate repetitive work, while intrinsic fulfilment, creativity, pride and status improve attitude. Work ethic, identity/status, flexible and pleasant conditions, background, experience, peers and social contact also influence it.
PART (b)(ii) [15 marks]
  Q: Discuss giving examples, how a community can benefit from the work of volunteers.
  A: Volunteers quickly support older, disabled, disadvantaged or homeless people; complement statutory bodies and provide personal local services; fill gaps in state provision; improve environments and reduce poverty/social problems; build community spirit/friendship, act before problems grow and attract attention leading to reform.
PART (c)(i) [15 marks]
  Q: Explain the importance of the distribution of work tasks and childcare responsibilities in families.
  A: Shared childcare strengthens parent-child relationships; shared work and childcare reduce overload, conflict and stress; parents model gender equality; distributing cooking, cleaning and laundry builds children’s responsibility and pride.
PART (c)(ii) [15 marks]
  Q: Discuss how improvements in the provision of education have impacted on family life.
  A: Qualified preschool/Naíonra staff and after-school clubs support working families; SEN schools, SNAs, multisensory rooms and autism units support additional needs; fee abolition, grants and access schemes widen third-level access; adult/second-chance, PLC, SOLAS, VTOS and distance learning improve self-esteem, skills and employment prospects.

QUESTION homeeconomics-pp-2021-HI-sectionc-dairyeggs-Q4 (LC Home Economics Higher 2021 Paper BC — Q4; filed under hom-dairy-eggs)
PART (a)(i) [20 marks]
  Q: Discuss the nutritional significance of milk in the diet of young children.
  A: Milk supplies 3.5% HBV protein for growth/repair; 0.2–4% easily digested saturated fat; 4–5% lactose but little starch/fibre; vitamins A, B and D (whole) and B vitamins (skimmed), but little C; calcium/phosphorus for bones/teeth but little iron; 87% water for hydration. It may be fortified and should be balanced with starch/fibre, vitamin C and iron foods.
PART (a)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Describe one process used by manufacturers to extend the shelf life of milk.
Refer to:
• name of the process
• how the process is carried out
• packaging and labelling.
  A: Pasteurisation: homogenise milk, heat to 72–75°C for 15–25 seconds and rapidly cool to 10°C. Package in glass bottles, metal cans, plastic cartons or waxed cardboard/carbon-neutral packs in stated quantities. Label milk type, brand, quantity, nutrition, date and storage instructions.
PART (a)(iii) [12 marks]
  Q: Outline the measures (initiatives) taken by the dairy industry to meet current consumer trends.
  A: Milk: low-/semi-/skimmed, fortified, varied sizes and infant formula. Cheese: low-fat, grated/sliced/cubed/snack/flavoured, family and resealable packs. Yoghurt: flavours, functional/probiotic, set/drink/stirred/snack/frozen/Greek/unsweetened. Butter: sustainable packs, low-fat, unsalted, flavoured/spreadable. Cream: single/double/sour/flavoured/whipped and varied sizes.
PART (b)(i) [20 marks]
  Q: In relation to diabetes, give an account of:
• types of diabetes
• dietary requirements that should be followed for individuals with diabetes.
  A: Type 1: deficient insulin production and daily insulin required. Type 2: ineffective insulin use, linked largely to excess weight/inactivity and now seen in children. Gestational: hyperglycaemia first recognised in pregnancy. Diet: three regular meals; reduce sugar/use sweeteners; 3–5 fruit/vegetable servings; low-GI diet; reduce saturated fat; increase fibre and starchy carbohydrate.
PART (b)(ii) [10 marks]
  Q: Outline the uses of sweeteners in food production.
  A: Sweeten and improve palatability; preserve jam; manufacture sweets; replace sugar in diabetic foods; humectant preventing cakes drying; bulking agent; soft drinks; slimming/low-calorie recipes.
PART (c)(i) [12 marks]
  Q: Name and describe two methods of consumer research.
  A: Desk research: collects existing data from agencies/internet quickly and cheaply; typically broad quantitative data such as phone/written surveys. Field research: primary, detailed qualitative information gathered through observation, interviews, surveys, vox pops, panels and focus groups to study behaviour/attitudes.
PART (c)(ii) [18 marks]
  Q: Outline the benefits of consumer research for both the consumer and the retailer.
  A: Consumers’ requirements are identified, product quality can improve and competition may lower prices. Retailers identify the likely user, how well the product meets needs, and the size and location of the target market.
