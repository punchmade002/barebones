TARGET pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1: 1. Benefits of physical activity participation
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.b] Physical activity runs on a continuum, from healthy-lifestyle activity to competing to win. The other concepts describe its settings and purposes.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.0] Physical activity: any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that uses energy. Examples: a walk to school; a professional match.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.1] Play: spontaneous, freely chosen activity done for its own sake, with flexible or self-made rules, mainly associated with children. Examples: chasing games in a playground; kicking a ball around a park with self-made goals.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.2] Leisure and recreation: activity chosen in free time for enjoyment, relaxation and wellbeing, with little emphasis on competition. Examples: a walk; a leisurely cycle.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.3] Physical education: planned, structured learning in, through and about physical activity within the school curriculum, taught by a qualified teacher. Examples: a PE class learning the technique of a striking action; a lesson on how the body responds to exercise.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.4] Mass-participation sports: organised events in which large numbers of people of mixed ability take part at the same time, usually for personal challenge or charity rather than to win. Examples: city marathons; parkrun.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.5] Outdoor and adventure activities: activities in the natural environment that involve challenge, managed risk and uncertainty.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.6] Land-based outdoor and adventure activities: hill walking, orienteering, rock-climbing and mountain biking.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.7] Water-based outdoor and adventure activities: canoeing, kayaking, sailing and surfing.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.0.8] Sport: organised, competitive physical activity governed by formal rules and a governing body, with an emphasis on performance and winning. Examples: a club league in Gaelic football; a national championship in swimming.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.0] Lower risk of heart disease and stroke.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.1] Lower risk of type 2 diabetes.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.2] Lower risk of obesity, which supports weight management.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.3] Lower risk of some cancers.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.4] Stronger bones and a lower risk of osteoporosis.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.5] Better fitness, which makes everyday physical tasks easier.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.1.6] Better sleep.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.2.0] Improved mood and self-esteem.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.2.1] Reduced stress and anxiety.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.2.2] Reduced symptoms of depression.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.2.3] Better concentration.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.2.4] Enjoyment and a sense of achievement, which sustain motivation to keep taking part.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.3.0] Making friends and belonging to a group.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.3.1] Teamwork, communication and leadership skills develop through group activity.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.3.2] A social outlet and a positive use of free time.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.3.3] Reduced isolation, because activity brings people together regularly.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.3.4] Community cohesion, as people from different backgrounds meet through shared activity.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.0] For society: lower healthcare costs, because fewer people need treatment.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.1] For society: fewer sick days and higher productivity at work.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.2] For society: employment in the sport and leisure industry.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.3] For society: income from tourism and events.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.4] For society: the value of volunteering, which keeps clubs and events running.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.5] For the individual: lower medical costs, since fewer treatments are needed.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.6] For the individual: career opportunities in coaching, officiating, teaching and the leisure industry.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.4.7] For the individual: free activities such as walking cost less than health treatment for conditions that inactivity can cause.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.0] Children: motor-skill development, such as running, jumping, throwing and catching.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.1] Children: healthy growth, supported by play and daily activity.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.2] Children: social skills learned through play with others.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.3] Adolescents: self-esteem and body image, which are formed during the teenage years.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.4] Adolescents: stress relief during the pressures of school and exams.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.5] Adolescents: friendships and belonging to a peer group.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.5.6] Adolescents: lifelong habits that can carry into adult life.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.0] Adults: weight and disease prevention, which protects health across working life.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.1] Adults: stress relief from work and family demands.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.2] Adults: social contact outside work.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.3] Adults: work productivity, which keeps people able to meet job demands.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.4] Older adults: balance and strength, which help prevent falls.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.5] Older adults: bone density, which helps protect against fractures.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.6] Older adults: independence in daily life for longer.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.7] Older adults: social contact and reduced loneliness.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.6.8] Older adults: cognitive health, including thinking and memory.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.7.b] Get Ireland Active sets the national activity guidelines.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.7.0] Children and young people: at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity every day.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.7.1] Adults: at least 30 minutes of moderate activity on five days a week, which is 150 minutes a week.
[pe-promoting-physical-activity-s1.7.2] Older adults: the adult target of 30 minutes of moderate activity on five days a week, plus balance and strength activities.
