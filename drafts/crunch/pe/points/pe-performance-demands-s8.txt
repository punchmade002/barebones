TARGET pe-performance-demands-s8: 8. Diet, nutrition and hydration
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.0] Carbohydrate: the body's main energy source for moderate and high-intensity exercise; stored as glycogen in the muscles and liver. Sources: bread, pasta, rice, potatoes, cereals and fruit.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.1] Protein: growth and repair of muscle and other tissue. Sources: meat, fish, eggs, dairy and pulses.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.2] Fat: a concentrated energy source for low-intensity, long-duration exercise; also insulates the body and carries fat-soluble vitamins. Sources: oils, nuts, dairy and oily fish.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.3] Iron: carries oxygen in red blood cells; a deficiency causes tiredness and reduces endurance.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.4] Calcium: needed for strong bones and muscle contraction.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.5] B vitamins: help release energy from food.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.6] Fibre: keeps the digestive system healthy, but large amounts just before exercise can cause discomfort.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.0.7] A balanced diet for a performer is mostly carbohydrate-based, with enough protein, limited saturated fat, plenty of fruit and vegetables, and energy intake matched to the training load.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.1.0] A meal about 3 to 4 hours before: high in carbohydrate, moderate in protein, low in fat and fibre, so glycogen stores are topped up and digestion is complete, such as pasta with chicken.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.1.1] A small carbohydrate snack about 1 to 2 hours before if needed, such as a banana or toast.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.1.2] Familiar foods only, to avoid stomach upsets on competition day.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.1.3] Plenty of fluid in the hours beforehand.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.1.4] Carbohydrate loading for endurance events lasting more than about 90 minutes: carbohydrate intake is increased over the days before while training tapers, to maximise glycogen stores.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.2.0] During short events: water is usually enough.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.2.1] During events lasting more than about an hour: carbohydrate from a sports drink, gel or banana, with fluid, maintains blood glucose and delays fatigue.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.2.2] After: carbohydrate restores muscle glycogen and protein repairs muscle, ideally within about 30 to 60 minutes, such as chocolate milk or a chicken and rice meal.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.2.3] After: rehydrate with fluid and electrolytes to replace sweat losses.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.2.4] Match-day example for Gaelic football: a pasta lunch three to four hours before throw-in; a banana an hour before; water and a sports drink at half-time; chocolate milk straight after and a chicken and rice dinner that evening.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.0] Water regulates body temperature through sweating, transports nutrients and maintains blood volume.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.1] Effects of dehydration: raised heart rate and body temperature, fatigue, headache, cramp, reduced concentration and poorer skill.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.2] A fluid loss of about 2% of body mass can impair performance.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.3] Fluid needs are higher in long or high-intensity activities, in hot or humid conditions, in indoor halls, when wearing heavy kit, and at altitude; swimmers also sweat and need fluid.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.4] Monitoring: urine colour (pale straw is good), weighing before and after exercise, and thirst, which is a late sign.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.5] Replace about 1.5 times the fluid lost: 1 kg of body mass lost needs about 1.5 litres.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.3.6] Drinking large amounts of plain water in very long events can dilute blood sodium (hyponatraemia), so drinks should include electrolytes.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.4.0] Hypotonic: a lower concentration than body fluid; absorbed fastest, for rapid rehydration.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.4.1] Isotonic: about 6 to 8% carbohydrate, the same concentration as body fluid; provides fluid and energy together and is the most common drink during exercise.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.4.2] Hypertonic: a higher carbohydrate concentration; provides energy, usually after exercise.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.4.3] Benefits: replace fluid, electrolytes (sodium and potassium) and carbohydrate in one convenient drink.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.4.4] Disadvantages: sugar and calories (dental decay and weight gain for inactive users), cost, and unnecessary for short or low-intensity activity.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.0] Protein powders: muscle repair and convenience after training.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.1] Creatine: raises phosphocreatine stores in muscle, improving repeated short, maximal efforts.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.2] Caffeine: a stimulant that can reduce perceived effort and improve alertness and endurance.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.3] Energy gels: rapid carbohydrate during long events.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.4] Vitamin and mineral supplements: to correct a diagnosed deficiency, such as iron.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.5] Challenge: contamination with prohibited substances; under strict liability the athlete is responsible for anything found in their sample.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.6] Challenge: side effects, such as stomach upset from caffeine or weight gain from creatine.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.7] Challenge: cost, unregulated claims, and possible dependence.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.5.8] Challenge: usually unnecessary when the diet is balanced (food first); batch-tested products reduce but do not remove the risk.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.6.0] Cover a training day and a competition day, with meals and snacks before, during and after activity and a fluid plan.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.6.1] Give a rationale for each choice, linked to the activity's duration, intensity and main energy system.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.6.2] Aspect 1, pre-event carbohydrate: maximises glycogen for high-intensity efforts.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.6.3] Aspect 2, post-event carbohydrate and protein: restores glycogen and repairs muscle for the next session.
[pe-performance-demands-s8.6.4] Aspect 3, hydration plan: prevents dehydration and keeps concentration and skill levels high.
