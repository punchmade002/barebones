TARGET geo-option-global-interdependence-s4 (part 1 of 2): 4. Sustainable development as a model for the future
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.0.b] Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs (Brundtland, 1987). It is the model this whole section asks you to judge.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.0.0] It has three pillars, environmental, economic and social, which must be balanced against each other, because gains in one at the expense of the others do not count as sustainable.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.0.1] It contrasts with growth-only models, which measure development by rising output (the total goods and services produced), such as Rostow’s stages ending in mass consumption. There is more on these models in Views of development and underdevelopment.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.b] The idea grew over several decades, from a warning about limits in 1972 to global targets in 2015. Knowing the sequence lets you give a dated origin for the model.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.0] “The Limits to Growth” (1972), a report for the Club of Rome, used computer models to warn that unchecked population and economic growth would exhaust resources within about a century.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.1] The Brundtland Report, “Our Common Future”, was produced by the UN World Commission on Environment and Development in 1987. It gave the definition above.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.2] The Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro (1992) produced Agenda 21 (a global action plan) and the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (a treaty on tackling climate change).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.3] The Millennium Development Goals (eight goals, 2000–2015) were followed by the Sustainable Development Goals: 17 goals adopted in September 2015, with a target year of 2030.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.4] The Paris Agreement (2015) committed countries to tackle climate change.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.1.5] The world is off track to meet most of the Sustainable Development Goals, so the targets are not being reached on time.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.2.b] The case for making sustainability a priority rests on the fact that today’s growth can undermine tomorrow’s. Use these reasons when an essay asks whether it should be a priority.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.2.0] Resources are finite (there is a fixed amount) and are being overused, so they cannot support unlimited use.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.2.1] Climate change, biodiversity loss (the decline of plant and animal species) and inequality between and within generations threaten future development.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.2.2] The poorest people suffer first when resources are degraded, because they depend most directly on land, water and fuel and have least to fall back on.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.2.3] Growth that destroys the resources it depends on cannot last, because the base of that growth is used up.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.3.b] The model is widely accepted but hard to apply. A good evaluation says where it falls short as well as where it works.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.3.0] It is vague and open to greenwashing, where firms and governments claim sustainability without real change, because nobody can say exactly what counts as sustainable.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.3.1] The pillars conflict: jobs can be at odds with protecting the environment, so balancing all three is difficult.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.3.2] Poorer countries argue that rich countries grew without limits and now ask them to slow down, which they see as unfair.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.3.3] The costs of change fall unequally, and progress on the Sustainable Development Goals is behind schedule.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.4.b] If development is not just rising output, it needs other yardsticks. Two alternatives measure wellbeing and environmental pressure instead.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.4.0] Bhutan’s Gross National Happiness, put forward in the 1970s by King Jigme Singye Wangchuck, measures wellbeing beyond income.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.4.1] The ecological footprint measures the land and resources a population needs to support its consumption.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.5.b] Sustainable resource use means using resources so that they last. The principles below differ for resources that regrow and those that do not.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.5.0] Renewable resources (those that regenerate, such as fish and forests) should be used no faster than they regenerate. This rate is the sustainable yield.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.5.1] Non-renewable resources (those that cannot be replaced, such as fossil fuels) should be used efficiently and replaced by alternatives where possible.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.5.2] Reduce, reuse and recycle waste, so that fewer resources are taken and less waste is dumped.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.5.3] Protect biodiversity (the variety of living things).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.5.4] Involve local people in decisions about resources, since they live with the results.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.b] Fish are a renewable resource, but only if catches stay below what the stock can replace. Irish fishing policy has been shaped by what happened when this was ignored.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.0] Irish fishing has been shaped by past overfishing in EU waters, which is why rules now limit catches.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.1] The Common Fisheries Policy of 1983 was reformed in 2013, introducing maximum sustainable yield (the largest catch that can be taken without shrinking the stock) and a landing obligation (a discard ban), phased in during 2015–2019.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.2] The Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority was set up in 2007.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.3] Killybegs, Co. Donegal, is Ireland’s largest fishing port.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.4] The North Sea herring fishery was closed from 1977 to the early 1980s and recovered under quotas (limits on catch), showing that management can work.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.6.5] The Grand Banks cod moratorium (a ban on fishing) of 2 July 1992 cost about 30,000 jobs off Newfoundland and is the warning for overfishing.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.7.b] Bord na Móna has moved from burning peat to renewable energy under its “Brown to Green” strategy. Peat is partly decayed plant matter dug from bogs, once burned for electricity.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.7.0] Bord na Móna was founded in 1946.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.7.1] The Lanesborough and Shannonbridge peat stations closed in December 2020, and peat harvesting ended in January 2021.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.7.2] Cutaway bogs (bogs where the peat has been removed) are being rehabilitated and rewetted, and peatland is valued as a store of carbon.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.7.3] Lough Boora Discovery Park, Co. Offaly, is one of the rehabilitated sites.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.7.4] Bellacorick, Co. Mayo, has wind farms on cutaway bog.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.8.b] Moving electricity generation from fossil fuels to renewables is a central part of sustainable resource use. Ireland has set targets in law.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.8.0] Wind provides about a third of Irish electricity, and renewables about two-fifths (early 2020s).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.8.1] The Climate Action Plan targets 80% renewable electricity by 2030 and at least 5 GW (gigawatts, a measure of power capacity) of offshore wind by 2030.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.8.2] The Climate Act 2021 requires a 51% cut in emissions by 2030 (from 2018) and net zero (no more greenhouse gas added than removed) by 2050.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.8.3] The East–West Interconnector (2012), a cable that lets electricity flow between countries, links Ireland and Britain, and the Celtic Interconnector to France is due in the late 2020s.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.b] Farming shapes most Irish land, so how it is run matters for sustainability. Schemes reward good practice, but dairy expansion pulls the other way.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.0] BurrenLIFE (2005–2010) and then the Burren Programme pay farmers according to habitat scores for each field, so they are paid for results.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.1] Agri-environment schemes (payments to farmers for protecting the environment) have included REPS (1994), GLAS (2015) and ACRES (2023).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.2] Origin Green, Bord Bia’s sustainability programme, began in 2012.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.3] Milk quotas (limits on how much milk each farm could produce) were abolished on 1 April 2015, and the dairy herd grew strongly afterwards.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.4] Agriculture produces over a third of Irish greenhouse gas emissions.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.5] Only about half of Irish waters are in good or high ecological status (EPA, 2016–2021).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.9.6] Dairy expansion is therefore in tension with water quality and emissions targets.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.10.b] Sustainability also depends on changing everyday behaviour. Two Irish measures show how charges and deposits can cut waste.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.10.0] The plastic bag levy (a charge on each bag) began in March 2002 at 15 cent, and plastic bag use fell by over 90%.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.10.1] The Deposit Return Scheme (Re-turn) began on 1 February 2024.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.11.b] Only about 11% of Ireland is forest, and much of that is not native. Forestry is a final Irish example of resource use.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.11.0] Forest cover in Ireland is about 11%, mostly conifer plantations of Sitka spruce.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.11.1] Coillte was set up in 1988 to manage state forests.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.11.2] Remnants of native woodland, such as the Killarney oakwoods, show what Ireland has lost.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.11.3] Ireland needs more native broadleaf woodland, because plantations of one conifer support less variety of life than native woods.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.11.4] Managed Scandinavian forests are another example of forest use. There is more on this in An interdependent global economy.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.b] Costa Rica, in Central America between Nicaragua and Panama, shows how a country can reverse resource loss.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.0] In the 1970s and 1980s forests were cleared for cattle pasture, and by the late 1980s forest covered only about a fifth of the country.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.1] The Payment for Environmental Services programme, started in 1997 and run by the national forestry fund FONAFIFO, pays landowners to protect and replant forest, so keeping trees earns income.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.2] The programme is paid for largely by a tax on fuel.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.3] Forest cover has since risen to over half of the country.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.4] About a quarter of the land is in national parks and other protected areas.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.12.5] Ecotourism (tourism based on nature) is one of the largest sources of foreign income, giving forests an economic value.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.13.b] Costa Rica’s sustainability also shows in how it generates power and spends public money.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.13.0] Since 2015 almost all of Costa Rica’s electricity has come from renewable sources (over 98% in most years, but about 95% in 2023, when drought cut hydropower), mainly hydroelectric (from flowing water), with geothermal (from underground heat) and wind.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.13.1] Costa Rica abolished its army in 1948 and spends instead on education and health.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.13.2] In 2021 Costa Rica won the first Earthshot Prize (Protect and Restore Nature) for its forest recovery.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.14.b] Costa Rica is not a perfect model. Two weaknesses are worth including in an evaluation.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.14.0] Transport still runs mainly on imported oil, so it has not left fossil fuels behind.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.14.1] Tourism puts pressure on protected areas.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.15.b] Where resources are used unsustainably, the damage can be severe and lasting. Two cases serve as warnings.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.15.0] The Aral Sea: rivers were diverted from the 1960s to irrigate Soviet cotton, and the sea shrank to a fraction of its size; the Kok-Aral dam was built in 2005.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.15.1] Amazon deforestation is another warning of unsustainable use. There is more on this in An interdependent global economy.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.b] Fair trade exists because farmers in poorer countries often get a poor deal from world markets. The six points below are the problems it tries to solve.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.0] Commodity prices (prices of raw products such as coffee and cocoa) are low and unstable, and small farmers receive a tiny share of the final price.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.1] Middlemen (traders between the farmer and the buyer) take a large share of the value of farmers’ crops.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.2] Poor working conditions and child labour are common in producing countries.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.3] Terms of trade decline as the prices of raw materials fall relative to manufactured goods, so the same crop buys less each year.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.4] Tariff escalation (tariffs, or import taxes, that rise as a product is processed): coffee beans enter rich markets with low tariffs, but roasted, processed coffee faces higher ones.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.16.5] Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana grow over half of the world’s cocoa, mostly exported as beans.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.17.b] Fair trade is a response to those problems, with a short but dated history.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.17.0] Fair trade is a trading partnership based on dialogue, transparency and respect that seeks greater equity (fairness) in international trade.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.17.1] The first fair trade label, Max Havelaar, was launched in the Netherlands in 1988 for Mexican coffee.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.17.2] Fairtrade International coordinates the Fairtrade Mark, the certification label on products.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.17.3] Garstang, Lancashire, England, declared itself the world’s first Fairtrade town in 2000.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.b] Fairtrade protects producers through prices, contracts, co-operatives and standards. Each feature solves part of the problem set out above.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.0] The Fairtrade Minimum Price is a floor that covers the cost of sustainable production.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.1] The minimum is a floor, not a guaranteed higher price: when the market price is above the minimum, producers receive the market price plus the premium.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.2] The Fairtrade Premium is an extra sum paid to the co-operative, spent on projects the members choose democratically.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.3] Long-term contracts and pre-finance give producers credit before the harvest, so they are not forced into debt to middlemen.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.4] Producers are organised in democratic co-operatives (groups owned and run by their members).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.5] Labour standards ban forced and child labour, protect the freedom to join unions and require safe conditions.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.6] Environmental standards restrict pesticides, protect water and forests and ban GM (genetically modified) crops.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.18.7] The Fairtrade Mark is a guarantee to consumers that these standards have been met.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.19.b] Fairtrade covers many crops and reaches a large number of people, but it remains small in world terms.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.19.0] Fairtrade products include crops such as coffee, cocoa, tea, bananas, sugar, cotton and flowers.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.19.1] Close to 2 million farmers and workers belong to Fairtrade-certified producer organisations in about 70 countries.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.b] Kuapa Kokoo is a Fairtrade cocoa farmers’ co-operative in Ghana, the world’s second-largest cocoa producer. It stands out because the farmers also part-own a chocolate brand.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.0] Kuapa Kokoo, meaning “good cocoa farmer” in Twi, was founded in 1993.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.1] Divine Chocolate was set up in Britain in 1998, part-owned by Kuapa Kokoo. Since 2020 the German chocolate maker Ludwig Weinrich has held the majority; Kuapa Kokoo kept about 20% of the shares and seats on the board.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.2] Farmers receive a share of Divine’s profits as well as the Fairtrade price and premium.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.3] Premium spending has included wells for clean water, school buildings and mobile health clinics.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.4] Kuapa Kokoo encourages women to take leadership roles in its village societies.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.20.5] Farmers share in the profits of the brand, not just the beans they sell, so some of the value added in making chocolate stays with them.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.21.b] The Oromia union in Ethiopia shows how a co-operative can sell directly to buyers.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.21.0] The union was founded in 1999 and led by Tadesse Meskela.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.21.1] It sells members’ coffee directly to buyers, including Fairtrade coffee, cutting out middlemen who would otherwise take a share of the price.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.21.2] Its premium has paid for schools, clinics and water.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.21.3] Its story is told in the documentary “Black Gold” (2006).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.21.4] Ethiopia is the original home of arabica coffee.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.b] Fair trade can contribute to development by changing the position of producers in trade. This is the core of an essay on what fair trade can contribute.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.0] Higher and more stable income gives farmers money to invest and plan.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.1] The premium funds community projects such as education, health and clean water.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.2] Democratic co-operatives empower producers (give them control) to take decisions together.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.3] Women’s participation in co-operatives gives women a voice and income.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.4] Organic farming and reduced clearing of land help environmental sustainability.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.5] For example, child labour can be reduced when families earn a fairer income, because children are less needed to earn money.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.6] Producers learn processing and marketing skills.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.22.7] Fair trade raises consumer awareness in rich countries and pressures large firms to change.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.23.b] Fair trade is also visible in Irish towns, schools and shops.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.23.0] Fairtrade Ireland licenses the Fairtrade Mark in Ireland.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.23.1] Clonakilty, Co. Cork, became Ireland’s first Fairtrade town in 2003.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.23.2] Towns, schools and universities can apply for Fairtrade status.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.23.3] Fairtrade Fortnight takes place each year in late February and March.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.23.4] Supermarkets stock Fairtrade ranges, and Irish NGOs (non-governmental organisations, voluntary groups) promote fair trade.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.b] Fair trade helps those it reaches, but it has real limits. A balanced essay includes them.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.0] Fair trade is only a tiny share of world trade.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.1] Higher prices put off some consumers.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.2] Certification costs exclude the poorest farmers.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.3] A 2014 study by the School of Oriental and African Studies (SOAS), University of London, of Ethiopia and Uganda found that hired labourers on some Fairtrade-certified farms were paid less than those on comparable non-certified farms; Fairtrade disputed parts of the findings.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.4] Benefits go mainly to co-operative members rather than hired labourers.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.5] Market prices sometimes exceed the minimum price, so the floor then gives no extra protection.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.6] Fair trade does not change the trade rules, such as subsidies and tariff escalation.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.24.7] Cadbury Dairy Milk carried the Fairtrade Mark from 2009 and switched in 2017 to Mondelēz’s own Cocoa Life scheme in Britain and Ireland. Most value is still added in rich countries unless producers process their own crops, which Divine Chocolate does.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.25.b] Fair trade is a model of sustainable, just trade that improves the lives of those involved and shows what is possible.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.25.0] Its impact on development depends on its scale and on wider changes in trade rules.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.26.b] Sustainable development is about fairness as well as resources. A project cannot be called sustainable if it harms the people in its way.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.26.0] Development is not sustainable if it excludes or harms groups of people.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.26.1] Minority groups often lack political power, so their land and rights are taken for development projects such as dams, mines, plantations and wind farms.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.27.b] International law sets out the rights of minorities and indigenous peoples (the original inhabitants of a region). Three documents form the framework.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.27.0] The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted on 10 December 1948.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.27.1] ILO Indigenous and Tribal Peoples Convention (No. 169) was adopted in 1989.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.27.2] The UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples was adopted by the UN General Assembly on 13 September 2007; it includes free, prior and informed consent, meaning communities must agree in advance and with full information.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.28.b] Indigenous peoples of the Amazon in Brazil show how development can take land and harm health. Their forests are also central to sustainable resource use.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.28.0] Indigenous peoples have lost land to ranching, soya, logging, mining and dams.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.28.1] Brazil’s Census 2022 counted about 1.7 million indigenous people, under 1% of the population.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.28.2] In 2023 the Brazilian government declared a public health emergency in Yanomami territory after illegal gold mining caused malnutrition and disease.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.28.3] The Belo Monte dam on the Xingu River, completed in 2019, was opposed by the Kayapó and displaced thousands of people.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.28.4] Indigenous territories are among the best-protected forests, linking justice to sustainable resource use.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.29.b] Chico Mendes became a symbol of the struggle to protect the Amazon forest and the people who live from it.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.29.0] Chico Mendes, a rubber tapper (a worker who collects rubber from trees), was murdered on 22 December 1988 at Xapuri, Acre.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.29.1] Extractive reserves, where forest is harvested sustainably, were set up after his death.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.b] Irish Travellers are an ethnic group, not a race. Their case shows how inequality and discrimination block sustainable development for a minority.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.0] Irish Travellers were formally recognised as an ethnic minority on 1 March 2017.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.1] The 2022 Census counted about 33,000 Travellers, under 1% of the population.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.2] The All Ireland Traveller Health Study (2010) found that Traveller men lived about 15 years less than men in the general population, and Traveller women about 11.5 years less.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.3] The same study found that the suicide rate among Traveller men was about six times the general rate.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.4] The 2016 Census found that about 80% of Travellers in the labour force were unemployed.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.5] Accommodation shortages persist, and local authority Traveller accommodation budgets have gone unspent.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.6] Discrimination in services is outlawed by the Equal Status Acts (2000).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.30.7] Responses include equality law, ethnic status and the National Traveller and Roma Inclusion Strategy 2017–2021.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.31.b] The Roma are a European minority whose case reached the European Court of Human Rights. They show justice issues beyond Ireland.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.31.0] The Roma are Europe’s largest ethnic minority, about 10–12 million people (Council of Europe estimate), many in Romania, Bulgaria, Hungary, Slovakia and Spain.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.31.1] The Roma face poverty, segregated housing and schools, and discrimination; they were persecuted in the Holocaust.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.31.2] In D.H. and Others v Czech Republic (European Court of Human Rights, 2007) the court ruled that placing Roma children in “special schools” for children with learning disabilities was unlawful discrimination.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.31.3] EU frameworks include the Framework for National Roma Integration Strategies (2011) and the Roma strategic framework for equality, inclusion and participation (2020–2030).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.b] The Fosen case shows that green development must also be just. A renewable energy project can still harm a minority.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.0] The Sámi are the indigenous people of northern Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia’s Kola Peninsula; reindeer herding is central to their culture.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.1] Fosen, in central Norway, is one of Europe’s largest onshore wind farms.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.2] On 11 October 2021 Norway’s Supreme Court ruled that the turbines, built on reindeer grazing land, violated the herders’ right to enjoy their culture.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.3] The turbines were not removed and kept running after the ruling, which was the cause of the protests.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.4] In 2023 Sámi activists, joined by Greta Thunberg, blocked government buildings in Oslo in protest.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.32.5] In 2023–24 the herders reached agreements with the state and the wind-farm owners.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.33.b] Two more minority groups belong to other chapters of the course, where they are taught in full.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.33.0] The Rohingya and Uyghurs are discussed in An interdependent global economy.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.33.1] Aboriginal Australians are discussed in Racial groupings.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.34.b] Self-reliance is the last aspect of the model: development as self-help, in which people rely on themselves rather than outsiders.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.34.0] Self-reliance means communities and countries meet their own needs with their own resources, skills and decisions.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.34.1] It reduces dependence on aid, loans, foreign companies and imports, and links to bottom-up development (development that starts with local people) and appropriate technology (technology suited to local needs and skills).
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s4.35.b] Tanzania under Julius Nyerere, its first president, is a mixed example: strong social gains, but economic failure.
