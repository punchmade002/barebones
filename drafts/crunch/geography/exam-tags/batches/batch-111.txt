PART 5B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region – India:
India is a subcontinental region of about 3.3 million square kilometres.
It is bounded by the Himalayas to the north, the Arabian Sea to the west and the Bay of Bengal to the east.
Its population is very unevenly distributed because of a mix of physical and human factors.
Dense area – the Ganges plain:
The Ganges plain is densely populated, with Uttar Pradesh and Bihar as major population centres.
Himalayan rivers deposit fertile alluvium on the plain, which gives high crop yields.
The land is flat, so farming, roads and railways are easy to build.
Perennial rivers fed by Himalayan snow melt supply water for irrigation all year.
Monsoon rain from June to September feeds the rivers and waters the crops.
Farming here supports many people per square kilometre, and rice and wheat are grown intensively.
The plain has been settled for thousands of years, so populations have built up over a long time.
Dense area – cities and coasts:
Mumbai, Delhi and Kolkata are densely populated cities.
Mumbai is on the west coast, Kolkata is on the Hooghly near the Bay of Bengal, and Delhi is on the Yamuna in the north.
Jobs, industry and services in these cities attract rural migrants.
Poor, overcrowded farming areas push people out, so rural-to-urban migration adds to city densities.
The Malabar coast of Kerala is densely populated because heavy monsoon rain and laterite soils support crops such as rice and coconuts.
Sparse area – the Thar Desert:
The Thar Desert in Rajasthan is sparsely populated.
Rainfall is very low there, because the Arabian Sea monsoon passes parallel to the Aravalli Range and has no barrier to force it upwards.
The desert soils are sandy, low in humus and sometimes saline, so farming is difficult.
Only places reached by canals, such as along the Indira Gandhi Canal, can support farming and settlement.
Sparse area – the Himalayas and the Deccan:
The Himalayas are sparsely populated because they are cold, and temperatures fall with altitude.
Steep slopes and thin, immature mountain soils limit farming in the Himalayas.
The Deccan Plateau lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats, so it is much drier than the coast.
Lower rainfall on the Deccan makes farming less productive, so its densities are lower than on the Ganges plain.
Sketch map locations:
On a sketch map the Ganges flows south-east from the Himalayas across the northern plain to its delta on the Bay of Bengal.
The densest area is shaded along this river, and the Thar Desert is shown as a sparse area in the north-west.

PART 5C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region – the Paris Basin, France:
The Paris Basin in northern France is a core European region centred on Paris.
Specific example – car industry and high technology:
Renault has a plant at Flins and PSA, now Stellantis, has a plant at Poissy, both west of Paris.
Paris-Saclay, south-west of Paris, is a high-technology cluster of research institutes and company laboratories.
Factor 1 – Markets:
The Paris region is the largest market in France, so firms locate close to their customers.
Île-de-France has about 12 million people, about 18 per cent of the population of France.
Île-de-France produces about 30 per cent of the GDP of France, so incomes and demand are high.
The head offices of large firms and their finance are based in Paris, so manufacturers stay close to decision makers and capital.
The dense population of about 1,000 people per square kilometre concentrates customers, which lowers distribution costs.
The large market attracted industry and services, which drew in migrants and enlarged the market further.
Food and drink processing sells to the Paris market and uses local sugar beet, cereals and Champagne grapes.
Factor 2 – Transport:
Paris is the hub of road, rail, river and air transport in France.
The road and rail network is radial, centred on Paris, and the Boulevard Périphérique ring road encircles the city.
The first TGV line, Paris–Lyon, opened in 1981, and the Channel Tunnel opened in 1994 to link Paris with London.
Charles de Gaulle Airport is the busiest airport in France.
The Seine carries bulk goods to factories by water, and the Port of Paris at Gennevilliers is one of Europe's largest river ports.
Le Havre, the largest container port in France, and Rouen on the Lower Seine link the basin to overseas markets.
Good transport lowers the cost of getting inputs in and products out, so it reinforces the location advantage of the region.

PART 6C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named tertiary activity – Tourism in India:
Tourism is a tertiary activity in India, a South Asian subcontinental region of about 3.3 million km².
