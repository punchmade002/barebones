PART "5B" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region – India:
Agriculture employs the largest share of India's workers, about 55% in Census 2011, so its development is vital to the subcontinent.
Factor 1 – Climate and the monsoon:
Tropical temperatures allow two or three crops a year.
The south-west monsoon brings seasonal rain from June, which supports kharif crops such as rice, cotton and maize.
Rabi crops such as wheat are sown in October and November and harvested in March and April, and they depend on irrigation and winter rain from western disturbances.
The monsoon is unreliable, bringing drought in some years and floods in others, which limits farming in some areas.
Factor 2 – Soils and relief:
Fertile alluvium deposited by Himalayan rivers covers the Northern Plains, making them the agricultural heartland.
The flat land of the plains eases cultivation and transport.
Black soils weathered from the Deccan basalt hold moisture and are ideal for cotton in Maharashtra and Gujarat.
Laterite soils on the Western Ghats and in Kerala are infertile but support tea, coffee and cashew when fertilised.
Steep slopes limit cultivation, but terraces on Himalayan slopes grow tea and fruit.
Factor 3 – Rivers and irrigation:
Perennial Himalayan rivers, fed by snow and glacier melt as well as monsoon rain, supply water for irrigation.
Canals such as the Indira Gandhi Canal bring water into the Thar Desert, and tubewells draw groundwater.
Overuse of groundwater has lowered the water table in Punjab, and irrigation has caused salinisation and waterlogging.
Factor 4 – Technology and government policy:
The Green Revolution from the mid-1960s brought high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice, fertilisers, pesticides, irrigation and machinery.
Food-grain output rose from about 51 million tonnes in 1950–51 to about 330 million tonnes in 2022–23, and India became self-sufficient in food.
The government sets minimum support prices, buys grain stored by the Food Corporation of India, and has paid PM-KISAN support to farm families since 2019.
Operation Flood from 1970 built co-operative dairying, such as the Amul co-operative at Anand in Gujarat, and made India the world's largest milk producer.
A huge domestic market of over 1.4 billion people drives demand for farm produce.
Average holdings are only about 1.1 hectares and about 86% are small or marginal, which limits productivity.
Named examples – Punjab wheat and Assam tea:
Punjab is a core Green Revolution area where canal irrigation supports commercial irrigated wheat farming.
Assam in the Brahmaputra valley has plantation farming of tea.

PART "5C" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region – The Paris Basin, France:
The Paris Basin is a large, low sedimentary lowland in northern France centred on Paris, with Île-de-France as its core.
Pattern of distribution:
Île-de-France has about 12 million people, about 18% of the population of France.
It has about 1,000 people per km², and the City of Paris has about 2.1 million people on about 105 km², about 20,000 people per km².
Densities are much lower in rural Champagne and across the outer basin.
Factor 1 – Economic opportunity:
Paris is the capital, so government, finance and head offices are concentrated there and the jobs attract migrants.
The largest market and labour force in France drew manufacturing and services to the Paris area.
La Défense, west of the city, is one of Europe's largest purpose-built business districts.
Île-de-France produces about 30% of the GDP of France.
Rural-to-urban migration into Paris continued as jobs and services concentrated there.
The book Paris et le désert français in 1947 argued that Paris drained people and wealth from the rest of France.
Factor 2 – Transport:
The national transport network is focused on the region, and Paris is the hub of a radial road and rail network that attracts settlement along its routes.
The Seine and its tributaries, the Marne and the Oise, converge on Paris, and their gentle gradients make them navigable.
Factor 3 – Physical factors:
The land is mostly below 300 m with gentle relief, so building, farming and travel are easy across most of the basin.
Deep, well-drained, fertile limon soils on the Beauce, Brie and Picardy plateaux supported farming populations and fed the city.
Paris began on the Île de la Cité, an island at a crossing point of the Seine.
Factor 4 – Decline in the outer basin:
Fewer farmers on larger holdings leave the outer basin with a declining rural population.
The outer basin is part of the diagonale du vide, a band of low population density running from north-east to south-west France.
Commuter villages near Paris are growing as residents travel to jobs in the city.
High housing costs push people out to the suburbs, where large estates such as Sarcelles were built in the 1950s to 1970s.
