PART 10B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named city – Dublin:
Dublin is Ireland's capital and shows the land-use zones of a modern European city.
Zone 1 – Central business district:
The CBD lies on both sides of the Liffey, around O'Connell Street, Grafton Street and Henry Street.
Land values are highest here because accessibility is greatest, so land is used most intensively.
Tall buildings hold offices, banks, department stores and chain stores.
Few people live in the CBD, so it is crowded by day and quiet at night.
Grafton Street and Henry Street are pedestrianised to cope with crowds.
Zone 2 – Zone in transition:
The docklands and north inner city are in transition, with older industrial and port land being renewed.
Decay and renewal exist side by side, and the IFSC, established in 1987 on derelict dockland, and Google at Grand Canal Dock are examples of regeneration.
Zone 3 – Suburbs:
Older terraced housing close to the centre forms an inner residential zone.
The outer suburbs of Tallaght, Clondalkin, Lucan and Blanchardstown were planned after the Wright Report of 1967 with lower-density semi-detached housing.
Suburban shopping centres such as Blanchardstown Centre, opened in 1996, and Dundrum Town Centre, opened in 2005, lie beside major roads where land is cheaper.
Zone 4 – Industrial and business parks:
Industry moved out of the inner city to business parks near the M50 and N7, such as Grange Castle, Citywest and Sandyford, for cheaper land and road access.
Jacob's biscuit factory moved from Bishop Street to Tallaght in the 1970s, while Guinness stayed at St James's Gate through industrial inertia.
Open space and transport:
Phoenix Park, about 700 hectares, is open space, and Dublin Port and Dublin Airport are major transport land uses.
Explanation of the pattern:
Bid-rent theory explains that land values fall with distance from the centre, so commerce outbids industry and industry outbids housing for central sites.
A labelled diagram would show the CBD at the centre, the zone in transition around it, then inner and outer suburbs, with business parks along the M50 ring.
Dublin fits the multiple nuclei and sector models better than the concentric model, because suburban centres are separate nuclei and growth follows main roads.

PART 10C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Ethnic issue – Racism and segregation:
Racism, which is prejudice or discrimination based on ethnicity, arises when migrants arrive in a host society.
Visible difference such as skin colour makes minorities targets of prejudice.
Competition for jobs, housing and services, especially in recessions, and fear that wages are undercut cause resentment.
Migrants cluster in particular areas by choice for support and cheap housing or because of discrimination, which reinforces poverty and separation.
Language barriers in schools, work and services add to the problem.
Far-right parties and misinformation on social media exploit these tensions.
Named example – France:
Post-war migrants from Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia settled in banlieues, which are segregated high-rise estates with high unemployment.
Riots began in Clichy-sous-Bois in October 2005 after two teenagers died fleeing police and spread across France, leading to a state of emergency.
Religious issue – Clashes over religious practice:
Religious practice in public space, such as places of worship, dress like the hijab, halal food and holy days, can clash with secular or majority-religion norms.
France has the largest Muslim population in western Europe and applies laïcité, the strict separation of religion and state, from the 1905 law.
A 2004 law banned conspicuous religious symbols including the headscarf in state schools, and a 2010 law banned face coverings in public.
Islamophobia rose after the Charlie Hebdo attack in January 2015 and the Paris attacks of November 2015.
Named example – Ireland:
Ireland moved from a mostly white Catholic society to a diverse one, and in Census 2022 about one in five people were born outside Ireland while Catholics fell to about 69% from 78% in 2016.
About 9 in 10 primary schools were under Catholic patronage, so religion decided admissions until the Education (Admission to Schools) Act 2018.
Protests at East Wall from November 2022 and the Dublin riot of 23 November 2023 show how hostility can turn to violence.
Named example – Northern Ireland:
The Plantation of Ulster from 1609 settled Protestant colonists on land taken from Catholic Irish, and the religious division led to the Troubles, in which about 3,500 people were killed.
Government responses:
The Employment Equality Act 1998 and Equal Status Act 2000 ban discrimination on grounds including race and religion, and the National Action Plan Against Racism was published in March 2023.

MODEL ANSWER: Impact of falling fertility on population structure:
Falling fertility narrows the base of the population pyramid because fewer children are born in each cohort.
Impact of falling mortality on population structure:
Falling mortality raises life expectancy and widens the top of the population pyramid.
Named example – Ireland:
Ireland is an example where both fertility and mortality have changed and altered the population structure.
Effects of changing fertility:
Ireland's total fertility rate fell from about 4 in the early 1970s to about 1.5 in 2023, well below the replacement level of about 2.1.
Fewer births mean smaller young cohorts, so only about one-fifth of Irish people are now under 15 and the pyramid is no longer young and wide-based.
Smaller youth cohorts reduce the young dependency ratio, which eases pressure on schools and maternity services.
By contrast Niger has a fertility rate of about 6 children per woman, a median age of about 15 and a very wide-based pyramid.
High fertility gives a high young dependency ratio, creating demand for schools, child health care and future jobs.
Effects of changing mortality:
Irish life expectancy at birth is about 82–83 years and infant mortality is about 3 per 1,000 live births.
More people survive into old age, so the top of the pyramid widens and about 776,000 people, about 15% of the population, were aged 65 or over in 2022.
Women live about four years longer than men, so women outnumber men in the oldest age groups of the pyramid.
Falling mortality raises the old-age dependency ratio, which increases pension, health care and nursing home costs.
Combined effect on the pyramid:
When fertility falls and life expectancy rises together the pyramid becomes top-heavy, as in Japan where about 29% of people are aged 65 or over.
Japan's fertility rate is about 1.2 and its population peaked at about 128 million around 2008–2010, so it is now falling.
A shrinking workforce must support more pensioners, so the tax base falls and the pension age comes under pressure to rise.
In Niger deaths have fallen faster than births, so the young population is growing by over 3% a year.
Average age in Ireland rose from 37.4 in 2016 to 38.8 in 2022, showing the pyramid ageing as fertility stays low and life expectancy rises.
