PART 8B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Internal trading pattern:
About three-fifths of members' goods exports stay within the EU, with intra-EU goods exports of about €4.0 trillion in 2024.
Germany is by far the largest intra-EU trader, and the core of the EU, including the Benelux countries and the Paris Basin, dominates internal trade.
Manufactured goods dominate internal trade, such as machinery, vehicles and parts, and chemicals and pharmaceuticals.
Members swap similar goods, such as cars for cars, and components cross borders several times in supply chains.
Eastern members depend most on internal trade, as intra-EU trade was about 81% of the goods trade of Czechia in 2024.
Supply chains link eastern members to German car makers, as Volkswagen has owned Škoda in Czechia since 2000.
External trading pattern:
Extra-EU goods exports were about €2.58 trillion in 2024 and imports about €2.43 trillion, a goods surplus of about €150 billion.
The USA is the largest export partner at about 21%, followed by the UK at about 13% and China at about 8%.
China is the largest import partner at about a fifth of imports, and the EU has a large deficit with China but a surplus with the USA.
Machinery and vehicles were about 39% of extra-EU exports, and chemicals and pharmaceuticals about 22%.
Energy products, mainly oil and gas, were about 15% of EU imports by value in 2024, down from about 23% in 2022.
Brexit moved the UK from internal to external partner, so trade with the UK now involves customs declarations and rules-of-origin checks.
Factor 1 – Single market and customs union:
Removal of tariffs and border checks through the customs union and single market cuts the cost of trade between members.
Common standards, such as the CE mark, replace different national product rules, so a good made in one member can be sold in all others.
The common external tariff means all members apply the same duties to imports from outside, so the EU bargains as one bloc.
Factor 2 – The euro and proximity:
The euro, used by 21 countries from 1 January 2026, removes exchange costs and exchange-rate risk among them.
Short distances and dense motorway, rail and river networks such as the Rhine make internal trade cheap and quick.
Proximity to Germany explains why eastern members trade so heavily within the EU.
Factor 3 – Market size and politics:
The EU's market of about 450 million wealthy consumers gives it bargaining power, and the Commission negotiates trade for all 27 members as one bloc.
Globalisation and low-cost manufacturing in China and Asia draw in imports and explain the large deficit with China.
Politics redirects trade, as Russian gas fell from about 45% of EU gas imports in 2021 to about 19% in 2024 after the invasion of Ukraine.
A 15% cap on US tariffs on most EU goods, agreed in July 2025, shows how trade disputes shape the pattern of exports to the USA.

PART 8C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named developed economy – Republic of Ireland:
Factor 1 – Physical factors:
Footloose industries such as pharmaceuticals, medical devices and microchips are not tied to raw materials or a coalfield, so Ireland's physical setting matters through site and access.
Large, flat, well-drained greenfield sites with water and electricity supplies suit plants, as at the Intel campus at Leixlip beside the M4 motorway.
Deep-water berths at the Port of Cork at Ringaskiddy suit the import and export of pharmaceutical goods and support the Cork Harbour cluster.
Cork Harbour has sheltered deep water close to flat zoned land, where Pfizer, Janssen and Novartis have plants at Ringaskiddy.
Ireland's cool, mild climate reduces cooling costs for data centres, which used about 21% of the country's metered electricity in 2023.
Light, high-value products are cheap to move by air, and Dublin Airport handled over 30 million passengers in 2023.
Ireland lies on the western edge of Europe, but sea and air links to the USA and the EU offset this disadvantage.
An attractive environment and quality of life help firms to recruit skilled staff.
Factor 2 – Political factors:
IDA Ireland, set up in 1949, offers grants, serviced sites and advance factories to attract firms.
Corporation tax was 12.5% from 2003, rising to 15% for very large groups from 2024.
Membership of the EU since 1973 gives tariff-free access to the single market from an English-speaking base.
Enterprise Ireland, set up in 1998, supports Irish-owned firms, and Údarás na Gaeltachta, set up in 1980, supports enterprise in Gaeltacht areas.
EU structural funds paid for roads, ports, airports and broadband, which improved access for firms.
The European Social Fund co-financed training that created a skilled workforce, which helped to attract foreign direct investment.
A stable democracy and a pro-business government make Ireland a safe place for investment.
Political decisions can also limit firms, as EirGrid stated in 2022 that it would not connect new data centres in the Dublin region until about 2028.

[geo-graph-skills-s1.4.7] To find a number from a percentage: number = percentage ÷ 100 × total.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.8.2] Parts of a whole, or percentage shares adding to 100%, e.g. car sales by fuel type in one year: pie chart or divided bar.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.8.4] Three components that add to 100%, e.g. sand, silt and clay at a soil site: triangular graph.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.9.4] Percentage compound bar: every bar reaches 100%, so the segments show shares rather than totals.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.11.0] Pie chart: parts of a whole. Degrees = percentage × 3.6, because 360° ÷ 100 = 3.6.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.11.4] Illustrative Country Q, electricity sources (gas 40%, wind 25%, solar 10%, coal 5%, hydro 5%, other 15%): angles 144°, 90°, 36°, 18°, 18° and 54°, totalling 360°.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.13.3] Calculations from the graph: annual temperature range = maximum mean monthly temperature minus minimum; mean annual temperature = sum of the 12 monthly means ÷ 12; total annual rainfall = sum of the 12 bars.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.15.0] Triangular graph: shows three components that always total 100%, e.g. the percentage employed in the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors.
[geo-graph-skills-s1.15.2] Check that the three readings add to 100, which confirms you have read the scales correctly.
