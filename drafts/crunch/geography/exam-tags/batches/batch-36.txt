PART 6B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region studied – India:
India is a subcontinental region in South Asia, bounded by the Himalayas to the north, the Arabian Sea to the west and the Bay of Bengal to the east.
Factor 1 – Raw materials:
The Chota Nagpur Plateau in Jharkhand holds most of India's coal and iron ore, which are the basis of the steel industry.
Coal is mined in the Jharia coalfield and the Damodar valley coalfields, so steel makers have fuel close to their plants.
Iron ore, limestone and manganese from the same plateau supply the other inputs needed to make steel.
Because these raw materials are heavy and lose weight in processing, siting plants close to them cuts transport costs.
The Tata Iron and Steel Company was founded in 1907 at Jamshedpur on this coal and iron ore, making it India's first large steel works.
The state steel plants at Bhilai, Rourkela and Durgapur were later built to use the same mineral resources.
Cotton grown on the black soils of the Deccan supplied the cotton textile mills of Mumbai and Ahmedabad.
Jute grown in the Ganges delta supplied the jute mills along the Hooghly near Kolkata, so raw materials shaped the location of several industries.
Factor 2 – Labour:
India has a huge, young and low-cost labour force, which keeps production costs low for manufacturers.
Low wages make Indian goods competitive on export markets and attract foreign firms.
Graduates from the Indian Institutes of Technology, the first of which opened at Kharagpur in 1951, give a large pool of skilled engineers.
This skilled labour supports high-technology industries such as aerospace and defence in Bengaluru, where Hindustan Aeronautics Limited and the headquarters of ISRO are based.
Foxconn assembles Apple iPhones at Sriperumbudur near Chennai, using this large workforce for electronics assembly.
Cheap labour supports labour-intensive garment making, as in the knitwear exports of Tiruppur in Tamil Nadu.
Maruti Suzuki, a joint venture with Suzuki of Japan, makes cars at Gurugram and Manesar, drawing on the large labour pool near Delhi.
Most workers are in the informal sector, which keeps costs low but limits wages and working conditions.

PART 6C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Concept of a region:
A region is an area of the earth's surface that shares one or more criteria which make it different from the areas around it.
Physical criteria such as rock type and climate can therefore be used to define geomorphological and climatic regions.
Example 1 – The Burren, Co. Clare (geomorphology):
A geomorphological region is an area with a distinctive landscape produced by the same rock type, structure or landforming processes.
The Burren in north-west Co. Clare is defined by its Carboniferous limestone, which gives a karst landscape.
Limestone pavement forms blocks called clints separated by fissures called grikes.
Turloughs are seasonal lakes in hollows that fill in winter and drain in summer.
Caves such as Aillwee Cave and Doolin Cave, which holds the Great Stalactite, show underground solution of the limestone.
There are very few surface rivers because drainage is underground.
Granite erratics from Connemara rest on the limestone, showing that glaciation scraped off the soil and exposed the pavement.
Arctic-alpine and Mediterranean plants grow side by side on the limestone.
Human activity reflects the region, with winterage grazing of cattle on the upland limestone and tourism based on Poulnabrone dolmen and Burren National Park.
Example 2 – Cool temperate oceanic climate region:
A climatic region is a large area with similar temperature, rainfall and seasonal pattern, usually with similar vegetation and soils.
Ireland and North West Europe form a cool temperate oceanic region, which also includes Britain, Brittany, Belgium, the Netherlands, Denmark and coastal Norway.
Winters are mild, with January means of about 4 to 7°C, and summers are cool, with July means of about 14 to 16°C.
The annual temperature range is small, only about 10°C, and rain falls in every month.
The North Atlantic Drift, the warm current from the Gulf Stream, keeps winters mild.
Prevailing south-westerly winds carry mild, moist Atlantic air and frequent depressions over the region.
Moving east, the continental influence brings colder winters, warmer summers and less rain, so the boundary is a transition zone and not a sharp line.
Within Ireland the west receives about 1,000 to 1,250 mm of rain a year while parts of the east around Dublin receive about 750 mm, because the east lies in a rain shadow.
The climate gives deciduous forest and brown earth soils, and grass grows for most of the year, which favours pastoral farming in Ireland.

[geo-region-concept-s1.0.b] Geographers divide the earth into regions so that places can be studied as groups instead of one by one. A region is an area of the earth's surface that shares one or more characteristics, called criteria, which make it different from the areas around it.
[geo-region-concept-s1.0.0] Physical criteria: climate (the usual weather of a place), relief (the height and shape of the land) and rock type, such as limestone.
[geo-region-concept-s1.0.4] Urban criteria: a city and its hinterland (the surrounding area that depends on the city for services and jobs).
[geo-region-concept-s1.0.5] These five types of region are the five subsections of this chapter, and this subsection deals with physical regions.
[geo-region-concept-s1.1.0] Regions exist at different scales. A local region is a county or a city's hinterland, e.g. Co. Clare; a national region is the West of Ireland; a continental region is North West Europe; a global region is the Islamic world.
[geo-region-concept-s1.1.1] A single index (one measurable criterion) defines a region by that one thing: rock type alone gives a karst region (a limestone landscape shaped by water dissolving the rock).
[geo-region-concept-s1.1.2] Multiple indices combine several criteria. For example, a peripheral region (one on the edge of a country's economic activity, far from its centre) can be defined by income, unemployment and out-migration (people leaving the area) together.
[geo-region-concept-s1.1.3] Physical and cultural regions usually merge into their neighbours through transition zones (a gradual belt of change) rather than ending at a sharp line.
[geo-region-concept-s1.1.5] Regions also change over time as their criteria change.
[geo-region-concept-s1.2.b] Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over many years. A climatic region is a large area with similar temperature, precipitation (rain, snow and other moisture falling from the sky) and seasonal pattern, usually with similar natural vegetation and soils.
