MODEL ANSWER: Region – India:
India is a subcontinental region in South Asia, bounded by the Himalayas, the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal.
Census 2011 recorded about 1.21 billion people, with an average density of 382 people per square kilometre.
Dense area 1 – The Ganges plain:
The Ganges plain in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar is one of the most densely populated areas of India.
Bihar is the densest state, with about 1,100 people per square kilometre.
The plain is flat and built from fertile alluvium deposited by rivers, so it supports intensive farming.
The Ganges and its tributaries, together with monsoon rain, provide a reliable water supply for irrigation.
Human factors include a very long history of settlement and large numbers of farmers.
Dense area 2 – Coastal plains and cities:
Coastal plains and deltas, such as Kerala and West Bengal, have flat land, fertile soil and heavy rainfall.
Around large cities such as Mumbai, Delhi and Kolkata, population is dense because of jobs in industry and services.
Rural to urban migration moves people from Bihar and Uttar Pradesh to the cities.
Sparse area 1 – The Thar Desert:
The Thar Desert in Rajasthan is sparsely populated because of aridity.
It is the driest region, because the Arabian Sea branch of the monsoon passes parallel to the Aravalli Range and has no barrier to force it upwards.
Water is scarce and soils are poor, so farming and settlement are very limited.
Sparse area 2 – The Himalayas:
The Himalayas are sparsely populated because of cold temperatures, steep slopes and thin soils.
Arunachal Pradesh in the north-east is the least dense state, with about 17 people per square kilometre.
The mountains are also prone to earthquakes and landslides and are a barrier to transport, so access is poor.
The forested hills of the north-east are also sparsely populated.

PART 6B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region 1 – Greater Dublin Area (Meath and Kildare):
The Greater Dublin Area is a core region on the east coast and has some of the best farmland in Ireland.
Factor 1 – Climate in the east:
The Wicklow Mountains and uplands to the west create a rain shadow, so the east is the driest part of Ireland, with about 750 to 1,000 mm of rain a year.
The dry, relatively sunny east suits tillage and horticulture, for example field vegetables around Rush and Lusk in Fingal.
Factor 2 – Relief and soils in the east:
The lowland of Meath and Kildare is low-lying and gently undulating, so large fields can be worked with machinery.
Deep, well-drained brown earths on limestone boulder clay support both tillage and grassland, so Meath has beef fattening and tillage on large farms.
The free-draining sand and gravel of the Curragh supports well-drained grassland used for horse breeding and training.
Poorer land such as the Bog of Allen and the acidic podzols of the Wicklow uplands is unsuitable for crops, and the uplands are used for sheep grazing.
Region 2 – West of Ireland (Galway and Mayo):
The West is a peripheral region on the Atlantic coast, where physical conditions limit farming.
Factor 1 – Atlantic climate:
Prevailing south-westerly winds bring depressions and relief rainfall, with 1,000 to 1,250 mm a year on the lowlands and over 2,000 mm on some mountains.
Mild winters mean grass grows well, but wet weather makes tillage difficult and hay hard to save.
Wet weather, waterlogged land and exposure to Atlantic gales give a short effective grazing season.
Exposure to Atlantic gales limits the land that can be farmed and the crops that can be grown.
Factor 2 – Relief and soils in the West:
Rugged uplands such as the Twelve Bens and Mweelrea are too steep and cold for crops and are used for hill sheep grazing on the commonage.
Blanket bog covers much of west Mayo and Connemara, and heavy gley soils lie on the drumlins, so most land is restricted to grazing.
Tillage is limited to the better-drained brown earths on the limestone of east Galway and Roscommon.
Cattle rearing is the main enterprise, with suckler herds rearing calves that are sold to farmers in the east to be fattened.

PART 6C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Example of a region defined by language – the Gaeltacht:
A cultural region is an area where people share a trait such as language that sets them apart from neighbouring areas.
The Gaeltacht is the officially recognised area where Irish is, or was, the community language.
It is not one continuous region, because it is made up of pockets in seven counties, including Donegal, Mayo, Galway, Kerry, Cork and Waterford.
Conamara and the Aran Islands in Co. Galway form the largest Gaeltacht area.
Irish survived in remote, mountainous areas with poor farmland and poor communications, which had the least contact with English-speaking trade, towns and administration.
Irish is the first official language under Article 8 of the 1937 Constitution.
Údarás na Gaeltachta, set up in 1980, promotes employment and the language in the Gaeltacht.
Raidió na Gaeltachta and TG4 broadcast in Irish.
The two Meath Gaeltachtaí were created in the 1930s by resettling Irish-speaking families from Connemara.
Threats include out-migration of young people, in-migration of English speakers, holiday homes and English-language media.
Second example – Belgium:
Belgium is divided by a language border running east to west, fixed by law in 1962 and 1963.
Flanders in the north is Dutch-speaking, and Wallonia in the south is French-speaking.
A small German-speaking community lives in the east around Eupen, and the Brussels-Capital Region is officially bilingual in French and Dutch.
Belgium became a federal state in 1993, with three regions and three language communities, each with its own parliament.
Schools, media and political parties are divided by language, and the university at Leuven split in 1968, when its French-speaking section moved to Louvain-la-Neuve.
After the 2010 election Belgium went 541 days without a new government because the Flemish and French-speaking parties could not agree.
The language divide matches an economic divide, because Flanders became the wealthier region.

[geo-region-concept-s3.0.2] Cultural regions rarely match political borders (the lines between states). One state can contain several, as Belgium and Switzerland do, and one region can cross several states, as the Basque Country and the Islamic world do.
