PART "6B" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region – West of Ireland:
The West Region of Galway, Mayo and Roscommon is a peripheral Irish region far from Dublin and from European markets.
Factor 1 – Relief:
Rugged uplands such as the Twelve Bens and Maumturks in Connemara and Mweelrea and Nephin in Mayo have steep slopes and thin soils, so only rough grazing is possible there.
Sheep rearing is a main type of agriculture, with sheep grazing the hill commonage of Connemara and the Mayo mountains.
Blanket bog covers much of west Mayo and Connemara and leached podzols lie on the uplands, which restricts most of the region to grazing.
The ice-scoured Connemara lowland has bare rock and many small lakes, which limits farmland.
Heavy, poorly drained gley soils lie on the drumlins and clay lowlands, so these are used mainly for grassland.
The low rolling limestone lowland of east Galway and Roscommon has better-drained brown earths, the most fertile farmland in the region.
Tillage is therefore restricted to east Galway and Roscommon.
Cattle rearing is the other main type of agriculture, with suckler herds rearing calves on the lowlands.
Young store cattle are sold through local marts to farmers in the east to be fattened.
Factor 2 – Climate:
The cool temperate oceanic climate has prevailing south-westerly winds that bring frequent Atlantic depressions and heavy rainfall.
Annual rainfall is about 1,000 to 1,250 mm on the lowlands and over 2,000 mm on some mountains.
Mild winters, with January averages of about 4 to 7 °C because of the North Atlantic Drift, mean grass grows for much of the year.
Grass grows well, so grassland suits cattle and sheep rearing.
Waterlogged land and wet weather make tillage difficult and hay hard to save.
Exposure to Atlantic gales limits the land that can be farmed and the crops that can be grown.
High rainfall, waterlogged soils and exposure give a short effective grazing season, which reduces farm output.

PART "6C" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region – The Ruhr in Germany:
The Ruhr in North Rhine-Westphalia is Europe's largest coal and steel region, with cities including Essen, Dortmund and Duisburg.
Causes of decline:
Coal seams were exhausted or became too costly to work.
Cheaper coal and steel imports and competition from newly industrialised countries undercut Ruhr producers.
Coal was replaced by oil and gas as an energy source.
Out-of-date plant made firms uncompetitive.
Automation needed fewer workers.
Impacts of decline:
High unemployment resulted, especially among older male workers.
Derelict factories, mines and spoil heaps scarred the landscape.
Polluted land remained after mining and heavy industry.
Out-migration of young people took place as jobs disappeared.
Falling population led to a decline in services.
The region's poor image deterred new investment.
Industry in decline and responses:
Coal mining is the industry in decline, and Prosper-Haniel in Bottrop, the last hard-coal mine in Germany, closed in 2018.
The Zollverein coal mine complex in Essen became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001 and is now a cultural centre.
Responses included retraining, clearing derelict land and attracting new light industry, services and universities.

Intel Corporation is an American multinational with its headquarters in Santa Clara, California, in Silicon Valley.

Silicon wafers, refined from quartz sand, are bought from specialist wafer makers.

Each of these machines costs hundreds of millions of euro, which makes them a major part of the investment in Fab 34.
The M4 motorway links Leixlip to Dublin, so imported inputs can be moved to the plant by road.
Intel depends on Asian supply chains, and the global chip shortage of 2021 showed the danger when COVID-19 disruption stopped car and electronics production worldwide.
Factor 2 – Location of processing units:
Stage one is wafer processing in fabs at Leixlip, Hillsboro in Oregon, Chandler in Arizona and Kiryat Gat in Israel.

PART "7C" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Sustainable development defined:
Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
It balances environmental protection, economic growth and social fairness, which is how the environmental impact of economic activity is controlled.
Activity 1 – Agriculture:
Agriculture is the largest single source of Irish greenhouse gas emissions, at about 38 per cent, so its impact must be controlled.
Agri-environment payments such as REPS, now succeeded by ACRES, pay farmers for conservation.
Nitrates rules limit the nitrogen fertiliser and manure that farms can apply, which reduces pollution of rivers and lakes.
BurrenLIFE paid Burren farmers to manage species-rich limestone grassland through winter grazing, protecting biodiversity.
Origin Green, launched by Bord Bia in 2012, is a sustainability programme for Irish food and drink producers.
Activity 2 – Fishing:
Fishing is controlled by total allowable catches, which divide a fish stock into national quotas under the Common Fisheries Policy.
Quotas aim to keep catches at the maximum sustainable yield, the largest catch that can be taken year after year without reducing the stock.
The landing obligation of the 2013 reform aims to end discards, which are fish thrown back dead.
Sustainable fishing keeps fish stocks and coastal jobs in places such as Killybegs for future generations.
Activity 3 – Energy and peat extraction:
Bord na Móna ended peat harvesting in January 2021, because harvesting removed raised bogs, released stored carbon and destroyed habitats.
Cutaway bogs are being rehabilitated and rewetted, as at Lough Boora Discovery Park in County Offaly, which is now parkland, wetlands and trails.
At Bellacorick in County Mayo wind farms now stand on cutaway bog, replacing fossil fuel generation with renewable energy.
Controls on environmental impact:
The Environmental Protection Agency licenses large industries and sets emission limits for air, water, waste and noise.
Environmental impact assessment is required before planning permission for large projects such as motorways, wind farms and quarries, so damage is predicted and reduced before it happens.
The polluter pays principle is applied through the carbon tax introduced in 2010 and the plastic bag levy introduced in 2002.
Ireland's Climate Act 2021 sets a 51 per cent cut in emissions by 2030.
