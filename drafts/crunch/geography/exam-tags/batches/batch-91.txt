PART 7B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: GNP as a measure of development:
Gross national product is the value of goods and services produced by a country's residents and firms in a year, plus income earned abroad minus profits sent out to foreign owners.
GNP per head divides the total by the population, so countries of different size can be compared.
GNP per head is a simple single figure that is available for almost every country.
It allows change in a country's income to be tracked over time.
It is closely linked to the ability of a government to fund health, education and other services.
However, it is an average, so it hides inequality between rich and poor and between regions.
It ignores the informal economy, subsistence farming and unpaid work, which are large in developing countries.
It also ignores environmental damage and counts spending on pollution clean-up as if it added to well-being.
HDI as a measure of development:
The Human Development Index combines health, education and income into one value between 0 and 1, and the United Nations Development Programme first published it in 1990.
It uses life expectancy at birth, mean and expected years of schooling, and GNI per head adjusted for purchasing power.
Countries are grouped as very high, high, medium or low human development, and the closer the value is to 1 the higher the development.
It is broader than income alone and focuses on people and outcomes rather than output.
It shows countries that achieve good health and education on modest incomes, such as Cuba, and oil-rich Gulf states whose income is higher than their human development.
However, it is a national average that hides inequality and leaves out political freedom, gender equality and sustainability.
Data are poor or out of date in some developing countries, and the figures are published about two years after the data year.
Example – Ireland:
Irish GDP rose by about 26% in 2015 mainly because multinationals relocated intellectual property and aircraft-leasing assets to Ireland, not because Irish people became richer.
Multinationals send profits abroad, so Irish GNP is well below GDP, and the modified measure GNI* introduced in 2017 was only a little over half of GDP in the early 2020s.
Ireland was joint second in the world on HDI in the 2020 report, which shows that high human development can sit beside a distorted income figure.
Second example – Niger:
Niger ranked last of 189 countries on HDI in the 2020 report, which reflects low incomes, short life expectancy and little schooling.
Comparing the two measures:
GNP measures output and income, while HDI measures human outcomes, so a country such as Equatorial Guinea has an income rank far higher than its HDI rank because of oil wealth.
Using both measures together, with other indicators, gives the fullest picture of development.

PART 7C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Sustainable development and two activities:
Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs, which was defined in the Brundtland Report of 1987.
Two economic activities examined here are fishing and mining, and sustainable methods control the environmental impact of both.
Activity 1 – Fishing:
Fish are renewable only while catches stay below the rate at which the stock breeds and grows.
The maximum sustainable yield is the largest catch that can be taken year after year without reducing the stock.
Overfishing is caused by technology such as sonar and factory ships, by too many boats and by the capture of young fish before they can breed.
The Grand Banks cod fishery off Newfoundland collapsed after factory trawlers arrived in the 1960s, Canada banned northern cod fishing in 1992, and about 30,000 people lost their jobs.
The EU Common Fisheries Policy, from 1983, sets a total catch limit for each stock and divides it into national quotas.
Conservation rules include minimum mesh sizes, minimum landing sizes, closed areas and seasons, and the scrapping of boats.
The 2013 reform introduced a landing obligation to end discards, which stops dead fish being thrown back into the sea.
Satellite tracking, the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority and Naval Service patrols monitor Irish waters to control illegal fishing.
Marine protected areas and aquaculture reduce the pressure on wild stocks.
North Sea herring recovered after the fishery was closed from 1977 and reopened under strict quotas, which shows that such controls work.
Activity 2 – Mining:
Mining can damage the environment through acid drainage, waste tailings and scarred landscapes.
Copper mining at Avoca in County Wicklow, carried out without controls, left acid mine drainage that still pollutes the Avoca River.
Tara Mines at Navan in County Meath, Europe's largest zinc mine, operates under an EPA licence that sets emission limits.
Its tailings are stored in a lined facility and the water is monitored to prevent pollution.
The Lisheen zinc mine in County Tipperary closed in 2015 and the site has been rehabilitated.
A new mine needs an environmental impact assessment report and a closure and restoration plan backed by a financial bond before it opens.
Controls on environmental impact:
The Environmental Protection Agency, set up in 1992, licenses large industries and uses integrated pollution control, so one licence covers air, water, waste and noise.
An environmental impact assessment is required before planning permission for large projects such as mines, ports and wind farms, which prevents damage before it happens.
The polluter pays principle makes those who cause damage bear the cost, as with the EU Emissions Trading System from 2005.
