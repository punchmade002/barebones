PART 4B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named example – The Gaeltacht:
The Gaeltacht is a cultural region in Ireland defined by the use of the Irish language as the community language.
It is made up of scattered pockets in seven counties, Donegal, Mayo, Galway, Kerry, Cork, Waterford and Meath.
Main areas include Gaoth Dobhair in Donegal, Conamara and the Aran Islands in Galway, Corca Dhuibhne in Kerry and Múscraí in Cork.
Conamara in County Galway is the largest Gaeltacht area.
Most of the pockets lie along the western seaboard, so the Gaeltacht overlaps with the peripheral West of Ireland.
Irish survived there because remote, mountainous areas with poor farmland and poor communications had the least contact with English-speaking trade, towns and administration.
Language is the cultural trait that sets the Gaeltacht apart from the English-speaking areas around it.
The State even created part of the region deliberately, as the Meath Gaeltachtaí at Ráth Chairn and Baile Ghib were formed in the 1930s by resettling Irish-speaking families from Connemara.
Support for the cultural region:
Irish is the first official language of the State under Article 8 of the 1937 Constitution.
Údarás na Gaeltachta, set up in 1980, promotes employment and the Irish language in the region.
Raidió na Gaeltachta, from 1972, and TG4, from 1996, broadcast in Irish and strengthen the identity of the region.
Irish-medium schools and summer colleges bring learners into the Gaeltacht and help to sustain the language.
The culture is threatened by out-migration of young people, by in-migration of English speakers and holiday homes, and by English-language media.
Census 2022 showed that about 1.9 million people in the State could speak Irish but only about 72,000 spoke it daily outside the education system.
Second example – Belgium:
Belgium is a cultural region divided by a language border that runs east to west, with Dutch-speaking Flanders in the north and French-speaking Wallonia in the south.
A small German-speaking community lies in the east around Eupen, and the capital Brussels is officially bilingual.
The language divide shapes politics, as Belgium became a federal state in 1993 with three regions and three language communities, each with its own parliament.
Schools, media and political parties are divided by language, which shows how culture can define a region.

PART 4C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named region – Cork Harbour region:
The Cork Harbour region in the south of Ireland has developed into one of the country's main centres of secondary economic activity.
Factor 1 – Government policy:
The Industrial Development Authority was set up in 1949 to attract industry to Ireland.
Export Profits Tax Relief in 1956 removed tax on profits from exports, which encouraged firms to manufacture for export.
The 1958 Programme for Economic Expansion set a policy of free trade and foreign investment, which opened Cork to overseas manufacturers.
Irish membership of the EEC from 1973 and the Single Market from 1993 gave factories in Cork tariff-free access to a huge European market.
IDA grants and serviced sites made it cheap and quick for firms to set up in the Cork Harbour area.
A low corporation tax rate of twelve and a half per cent on trading profits from 2003 attracts multinationals to Cork.
This policy helped the pharmaceutical and chemical cluster at Ringaskiddy and Little Island to grow into one of the world's largest.
The National Planning Framework of 2018 aims for Cork to grow by at least 50% by 2040, which supports continued investment in the region.
Factor 2 – Labour:
Free second-level education, introduced in 1967, produced a better-educated workforce in the decades that followed.
A young, educated, English-speaking workforce attracts firms that need skilled staff.
Third-level colleges in Cork, including University College Cork, supply graduates in science and engineering.
Pharmaceutical and chemical plants need trained chemists and technicians, so the supply of skilled labour suits them.
The closure of the Dunlop tyre factory in 1983 and the Ford and Verolme dockyard plants in 1984 left skilled workers available for new employers.
The large labour force of Cork city and its commuter towns lets plants recruit locally.
A pool of skilled workers attracts more firms, as clusters of similar firms form.
The growth of such clusters keeps wages and jobs rising and draws in more workers from other areas.
Specific examples of secondary activity:
Pfizer, Janssen and Novartis have pharmaceutical plants at Ringaskiddy in Cork Harbour.
Eli Lilly has a plant at Kinsale in County Cork.

PART 5B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Region 1 – Paris Basin, France:
The Paris Basin is a low lowland in northern France centred on Paris and the River Seine, and its climate strongly shapes farming.
Its climate is transitional, between cool oceanic conditions in the west and more continental conditions in the east.
Paris has an average January temperature of about 5°C and an average July temperature of about 20°C.
Annual rainfall of about 600 to 650 mm is spread fairly evenly through the year, which gives crops reliable moisture.
Warm summers ripen cereals and grapes, and the long growing season gives crops time to develop.
Dry weather at harvest lets crops be gathered in good condition.
This climate supports wheat, barley and maize in the Beauce, south-west of Paris, which is known as the granary of France.
Champagne lies near the northern limit of commercial vine growing, so its warm summers are essential for ripening the grapes grown on the chalk slopes.
The cooler, wetter oceanic west favours grassland, so Normandy has dairy farming that produces Camembert cheese.
Region 2 – Mezzogiorno, southern Italy:
The Mezzogiorno is the peripheral south of Italy, with a Mediterranean climate that strongly affects its primary activities.
Summers are hot and dry because the subtropical high-pressure belt moves north over the region.
Lowland July temperatures average around 25°C or more.
Winters are mild and wet, with most rain falling between October and March.
Annual rainfall is under 600 mm on parts of the Puglia lowlands and Sicily, so summer drought causes water shortage.
Farmers respond with irrigation and drought-tolerant crops.
Puglia is Italy's leading olive-oil region, and durum wheat for pasta is grown on the Tavoliere di Puglia around Foggia.
Hot, sunny summers and mild winters suit lemons on the Amalfi coast and blood oranges on the plain of Catania near Etna.
The sirocco, a hot, dry, dusty wind from the Sahara, adds to summer dryness, and intense autumn and winter downpours cause flash floods and soil erosion on bare slopes.

PART 5C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named urban area – Galway city:
Galway is a city on the west coast of Ireland, at the mouth of the River Corrib, and it is the regional capital of the West.

Galway grew because of its harbour site at the mouth of the River Corrib on Galway Bay.

Queen's College Galway was founded in 1845 and is now the University of Galway, which brings students, staff and research to the city.
