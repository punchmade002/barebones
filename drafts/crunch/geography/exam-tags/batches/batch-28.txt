PART 11B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Variation 1 – Dense settlement on the Sligo lowlands:
Settlement is densest in and around Sligo town, where the built-up area at G 692 365 is crowded with streets, schools, hospitals and post offices.
Houses are also numerous on the low ground between Sligo and Rathcormack at G 693 412, where roads, a school and many black house symbols cluster.
Variation 2 – Sparse settlement on the uplands and water:
Settlement is almost absent on Benbulbin at G 692 463, on Kings Mountain at G 705 441 and around Cope's Mountain at G 733 412.
There are no houses on Sligo Harbour at G 668 384, on Drumcliff Bay at G 669 418 or on Colgagh Lough at G 740 363.
Map evidence for relief:
On the uplands the contour lines are packed closely together and heights reach 526 metres at the summit of Benbulbin, so the ground is too steep and exposed for building.
Spot heights near Rathcormack and Springfield at G 680 398 are only about 10 to 60 metres, and the gently sloping ground there carries many houses.
Factor 1 – Relief and soils:
Steep slopes, thin soils and exposure make upland areas costly to build on and unsuited to farming.
Gently sloping, well-drained lowland with better soils lets farmhouses be built almost anywhere, which gives a dispersed pattern.
Upland land is used for rough grazing and forestry, which support few people, so houses there are widely spaced.
Factor 2 – Routes and accessibility:
The N4, N15, N16 and R287 meet at Sligo, which makes it a route focus that attracts people and functions.
Houses string out along roads such as the R291 coast road through Ballincar at G 673 387, forming linear (ribbon) settlement.
Few roads climb the uplands, so there are few houses there.
Ribbon development along roads such as the N15 north of Sligo is cheaper because road access, water and electricity are already in place.
Factor 3 – Site and functions of Sligo:
Sligo grew beside a sheltered harbour at the mouth of the Garavogue River, a good site for trade and crossing the river.
It has many functions, including schools, hospitals, shops, an abbey and tourism, and these draw people and housing into the town.
Factor 4 – History and services:
Megalithic tombs at G 700 357 and G 729 406 and a castle near Cregg at G 668 390 show that the lowlands have been settled for a very long time.
Drumcliff at G 675 419 is a small cluster around a church and round tower, and Rathcormack shows nucleated settlement around a crossroads and school.
Commuters from rural areas near Sligo add to housing around the town, as cars let people live in the countryside and work in the town.

PART 11C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Issue 1 – Housing supply and affordability:
Demand for homes rises with population growth, smaller households and migration, and supply has not kept pace in successful cities such as Dublin.
High rents and house prices put homes beyond the reach of many households.
Homelessness is a visible result, with over 15,000 people in emergency accommodation in Ireland in 2025.
Housing for All in 2021 aims for about 33,000 new homes a year to 2030.
Rent Pressure Zones, introduced from 2016, limit rent increases in the areas where they apply.
Higher-density apartments near transport use land and services more efficiently.
Public and cost-rental housing provide homes at below-market rents.
Gentrification in areas such as the Dublin Docklands pushes lower-income residents out unless social housing is built.
Issue 2 – Transport and sustainable mobility:
Car dependence causes congestion and vehicle emissions in growing cities.
Dublin plans MetroLink and BusConnects as part of the Greater Dublin Area Transport Strategy 2022 to 2042.
The Grand Paris Express will add about 200 km of new automatic metro lines and 68 stations in the Paris region.
Paris is promoting the 15-minute city, where everyday needs can be met within 15 minutes on foot or by bike.
London's Ultra Low Emission Zone began in 2019 and was extended London-wide in August 2023 to cut vehicle emissions.
Copenhagen is a cycling city, where cycling is the most common way of commuting to work or study.
Vauban in Freiburg is a low-car eco-district built from the late 1990s on a former military barracks.
Electric vehicles reduce emissions from the cars that remain on the roads.
