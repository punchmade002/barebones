QUESTION geography-pp-2021-higher-q117 (LC Geography Higher 2021 — Part Two — Q17, 80 marks)

PART "17" (80 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Soil development is influenced by the amount and nature of its components, so the effects of mineral matter, organic matter and water are discussed in turn.
Aspect 1 – Mineral matter:
Mineral matter is particles of weathered rock, from stones down to clay, and it makes up about 45% of the volume of a good loam.
It forms the skeleton of the soil and decides its texture, which is the proportion of sand, silt and clay.
Sand is the largest particle and clay the smallest, and clay is the chemically active part of the mineral matter.
Sandy soils are free-draining and well aerated, but they hold few nutrients and are easily leached.
Clay soils hold water and nutrients, but they drain slowly and become waterlogged and cold.
A loam of roughly 40% sand, 40% silt and 20% clay combines good drainage with good nutrient retention and is the best soil for farming.
Parent rock controls the result, as limestone such as that of the Burren gives base-rich, less acidic soils.
Granite such as that of the Wicklow Mountains gives sandy, acidic soils with quartz grains and clay from weathered feldspar.
As mineral matter weathers it releases nutrients such as calcium, magnesium and potassium for plants.
Aspect 2 – Organic matter:
Organic matter is living organisms plus dead plant and animal remains, and it makes up about 5% of a good loam.
Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down litter into humus, a dark, jelly-like substance that is the end product of decomposition.
Humus darkens the soil and binds particles into crumbs, which give the best structure for farming.
Humus holds water and nutrients and releases the nutrients slowly to plant roots.
Humus is the main store of nitrogen in the soil.
Earthworms mix and aerate the soil, drag litter down and improve structure with their casts.
Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the root nodules of legumes such as clover add nitrogen to Irish grassland soils.
Mull humus forms under deciduous woodland and grassland, is well decomposed and crumbly, and gives fertile brown earths.
Mor humus forms under coniferous forest and heath, is acidic and poorly decomposed, and gives infertile podzols.
Aspect 3 – Water:
Water enters the soil as precipitation and is held in its pores, and it shares the pore space with air so that when one rises the other falls.
A good loam holds about 25% water by volume.
Dissolved nutrients form the soil solution, from which roots absorb them.
Capillary water is held in small pores by surface tension and is available to plants, while hygroscopic water is bound tightly to particles and cannot be used by plants.
Gravitational water drains down through large pores after rain and carries nutrients away, which is called leaching.
In Ireland precipitation exceeds evapotranspiration, so water moves down through the soil and leaches it.
Where evaporation exceeds precipitation, as in deserts, capillary action raises water and leaves salts and lime at the surface.
Too much water fills all the pores and causes waterlogging, which leads to gleying with grey, rust-mottled colours.
Waterlogged soils are anaerobic, so decomposition is slow and organic matter builds up as peat.

QUESTION geography-pp-2021-higher-q118 (LC Geography Higher 2021 — Part Two — Q18, 80 marks)

PART "18" (80 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Human activities alter biomes by removing, replacing or polluting natural vegetation, and the felling of tropical rainforests, intensive agriculture and industrial development each do so in a different way.
Aspect 1 – Felling of tropical rainforests in the Amazon:
Satellite records show that clearance in the Brazilian Amazon peaked at about 27,800 km² in 2004, and close to a fifth of the original forest has been cleared.
Cattle ranching is the largest single cause and accounts for most of the cleared land.
Soya farming spread north from Mato Grosso, and much of the soya is exported as animal feed to Europe and China.
Road building such as the Trans-Amazonian Highway, begun in the early 1970s, opened the forest to settlers, and BR-364 into Rondônia produced a fishbone pattern of clearings.
Mining at Carajás in Pará, one of the world's largest iron-ore mines, and dams such as Belo Monte on the Xingu River cleared and flooded forest.
Clearance destroys habitat and species, many of them endemic, and fragments the remaining forest so that its edges become drier and hotter.
The nutrient cycle is broken because cleared latosols lose their fertility within a few years, and soil erosion and a hard laterite crust follow.
Less evapotranspiration means less rainfall downwind and more frequent fires, and burning and decay release stored carbon as carbon dioxide.
Indigenous peoples such as the Yanomami lose land and suffer disease and violence, and illegal gold mining led to a public health emergency in Yanomami territory in 2023.
Aspect 2 – Intensive agricultural practices:
Intensive farming uses high inputs of capital, machinery, fertiliser, pesticides and water to get maximum output per hectare, and it creates monocultures and larger fields.
After EU milk quotas ended on 1 April 2015, Irish dairy herds expanded.
Hedgerows were removed, wetlands and callows were drained and species-rich meadows were reseeded with ryegrass.
Mechanised silage cutting destroys the nests of ground-nesting birds such as the corncrake, which has declined to a small population in north-west Donegal, west Mayo and the Shannon Callows.
Heavy use of fertiliser and slurry raises nitrate levels in rivers and causes eutrophication of lakes.
Wild bees and farmland birds have declined, and agriculture produces over a third of Ireland's greenhouse gas emissions.
In Europe the Common Agricultural Policy, begun in 1962, used guaranteed prices that encouraged farmers in East Anglia to remove hedgerows and drain wetlands.
At the Aral Sea, on the border of Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, Soviet planners diverted the Amu Darya and Syr Darya from the 1960s to irrigate cotton and rice, and the sea shrank to a fraction of its former size.
Salinity rose and the fishing industry collapsed at ports such as Moynaq, and dust storms now carry salt and pesticide residues from the exposed seabed onto farmland and people.
Aspect 3 – Industrial development:
Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from coal- and oil-burning power stations and industry form acids that are carried downwind across national borders as acid rain.
Acid rain acidified thousands of lakes in southern Norway and Sweden from the 1960s to the 1980s, killing fish and releasing aluminium into the water.
In the Black Triangle where Germany, Poland and the Czech Republic meet, lignite-burning power stations killed large areas of spruce forest in the Ore Mountains in the 1970s and 1980s, a process known as Waldsterben.
Responses included the 1979 Convention on Long-Range Transboundary Air Pollution and flue-gas desulphurisation, and European sulphur dioxide emissions have since fallen by well over half.
In Ireland Bord na Móna, set up in 1946, harvested milled peat on a large scale from the Midland raised bogs for power stations.
Peatlands cover about a fifth of Ireland, and harvesting ended in 2021 after the Shannonbridge and Lanesborough power stations closed in December 2020.
Cutaway bogs are being rehabilitated, as at Lough Boora Discovery Park in County Offaly, which has become wetlands, woodland and amenity land.
The Athabasca oil sands in Alberta, Canada, are mined by open-pit methods that strip away boreal forest and muskeg and leave large tailings ponds of polluted water.
Repeated oil spills in the Niger Delta in Nigeria have polluted creeks, mangrove forest and farmland, and a 2011 UNEP study of Ogoniland found that a clean-up could take 25 to 30 years.

QUESTION geography-pp-2021-higher-q119 (LC Geography Higher 2021 — Part Two — Q19, 80 marks)

PART "19" (80 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Conflict arises when state boundaries and governments cut across, deny or divide cultural groups, as the cases of the Kurds, Spain and Northern Ireland show.
Aspect 1 – The Kurds in Turkey, Iraq and Syria:
The Kurds number 30 million or more and are the largest ethnic group without their own state.
Most live in a mountainous region, Kurdistan, which is divided between Turkey, Iran, Iraq and Syria.
The Treaty of Sèvres in 1920 provided for a possible Kurdish state, but the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923 replaced it and drew the borders of modern Turkey with none.
Turkey long denied a separate Kurdish identity and banned Kurdish in public life until 1991 and in broadcasting until the early 2000s.
The PKK, founded in 1978, began an armed campaign in 1984, and the conflict cost about 40,000 lives.
On 16 March 1988 Saddam Hussein's forces attacked the Kurdish town of Halabja with chemical weapons, killing about 5,000 people.
Iraq's 2005 constitution recognised the Kurdistan Region as autonomous, with its own government and parliament in Erbil.
About 93% voted for independence in a referendum on 25 September 2017, but Iraq's government rejected it and Iraqi forces retook Kirkuk.
The PKK announced its dissolution in May 2025.
Aspect 2 – Basques and Catalans in Spain:
Basques and Catalans have their own languages, autonomy statutes and strong regional identities within Spain.
Basque and Catalan were suppressed under Franco from 1939 to 1975.
Spain's 1978 constitution created 17 autonomous communities, and the Basque Autonomous Community received the Statute of Gernika in 1979.
ETA, founded in 1959, killed over 800 people in its campaign for an independent Basque state.
ETA declared a definitive end to violence in 2011 and dissolved in 2018.
In 2010 Spain's Constitutional Court struck down parts of Catalonia's 2006 autonomy statute, including its description of Catalonia as a nation, which fuelled the independence movement.
On 1 October 2017 Catalonia held an independence referendum that Spain's courts had declared illegal, and police tried to stop the voting.
About 90% of those voting backed independence, on a turnout of about 43%.
Spain imposed direct rule using Article 155 of its constitution, and the regional president Carles Puigdemont fled to Belgium.
Nine Catalan leaders were jailed in 2019 and pardoned in 2021.
Aspect 3 – Northern Ireland:
The Government of Ireland Act 1920 created two administrations on the island, and Northern Ireland was established in 1921.
Northern Ireland consists of six of Ulster's nine counties, while Cavan, Donegal and Monaghan went to the Irish Free State in 1922.
The border is about 499 km long and follows old county boundaries rather than physical features, so it cuts through communities and farms.
The state was created around a unionist majority with a large nationalist minority.
Discrimination against nationalists fuelled the civil rights movement from 1968.
The Troubles, from the late 1960s to 1998, killed over 3,500 people.
The Good Friday Agreement was signed on 10 April 1998, and referendums on 22 May 1998 returned about 71% Yes in Northern Ireland and about 94% Yes in the Republic.
It set up a power-sharing Assembly and Executive, a North/South Ministerial Council and the consent principle, but it did not move the border.
Brexit challenged the Agreement, as Northern Ireland voted about 56% Remain in the 2016 referendum, and the Windsor Framework of 2023 keeps the land border free of checks.
