PART "12B" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named city – Dublin:
Dublin is a large European capital city with clearly separated zones of land use.
Land-use zone 1 – Central business district:
Dublin's CBD lies on both sides of the River Liffey and has the highest land values in the city because accessibility is greatest.
Tall buildings with several floors of offices or shops make the most of the expensive sites.
Department stores and chain stores line the pedestrianised shopping streets of Grafton Street and Henry Street.
Banks, offices and company head offices cluster together in the centre.
O'Connell Street is the main street, and government and cultural buildings are located centrally where they are most accessible.
Few people live in the CBD, so it is crowded during the working day and quiet at night.
Land-use zone 2 – Zone in transition:
The inner city around the north inner city and the Docklands is a zone in transition of old housing, warehouses and small industry.
Decay and renewal exist side by side here, with derelict sites close to new apartments and offices.
Old port and industrial land has been regenerated, for example at Grand Canal Dock where Google has its European headquarters.
Industry such as Jacob's biscuit factory moved out to the edge of the city because inner-city sites lacked space and land was cheaper on the edge.
Land-use zone 3 – Suburbs:
The suburbs are lower-density areas of mainly semi-detached housing, built later and further from the centre.
Land values fall with distance from the centre, so there is space for gardens and large developments.
The western new towns of Tallaght, Clondalkin, Lucan and Blanchardstown were planned after the Wright Report of 1967.
Suburban shopping centres such as Dundrum Town Centre and Blanchardstown Centre, and business parks beside the M50, serve the outer city.

PART "12C" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Example – the Sahel and Niger:
The Sahel, including Niger, is an example of an area where population is too large for its resources to support an acceptable standard of living.
Cause 1 – Development of resources:
Where resources are not developed, are degraded or shrink, the same population becomes too large for them to support.
The Sahel is a semi-arid belt along the southern edge of the Sahara with unreliable rainfall of about 200 to 600 mm a year.
Overgrazing, overcultivation without fallow periods and the cutting of trees for fuelwood strip the vegetation from the land.
Drought, wind and rain then erode the bare soil, so desertification reduces the land available for crops and grazing.
Lake Chad has shrunk by about 90% since the 1960s because of irrigation demand and drought, so fishing and farming resources have fallen.
Subsistence farming with little technology keeps the carrying capacity of the land low.
Poverty limits the ability of Sahel countries to develop resources or import food, so numbers exceed what the resources can support.
Cause 2 – Society and culture:
Religious beliefs and cultural attitudes to contraception keep birth rates high.
In Niger early marriage of girls is common, so women have children over a longer period.
Female literacy is low and women have a low status, so women have little say in family size.
Large families carry prestige, and a preference for sons encourages couples to keep having children.
Children are valued as farm labour and as security in old age.
Niger's total fertility rate is about 6 children per woman, among the highest in the world.
Population growth is over 3% a year and the median age is about 15, so numbers rise faster than resources.

PART "13" (80 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Desertification and deforestation show that decisions and actions taken in one area, whether by governments, companies or consumers, have environmental and economic impacts on other areas, which is the essence of an interdependent global economy.
Aspect 1 – Deforestation in the Amazon:
Demand in other regions for beef and soya drives cattle ranching, the largest cause of clearance in the Amazon rainforest, about 60% of which lies in Brazil.
Brazil is the world's largest exporter of soya beans, and much of it is exported as animal feed to Europe and China.
Decisions to build the Trans-Amazonian Highway from the early 1970s, and later roads, mines and dams, opened the forest to settlers and clearance.
Annual clearance in the Brazilian Amazon was about 27,800 km² in 2004, fell to about 4,600 km² in 2012 and rose again to about 13,000 km² in 2021, as government policy changed.
The carbon released by clearing the forest adds to global warming, which affects every country.
The loss of the forest reduces rainfall recycling, which affects farming beyond the forest, and it destroys biodiversity and threatens indigenous peoples.
The Amazon Soy Moratorium of 2006, agreed by traders, and the Amazon Fund of 2008, with Norway as the largest donor, show other countries acting to reduce clearance in Brazil.
Aspect 2 – Palm oil deforestation in South-East Asia:
Indonesia and Malaysia produce about 85% of the world's palm oil, which is used in foods, cosmetics and biofuels sold worldwide.
Demand from consumers in other regions drives the clearance of rainforest for oil palm plantations.
Peat swamp forest in Borneo and Sumatra is cleared and burned to plant oil palms.
The burning caused regional haze in 1997 and 2015 that affected Singapore and Malaysia, so clearance in one country harms the health and economy of its neighbours.
The Bornean orangutan was listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN in 2016 because its forest habitat is being lost.
Ireland imports products linked to tropical deforestation, such as palm oil, so Irish consumers are part of the chain.
The EU Deforestation Regulation, adopted in 2023, will require firms selling palm oil in the EU to prove that the goods were not grown on land deforested after 2020, so a decision in Europe affects producers in Asia.
Aspect 3 – Desertification in the Sahel:
The Sahel is the semi-arid belt along the southern edge of the Sahara, from Senegal and Mauritania to Sudan, with rainfall of about 200 to 600 mm in one short wet season.
Unreliable rainfall and drought, notably in the 1970s and 1980s, were the physical causes of desertification.
Global warming, linked to emissions in distant industrialised regions, adds to drought in the Sahel.
Population growth, overgrazing, overcultivation and fuelwood collection degrade the land.
Cash crops such as groundnuts in Senegal and cotton take the best land for export, so food crops are pushed onto poorer land.
Famine, conflict over land and water, and migration to cities and to Europe spread the effects to other areas.
Lake Chad has shrunk by about 90% since the 1960s.
The Great Green Wall, launched by the African Union in 2007, is a planned band about 8,000 km long from Senegal to Djibouti that aims to restore 100 million hectares by 2030.
Farmer-managed natural regeneration in Niger has regreened about 5 million hectares since the 1980s.
Aspect 4 – Desertification at the Aral Sea:
In 1960 the Aral Sea, between Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, was the world's fourth-largest lake.
From the 1960s Soviet planners diverted the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers to irrigate cotton.
The sea shrank to a small fraction of its size and split.
Fishing collapsed, and the port of Moynaq was left far from the shore.
Salt and pesticide dust storms now blow from the exposed seabed.
The loss was caused by irrigation and not by climate change, so a decision in one place created a desert.
The Kok-Aral Dam, built in 2005 in Kazakhstan with World Bank funding, raised the North Aral Sea and fishing has partly recovered.

[geo-option-global-interdependence-s2.13.b] Deforestation means clearing forest permanently, usually for farming or mining. It is a global issue because the land is cleared where trees grow but the demand comes from other regions.

[geo-option-global-interdependence-s2.14.0] The Amazon is the world's largest tropical rainforest, with about 60% of it in Brazil.

[geo-option-global-interdependence-s2.15.2] Brazil's Forest Code requires 80% of Amazon land to be kept as a legal reserve, so only 20% of a holding may be cleared.
[geo-option-global-interdependence-s2.15.4] Responses involving other countries: the Amazon Soy Moratorium (2006), agreed by traders; the Amazon Fund (2008), with Norway as the largest donor; and the EU Deforestation Regulation, which sets rules on goods sold in the EU.

[geo-option-global-interdependence-s2.21.b] The Sahel is the semi-arid belt along the southern edge of the Sahara, from Senegal and Mauritania through Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger and Chad to Sudan. Natural and human causes combine there, and some of the causes lie in other regions.
