PART "9B" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named example – Corrib gas, Co. Mayo:
The Corrib gas field lies about 80 km off north-west Co. Mayo and was discovered in 1996.
Economic case for exploitation:
The gas gave energy security through a domestic supply and for a time supplied over half of Ireland's gas.
It brought revenue for the State and the companies and construction jobs.
Environmental and local objections:
The original plan piped high-pressure unprocessed gas onshore near homes at Rossport, which created a safety risk for residents.
The Shell to Sea campaign, from 2005, argued that the gas should be processed at sea.
Sruwaddacon Bay, a protected Special Area of Conservation, lay on the pipeline route.
The Rossport Five were jailed in June 2005 for refusing to obey a court order and were released after 94 days.
The protests were heavily policed for years and the dispute divided the local community.
How the conflict ended:
The onshore route was changed to keep the pipeline further from houses, and a tunnel was bored under Sruwaddacon Bay.
Gas first flowed in December 2015 through the Bellanaboy terminal after a long delay, and Shell sold its share in 2018.
Second example – Mullaghmore, the Burren:
In the early 1990s the OPW began building an interpretative centre near Mullaghmore in the Burren, Co. Clare, where limestone pavement supports rare Arctic-alpine and Mediterranean plants.
Supporters wanted tourism jobs in a poor rural area, but the Burren Action Group feared traffic, sewage and visitor pressure on a fragile landscape.
In 1993 the Supreme Court ruled that the State needed planning permission, and in 2000 An Bord Pleanála refused it and the site was restored.
Resolving conflicts of interest:
Conflicts are resolved by planning permission, environmental impact assessment, EU law such as the Habitats Directive, court cases and compensation.
Turf cutting was banned on 53 protected raised bogs from 2011, with compensation of about €1,500 a year for 15 years per turf cutter.

[geo-mapwork-s1.38.6] 30-mark map-evidence questions: three reasons a town developed at its location; three rural settlement patterns; variations in settlement distribution; absence and high density of settlement.
[geo-mapwork-s1.38.7] More 30-mark map-evidence questions: three phases of historic settlement; site suitability with a six-figure reference; economic activity and tourism potential.
