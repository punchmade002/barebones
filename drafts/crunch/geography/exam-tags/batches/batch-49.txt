PART 8B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named developing economy – India:
India is a developing economy in South Asia, ranked 134th of 193 countries on the Human Development Index.
Impact 1 – Colonial trade in raw materials:
Under British rule India exported raw cotton, jute, indigo and tea to Britain and imported manufactured goods.
India's handloom textile industry was destroyed by cheap machine-made cloth from the cotton mills of Lancashire.
Cash crops replaced food crops, and famines such as the Bengal famine of 1943 occurred while grain was still exported.
Railways and ports at Bombay and Calcutta were built mainly to carry raw materials to the coast for export rather than to link Indian regions.
India's share of world GDP fell from about a quarter around 1700 to about 4% by 1950, according to the estimates of Angus Maddison.
Impact 2 – Protection after independence:
After 1947 India used import substitution behind high tariffs, and the Licence Raj limited competition and investment.
Growth was slow, which earned the nickname the Hindu rate of growth.
By 1991 foreign-exchange reserves had fallen to only a few weeks of imports, so India pledged gold and borrowed from the IMF.
Impact 3 – Opening to world trade after 1991:
Reforms under Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh removed most licences, cut tariffs, welcomed foreign investment and devalued the rupee.
India was a founding member of the WTO in 1995, which gave it better access to world markets.
Growth became fast, and services now make up over half of GDP.
An English-speaking workforce helped build the IT and outsourcing industry in Bengaluru, with firms such as TCS, Infosys and Wipro.
Multinationals invest in India, encouraged by the Make in India programme of 2014, and Foxconn and Tata assemble a growing share of iPhones around Chennai and Bengaluru.
Impact 4 – Uneven benefits of trade:
About nine-tenths of Indian workers are in the informal sector, without contracts or pensions.
Agriculture still employs about 55% of workers and has low productivity.
Literacy was about 94% in Kerala and about 62% in Bihar, which shows inequality between states.
