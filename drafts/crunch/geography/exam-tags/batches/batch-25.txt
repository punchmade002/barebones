PART "7B" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named multinational company – Intel:
Factor 1 – The product life cycle:
Product life cycle theory says a product passes through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, and its best location changes at each stage.
Each Intel chip generation has a short life of a few years, so the company must keep developing new products.
New processes are developed at Hillsboro in Oregon, then copied exactly into fabs such as Leixlip under Intel's Copy Exactly policy.
Fab 34 at Leixlip, opened in 2023, makes the Intel 4 and Intel 3 generations, so Ireland keeps its place by winning each new generation.
Older fabs are upgraded or closed as products move into maturity and decline.
Intel missed the start of the smartphone and artificial intelligence chip cycles, which let rivals such as Nvidia take the lead in AI chips.
Apple began replacing Intel chips in its Mac computers with its own M1 chip in November 2020, which cut demand for a mature Intel product.
Falling profits led Intel to announce about 15,000 global job cuts in August 2024, an example of decline forcing rationalisation.
Factor 2 – Developments in information technology:
Global communication lets the headquarters at Santa Clara in California run fabs and assembly plants on several continents.
Computer-aided design and automation are used in the fab, which raises precision and cuts costs.
Moore's Law, stated by Gordon Moore in 1965, says the number of transistors on a chip doubles about every two years, so advances in IT drive the design and sales of the product.
Real-time stock control and just-in-time supply reduce the stock held at each plant.
Wafers processed at Leixlip are flown to assembly and test plants in Penang, Kulim, Ho Chi Minh City and Chengdu, which IT links into one production chain.
New technology changes demand, as the shift from PCs to smartphones, cloud computing and artificial intelligence creates new markets and destroys older ones.
Data centres need server chips such as Xeon for cloud computing, a market created by developments in IT.
Dependence on Asian supply chains meant the 2021 chip shortage, caused by COVID-19 disruption, halted car and electronics production worldwide.

PART "7C" (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named example 1 – Corrib gas, County Mayo:
The Corrib gas field lies about 80 km off north-west County Mayo and was discovered in 1996.
The economic case was energy security, a domestic gas supply, revenue for the State and the companies, and construction jobs.
After it first flowed in December 2015, Corrib for a time supplied over half of Ireland's gas.
The original plan to pipe high-pressure, unprocessed gas onshore near homes at Rossport created a safety risk for residents.
Sruwaddacon Bay, a protected estuary, lay on the pipeline route, so environmental interests were threatened.
The Shell to Sea campaign from 2005 argued that the gas should be processed at sea rather than onshore.
The Rossport Five were jailed in June 2005 for refusing to obey a court order, and the dispute divided the local community.
The conflict was eased by compromise, as the route was moved further from houses and a tunnel was bored under Sruwaddacon Bay.
Named example 2 – Amazon rainforest, Brazil:
Cattle ranching is the main driver of clearing in the Amazon, and most cleared land becomes pasture, with soya farming spreading in the south.
The Trans-Amazonian Highway, begun in 1970, opened the forest to settlers, and clearings along roads form a fishbone pattern, as in Rondônia.
Carajás in Pará has the world's largest iron-ore mine, run by Vale, and the Belo Monte dam on the Xingu River was completed in 2019 despite opposition from indigenous groups including the Kayapó.
The forest holds biodiversity, stores large amounts of carbon and recycles rainfall, so its loss is a global environmental concern.
Clearing fell by over 80% between 2004 and 2012 but rose again to about 13,000 km² in 2021, showing that policy swings shape the conflict.
Protection measures include the Forest Code, the 2006 soy moratorium and the Amazon Fund of 2008, which is paid for mainly by Norway and Germany.
Chico Mendes, a rubber tapper who campaigned to protect the forest, was murdered at Xapuri in 1988, showing that the conflict is between people as well as interests.
