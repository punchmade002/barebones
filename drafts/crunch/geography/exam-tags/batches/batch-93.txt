PART 9B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Internal trading pattern:
Most EU trade is internal, with about three-fifths of members' goods exports staying within the EU.
In 2024 intra-EU goods exports were about €4.0 trillion, against about €2.6 trillion of extra-EU goods exports.
Germany is by far the largest intra-EU trader, with intra-EU goods exports of about €845 billion in 2024.
The core of the EU, the Blue Banana from south-east England through the Benelux countries and the Rhine valley to northern Italy, dominates internal trade, while peripheral members such as Cyprus and Greece trade much less within the EU.
Eastern members depend most on intra-EU trade, which was about 81% of Czechia's goods trade in 2024 against about 30% for Cyprus.
Manufactured goods dominate internal trade, especially machinery, vehicles and parts, and chemicals, and much of it is intra-industry trade such as cars for cars.
Since 2004 the car industry has linked Germany with Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary and Poland, and Volkswagen has fully owned Škoda of Czechia since 2000.
Fruit and vegetables from Almería in southern Spain are trucked north while northern manufactured goods flow south and east.
Rotterdam and Antwerp are gateway ports, so goods recorded as trade with the Netherlands and Belgium may be going on to other member states.
External trading pattern:
The EU also trades heavily with the rest of the world, mainly with the USA, the UK and China.
The USA is the largest export market at about 21% of extra-EU goods exports, followed by the UK at about 13% and China at about 8%.
China is the largest source of imports at about a fifth of the total, followed by the USA at about 13 to 14%.
Extra-EU goods exports were about €2.58 trillion and imports about €2.43 trillion in 2024, a goods surplus of about €150 billion, with a surplus with the USA and a large deficit with China.
Exports are mainly machinery and vehicles (about 39%) and chemicals and pharmaceuticals (about 22%), while energy products made up about 15% of imports in 2024, down from about 23% in 2022.
Factor 1 – Removal of trade barriers and closer links:
The customs union removes tariffs between members, and the single market, in force since 1993, guarantees free movement of goods, services, capital and people.
Common standards such as the CE mark replace national rules and border checks are removed, which cuts costs and delays for traders.
Twenty-one countries used the euro from 1 January 2026, so trade among them has no exchange costs or exchange-rate risk.
Short distances and dense transport links such as the Rhine, the Channel Tunnel (1994) and the Øresund Bridge (2000) make internal trade cheap.
Enlargement in 2004 opened new markets and lower-cost production sites in the east, which increased trade with Germany.
Factor 2 – Market size, common policy and world events:
The single market of about 450 million wealthy consumers gives the EU strong bargaining power in trade negotiations.
Under the Common Commercial Policy the European Commission negotiates for all 27 members behind one common external tariff, and agreements such as CETA with Canada and the EU-Japan agreement widen external trade.
The EU lacks energy and some raw materials, so it depends on imports, and Russian gas fell from about 45% of EU gas imports in 2021 to about 19% in 2024 after sanctions following the invasion of Ukraine.
Low-cost manufacturing in China and Asia draws in large volumes of imports of electronics and other manufactured goods.
Brexit moved the UK from internal to external partner, and although the Trade and Cooperation Agreement of 2021 has zero tariffs on qualifying goods, customs checks and paperwork made trade more costly.
The EU-USA agreement of July 2025 capped the US tariff on most EU goods at 15%, which shows how politics redirects external trade.

PART 9C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named MNC – Intel:
Intel Corporation is an American multinational with its headquarters in Santa Clara, California, and chip fabrication plants in the USA, Ireland and Israel and assembly plants in Malaysia, Vietnam and China.
Factor 1 – Product life cycle:
Product life cycle theory, put forward by Raymond Vernon in 1966, says a product passes through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, and its production location changes at each stage.
Each Intel chip generation has a short life of a few years, so Intel must keep launching new generations to stay ahead.
New processes are developed first at Hillsboro, Oregon, close to headquarters, research staff and wealthy early customers.
Intel then copies each new process exactly into fabs such as Leixlip under its Copy Exactly policy, which copies equipment and procedures from the development fab.
Fab 34 at Leixlip opened in 2023 and makes the Intel 4 and Intel 3 generations, so Ireland keeps its place by winning each new generation.
As a generation matures, older fabs are upgraded to make newer chips or closed.
Finished wafers are flown to assembly and test plants in Penang and Kulim in Malaysia, Ho Chi Minh City in Vietnam and Chengdu in China, where the standardised later stages of production are cheaper.
Intel missed the start of the smartphone and AI chip cycles, Nvidia took the lead in AI chips and Apple dropped Intel chips from its Macs from 2020, so falling demand for mature products led to about 15,000 job cuts in 2024.
Factor 2 – Information technology:
Computer-aided design and automation are used in Intel's fabs to design and make chips with billions of tiny transistors.
Global communication lets headquarters in Santa Clara run plants on several continents as one company.
Real-time stock control and just-in-time supply reduce the stock held at each plant.
Moore's Law, stated by Intel co-founder Gordon Moore in 1965, says the number of transistors on a chip doubles about every two years, which forces constant new products and new fabs.
Developments in IT created new markets as demand moved from PCs to smartphones, cloud computing and AI, and Intel sells Xeon chips for the servers in data centres.
The same changes destroyed older markets, because PC demand slowed and rivals such as AMD, TSMC and Samsung competed for customers.
Intel's customers such as Lenovo, HP and Dell sell computers worldwide, so IT links connect Leixlip's output to markets in North America, Europe and Asia.
Extreme ultraviolet lithography machines from ASML in the Netherlands, the only maker of them, let Fab 34 make chips in high volume.

[geo-economic-eu-s1.0.b] A trading bloc is a group of countries that remove trade barriers between themselves and act together in trade with the rest of the world. The EU is the biggest example, and its two founding trade tools are a customs union and the single market.
[geo-economic-eu-s1.0.0] The EU accounted for about 13% of world goods trade in 2024, second to China in goods. Counting goods and services together, the EU is the world's largest trader, with China and the USA as the other two of the three largest.
[geo-economic-eu-s1.0.1] Customs union: completed on 1 July 1968 (as the EEC, the European Economic Community, the EU's earlier name), it removes tariffs (taxes on imports) between members and applies a common external tariff to imports from outside the EU, so every member charges the same rate.

[geo-economic-eu-s1.1.1] It removes non-tariff barriers (obstacles to trade other than taxes): border checks, different national product standards and national preference in public contracts (governments favouring their own firms).
[geo-economic-eu-s1.1.2] Common standards, such as the CE mark (a label showing a product meets EU rules), replace those barriers, so one product can be sold in every member state.
[geo-economic-eu-s1.2.0] Imports are goods or services bought from abroad; exports are goods or services sold abroad.
[geo-economic-eu-s1.2.4] Intra-EU (internal) trade is trade between member states; extra-EU (external) trade is trade with countries outside the EU.

[geo-economic-eu-s1.3.3] Peripheral members (those on the edge of the EU, far from the core), such as Cyprus and Greece, trade much less within the EU.

PART 10C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Impact of changing fertility rates:
Falling fertility narrows the base of the population pyramid, because fewer children are born in each age group.
When the total fertility rate falls below the replacement level of about 2.1, each generation is smaller than the one before, so the young dependency ratio falls.
Fertility falls because of female education and paid work, widely available contraception, later marriage and the higher cost of raising children.
By contrast, high fertility gives a wide base, as in Niger, where the total fertility rate is about 6 and the median age is about 15.
Impact of changing mortality rates:
Falling mortality, especially infant mortality and deaths at older ages, raises life expectancy and widens the top of the pyramid.
Falling fertility together with rising life expectancy gives an older population with a higher median age and a higher old-age dependency ratio.
Medical technology such as vaccines and antibiotics, clean water and a better diet reduce death rates.
When more babies survive and more adults live longer, the sides of the pyramid become straighter and the pyramid becomes top-heavy.
An ageing structure raises pension and health costs and leaves a smaller tax base of workers.
Example – Ireland:
Ireland's total fertility rate was about 4 in the early 1970s, fell below replacement level around 1990 and was about 1.5 in 2023.
Births peaked at about 74,000 in 1980 and were about 55,000 in 2023, so the base of the pyramid has narrowed.
Fertility fell because of free secondary education from 1967, the end of the marriage bar in 1973, the legalising of contraception in 1979 and more women in work.
Life expectancy at birth is now about 82 to 83 years and infant mortality is about 3 per 1,000 live births, which widens the top of the pyramid.
Census 2022 recorded about 776,000 people aged 65 and over, about 15% of the population and up about 22% since 2016.
Japan shows the extreme case, with a total fertility rate of about 1.2, about 29% of people aged 65 or over, and a top-heavy pyramid in a population that is falling from its peak of about 128 million.
