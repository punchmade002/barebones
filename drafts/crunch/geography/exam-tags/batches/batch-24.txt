PART 5A(ii) (4 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Safety: EU states offer protection from the war and persecution that applicants flee in countries such as Syria and Afghanistan.
They guarantee the rule of law and are bound by the 1951 Refugee Convention not to return applicants to places where they face persecution.

X = 100% - 71% = 29%.

PART 5A(iv) (8 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Challenge 1: Overcrowded reception accommodation.
Arrivals in 2015-16 far outnumbered the reception places built by member states, so many people had to be housed in emergency camps.
Challenge 2: Unequal burden between member states.
Under the Dublin Regulation the first state of arrival must process the application, so Greece and Italy carry most of the burden.

PART 5B (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named region – India:
Factor 1 – Cultural and historic attractions:
India has exceptional built heritage, such as the Taj Mahal at Agra, a marble tomb built in the 17th century by Shah Jahan that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
The Golden Triangle links Delhi, Agra and Jaipur, the Pink City of Rajasthan, and is a classic route for foreign visitors.
Religious sites such as Varanasi on the Ganges, the Golden Temple at Amritsar and the Kumbh Mela at Prayagraj draw spiritual tourists and pilgrims.
Factor 2 – Physical landscapes and climate:
The landscapes range from the beaches of Goa to Kerala's backwaters around Alappuzha and the Himalayas.
Wildlife parks such as Ranthambore in Rajasthan for tigers and Kaziranga in Assam for the one-horned rhinoceros attract nature tourists.
A warm, dry winter from October to March matches the European winter, so foreign arrivals peak in those months.
Factor 3 – Low costs and special interests:
Low costs for travellers make India attractive to visitors from Europe and beyond.
Spiritual tourism, yoga, Ayurveda and medical tourism add further markets, especially in Kerala.
Factor 4 – Government support:
The Incredible India marketing campaign began in 2002 to promote the country abroad.
E-visas, introduced in 2014, made entry easier for foreign tourists.
Airport expansion and low-cost airlines, led by IndiGo, have improved access to the main destinations.
About 11 million foreign tourist arrivals were recorded in 2019, before COVID-19, showing the success of these measures.
The Supreme Court has restricted polluting industry around the Taj Mahal to protect its marble.
Factor 5 – Transport links:
Indian Railways runs a network of about 68,000 km, one of the four largest in the world, which carries huge numbers of passengers.
The Golden Quadrilateral highway of about 5,800 km, built from 2001, links Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata.
Factor 6 – Colonial heritage:
Hill stations such as Shimla, summer capital of British India, and Darjeeling attract visitors escaping the heat.
Goa was a Portuguese colony until 1961 and combines colonial heritage with its beaches.

PART 5C (30 marks)
MODEL ANSWER: Named Irish region – Cork Harbour region:
Factor 1 – Raw materials:
Raw materials are the natural resources and inputs that a factory processes, and they strongly influence what industry locates in the Cork Harbour region.
Weight-losing raw materials pull processing towards their source or towards a port where they arrive, so plants cluster on the harbour.
The Port of Cork has deep-water berths at Ringaskiddy, which suit the import of bulk raw materials and chemicals by sea.
Whitegate, in Cork Harbour, is Ireland's only oil refinery, opened in 1959, and it processes crude oil imported by tanker.
The pharmaceutical plants at Ringaskiddy, such as Pfizer and Janssen, import chemical inputs and export finished medicines through the deep-water port.
Irish Distillers at Midleton makes Jameson whiskey close to the tillage farms of County Cork, which supply its barley.
Ireland has few industrial raw materials of its own, so Cork industry has specialised in processing agricultural and imported inputs rather than heavy resource-based manufacturing.
Pharmaceutical products are light and valuable compared with their raw materials, so the lack of local raw materials adds little to cost.
The Irish Steel works at Haulbowline in the harbour closed in 2001, showing how heavy industry based on bulky inputs declined while light, high-value processing grew.
Factor 2 – Markets:
Markets are the customers who buy a factory's products, and their size, wealth and accessibility shape where firms locate.
Cork is the second city of the State and provides a local market and a labour pool.
Membership of the EU gives plants in Cork tariff-free access to the single market of about 450 million people.
The Port of Cork at Ringaskiddy has ferry links to Roscoff in France, giving direct access to continental markets.
Cork Airport carries light, high-value goods and business travellers to overseas markets.
Pharmaceuticals and chemicals are Ireland's largest goods exports, and the plants of Cork Harbour sell mainly to markets abroad.
Pfizer at Ringaskiddy exports medicines to the USA and the EU, so the plant is located for access to large overseas markets by sea.
Irish Distillers at Midleton, owned by Pernod Ricard, sells Jameson in export markets around the world.
Ireland's time zone, between the USA and Asia, suits firms in Cork Harbour that serve markets on both sides.
