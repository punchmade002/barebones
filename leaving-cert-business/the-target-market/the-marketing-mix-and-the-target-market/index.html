Overview of the marketing mix

The marketing mix is the set of decisions a business controls in order to satisfy its target market, and this course uses seven Ps.

  • The seven Ps are product or service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process and place.
  • The Ps are interdependent: a premium price, for example, affects the packaging, the promotion and the choice of outlets.
  • The target market shapes each element, since what customers want, can afford and respond to determines the mix.
  • The mix determines whether customers buy, how much they pay and whether the business earns a profit.

Product or service

The product or service is the core offer, and decisions about it cover features, quality, branding and the range offered.

  • Decisions on features and quality set what the product does and how well it does it, judged against what the target market values.
  • Branding gives the product a name, identity and reputation that help customers recognise and trust it.
  • The product life cycle (assumed knowledge) runs through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, and each stage needs different marketing decisions.
  • A service differs from a product because it is intangible, variable and perishable, so its quality depends on the people delivering it.

Pricing

Pricing sets the amount customers pay, and the strategy chosen must reflect costs, competitors and what the target market will pay.

  • Cost-plus pricing adds a fixed mark-up to the cost of making or buying the product, which is simple but ignores what customers will pay.
  • Penetration pricing sets a low initial price to win market share quickly, often used for a new product in a competitive market.
  • Skimming pricing sets a high initial price to reach customers who will pay more, then lowers it as competition grows.
  • Competitive pricing sets prices in line with, or just below, rivals' prices, while premium pricing sets a higher price to signal quality or exclusivity.
  • Psychological pricing uses figures such as 9.99 euro, which can make a price appear lower than a round figure would.
  • Loss leader pricing sells one product below cost to attract customers who are expected to buy other, more profitable products.

Promotion

Promotion communicates the offer to the target market and persuades customers to buy, using several methods that can be combined.

  • Advertising is paid, non-personal communication through media such as television, radio, outdoor posters and online display.
  • Sales promotion uses short-term incentives such as discounts, coupons and competitions to encourage an immediate purchase.
  • Personal selling is one-to-one communication by a sales representative, which suits complex or high-value products.
  • Public relations builds goodwill through media coverage, press releases and events, and it is not paid for in the same way as advertising.
  • Sponsorship links a brand with an event, team or cause, so the brand's image benefits from the association.
  • Social media and influencer marketing create two-way engagement, with influencers paid to promote products to their own audiences.

People

People are the staff and others who deliver the product or service, and their skills, attitudes and behaviour shape the customer experience.

  • Recruitment and training ensure staff have the skills and knowledge to meet the expectations of the target market.
  • In service businesses the employee is often the part of the offer the customer experiences most directly, so attitude matters greatly.
  • Motivation and reward systems keep staff focused on service quality and reduce staff turnover.
  • Customer-facing staff should fit the image of the target market, such as knowledgeable advisers for a premium brand.

Packaging

Packaging protects the product and communicates with customers through its design, materials and labelling.

  • Functional packaging protects the product in storage and transport, and it makes the product easier for the customer to handle.
  • Design and colour create brand recognition and attract attention at the point of sale.
  • Labelling must give accurate information, such as ingredients, weight and use-by date, as required by law for many products.
  • Sustainable packaging, such as recyclable materials or reduced material use, can appeal to environmentally conscious customers.

Process

Process is the system by which the product or service is delivered, covering the steps, speed and ease of each stage for the customer.

  • Process decisions cover how orders are taken, how products are made or services delivered and how long each stage takes.
  • A simple, efficient process reduces waiting times and costs and improves the customer's experience.
  • Online ordering, self-service checkouts and tracked delivery are process choices that suit busy target markets.
  • A process must be consistent, so that customers receive the same quality each time they buy.

Place and channels of distribution

Place covers how the product reaches customers through a channel of distribution, which may be direct or indirect.

  • A direct channel sells from producer to customer, such as a farm shop or the business's own website.
  • An indirect channel uses intermediaries such as wholesalers and retailers, such as a manufacturer selling through supermarkets.
  • Online channels allow sale and delivery without physical outlets, widening reach but adding delivery costs.
  • Location decisions for a physical outlet depend on where the target market shops, travels and works.

Key terms

marketing mix
The set of controllable decisions a business makes about its offer, price, promotion and distribution to meet target market needs.
product life cycle
The stages a product passes through, from introduction to growth, maturity and decline.
cost-plus pricing
A pricing method that adds a fixed mark-up to the cost of making or buying a product.
penetration pricing
Setting a low initial price to win market share quickly, usually for a new product.
skimming pricing
Setting a high initial price to attract customers willing to pay more, then lowering it as competition grows.
loss leader
A product sold below cost to attract customers who are expected to buy other, more profitable products.
psychological pricing
Pricing designed to influence how customers perceive a price, such as ending it in .99 or .95.
sales promotion
Short-term incentives such as discounts, coupons and competitions that encourage customers to buy now.
channel of distribution
The route a product takes from producer to end customer, which may include wholesalers and retailers.
influencer marketing
Partnering with people who have an online audience so they promote a product to their followers.
