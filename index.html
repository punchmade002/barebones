Biology, Enzymes

Enzymes as catalysts

Catalysts and enzymes

  • A catalyst is a substance that speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up or permanently changed by it, so it can be used again and again.
  • An enzyme is a biological catalyst: a catalyst made by a living cell.
  • Almost all enzymes are globular proteins, folded into a precise three-dimensional shape.
  • Enzymes are needed only in small amounts, because each molecule is reused many times; catalase, among the fastest, breaks down millions of hydrogen peroxide molecules per second.

Chemistry, Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons: sources, uses and impact

Organic compounds and hydrocarbons

  • Organic chemistry is the study of the compounds of carbon, and hydrocarbons are the simplest of them.
  • Each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds and carbon atoms bond strongly to each other, giving stable chains, straight or branched, and rings of almost any length.
  • A hydrocarbon is a compound containing carbon and hydrogen only.
  • Other organic families, such as alcohols and carboxylic acids, can be thought of as hydrocarbons with one or more hydrogen atoms replaced by other atoms or groups.

Geography, Economic activity

Renewable and non-renewable resources

Resources and how they are classed

  • A resource is anything in the environment that people use to meet their needs. Resources are classed by whether nature can replace them.
  • Non-renewable (stock, finite) resources formed over millions of years, so they are used up far faster than they form: coal, oil, natural gas, peat, metal ores and uranium.
  • Renewable (flow) resources are replaced naturally and will not run out however much is used: wind, solar, hydro, wave, tidal and geothermal heat.
  • Some resources are renewable only if managed sustainably: forests, fish stocks, soils, groundwater and biomass.

History, Nationalism and state formation in Europe

Italy moves towards unity, 1815–1871

Key personality: Giuseppe Mazzini

  • Mazzini was born in Genoa in 1805, trained as a lawyer and joined the Carbonari before his arrest and exile in 1830.
  • In 1831 he founded Young Italy at Marseille, a secret society that aimed at a unified republic and whose members were mostly young.
  • He wanted a unitary republic, with the people as the sovereign, and his motto was God and the People (Dio e Popolo).
  • In 1848 he returned to Italy and took part in the Roman Republic of 1849, where he was one of the triumvirs who governed the city.
